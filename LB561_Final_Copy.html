Summary

The bill assigns the Department of Transportation (DOT) these responsibilities and authorities: issue overweight raw‑milk vehicle permits under section 60‑6,298; make permit applications available in county treasurers’ offices; create a statewide “bridge map” (by Jan. 1, 2026), publish it on DOT’s public website, and review it annually; revoke individual permits for violations and revoke all overweight raw‑milk permits if DOT determines federal aid is at risk; initiate legal actions to recover bridge‑repair costs when overweight raw‑milk vehicles damage prohibited bridges, with recovered monies remitted to the Roads Operations Cash Fund; and grant limited vehicle‑length exceptions to meet axle‑weight limits. The bill also contemplates DOT’s existing permit fee and application processes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates an “overweight raw‑milk vehicle” permitting regime administered by the Department of Transportation (DOT). Key DOT duties and powers:

- Define and enforce permit conditions (permit under §60‑6,298); vehicles limited to 107,500 lbs and must meet axle/group limits under the federal bridge formula (23 U.S.C. 127, as of 1/1/2025).

- Must carry and obey a DOT “bridge map” of prohibited bridges, which DOT must create by Jan 1, 2026, review annually, and publish online.

- May revoke individual permits for violations (with written notice and APA appeal rights) and may revoke all permits if permits jeopardize federal aid. DOT can authorize interstate travel only with FHWA written approval.

- DOT may sue to recover bridge‑repair costs from responsible owners; recovered funds are credited to the Roads Operations Cash Fund.

- DOT may grant vehicle‑length exceptions to meet axle weight limits.

No new appropriations or other budget allocations are specified beyond crediting recovered repair proceeds to the Roads Operations Cash Fund.
