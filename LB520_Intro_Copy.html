Summary

LB520 amends provisions affecting bed and breakfast establishments and assigns duties to state agencies. The State Fire Marshal must incorporate bed-and-breakfast-specific provisions into the State Fire Code, review plans, enforce the Code via inspections and orders, and apply existing plan‑review fee authority. The bill requires the (state) department that administers the Nebraska Pure Food Act to offer a food‑handling certification exam and to enforce food safety requirements for bed and breakfasts (storage, temperatures, prohibitions, sanitation, etc.). New fire‑safety mandates include smoke/heat alarms, emergency lighting, egress requirements (or sprinklers), extinguishers, and kitchen fire suppression for larger operations; establishments have 18 months to add a secondary egress or a sprinkler system. No changes to state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of "bed and breakfast" and extensive health and safety requirements. It repeatedly assigns roles to "the department" (unnamed): approving alternative thawing methods, prescribing food-safety standards (temperatures, storage, prohibited home-canned goods, use of pasteurized Grade A milk), offering and administering the food-handler certification exam, and requiring certification for operators. Fire/safety rules require smoke/heat detectors, emergency lighting, primary and secondary egress (with exemptions if sprinklered), an 18‑month compliance window to add egress or sprinklers, specified fire extinguishers, and commercial kitchen fire-suppression hoods for B&Bs serving hot meals to more than ten guests. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
