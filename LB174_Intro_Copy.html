Summary

LB174 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §25‑1558 (wage garnishment). It preserves existing garnishment limits for most creditors (generally the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings, the amount exceeding 30× the federal minimum hourly wage, or 15% for a head of family) and court exceptions for support orders, Chapter 13 bankruptcy, and tax debts. It creates a separate garnishment framework for medical debt sought by “medical debt buyers” or “medical creditors,” setting the maximum garnishable portion at the lesser of: 10% of disposable earnings, the 30× federal‑wage threshold, or 20% where the debtor is shown not to be a head of family by sworn affidavit. The bill adds statutory definitions (including medical debt, medical debt buyer/creditor, and health care services), directs the Commissioner of Labor to set equivalent rules for nonweekly pay periods, and repeals the original section. No state funding or appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds caps and definitions for garnishment of wages for medical debt. Garnishment may not exceed the lesser of: (a) 10% of disposable weekly earnings; (b) the amount by which weekly disposable earnings exceed 30 times the federal minimum hourly wage (citing 29 U.S.C. 206(a)(1)); or (c) 20% of disposable earnings if the debtor is not head of household, as alleged by the creditor in a sworn affidavit. New definitions: “health care services,” “medical debt,” “medical debt buyer,” and “medical creditor.” The provision cites the federal minimum-wage statute (29 U.S.C. 206(a)(1), administered by the U.S. Department of Labor). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budgets are included.
