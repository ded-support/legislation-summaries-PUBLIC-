Summary

LB620 creates the Neighborhood Revitalization Act, authorizing district courts to bring in-rem actions declaring blighted real property a public nuisance and to appoint certified receivers. Key government actors and duties:

- District courts: receive petitions, determine nuisance status, appoint receivers, approve plans, establish and enforce receiver's liens, and conduct auctions per court rules.

- Municipal corporations (cities, villages, counties): notified of filings, must inspect when needed, provide periodic updates to the court, may enter and secure subject parcels and charge owners for costs, and have discretionary enforcement authority.

- County offices (clerk; register's office/register of deeds): receive filings (notice of lien/lis pendens, court orders) and record receiver deeds and liens.

- Receivers: required to satisfy delinquent property tax liens within 30 days and may pay outstanding municipal fines and taxes from receivership funds.

No explicit changes to state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures giving courts (district courts) authority to deem real property a public nuisance, appoint certified receivers, and supervise abatement. Municipal corporations (cities/towns) must be notified, may inspect subject parcels, may enter and secure property, provide periodic updates to the court, and may recover costs from owners. County registrars/ registers of deeds must receive and record notices (lien lis pendens) and receiver's deeds. Receiver liens are prioritized over other encumbrances (except general property tax liens), accrue interest at the statutory property tax rate, secure receiver expenses and a fee (10% or at least $2,500), and are satisfied from auction proceeds; receivers must pay delinquent property tax liens within 30 days. No new appropriations or state budget/agency funding changes are created.
