Summary

The bill centralizes parole and reentry authority in the Department of Correctional Services (DOC), abolishing the Division of Parole Supervision and its director and reallocating duties to the DOC and Director of Correctional Services. DOC must administer parole services, supervise parole officers, develop risk/needs assessments and training, operate a pilot residential program for technical violations, expand reentry planning and lifetime community‑supervision procedures, and partner with probation on statewide community correctional/reentry facilities. By July 1, 2026 DOC must adopt a private‑provider procurement plan; the Reentry Continuity Advisory Board reports annually (first due Oct. 1, 2025). Key fiscal changes: intent to appropriate $1,000,000 GF for Community Work Release and Reentry Centers and a $25/month parole programming fee credited to a Parole Program Cash Fund that DOC may use and invest. Department of Banking and Finance must maintain/process service‑of‑process filings and is granted immunity. DHHS gains data access, licensing/EMPC certificate authority, maintenance of firearm‑disability/NICS data, and limits on overdose‑review disclosures; labs must forward paternity testing audits to DHHS. Department of Transportation must verify tourist‑oriented sign applicants’ anti‑discrimination compliance. Department of Revenue employees are excluded from the law‑enforcement definition under §77‑366.
