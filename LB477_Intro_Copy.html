Summary

LB477 amends the Workplace Privacy Act (sections 48-3502 and 48-3503). It bars employers from requiring employees or applicants to provide usernames/passwords, log into personal internet accounts, add employers to contacts, or change account privacy settings. It also prohibits mandatory use of apps or wearable electronic devices to track employee location, travel patterns, or contacts, except during a pandemic state of emergency proclaimed by the Governor under §81-829.40 — and then only for on‑site contact tracing during work hours, with data destroyed within 48 hours after the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s recommended tracing period. Data that associates an employee with tracked employer property must be stored by an independent third party and accessed only by warrant. The bill references the Governor and the CDC. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition on employers requiring employees to use or wear electronic tracking apps/devices, with a narrow exception if the Governor issues a pandemic state‑of‑emergency proclamation under §81‑829.40 that expressly allows employer contact tracing. Conditions: tracing only on employer premises during work hours; collected data excluded from personnel files and destroyed within 48 hours after the end of any tracing period recommended by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Data linking an employee to tracked property must be stored by an independent third party and is accessible only by warrant. The Governor (state executive) and the CDC are the only government entities specifically referenced. No funding, appropriation, or budget changes are included.
