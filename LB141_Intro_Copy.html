Summary

LB141 defines "Department" as the Department of Health and Human Services and assigns specific duties to it: conducting investigations and opening non‑court‑involved cases; maintaining an internal Review, Evaluate, and Decide Team; determining whether a report subject is a member of a military family and, if not unfounded, notifying appropriate military authorities and military family advocacy programs; negotiating memoranda of understanding with military family advocacy programs to set identification, reporting, and confidentiality protocols; and adopting rules to implement these requirements. The bill requires the department (along with law enforcement) to send written notice of investigation outcomes and preserves a subject's right to ask the department to amend or expunge central registry information. The department is also authorized access to the child protection tracking system and central registry as required by law. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and coordination duties for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) regarding reports of child abuse/neglect involving military families. Key points for DHHS:

- Defines "member of a military family" (active duty, reserve, National Guard, or dependent under 50 U.S.C. 3911) and defines "military family advocacy program" and "military installation."

- Requires DHHS, on receiving a child abuse/neglect report, to determine military-family status and, if not unfounded, notify appropriate military authorities and any military family advocacy program and share pertinent information.

- Requires DHHS to negotiate memoranda of understanding with military family advocacy programs at Nebraska installations setting procedures for identification, reporting, and confidentiality, and authorizes DHHS to adopt necessary rules.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
