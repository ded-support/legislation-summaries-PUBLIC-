Summary

LB668 assigns or clarifies multiple duties and authorities for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS):

- DHHS must notify subjects before entering names on the Adult Protective Services central registry or the central registry of child protection cases, and a timely appeal request prevents entry until DHHS issues a final order. DHHS may amend/expunge identifying information or remove substantiated reports per statutory procedures.

- DHHS shall maintain the child protection central registry, process name‑change cross‑references, and annually report registry data to the Governor and the Health and Human Services and Judiciary committee chairs.

- DHHS may charge up to $5 per central‑registry records check, remit fees to the State Treasurer for credit to the Health and Human Services Cash Fund, waive fees for hardship, and use fees to defray costs.

- Case managers for juveniles in DHHS care must be DHHS employees, except contracted providers may serve for defined prevention cases; DHHS case manager duties are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) must notify individuals at least 14 days before entering their name on the central registries (vulnerable adult or child protection) and inform them of the right to contest. Subjects have 14 days to appeal; appeals are handled under the Administrative Procedure Act, and if a timely appeal is filed DHHS may not enter the name until it issues a final order. DHHS may also use contracted providers as case managers for "prevention cases," defined by the department’s Families First prevention plan as: a household with at least one juvenile who meets the department’s candidate-for-foster-care criteria, no need for ongoing child-welfare services to maintain safety, and voluntary family engagement in community services. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
