Summary

LB113 amends provisions of the Nebraska Liquor Control Act and assigns responsibilities to the Nebraska Liquor Control Commission and local governing bodies. The commission must be notified and may authorize offsite storage facilities and rickhouses, adopt rules on craft brewery and microdistillery distribution rights, and process entertainment-district license applications (with filing copies sent to local clerks). Local governing bodies may designate or revoke entertainment districts, receive filings, collect an additional $300 entertainment-district license fee payable to the local clerk, impose an occupation tax under existing law, and cancel licenses for cause. The bill sets operational and safety requirements for commons areas and allows specified licensees up to eight licensed locations. No appropriations or other funding/budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added language sets conditions for streets/openings associated with an “entertainment district license.” It requires reasonable pedestrian safety measures (signage, lighting, reduced motor-vehicle speeds where vehicles are near pedestrians) and explicitly prohibits carrying open alcoholic beverage containers or consuming alcohol on any open street or highway, except when briefly crossing at a designated crosswalk. No government agencies, departments, funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are referenced or changed.
