Summary

LB706 requires law enforcement agencies to screen dispatch calls for mental health indicators, flag them as "Mental Health Priority," notify and, unless unsafe or impracticable, dispatch an APS social worker (Department of Health and Human Services) to accompany officers and lead de-escalation and trauma-informed interactions. The Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice must set minimum policy standards, may provide a model policy, and will annually review agency compliance within its existing appropriations (including grant funds) and report to the Governor and Legislature. Noncompliant agencies become ineligible for loans, grants, funds, or donations administered by the commission until corrected. The Law Enforcement Training Council (under the commission) may suspend or revoke officer certificates for willful failure to comply; administrative fines remain authorized and are collectible by the Attorney General. No new appropriations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires all law enforcement agencies to screen dispatch calls with a checklist for signs of mental illness, flag qualifying incidents as a "Mental Health Priority," notify the nearest unit and an APS social worker, share mental‑health information with responders, and—unless unsafe—have officers accompanied by an APS social worker who takes the lead using de‑escalation/trauma‑informed care. Agencies must adopt written dispatch policies and file them with the commission, which will set minimum standards and may offer a model policy. The commission will annually review compliance within existing appropriations (including grants), report to the Governor and Legislature, and may bar noncompliant agencies from receiving loans, grants, funds, or donations it administers until corrected.
