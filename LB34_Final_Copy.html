Summary

LB34 revises Nebraska statutes governing time and related administrative deadlines. Key agency/office impacts:

- State-level: Director of Personnel retains rulemaking authority to implement vacation/sick‑leave provisions; heads of employing agencies must provide opportunity to use vacation or pay forfeited leave; Governor may extend annual‑leave deadlines in special cases; Executive Board of the Legislative Council holds analogous discretion for legislative employees.

- Retirement: Public Employees Retirement Board — cash payment for denied forfeited vacation counts as compensation only for defined‑contribution or cash‑balance plans, not other defined‑benefit plans.

- Elections: Poll opening/closing and ballot receipt times are aligned to mountain or central time for judges and clerks of election.

- Federal roles: operability of year‑round daylight saving/standard time tied to actions/orders by U.S. Secretaries of Transportation, Commerce, and Navy and adoption by three adjacent states.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a new state standard-time provision: Nebraska would set standard time at UTC−5 in the central zone and UTC−6 in the mountain zone. It defines "Coordinated Universal Time" as the scale maintained by the General Conference on Weights and Measures and interpreted/modified for the U.S. by the U.S. Secretary of Commerce in coordination with the U.S. Secretary of the Navy. The provision only becomes operative after (1) authorization by U.S. law or an order of the U.S. Secretary of Transportation (or no longer being in conflict with them) and (2) three adjacent states adopt a single year‑round standard time. It also states the section becomes null and void if subsection (2) of §49‑1301 becomes operative. No agencies are assigned funding, and no appropriations or budget allocations are specified.
