Summary

LB135 standardizes election procedures for school-district-related matters by requiring many bond, levy, budget override, and excess-levy questions involving school districts or educational service units to be submitted in compliance with subsection (3) of §32-559. It clarifies roles: school boards initiate notices; election commissioners or county clerks conduct elections and certify results; county canvassing boards canvass; the Secretary of State and Auditor of Public Accounts receive specified filings; and the Property Tax Administrator receives levy-allocation reports. Joint entities and joint public agencies that include school districts/ESUs must submit bond questions to each district’s electors (50-day notice). Budget/levy provisions (§13-519, §77-3444, §79-1029) retain voter-approval processes but now reference §32-559(3). Fiscal change: election commissioners/county clerks may bill school districts directly for the costs of special elections under §32-559. Several original sections are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds detailed procedures for school-district elections and assigns responsibilities to specific agencies: school boards must file/ certify ballot questions by set deadlines (March 1 for statewide primary, Sept. 1 for statewide general, eighth Friday before a local general election). Election commissioners or county clerks conduct and count ballots (with the receiving board and two “disinterested persons”), furnish early-voting ballots 21 days prior, and handle intercounty registration-book certifications and early-ballot requests. Notices: first-class mail at least 30 days and a sample ballot published 7–15 days before the vote. When districts cross counties, county clerks must cooperate and the school board must certify out‑of‑county voter lists five days before the election. Pay: the two disinterested persons must receive at least the hourly minimum wage specified in section 48‑1203. No other funding or appropriation changes are included.
