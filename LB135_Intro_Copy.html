Summary

LB135 harmonizes election procedures across multiple statutes by requiring many school-related and overlapping political-subdivision ballot questions to be held "in compliance with subsection (3) of section 32-559." Affected entities include school boards, Nebraska school districts, educational service units, joint entities and joint public agencies, participating municipalities/counties, and a wide range of political subdivisions (cities, villages, townships, public power districts, NRDs, community college areas, hospital districts, etc.).

Key agency roles and changes:

- Election commissioners and county clerks: designated to conduct school bond and related school elections (including counties with the greatest number of district electors), handle certification, early voting, ballot counting, canvass certification, cancellations/withdrawals, and may bill school districts directly for special-election costs.

- Secretary of State: receives cancellation/withdrawal notices by specified deadlines.

- County canvassing boards: conduct canvass with same effect as governing bodies.

- Property Tax Administrator: receives levy-allocation reports for joint public agencies.

- Auditor of Public Accounts: receives records when resident meetings approve allowable-growth exceedances.

No direct appropriations or new funding allocations are created; the bill clarifies election timing, notice, and administrative responsibilities.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds detailed election procedures for school-district ballot questions, repeatedly requiring compliance with subsection (3) of §32‑559. Agencies specified: school boards, election commissioners, county clerks, the receiving/counting board, and two “disinterested persons” appointed by the election commissioner or county clerk. Key provisions: deadlines to file/certify ballot questions (March 1 for primary, Sept. 1 for general, or eighth Friday before a local general election); school boards must give first‑class mail notice ≥30 days before and publish a sample ballot 7–15 days prior (election commissioners/county clerks need not give notice); election commissioners/county clerks conduct and count ballots with two appointees; early‑voting ballots must be ready 21 days prior; intercounty certification/transfer of registration books and early‑vote requests; school boards must certify out‑of‑county voter lists five days before. The two appointees must be paid at least the minimum hourly rate in §48‑1203. No new appropriations or funding allocations are added.
