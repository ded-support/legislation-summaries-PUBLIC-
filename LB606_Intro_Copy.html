Summary

LB606 centralizes parole and community supervision authority in the Department of Correctional Services (DCS), abolishing the Division of Parole Supervision and its director and expanding DCS duties (custody, parole administration, recordkeeping, risk‑and‑needs assessment, training, reentry planning, sanctions, officer responsibilities). DCS must set standards and monitor community correctional facilities and reporting centers, may contract with private providers for work‑release/reentry centers, and collect/share parole data. Parole programming fees are credited to a Parole Program Cash Fund controlled by DCS. The Legislature intends a $1,000,000 General Fund appropriation for the Community Work Release and Reentry Centers Act. The Department of Health and Human Services may release health/social‑services records for overdose fatality reviews or licensing subpoenas subject to HIPAA and other federal limits, with requests due within 30 days (extensions allowed); subpoenas may be issued by a county attorney or the Attorney General. The Department of Revenue’s employees are excluded from the statute’s definition of “law enforcement officer.”

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Correctional Services: New language requires the department to maintain all records and files for the Board of Parole, including records for individuals under lifetime community supervision (sec. 83-174.03), while clarifying it may still keep daily records/files for the Board of Pardons. Provisions also authorize DOC to assess and implement operational, treatment, medical, or vocational programming in facilities when such services will substantially improve an offender’s capacity for law‑abiding reentry.

Department of Health and Human Services & Department of Revenue: No substantive additions or funding, appropriation, or budget allocations affecting these agencies appear in the added text.
