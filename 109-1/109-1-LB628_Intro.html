Summary

LB628 adopts the Recreational Trail Easement Property Tax Exemption Act and amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §§76‑214 and 77‑202. Key agency actions:

- Tax Commissioner: prescribes the recording statement form and rules/regulations for forwarding statements.

- Register of Deeds: continues to require and forward prescribed statements to the county assessor; exceptions added so recorded easements and mineral leases generally need no statement except for conservation, preservation, and easements that seek the new recreational trail exemption.

- County Assessor & Property Tax Administrator: county assessors must process statements per instructions of the Property Tax Administrator and forward to the Tax Commissioner.

- State Records Administrator: retains records retention/disposition oversight.

Section 77‑202 is amended to add a property tax exemption for a portion of property as provided by the new Act. Operative date: January 1, 2026. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included. Original sections are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements that guidance address: alignment with public access and conservation goals; procedures for certifying the public benefit of qualifying easements (including connectivity to existing or planned trail systems); and methods to ensure ongoing compliance with the act’s public-purpose requirements. It also clarifies that easements (and a portion of taxpayer-owned property) may qualify for the property tax exemption under the Recreational Trail Easement Property Tax Exemption Act. No specific government agencies are named and no funding or appropriation changes are included.
