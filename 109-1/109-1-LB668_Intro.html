Summary

LB668 amends multiple statutes involving the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). Key provisions referencing DHHS:

- DHHS must notify subjects of adult- and child-protection investigations of the right to contest and may amend/expunge identifying information or remove substantiated reports; subjects cannot be entered on registries while a timely appeal is pending and final DHHS order is outstanding.

- DHHS maintains the central registry of child protection cases, will cross-reference court-ordered name changes, and must report annually to the Governor and legislative committee chairs on minor-subject entries.

- DHHS may charge a records-check fee (not to exceed $5), remit receipts to the State Treasurer for credit to the Health and Human Services Cash Fund, waive fees for hardship, and use fees to defray records-check costs.

- Case managers for DHHS juvenile custody and voluntary cases must be DHHS employees, except prevention cases may use contracted providers.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Health and Human Services must notify any person before entering them on adult or child protection registries of their right to contest. A subject has 14 calendar days after the department’s determination notice to appeal; if a proper appeal is filed, the department may not enter the person’s name on the registry until a final departmental order is issued. Appeals proceed under the Administrative Procedure Act. The bill also allows a case manager for “prevention cases” to be a contracted provider and defines a prevention case as one where (1) at least one juvenile meets the department’s “candidate for foster care” criteria under the Families First Prevention Services Act (section 471(e)(4)), (2) ongoing child welfare services are not required to keep the child safe, and (3) the family voluntarily engages community services. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
