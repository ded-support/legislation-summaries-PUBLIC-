Summary

The bill assigns administration of the Kratom Consumer Protection Act to the Department of Revenue (registration, applications, rulemaking), authorizes third‑party testing by Department‑selected labs, civil penalties (first up to $1,000; second up to $5,000; third+ $5,000–$20,000 and possible three‑year sales bar), forfeiture of registration for convictions/false statements, notices and Tax Commissioner hearings, and allows use of the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund for Act enforcement.

It (re)establishes the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund and directs at least 80% of tax‑amnesty receipts to the General Fund and 10% to the Enforcement Fund (capped at $500,000; excess to General Fund). For FY2005‑06 all Enforcement Fund proceeds are appropriated to the Department to hire investigators/agents/auditors; thereafter limited ongoing transfers (including up to $750,000 tied to additional revenue and 10% of certain contract proceeds). The Fund may receive specified statutory receipts (including Kratom Act receipts), and the Department must report annually on amnesty and enforcement results.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Revenue focus):

- The bill defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and vests it with primary regulatory authority over kratom products.

- The Department may adopt rules and regulations, require independent third‑party testing of suspected adulterated kratom products by a laboratory selected by the Department, and impose civil penalties (up to $1,000 first, $5,000 second, $5,000–$20,000 for third+; processors can be barred from in‑state sales for three years).

- Hearings on alleged violations are to be handled by the Tax Commissioner.

- Forfeited registration certificates and related monies (from registration/forfeiture) are expressly directed to be used by the Department of Revenue to defray costs of administering the Act.
