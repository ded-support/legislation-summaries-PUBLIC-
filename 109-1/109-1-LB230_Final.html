Summary

The bill designates the Department of Revenue as the lead regulator for the Kratom Consumer Protection Act, empowering it to adopt rules, require independent third‑party lab testing of suspected adulterated kratom products, hold hearings via the Tax Commissioner, and impose civil penalties on processors/retailers (up to $1,000 first, $5,000 second, $5,000–$20,000 third+; third+ violations may trigger a three‑year processor sales ban). It (re)establishes the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund with detailed deposit and use rules: 80% of tax‑amnesty receipts to the General Fund, 10% (capped at $500,000) to the Enforcement Fund (excess to General Fund). For FY2005‑06 all Enforcement Fund proceeds are appropriated to the Department to hire investigators/agents/auditors; thereafter up to 20% of revenue attributable to those hires (capped at $750,000) may be deposited for continued staffing. Ten percent of §77‑367 contract proceeds, specified credits (including costs to implement the Kratom Act), transfers, and discretionary legislative transfers may also fund the Enforcement Fund. Investment earnings are credited to the General Fund beginning Oct. 1, 2024.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added text designates "Department" as the Department of Revenue and assigns it primary administrative and enforcement duties under the Kratom Consumer Protection Act. The Department may adopt rules, require independent third‑party testing by a laboratory of its choosing, impose civil penalties (up to $1,000 first, $5,000 second, $5,000–$20,000 third+; processors face three‑year sales prohibition on repeat offense), and forfeit registration certificates and associated fees for convictions or false application statements. Determinations require notice and, if requested, a hearing before the Tax Commissioner. Forfeited registration monies are to be used by the Department of Revenue to defray administration costs of the Act. The Attorney General also has enforcement authority; no private cause of action is created.
