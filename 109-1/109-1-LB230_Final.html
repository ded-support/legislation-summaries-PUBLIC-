Summary

The bill defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and assigns it primary administration and enforcement of the Kratom Consumer Protection Act, including rulemaking, requiring independent third‑party lab testing of suspected adulterated kratom products, and imposing civil penalties (up to $1,000 first, up to $5,000 second, $5,000–$20,000 for third and subsequent violations plus a three‑year processor sales prohibition). Violations require notice and, if requested, a hearing before the Tax Commissioner. The measure creates a Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund: tax‑amnesty receipts split (≥80% General Fund; 10% to the Enforcement Fund, capped $500,000; excess to General Fund); FY2005‑06 proceeds are appropriated to the Department to hire investigators/agents/auditors. Thereafter, 20% of new revenue attributable to those hires (cap $750,000) and 10% of specified contract proceeds are deposited to the Fund. The Fund may receive transfers/credits; investment earnings (beginning Oct 1, 2024) flow to the General Fund. The Department must issue annual program reports.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Revenue focus):

- "Department" is defined as the Department of Revenue.

- The Department may require an independent third‑party test of a suspected adulterated kratom product by a laboratory of the Department’s choosing.

- The Department may adopt and promulgate rules to implement the Kratom Consumer Protection Act.

- The Department is authorized to impose civil penalties on processors/retailers (up to $1,000 first, up to $5,000 second, $5,000–$20,000 for third/subsequent violations) and, for processors on a third violation, ban sales in-state for three years. Determinations require notice and a Tax Commissioner hearing if requested.

- Money paid for kratom registration certificates (including forfeited fees) shall be used by the Department of Revenue to defray administrative costs of the Act.
