Summary

The bill gives the Department of Economic Development (DED) lead authority over Good Life Transformational Projects and districts: confidential review, approval, monitoring, termination, limits on number/size, required evidence of commitments and annual reporting, transmission of approved district maps, retailer lists and ownership updates to cities and the Department of Revenue (DOR), and rulemaking authority. The Tax Commissioner (DOR) must begin monthly remittance to cities on Oct 1, 2025, of "allocated sales taxes" (generally 50% of specified state sales tax from qualifying transactions/retailers), provide parcel‑level remittance reports to cities and DED, offset refunds from allocations, and enforce recapture returning unencumbered balances to the General Fund on district termination. Both agencies may share confidential information. No other direct appropriations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Economic Development (DED)

- May adopt rules to implement the Good Life Transformational/Good Life District Acts, review and approve project area applications, require applicant reports (financial viability, land/control, ownership), and transmit approved district maps, retailer lists, and ownership updates to the Department of Revenue.

- May terminate districts pre-program on request; unencumbered amounts in a terminated district's development fund must be recaptured to the General Fund.

Department of Revenue / Tax Commissioner

- State sales tax rate set at 5.5% beginning Oct. 1, 2025.

- Creates "allocated sales taxes": after Oct. 1, 2025, 50% of specified state sales taxes collected in good life districts will be allocated to the city’s good life district economic development fund (with detailed categories, a $5 million yearly cap on certain relocated-retailer allocations, and carve-outs/exceptions).

- Tax Commissioner must remit allocated funds monthly (to trustee banks for project areas), deduct refunds or offset them from local remittance, supply confidential parcel/retailer remittance reports to the city and DED, and notify cities of monthly offsets; unencumbered allocated funds remain subject to statutory recapture.
