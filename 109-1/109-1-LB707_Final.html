Summary

The bill assigns administration of the Good Life District Economic Development Act primarily to the Department of Economic Development (DED) and the Department of Revenue (Tax Commissioner). DED is authorized to review and approve project/district applications, receive applicant materials, transmit approved district maps, retailer lists and ownership updates to the Department of Revenue, adopt rules, and terminate districts that fail investment benchmarks. When a district is terminated, unencumbered funds in its economic development fund are recaptured to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund. The Tax Commissioner must allocate and remit specified portions of state sales tax (generally 50% for categories defined after Oct. 1, 2025) to cities monthly, deducting or offsetting refunds, and provide detailed monthly reports to cities and DED. Both agencies may share confidential information to administer the Act. The bill contains no explicit appropriations or departmental budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- The Department of Economic Development (DED) and Department of Revenue (DOR) may share confidential information and DED may adopt rules to implement the Good Life Transformational/Good Life District Economic Development Acts.

- Beginning Oct. 1, 2025, the Tax Commissioner will allocate "allocated sales taxes" (50% of certain state sales taxes) to cities as local revenue for Good Life District programs. Allocations include: 50% from new-to-market retailers in the district; 50% from existing district retailers/applicants up to a $5M annual cap (with limited exceptions); and 50% of state sales taxes paid on qualifying new development costs. The Tax Commissioner remits monthly (after deducting refunds and offsetting any uncovered refunds from local remittances), provides confidential parcel-level reports to the city and DED, and deposited funds for project areas into trustee bank accounts. Unencumbered amounts are subject to recapture to the General Fund. The state sales tax rate is set to 5.5% effective Oct. 1, 2025.
