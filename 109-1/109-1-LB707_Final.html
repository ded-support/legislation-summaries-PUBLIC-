Summary

The bill designates the "Department" as the Department of Economic Development (DED) and gives DED authority to review, approve and monitor Good Life district and project applications, receive applicant reports, approve boundary adjustments, terminate districts that fail thresholds, and adopt rules to implement the Act. DED must transmit district maps, retailer lists, and ownership updates to the Department of Revenue (DOR). The DED and DOR may share confidential information as needed to administer the Act. Beginning October 1, 2025, the Tax Commissioner (DOR) will allocate and remit specified "allocated sales taxes"—generally 50% of certain state sales taxes from qualifying retailers—monthly to cities for Good Life district programs, provide detailed remittance reports to cities and DED, deduct refunds and offset uncovered refunds from local remittances. Unencumbered city Good Life funds may be recaptured to the General Fund on district termination.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions affecting the agencies:

- Department of Economic Development (DED): may adopt rules and guidance for both the Good Life Transformational Projects Act and Good Life District Economic Development Act; must receive annual reports and ownership updates; may approve project areas and require memoranda of understanding tying local revenue use to eligible project-area costs. DED and Revenue may share confidential information to administer the programs.

- Department of Revenue / Tax Commissioner: beginning Oct. 1, 2025, state sales tax rate set at 5.5%. The Tax Commissioner will allocate ("allocated sales taxes") and remit monthly to cities 50% of certain state sales taxes from qualifying good life district transactions, administer 50% refunds for new development costs (subject to available allocation), enforce a $5M annual cap for relocated retailers with excess rules, offset refunds from local remittances, provide detailed confidential remittance reports (parcel, taxpayer, taxable sales) to cities and DED, deposit project-area funds with trustee banks, and enforce recapture of unencumbered funds to the General Fund on termination.
