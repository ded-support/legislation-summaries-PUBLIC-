Summary

LB556: agency provisions

- Department of Correctional Services: The bill clarifies that a "staff secure juvenile facility" expressly excludes any institution operated by the Department of Correctional Services. It also prohibits placement, assignment, or transfer of specified juveniles to adult correctional facilities or the secure youth confinement facility operated by the Department of Correctional Services.

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): Peace officers taking juveniles into temporary custody under specified statutes must deliver custody to DHHS when not released. DHHS must place the juvenile in the least restrictive temporary setting, supervise placement, and may consent to emergency medical, psychological, or psychiatric treatment; DHHS has no further authority absent a court order. Temporary DHHS custody ends if no court order is issued within 48 hours. For mental‑health emergency custody, officers may place juveniles at a mental health facility or deliver them to DHHS and must complete a department‑prescribed certificate.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added language clarifies juvenile-detention procedure: for felony arrests, the probation officer must recommend detention, release, or an alternative and forward intake info to a judge, who consults the officer and may consider (but is not bound by) the factors in §43-260.01. For non-felony arrests, the statute specifies criteria under which detention is unnecessary (no serious threat, no recent willful failures to appear) and when detention is immediately necessary (e.g., fleeing law enforcement, absconding from placements/home, violent or multiple property offenses, threats). It also notes circumstances requiring agreement of the juvenile’s counsel and the county or city attorney. No provisions reference the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
