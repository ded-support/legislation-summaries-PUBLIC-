Summary

LB496 amends §60-6,111 (Neb. Rules of the Road). It retains that persons riding animals or operating animal-drawn vehicles, farm tractors, or implements of husbandry have the rights and duties of vehicle drivers except where inapplicable, and requires those operators to move to the nearest available shoulder if their slowness obstructs traffic. The bill adds a livestock definition (horse, mule, donkey, cattle, swine, sheep, goat) and imposes specific duties on motor vehicle operators when encountering persons leading or herding livestock: exercise caution when approaching or passing; promptly stop if given a distress signal by hand (unless stopping would cause an accident); turn off the engine if requested; and yield the right of way to livestock. No state agency, appropriation, or budget changes are referenced.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds subsection (3), defining "livestock" as horse, mule, donkey, cattle, swine, sheep, or goat, and imposes duties on drivers encountering persons leading/herding livestock on highways: exercise caution when approaching/passing; promptly stop if given a distress signal (hand raised) unless movement is needed to avoid an accident; turn off the vehicle engine if requested by the livestock handler; and yield right of way to livestock being led or driven. No government agencies or departments are referenced, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
