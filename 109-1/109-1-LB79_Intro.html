Summary

LB79 requires the trial court, on a defendant’s motion in civil cases involving a commercial motor vehicle requiring a commercial driver’s license, to dismiss any claim of the defendant’s direct negligence if the defendant stipulates the alleged negligent person was their employee or independent contractor and was acting within the course and scope of employment or as an independent contractor. If those stipulations are made and the person’s negligence is found to have caused the damages, the defendant’s liability is limited to respondeat superior. The bill references the definition of commercial motor vehicle in section 60-316. No state agencies are assigned new duties or funding; no appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a defendant-triggered dismissal mechanism in civil suits involving a commercial motor vehicle that requires a commercial driver's license: if the defendant stipulates the alleged wrongdoer was the defendant’s employee or independent contractor and was acting within the scope of employment/contract, the court must dismiss any direct-negligence claim against the defendant. Liability then is adjudicated solely under respondeat superior. “Commercial motor vehicle” is defined by reference to section 60-316. No state agencies or departments are named and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are affected.
