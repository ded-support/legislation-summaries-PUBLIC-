Summary

The bill directs the Division of Medicaid and Long-Term Care of the Department of Health and Human Services to: (1) require medicaid managed-care contractors to comply with federal/state laws and EPSDT requirements for mental health and substance-use services; (2) make contractor surveys, financial analyses, contract audits, and parity reports and results public; (3) ensure access parity and provider access through review of claims, reimbursement procedures, network adequacy, and rate adequacy; (4) establish monthly electronic communications with all Medicaid providers about managed-care contract amendments; (5) define network adequacy; and (6) annually post the Division’s network adequacy assessment criteria and each MCO’s compliance on the Department website. No specific appropriations or budget allocations to the Department are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Dept. of Health and Human Services focus):

Adds detailed audit and program‑integrity protections and obligations. Program integrity contractors must provide written justification to a provider before an audit; limit records requests to documents proportional to the audit; give 90 days to adjust mis-billed claims; provide ≥10 business days’ notice for onsite audits; and include a detailed written notice of any alleged overpayment (beneficiary, dates, amount, claim IDs, evidence, payment method, recovery method, appeals info, and a 60‑day non‑recoupment period after notice). Contractors may not recoup until appeals are exhausted unless a department‑verified “credible allegation of fraud.” Contractors must supply appeal procedures, contact info, and answer provider inquiries within 10 business days. The Division of Medicaid and Long‑Term Care must enforce MH/SUD parity, ensure access and rate/network adequacy, publish contractor audits/parity reports, define network adequacy, provide monthly electronic provider communications about MCO contract changes, and annually post network adequacy criteria and MCO compliance. No explicit funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included.
