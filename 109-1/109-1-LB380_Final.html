Summary

The bill directs the Division of Medicaid and Long-Term Care of the Department of Health and Human Services to: require managed‑care contractor compliance with applicable federal/state laws for mental‑health and substance‑use disorder coverage (including EPSDT); publicly post contractor surveys, financial analyses, contract audits and parity reports; ensure provider access and parity via regulation, claims and rate reviews and network‑adequacy oversight; establish monthly electronic communications with all Medicaid providers about managed‑care contract changes; define network adequacy; and annually post network‑adequacy criteria and each MCO’s compliance on the Department of Health and Human Services website.

Separately, the bill creates the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund to receive returned contractor funds, first offset contractor losses and then fund specified Medicaid services; investment earnings beginning October 1, 2024, are credited to the General Fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Health and Human Services)

- The bill adds program-integrity/audit protections and procedures administered through DHHS: records requests must be limited and proportional; onsite audits require ≥10 business days' notice; providers get 90 days to adjust misbilled claims; overpayment notices must include ten specific items and recovery cannot begin for at least 60 days; contractors may not recoup until appeals are exhausted unless a DHHS‑verified “credible allegation of fraud” exists. Program integrity contractors must provide appeal procedures at audit commencement and respond to provider questions within 10 business days.

- The Division of Medicaid and Long‑Term Care (DHHS) must enforce federal/state law and EPSDT for MH/SUD coverage; publish contractor surveys, financial analyses, audits and parity reports; ensure access and parity for MH/SUD providers; set/define network adequacy; notify providers monthly of Medicaid MCO contract changes; and annually post network‑adequacy criteria and each MCO’s compliance on the DHHS website.

- No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
