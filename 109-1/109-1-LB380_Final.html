Summary

The bill assigns the Division of Medicaid and Long-Term Care of the Department of Health and Human Services the following duties:

- Enforce contractor compliance with federal/state laws and rules governing mental health and substance use disorder coverage, including EPSDT for children and youth.

- Publish contractors' surveys, financial analyses, contract audits, parity reports, and parity compliance results.

- Ensure provider access and parity for mental health/SUD services via regulation and review of claims, reimbursement procedures, network adequacy, and rate adequacy.

- Establish monthly electronic communications to all Medicaid providers about amendments or changes to managed-care contracts.

- Define "network adequacy."

- Annually post network adequacy assessment criteria and each managed care organization's compliance on the Department website.

No explicit funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Health and Human Services)

- DHHS must provide a clear written justification when commencing a provider audit and limit records requests to documents proportional to the services audited. Providers get 90 days after notice of overpayment to adjust a claim in certain circumstances.

- Onsite audits require at least ten business days' advance notice and a good-faith effort to agree on timing.

- Written overpayment notices must include ten specified items (patient, dates, amount, claim ID, detailed basis/evidence, payment method, adjustment/appeal procedures, recovery method) and overpayments cannot be recouped for at least 60 days after notice.

- Program integrity contractors may not recoup until appeals are exhausted (except verified credible fraud), must provide appeal procedures at audit start, and must respond to provider inquiries within 10 business days.

- Credible allegation of fraud must be verified by DHHS; fraud defined to include intentional deception under law.

- Contracts for recovery may limit contingency fees to no more than 12.5% and must comply with federal law.

- The Division of Medicaid and Long-Term Care must enforce parity and access for mental health/substance use services (including EPSDT), publish contractor surveys/analyses/audits/parity reports, define and annually post network adequacy criteria and MCO compliance, ensure network/provider access and reimbursement review, and create monthly electronic communications with Medicaid providers about MCO contract changes.

No explicit changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
