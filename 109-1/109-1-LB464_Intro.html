Summary

LB464 (109th Legislature, 2025) amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §28-101 to create and prohibit "organized retail crime," define related terms, establish penalties based on aggregated retail market value (which is an element prosecutors must prove beyond a reasonable doubt), allow aggregation of losses across victims in the same scheme, and specify that representing merchandise as stolen is criminal even if it was not actually stolen. The bill repeals the original §28-101. The bill was referred to the Judiciary Committee. No state agencies or departments are named, and there are no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations in the text.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No government agencies or departments are referenced, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.

Substantive additions: prior conduct relevant to classification is limited to acts within ten years of the current offense. The statute allows aggregating the retail market value taken under a single organized retail crime scheme from one or more victims in the same indictment/complaint for offense classification, but the same amounts cannot be counted toward more than one offense. Aggregate retail market value is made an essential element that must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt. It also eliminates the defense that merchandise was not actually stolen/embezzled/converted if it was explicitly represented as such.
