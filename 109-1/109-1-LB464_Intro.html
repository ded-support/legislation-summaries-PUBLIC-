Summary

LB464 amends criminal statutes (modifying section 28-101, RS Cumulative Supp., 2024) to create and define the offense of organized retail crime, establish related penalties, and repeal the prior version of section 28-101. Key provisions: prior convictions within ten years may be considered; retail market value of losses from a single organized retail crime scheme may be aggregated across victims for offense classification (but not aggregated into more than one offense); aggregate retail market value is an essential element that must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt; and it is not a defense that merchandise was not actually stolen if it was explicitly represented as stolen. No state agencies or departments are named, and no funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds three rules for prosecuting organized retail crime: (1) only conduct within the prior ten years is considered; (2) retail market-value losses from a single organized scheme may be aggregated across victims in one charging instrument to determine offense classification, but may not be aggregated into more than one offense; and (3) aggregate retail market value is an essential element that must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt. Also clarifies that it is not a defense that merchandise was not actually stolen if it was explicitly represented as stolen, embezzled, or converted. No government agencies or departments are mentioned, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
