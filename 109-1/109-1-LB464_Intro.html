Summary

LB464 (introduced by Bosn; referred to the Judiciary Committee) amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §28-101 and creates a criminal offense for organized retail crime. It defines key terms, allows aggregation of retail market value taken from one or more victims under a single charging instrument for offense classification (with limits), requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of aggregate retail market value, and bars as a defense that merchandise was not actually stolen if it was represented as stolen. Prior convictions within ten years affect classification. The bill repeals the original §28-101. No state agencies or departments beyond the Legislature’s Judiciary Committee are named, and no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules for aggregating retail market value in prosecutions of organized retail crime: losses from one scheme across multiple victims may be combined in the same charging instrument to determine offense classification, but may not be counted toward more than one offense. Limits lookback to ten years for prior acts. Establishes that aggregate retail market value is an essential element that the prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt. Clarifies that it is not a defense that merchandise was not actually stolen if it was explicitly represented as stolen, embezzled, or converted. No government agencies, departments, funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned or changed.
