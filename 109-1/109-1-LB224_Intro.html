Summary

LB224 establishes a paid maternity‑leave benefit for Nebraska state employees. Key provisions:

- Defines terms (maternity leave, maternity‑leave eligible employee, retaliatory action, state employee).

- Entitles eligible state employees who give birth and assume a parental role to 12 weeks paid maternity leave (prorated for part‑time employees); maximum 12 weeks in any 12‑month period.

- Leave may not begin before birth, must be used within six months, and is non‑intermittent except by mutual written agreement or medical necessity for the child; runs concurrently with the federal Family and Medical Leave Act.

- Requires ~30 days’ notice when practicable; the state may not charge other accrued leave for this maternity leave.

- No cash‑out on termination; employees must be reinstated to the same or equivalent position after leave.

No specific agency funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a state-level paid maternity leave entitlement: full‑time state employees receive 12 weeks (part‑time prorated) to bond with a newborn. The bill defines "state employee," "maternity leave," and "retaliatory action"; requires leave to begin no earlier than birth and be used within six months; bars intermittent use except by mutual written agreement or medical certification for the child; and makes state maternity leave run concurrently with the federal Family and Medical Leave Act. It requires ~30 days' notice (or as soon as practicable if unforeseen), prohibits charging other leave banks (except as FMLA allows), provides job restoration to the same or equivalent position, and disallows pay for unused maternity leave at termination.
