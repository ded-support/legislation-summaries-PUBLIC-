Summary

LB224 requires the State of Nebraska (state employers) to provide paid maternity leave to eligible state employees: twelve weeks for full-time employees, prorated for part-time. Leave may be used only after birth and within six months, generally not intermittently unless agreed in writing or certified by a health care provider. The leave runs concurrently with the federal Family and Medical Leave Act (29 U.S.C. 2601 et seq.). The state may not charge this maternity leave to sick, vacation, compensatory, or other leave, must restore the employee to the same or equivalent position after leave, and may not pay out unused maternity leave at separation. No agencies beyond “the state” and FMLA are named; the bill contains no appropriations or funding changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a state-paid maternity leave program for state employees: full‑time employees get 12 weeks paid leave (part‑time prorated) to bond after birth. Leave may not start before birth, must be used within six months, generally is not intermittent (unless agreed in writing or certified by a health care provider), and runs concurrently with the federal Family and Medical Leave Act. Definitions include "state employee," "maternity leave eligible employee," and "retaliatory action" (prohibited). The leave is not charged against sick, vacation, compensatory, or other leave (except as required by FMLA). No payout on termination; employees must be restored to the same or equivalent position. No specific agencies, departments, or funding/appropriations are named or altered.
