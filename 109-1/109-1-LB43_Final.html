Summary

LB43 amends Nebraska siting and notice requirements for electric generation and transmission. Key agency roles and changes:

- Nebraska Power Review Board (the "board") and its executive director: owners must notify and certify to the board for facilities/transmission within a 10-mile radius of defined military installations that no electronic-related equipment/components are manufactured by a "foreign adversary." The board must acknowledge compliance within ten days, may preapprove limited use of such components if no reasonable alternative exists, and enforces compliance (hearings, orders to cease construction/operation).

- North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC): compliance with NERC critical infrastructure protection (CIP) standards may exempt suppliers from the foreign-adversary certification; suppliers must notify the board of CIP compliance status.

- Nebraska Game and Parks Commission: owners must consult on measures to avoid, minimize, and mitigate impacts to listed species.

- Federal references: foreign adversary definition (15 C.F.R.) and military installation definitions (31 C.F.R.) are incorporated.

- Privately developed renewable facilities: new certification, decommissioning bond (by year six), and joint transmission development agreement requirements; exemption from certain board approvals if compliant.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. The act takes effect immediately.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements that electric suppliers and owners of generation or transmission facilities within 10 miles of specified U.S. Air Force sites (as defined by 31 C.F.R. 802.211(b)(3) and 802.227(c), regs as of Jan 1, 2025) must submit one‑time certifications to "the board" that their facilities contain no electronic equipment/components manufactured by a "foreign adversary" (as defined by 15 C.F.R. 791.4, as of Feb 7, 2025) and must notify the board if that changes. The board may preapprove use of such foreign‑made equipment only if no reasonable alternative exists and nonuse would cause greater harm. Owners of facilities meeting certain transmission voltage/nameplate thresholds must also certify compliance with North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) critical infrastructure protection standards. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
