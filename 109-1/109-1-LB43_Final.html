Summary

LB43 amends statutes governing electric generation and transmission. It revises definitions (including "foreign adversary" and "military installation") and requires owners to notify and certify to the board (and its executive director) that facilities within a ten‑mile radius of specified U.S. Air Force installations contain no electronic-related equipment or components manufactured by a foreign adversary. Retail suppliers compliant with NERC critical infrastructure protection (CIP) standards may certify that compliance instead. The board may preapprove use of such equipment only where no reasonable alternative exists and withholding it would cause greater harm. Privately developed renewable energy facilities must notify the board 30 days before construction, submit decommissioning plans and security, enter joint transmission development agreements, and consult the Nebraska Game and Parks Commission on species impacts; the executive director must acknowledge exemptions within ten days. The board retains hearing and enforcement powers. No changes to funding or appropriations. Emergency clause makes the act effective on passage.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and new compliance steps for electric suppliers and facility owners. Key agencies and references:

- United States Air Force (certain silos/bases identified by 31 C.F.R. citations as of Jan 1, 2025).

- The board (entity receiving certifications and preapproval requests).

- North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) — facilities connected at ≥100 kV and meeting specified nameplate/injection thresholds must certify future compliance with NERC critical infrastructure protection requirements.

- Federal regulations: foreign adversary defined by 15 C.F.R. 791.4 (as of Feb 7, 2025).

Requirements:

- Before building or acquiring generation/transmission >700 volts within 10 miles of a military installation, suppliers/owners must consult vendors and file a one‑time written certification to the board that no electronic-related equipment/components are manufactured by a foreign adversary, and must notify the board if compliance later lapses.

- The board may preapprove use of such equipment only if no reasonable alternative exists and nonuse would cause greater harm.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
