Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): required to apply for SNAP options to maximize federal funding and minimize General Fund use; hire staff and submit an annual report to the Legislature (Dec. 1) detailing options, actions, caseload and costs. DHHS must develop a state outreach plan meeting Food and Nutrition Service (USDA) criteria, may accept gifts/grants, partner with counties/nonprofits, submit the plan to USDA FNS for approval and request federal matching funds, and may be exempt from implementing the plan if private/federal funds are insufficient. DHHS shall create a TANF-funded categorical eligibility policy (temporary 165% gross income limit through 10/1/2025) with annual evaluation reports (Dec. 15). DHHS shall establish a Restaurant Meals Program (subject to federal approval), operational by July 1, 2026.

- Department of Labor: to the extent federal funds are available, partner on the SNAP Next Step Program encouraging eligible households (131–165% poverty) to participate until 9/30/2023; Legislature intends no General Funds be used by the Department of Labor for these processes.

Summary (underlined additions):

- Department of Agriculture: Requires the Department of Agriculture (the “department”) to establish a Restaurant Meals Program as part of SNAP, subject to any required federal state-plan approval. Eligible households (elderly/disabled and their spouses per 7 U.S.C. 2012(j), and homeless individuals per 7 U.S.C. 2012(l)) may redeem SNAP benefits at private vendors that contract with the department to provide concessional-priced meals under 7 U.S.C. 2018(h). The program must be operational by July 1, 2026, and the department may adopt rules to implement it.
