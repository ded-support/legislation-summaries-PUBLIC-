Summary

The bill directs the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to: apply for all appropriate SNAP options, maximize federal funding and minimize state General Fund use, employ staff to evaluate options, and submit an annual report to the Legislature by December 1. DHHS must develop a USDA‑Food and Nutrition Service‑approved outreach plan (may accept gifts/grants/donations and request federal matching funds) and may be exempt from implementation if private/federal funds are insufficient. DHHS shall create a TANF‑funded categorical eligibility policy (including a temporary gross income increase to 165% of OMB poverty guidelines through Oct 1, 2025) and annually report evaluations and administrative cost estimates. DHHS must establish a Restaurant Meals Program (operational by July 1, 2026). The bill references the Department of Labor’s SNAP Next Step Program and states no General Funds should be used by Labor for those processes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the state SNAP-administering department to create a Restaurant Meals Program (pending any needed federal state-plan approval). Under the program, elderly or disabled beneficiaries (and their spouses) and homeless individuals may use SNAP benefits at private, department‑contracted establishments that sell meals at concessional prices, in accordance with federal law. The program must be operational by July 1, 2026, and the department may adopt implementing rules and regulations.

Agency impacts:

- Department of Agriculture: If it administers SNAP, must establish, contract with eateries, and promulgate rules; implementation deadline July 1, 2026.

- Department of Health and Human Services: Same responsibilities if it is the SNAP administrator; public‑health oversight implications.

- Department of Labor: No direct duties, funding, or appropriations imposed.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
