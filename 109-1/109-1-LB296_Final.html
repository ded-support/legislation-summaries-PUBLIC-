Summary

LB296 requires the State Department of Education, the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), the Office of Probation Administration, and the State Court Administrator to enter a memorandum of understanding to share data on students under juvenile-court jurisdiction and to contract a consultant to develop related policies. The consultant must deliver a draft report to agency representatives, including the chief executive officer of DHHS, by September 1, 2024; a final report follows by December 1, 2024. DHHS is included as a collaborating party in developing policies. The bill directs that the centralized education records system and employment of registrars be funded from the State Department of Education Improvement Grant Fund (lottery transfers, repayments, and interest).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the State Department of Education to create and, beginning July 1, 2026, maintain a centralized education records system for students under juvenile court jurisdiction and to employ registrars. Registrars must ensure uninterrupted education services, prompt record sharing, accurate maintenance of records, analyze high school records for additional credit, and trigger Commissioner-awarded credits (per State Board rules) that schools must recognize. Beginning with 2026–27, eligible students who were in out‑of‑home placement may request graduation from a previously attended public high school or a state diploma from the Commissioner. The system and registrar costs are paid from the State Department of Education Improvement Grant Fund. The Department of Health and Human Services is not referenced and has no changes to duties, funding, or appropriations in the added text.
