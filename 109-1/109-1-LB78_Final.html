Summary

Creates the Domestic Violence and Sex Trafficking Survivor Assistance Act, designating the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to administer housing stability programs for domestic violence and sex‑trafficking survivors, including grants, loans, reimbursements, housing‑related assistance, formulaic distribution (developed in consultation with service providers), contracting with qualified third parties, and authority to direct up to 20% of excess assistance funds to certified organizations for housing acquisition/rehabilitation. Establishes the Domestic Violence and Sex Trafficking Survivor Housing Assistance Fund, administered by DHHS, funded by legislative transfers and public/private sources, and investable by the state investment officer.

Amends documentary stamp tax allocation: from each $2.32 collected the register retains $0.50 (county general fund); remitted amounts credit: $0.95 Affordable Housing Trust Fund, $0.25 Site and Building Development Fund, $0.25 Homeless Shelter Assistance Trust Fund, $0.30 Behavioral Health Services Fund, and $0.07 to the new Survivor Housing Assistance Fund. The Tax Commissioner/Department of Revenue issues rules; taxpayers may seek declaratory rulings, and the Department of Revenue may not overturn county assessor sale‑qualification determinations without its own review finding them incorrect.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions designate DHHS as lead, define terms, and require DHHS to promote housing stability for domestic violence and sex‑trafficking survivors by providing grants, loans, rental/utility/security deposit assistance and provider reimbursements. DHHS may contract with qualified 501(c)(3)s or tribal entities, must use a formula (developed with provider input) to allocate assistance, and may redirect up to 20% of any fully satisfied housing‑assistance obligations to certified organizations for housing acquisition/rehabilitation. The Domestic Violence and Sex Trafficking Survivor Housing Assistance Fund is created, to receive legislative transfers and public/private contributions, be administered by DHHS, and be invested by the state investment officer. The added text does not clearly assign any new duties or specific appropriations to the Department of Revenue.
