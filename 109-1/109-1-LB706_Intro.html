Summary

LB706 requires coordination between law enforcement and APS social workers for responses to calls involving individuals with mental illness. Key agencies and provisions:

- Department of Health and Human Services: referenced for APS social worker involvement.

- Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice (the commission): set minimum policy standards, distribute a model policy, annually review agency compliance “within the limits of its existing appropriations, including any grant funds,” and report results to the Governor and Legislature.

- Law enforcement agencies: must screen/flag mental-health calls, dispatch APS social workers, adopt written policies, and provide copies to the commission.

- Council/Training Center: may fine or revoke officer certification (including for willful failure to comply); sets tuition/fee rules; fees credited to the Nebraska Law Enforcement Training Center Cash Fund.

- Office of the Attorney General: authorized to collect fines; State Treasurer receives remitted fines.

- Funding: no new appropriations; commission reviews are limited to existing appropriations and grant funds. Noncompliant agencies become ineligible for commission‑administered loans, grants, funds, or donations until remedied.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates duties for state and local agencies: law enforcement agencies must require dispatchers to screen calls with a checklist (self-harm threats, disorientation/hallucinations/paranoia, caller knowledge of mental‑health history), flag qualifying calls as a "Mental Health Priority" with a designated code, notify the nearest unit and an APS (Adult Protective Services) social worker, and share mental‑health information with responders. Unless unsafe, officers responding to such calls must be accompanied by an APS social worker, who shall lead the interaction using de‑escalation and trauma‑informed care.

Agencies must adopt written dispatch/policy and provide it to "the commission," which will set minimum standards, may offer a model policy, and—within its existing appropriations and any grant funds—will annually review compliance and report electronically to the Governor and Legislature. The commission may withhold loans, grants, funds, or donations it administers from agencies with persistent noncompliance.
