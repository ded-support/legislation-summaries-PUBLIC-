Summary

LB706 mandates law-enforcement dispatch screening for mental‑health indicators and requires calls identified as "Mental Health Priority" to notify and, when safe and practicable, be responded to by an APS social worker accompanying the officer; the APS social worker must lead de‑escalation and trauma‑informed interaction. The Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice (the commission) must set minimum policy standards, may provide a model policy, and will annually review agency compliance within its existing appropriations (including grants), reporting to the Governor and Legislature. Agencies with a pattern of noncompliance are ineligible for commission‑administered loans, grants, funds, or donations until corrected. Willful failure to comply can trigger discipline including revocation/suspension of officer certification. Financial enforcement: fines are collectible by the Attorney General and remitted to the State Treasurer. The bill also assigns related duties to the Department of Health and Human Services concerning APS social workers.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires law enforcement agencies to have dispatch personnel screen calls with a checklist for mental‑health indicators, flag qualifying calls as a "Mental Health Priority," notify the nearest unit and an APS social worker, and share mental‑health information with responders. When safe/practicable, officers responding to such calls must be accompanied by an APS social worker, who will lead the interaction using de‑escalation and trauma‑informed care. Agencies must adopt written dispatch/APS‑accompaniment policies and submit them to the commission, which will set minimum standards and may distribute a model policy (not mandatory except for delinquent agencies). The commission will annually review compliance and report to the Governor and Legislature, using only existing appropriations (including any awarded grants). Agencies with patterns of noncompliance are ineligible for commission‑administered loans, grants, funds, or donations until cured.
