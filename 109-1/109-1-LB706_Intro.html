Summary

LB706 requires law enforcement dispatch to screen calls for mental-health indicators, flag "Mental Health Priority" calls, notify and, when safe and practicable, dispatch an APS social worker to accompany officers. APS social workers must lead interactions using de‑escalation and trauma‑informed care.

Agencies and duties:

- Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice (the commission): set minimum standards, accept agency policies, may issue a model policy, perform annual compliance reviews and report to the Governor and Legislature — limited to existing appropriations (including awarded grants) — and recommend improvements.

- Law enforcement agencies: adopt written dispatch/APS‑accompaniment policies and provide them to the commission.

- Department of Health and Human Services is identified as having duties in the bill (text excerpt does not specify details).

Sanctions and fiscal effects:

- Agencies with a pattern of noncompliance become ineligible for loans, grants, funds, or donations administered by the commission until corrected.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires law enforcement dispatchers to use a checklist/questionnaire to screen for mental‑health issues, flag such calls as "Mental Health Priority," use a designated code, notify the nearest unit plus an APS social worker, and share mental‑health info with responders. Unless unsafe, officers responding must be accompanied by an APS social worker, who shall lead interactions using de‑escalation and trauma‑informed care.

Each agency must adopt a written dispatch/APS policy and file it with "the commission," which will set minimum standards and may issue a model policy. The commission will annually review compliance within its existing appropriations (including any grants), report results to the Governor and Legislature (electronically), make recommendations, and may bar noncompliant agencies from receiving commission‑administered loans, grants, funds, or donations until corrected.
