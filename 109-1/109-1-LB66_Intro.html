Summary

Bill LB66 adopts the Uniform Health‑Care Decisions Act, establishing roles and procedures for courts, guardians, health‑care professionals, health‑care institutions, nursing homes and assisted‑living facilities (see §71‑5903), emergency medical responders, and medical‑records administrators. Key agency-related provisions: courts must hold hearings and determine incapacity within seven days by clear-and-convincing evidence; responsible health‑care professionals and record administrators must document and communicate advance health‑care directives and instructions; health‑care institutions may refuse care on conscience or capacity grounds but must notify and attempt transfer and provide limited continuing care; guardianship and default surrogate authorities and disqualifications are defined; emergency medical responders receive limited liability protections. The bill creates civil remedies with statutory damages and attorney fees. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a comprehensive advance health‑care directive scheme referencing courts for capacity determinations and enforcement and citing the federal Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1396r(a)(1)) for the nursing‑home definition. It regulates health‑care institutions, health‑care professionals (including emergency medical responders), guardians, agents, and default surrogates (with disqualification rules for nursing‑home staff). Includes electronic witnessing, an optional mental‑health‑only directive with limited irrevocability, required documentation in medical records, limits on agent consent for long nursing‑home placements and forced admissions, transfer/continuity obligations for providers, civil remedies and statutory damages, and interstate recognition. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
