Summary

LB66 adopts the Uniform Health‑Care Decisions Act. Key government entities and legal mechanics:

- Courts: establish procedures for capacity determinations, including a hearing and court determination by clear and convincing evidence (hearing and decision deadlines within seven days).

- Guardianship: court‑appointed guardians remain subject to limitations; agent decisions can prevail absent court order.

- Health‑care professionals/institutions: required to document advance instructions in medical records, may refuse care for stated reasons, must attempt transfer when refusing care, and are shielded from liability for good‑faith actions.

- Default surrogate and agent rules reference nursing homes (defined by 42 U.S.C. 1396r(a)(1)) and assisted‑living facilities (Neb. §71‑5903); emergency medical responders addressed.

- Remedies: statutory damages ($25,000; $50,000) and attorney fees for violations.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a Uniform Health‑Care Decisions Act establishing definitions and procedures for advance health‑care directives, powers of attorney, advance mental‑health directives, default surrogates, capacity findings, and surrogate/agent authority. Government entities referenced include courts (timing and standard for capacity hearings, ability to find lack of capacity and to order noncompliance by guardians), guardians and guardianship courts, and emergency medical responders (limited liability carve‑outs). The bill cites federal law (Social Security Act §1919) for the nursing‑home definition. No state agencies or departments (e.g., health department) are newly created or directed, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
