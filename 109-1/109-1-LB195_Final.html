Summary

LB195 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §28-470 to grant civil, criminal, and administrative immunity to emergency responders and to peace officers who, acting in good faith, obtain opioid overdose reversal medication from their agency and administer it to someone apparently experiencing an opioid-related overdose. Immunity does not apply for willful, wanton, or grossly negligent acts; agency or organization liability is preserved. The bill defines emergency responder (EMS personnel licensed under the Emergency Medical Services Practice Act or the EMS Personnel Licensure Interstate Compact), health professionals (licensed under the Uniform Credentialing Act), law enforcement agencies (police departments, town marshals, offices of sheriff, Nebraska State Patrol), and law enforcement employees (including contractors who handle evidence). The original section is repealed. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of "opioid overdose reversal medication" as any lifesaving drug approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to reverse opioid overdose, obtainable by prescription, from a health professional, or over‑the‑counter; explicitly cites naloxone and nalmefene as examples. No agencies beyond the FDA are referenced and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
