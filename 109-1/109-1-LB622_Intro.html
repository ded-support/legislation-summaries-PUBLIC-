Summary

The bill assigns multiple responsibilities and funding changes to the Department of Economic Development (DED) and references the Department of Revenue (including the Tax Commissioner).

- DED: The Affordable Housing Trust Fund may finance community projects, the new Statewide Housing Assistance Program, and departmental expenses as appropriated. DED sets matching-fund requirements for for‑profit recipients, must allocate at least 30% of annual funds to each congressional district, announce ≥90‑day application periods, develop a qualified allocation plan prioritizing lowest‑income projects and homebuyers, may recapture unspent awards, and may contract for services. The Statewide Housing Assistance Program will be administered by a nonprofit contracted by DED, provide downpayment loans (lien, payable on title transfer) up to $30,000, and be subject to DED rules. DED must report detailed fund, budget, and program metrics annually, including deposits from section 76-903.

- Department of Revenue/Tax Commissioner: Tax Commissioner designs documentary stamp tax stamps; taxpayers may seek declaratory rulings from the Department of Revenue; the department will not overturn county assessor sale‑qualification determinations without review. Documentary stamp tax set at $2.55 per $1,000 with remittance: $0.50 retained by register; $1.25 to the Affordable Housing Trust Fund; $0.25 to Site and Building Development Fund; $0.25 to Homeless Shelter Assistance Trust Fund; $0.30 to Behavioral Health Services Fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a Statewide Housing Assistance Program administered by a nonprofit contractor overseen by the Department of Economic Development. The program provides statewide downpayment assistance as a loan (secured by a lien) repayable on transfer of title, capped at $30,000 per homebuyer. Eligibility and application rules are to be set by the department; priority goes to lowest‑income buyers and buyers in enterprise zones, federal opportunity zones, or areas declared extremely blighted. No provisions in the added text change Department of Revenue responsibilities, nor are specific appropriations, funding sources, or budget allocations specified.
