Summary

The bill repeatedly references the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA): “eligible breakfast” and “eligible lunch” are defined as meals reimbursable under USDA regulations implementing the Child Nutrition Act and the Richard B. Russell National School Lunch Act; the “federal reimbursement rate” is defined as the payment levels published by the USDA (as of Jan 1, 2023); and the “identified student percentage” is determined under USDA regulations for high‑poverty schools. State reimbursements to schools are calculated using those USDA federal reimbursement rates (e.g., the state pays the difference between USDA free and fully paid rates for fully paid meals). The Legislature also states its intent to appropriate General Fund money to implement the Hunger‑Free Schools Act.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No direct changes to the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) or its funding are made. The bill repeatedly adopts USDA definitions, regulations, and the federally published "federal reimbursement rate" (per the Child Nutrition Act and National School Lunch Act) as the basis for state calculations. The new Hunger‑Free Schools Program is administered by the State Department of Education, which will reimburse qualified schools (per formulas tied to USDA reimbursement rates and fixed per‑meal supplements) to allow universal no‑cost school breakfasts and lunches. The Legislature expresses intent to appropriate General Fund dollars to carry out the act. In short: USDA roles remain definitional and regulatory only; no USDA appropriation or program changes are created.
