Summary

LB101 amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to (1) bar rental‑agreement waivers of state and federal rights and certain indemnifications, and (2) create/clarify a right to jury trial in eviction (restitution) actions. Procedural changes assign duties to courts and court staff: complaints for possession are filed with the clerk of the district or county court and may demand a jury; summonses must notify defendants of the right to a jury trial; courts must inform/confirm a defendant’s jury demand if not timely made; nonjury trials are set 10–14 days after summons and jury trials are scheduled as practicable (courts may require tenants to deposit rent if a jury trial is continued). If judgment issues, constables or sheriffs execute writs of restitution within ten days. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. Original sections amended are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural protections in possession actions requiring the complaint to notify defendants of the right to a jury trial. If no jury demand is made by first appearance, the court must inform the defendant and, in county court, ask whether they demand a jury or waive it. Timelines: bench trials must occur 10–14 days after summons; jury trials scheduled as practicable. If a tenant requests a continuance of a jury trial beyond the initial date, the court may require the tenant to deposit accrued rent with the clerk of the court. Agencies mentioned: courts (county court) and the clerk of the court. No funding or appropriation changes.
