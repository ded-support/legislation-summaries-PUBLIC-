Summary

The bill amends duties of the Economic Recovery and Incentives Division of the Department of Economic Development to administer federal and state grant funding. It requires a 50% advance to grantees with remaining funds paid monthly until fully paid or by Dec. 31, 2026, and authorizes recovery or reduction of payments for unused funds. The division must prioritize grant awards to qualified census tracts (QCTs) in metropolitan-class cities disproportionately impacted by COVID-19, emphasizing housing, small-business assistance, job training, and business development, and rely on studies under section 81-12,242.

It creates a Qualified Census Tract Recovery Grant Program (caps: $10M to QCTs in primary-class cities; $10M to QCTs outside metro/primary; remaining funds to metro-class QCTs) and authorizes additional division-administered grants with specified caps: $90M for a nonprofit to develop a business park in metro-class QCTs (with pre-release conditions), $6M for internships/crime prevention, $5M for a film, $20M each for affordable-housing interventions (metro and primary classes), $5M to a county agricultural society, and $1M to a postsecondary institution. Grants using federal ARPA funds must meet Treasury guidance; state-funded grants must follow ARPA intent.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a geographic restriction: where an inland port district lies within a city of the metropolitan class, grant funds may not be used in that city's downtown or northern downtown areas. For the Department of Economic Development, this creates a new prohibition on allowable grant expenditures by location but does not change funding levels, appropriations, or allocate any new budget resources.
