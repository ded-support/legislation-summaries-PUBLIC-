Summary

This bill amends definitions and directs duties for the Department of Correctional Services. It codifies “Department” and “Director of Correctional Services,” treats committed offenders and facilities as those under the department, and defines “restrictive housing.” It requires the department to limit use of restrictive housing (no more than 15 consecutive days; single-occupancy cells unless inmates consent), to adopt Administrative Procedure Act rules establishing restrictive-housing levels, placement criteria, and individualized transition plans developed with the committed offender. The department must promulgate rules addressing risks for vulnerable populations (bar placement in restrictive housing except for immediate segregation pending classification) and may develop secure mental-health housing that ensures meaningful access to social interaction, exercise, stimulation, and therapeutic programming. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions restrict use of restrictive housing: inmates may not be held in restrictive housing for more than 15 consecutive days, and single‑occupancy cells cannot hold more than one inmate unless all inmates voluntarily agree in writing. The bill adds definitions relevant to placement decisions and care: developmental disability, “member” (fragmented), serious mental illness (broad medical definition with examples: schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, delusional disorder, bipolar disorder, major depression, OCD), and traumatic brain injury (fragmented). No appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations for the Department of Correctional Services are included in the added text. Operational policy, screening, and housing practices for the Department would likely require revision.
