Summary

LB641 amends Medicaid estate-recovery provisions administered by the Department of Health and Human Services (the department). Key department authorities/requirements: recover Medicaid payments from a recipient’s estate (debts accrue in life but are collected after death subject to spouse/qualifying-child exceptions); refrain from foreclosing liens on a home in specified sibling/adult-child residency/care situations (physician attestation suffices); certify to personal representatives or other authorized parties whether reimbursement is due (applications may be delivered as designated, including email; department to post acceptable delivery methods); may release property, waive or compromise claims, and adopt rules and regulations; recover from third-party recoveries with pro rata share of attorney fees; retroactive application to Aug. 30, 2015. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

For the Department of Health and Human Services: the bill allows physician attestation that a sibling, adult child, or other relative provided care which delayed a recipient’s admission to a medical institution to serve as sufficient documentation for two purposes: (1) to enable the department to avoid foreclosure on its medical-assistance lien when such familial care occurred; and (2) to exclude a retained life‑estate interest in real property from the recipient’s estate — provided the life estate has been recorded at least 12 months and the relative resided solely and continuously with the recipient and demonstrates to the department that their care delayed institutionalization. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
