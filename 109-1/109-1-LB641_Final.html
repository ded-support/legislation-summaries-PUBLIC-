Summary

LB641 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §68-919 governing Medicaid estate recovery by the Department of Health and Human Services. Key provisions: expands the definition of “medical institution” to include assisted living facilities; restricts foreclosure on a recipient’s home where a sibling (with equity) or an adult child resided and provided care (physician attestation is sufficient); creates an additional exemption for life‑estate interests after 12 months where a relative resides solely and continuously and provided care (physician attestation suffices); requires the department to certify to estate representatives whether reimbursement is due and permits the department to release property, waive or compromise claims, enforce recovery, and adopt rules. Recovery actions may be brought within five years of specified triggering events. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that care by a sibling, adult child, or other relative — reimbursed or unreimbursed — that delayed a recipient’s admission to a medical institution may be documented by a physician’s written attestation. The Department of Health and Human Services must accept such an attestation as sufficient to (1) avoid foreclosure on a medical‑assistance lien where a sibling or adult child provided the care, and (2) exclude a retained life‑estate interest in real estate (after 12 months from deed recording) from the recipient’s estate when a relative resided solely and continuously with the recipient and established to the department’s satisfaction that the relative provided the delaying care. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
