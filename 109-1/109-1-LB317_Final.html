Summary

The bill assigns multiple administrative, regulatory, consultation, and funding duties across state agencies. Department of Administrative Services (DAS) gains accounting/certification authority for certain encumbrances, materiel-division review authority for municipal purchasing rebates, contract‑transparency and procurement preferences, and budget-division responsibility to schedule/authorize cash transfers (notably $1,000,000 from the Water Resources Cash Fund to the Nitrogen Reduction Incentive Cash Fund in FY2024–25, several specified transfers from the Water Sustainability Fund—including $500,000 to the General Fund—and authority over a $200,000 Clean‑burning Motor Fuel Fund transfer and issuance of warrants). Department of Agriculture houses the Riparian Vegetation Task Force, expands pesticide/regulatory duties, convenes livestock permitting experts, and may require environmental inspections (fees to the Environmental Safety Cash Fund). Department of Health and Human Services is added to multiple boards/committees, may participate in groundwater hearings, receives certain forfeited radioactive‑site funds, and had a directed July 1, 2021 transfer from its Cash Fund to the Environmental Safety Cash Fund. Departments of Revenue, Transportation, Economic Development, Banking and Finance, Insurance, Labor, Motor Vehicles, Correctional Services, Veterans’ Affairs, and the Military Department receive statutory consultation, reporting, session‑law copies, or specific administrative roles (Revenue administers fuel/production tax credits and appropriation changes; Transportation and Economic Development are engaged in flood/infrastructure planning).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Effective July 1, 2025 the Department of Natural Resources (Agency No. 29) is merged into the Department of Environment and Energy, renamed the Department of Water, Energy, and Environment (Agency No. 84). The Director is retitled Director of Water, Energy, and Environment; the former Director of Natural Resources becomes Chief Water Officer with prior DNR authorities. DNR positions and employees transfer to the new department with continuous service and retained personnel/bargaining rights (no new benefits). Any appropriations and salary limits previously enacted for Agency No. 29 are void and reappropriated to Agency No. 84. Unpaid DNR obligations as of June 30, 2025 may be certified as valid encumbrances to the accounting division of the Department of Administrative Services for processing. No substantive changes noted for the other listed agencies.
