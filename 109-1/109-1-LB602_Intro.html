Summary

Creates the "Delete Act" and assigns key implementation and enforcement roles to the Secretary of State and the Attorney General. The Secretary of State must establish (by Jan 1, 2028) and host an accessible deletion mechanism and maintain publicly accessible data-broker registration information. Data brokers must annually register with the Secretary of State (by Jan 31), provide specified disclosures, and pay a registration fee set by the Secretary (not to exceed $300). The Secretary may charge data brokers an access fee to cover reasonable costs. Noncompliance with deletion duties triggers a $200 per-day civil penalty payable to the Secretary of State. The Attorney General may enforce penalties, seek injunctive relief, and recover attorneys' fees. The Secretary of State will select independent auditors for triannual compliance audits and may adopt rules to implement the Act. Violations are added to Nebraska's deceptive trade practices statute.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds recurring roles for the Secretary of State and the Attorney General. Key Secretary of State duties: host publicly accessible data-broker registration info and establish (by Jan 1, 2028) an accessible deletion mechanism with security, accessibility, agent support, and audit/reporting processes; select independent auditors (first audits due by Dec 31, 2030); promulgate rules. Financial provisions: annual data-broker registration fee (set by Secretary, ≤ $300); the Secretary may charge data brokers an access fee to cover the mechanism's reasonable costs; civil penalties ($200/day per missed deletion request) are payable to the Secretary of State. The Attorney General may enforce civil penalties, seek injunctive relief, and recover attorneys' fees and costs.
