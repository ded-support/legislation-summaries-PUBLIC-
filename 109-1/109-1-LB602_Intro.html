Summary

LB602 (the "Delete Act") assigns primary administrative authority to the Secretary of State and enforcement powers to the Attorney General. Key agency actions and fiscal provisions:

- Secretary of State: annual data-broker registration (by Jan 31) with required disclosures; registration fee set by the Secretary of State (not to exceed $300); must publish registration information and create an accessible statewide deletion mechanism by Jan 1, 2028; may charge data brokers an access fee limited to the Secretary of State’s reasonable costs; may adopt implementing rules. Secretary of State selects independent auditors for triennial audits (first by Dec 31, 2030) and receives audit reports on request.

- Financial penalties and fees: civil penalty of $200/day per uncompleted deletion request payable to the Secretary of State; the Attorney General may sue to collect penalties, seek injunctive relief, and recover attorneys’ fees and costs.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds roles for the Secretary of State and Attorney General. The Secretary of State must host data-broker registration info and create an accessible deletion mechanism by Jan 1, 2028, set a registration fee (up to $300) and may charge data brokers an access fee not to exceed the Secretary's reasonable costs, adopt rules, select independent auditors for triennial audits (first by Dec 31, 2030), receive audit reports on request, and require retention of audit materials for six years. Data brokers register annually (by Jan 31) and use the deletion mechanism beginning Aug 1, 2028. Noncompliance triggers civil penalties ($200/day per uncompleted deletion request) payable to the Secretary of State. The Attorney General may enforce the Act, collect penalties, seek injunctive relief, and recover attorney fees. No explicit new appropriations; fees and penalties are the identified funding flows.
