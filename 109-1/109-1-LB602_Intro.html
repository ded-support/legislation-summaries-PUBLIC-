Summary

LB602 creates the "Data Elimination and Limiting Extensive Tracking and Exchange Act" and assigns primary administrative duties to the Secretary of State and enforcement to the Attorney General. Key agency actions:

- Secretary of State: establish by Jan 1, 2028 an accessible deletion mechanism (consumer single-request portal); publish data-broker registration and deletion-mechanism information on the Secretary of State website; set an annual data-broker registration fee up to $300; may charge data brokers an access fee not to exceed the Secretary’s reasonable costs; select independent third-party auditors for triennial audits (first by Dec 31, 2030); adopt rules to implement the Act.

- Attorney General: may seek civil penalties, injunctive relief, and recover attorney fees.

Monetary provisions: registration fee (≤ $300); civil penalty of $200 per day per unfulfilled deletion request; Secretary may collect access fees. No other appropriations or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions assign primary operational and enforcement roles to the Secretary of State and the Attorney General. Key Secretary of State duties: host public registration and the statewide accessible deletion mechanism on its website; establish the deletion system by Jan 1, 2028; adopt implementing rules; set an annual data-broker registration fee (not to exceed $300); and may charge data brokers an access fee not to exceed the Secretary's reasonable costs. Data brokers must register annually, use the deletion mechanism (beginning Aug 1, 2028), and submit triennial third‑party audit reports (first by Dec. 31, 2030). Noncompliance triggers a $200-per-day civil penalty per incomplete deletion request payable to the Secretary of State. The Attorney General may enforce penalties, seek injunctive relief, and recover attorneys' fees and costs. No direct appropriations are specified.
