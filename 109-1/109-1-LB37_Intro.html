Summary

LB37 adopts the Railroad Safety Act and amends the Public Service Commission's (PSC) jurisdiction to explicitly include railroad carrier safety pursuant to the Railroad Safety Act and specified statutory sections (74-918, 74-919, 74-1323, 75-401 to 75-430). The bill defines railroad safety terms, limits trains carrying hazardous substances on federally defined main lines to 8,500 feet, creates a civil-liability rule for trains exceeding 7,000 feet, and states the PSC lacks authority over railroad labor relations. The Federal Railroad Administration is referenced for main-line tonnage definitions. The act is operative July 1, 2026, repeals the prior version of section 75-109.01 and section 17-225. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions reference and rely on federal agencies and rules: definitions incorporate terms from 49 C.F.R. and 49 U.S.C., and "main line" is tied to timetables filed with the Federal Railroad Administration. The bill designates the Public Service Commission as "Commission" but expressly states the Railroad Safety Act does not give the Commission jurisdiction over railroad labor relations or collective‑bargaining matters. New operational limits include an 8,500‑foot maximum train length on state main lines and a civil‑litigation rule preventing reduction of plaintiff recovery for comparative fault where a train over 7,000 feet caused injury. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
