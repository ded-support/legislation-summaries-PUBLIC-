Summary

LB655 establishes a statutory "right of medical conscience" allowing health care providers and payors to opt out of participating in or paying for specific health care services based on sincerely held religious, moral, or ethical beliefs. It requires written notice and medical‑record documentation of objections and prohibits discrimination or adverse action for asserting such objections or for reporting perceived violations to government bodies. Agencies referenced: the Nebraska Attorney General (authority to receive complaints, investigate, subpoena, commence civil actions, seek damages or injunctive relief, and adopt rules), "the department" (unnamed state department) and any other state agency charged with protecting conscience rights, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and its Office for Civil Rights or other federal agencies. EMTALA and emergency‑treatment obligations remain unaffected. No appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a medical-conscience enforcement framework naming government entities: individuals may report alleged violations to the state Attorney General, "the department" (unnamed state department), other state agencies charged with protecting medical conscience, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, the Office for Civil Rights, or other federal agencies with similar responsibility. The Attorney General may investigate, administer oaths, take depositions, inspect (when authorized), issue and serve subpoenas, compel evidence and witnesses, commence civil suits for damages or injunctive relief, award attorney fees, and adopt implementing rules. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
