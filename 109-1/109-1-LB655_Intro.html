Summary

LB655 creates statutory protections for conscience-based objections by health care providers and payors and identifies enforcement channels involving government agencies. Key agencies referenced: the Nebraska Attorney General (authorized to receive complaints, investigate, determine violations, commence civil actions seeking damages, injunctive relief and attorney fees, issue subpoenas, compel testimony and evidence, conduct inspections, and promulgate rules), “the department” (statutory recipient of disclosures), any other state agency charged with protecting medical conscience, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and the HHS Office for Civil Rights (and other federal agencies with similar jurisdiction). The bill protects disclosures to those agencies and cites EMTALA (42 U.S.C. 1395dd) for emergency treatment obligations. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds enforcement and reporting provisions naming the state Attorney General, "the department" (unspecified state department), any state agency charged with protecting medical conscience, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, the Office for Civil Rights, and other federal agencies with similar authority as recipients of reports or complaints. Health care providers/payors may report suspected violations to these entities; complaints may be filed with the Attorney General, who may investigate, subpoena, take depositions, inspect (when authorized), commence civil actions (damages, injunctions, fees), and promulgate implementing rules. References federal EMTALA (42 U.S.C. 1395dd). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
