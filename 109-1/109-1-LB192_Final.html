Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) must apply for and use all appropriate SNAP options to maximize federal funding, minimize General Fund use, and employ staff to identify options and report to the Legislature. DHHS must submit an annual electronic report to the Legislature's Health and Human Services Committee detailing options, actions, caseload, costs and expenditures.

- DHHS shall develop a state outreach plan meeting the Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) of the U.S. Department of Agriculture's criteria, may accept gifts/grants/donations, partner with counties/nonprofits, and submit the plan to USDA FNS for approval and federal matching funds. DHHS is exempt from implementing the plan if private or federal funds are insufficient to cover implementation and caseload costs.

- DHHS shall create a TANF-funded policy to establish categorical SNAP eligibility, potentially raising gross income eligibility to 165% of OMB poverty guidelines (without raising net limits); this subsection is effective only if federally participable funds are received.

- DHHS is directed not to seek SNAP work-requirement waivers except as federally required, may use specified federal exemptions, and may require participation in employment and training under federal law.

- No specific state appropriations are made; the bill directs use/solicitation of federal, TANF, private funds and seeks to minimize General Fund use.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Dept. of Agriculture / Dept. of Health and Human Services):

- Prohibits the administering department from seeking, applying for, accepting, or renewing any SNAP work‑requirement waiver under 7 U.S.C. 2015(o), except it may use the state's option to grant the specific exemption authorized by 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(6).

- Authorizes the department to require individuals (unless exempt under federal law/regulation) to participate in SNAP employment and training programs per 7 U.S.C. 2015(d) and its definition at 7 U.S.C. 2015(d)(4).

Impacts: Agencies administering SNAP must not pursue federal work‑requirement waivers (other than the stated exemption) and may expand mandatory E&T participation and related administration. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
