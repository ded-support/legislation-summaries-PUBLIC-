Summary

LB578 requires the Department of Correctional Services (DOCS) to pay committed offenders wages at or above the Wage and Hour Act minimum, open deposit accounts for wage-earning inmates, and adopt rules on labor conditions, due process, restitution collection (with annual reporting to the Legislature), and compliance with federal OSHA. Wages earned in community employment are credited to inmate deposit accounts; on release DOCS must pay at least one‑third of the account immediately and the remainder within six months. The bill assigns the Reentry Cash Fund to DOCS, directs State Treasurer credits from section 33-157 (including a 25% distribution) and allows transfers from the Nebraska Crime Victim Fund to the DOCS Facility Cash Fund to cover initial LB510 implementation costs. It also eliminates DOCS provisions authorizing establishment of industries and farms. The bill references the U.S. Department of Labor’s Dictionary of Occupational Titles for occupational classification.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Labor: The bill expands the definition of "employer" to expressly include the State, its political subdivisions, jails, and the Department of Correctional Services (DOC), bringing those entities and inmate/committed-offender employment within the Wage and Hour Act’s scope and Dept. of Labor enforcement. Wages for jail/committed-offender work must meet or exceed Wage and Hour Act standards.

- Department of Correctional Services: DOC must open deposit accounts for committed offenders per §83-183.01 (with provision noting inmates may be given full control of those accounts), comply with federal Occupational Safety and Health Act requirements as if applicable, and follow Jail Standards Board rules on hours, compensation, due process, and restitution withholding/transfers. Administrators and the Jail Standards Board must provide annual restitution collection reports to the Legislature.

- Funding/appropriations: No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are provided.
