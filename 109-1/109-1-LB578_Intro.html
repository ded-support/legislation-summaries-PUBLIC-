Summary

- Department of Correctional Services (DCS)

- Committed offenders employed by DCS or others must be paid at least the state minimum/Wage and Hour Act levels (amends 83-183.01).

- DCS must open deposit accounts for wage-earning committed offenders; adopt rules on labor conditions, due process, and restitution collection; treat sentencing restitution orders as authorizations to withhold/transfer funds; and report annually to the Legislature on restitution collection (83-183, 83-183.01, 83-184.01).

- On release, offenders receive control of their deposit account funds (minimum one-third at release; full payment within six months) (83-187).

- The Reentry Cash Fund is administered by DCS; funds credited pursuant to section 33-157 and donations and used for tuition, fees, and reentry/reintegration costs for offenders in the incarceration work camp (83-917).

- Department of Labor

- The bill cites the U.S. Department of Labor’s Dictionary of Occupational Titles for occupational classification under the Wage and Hour Act (48-1202(4)).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Extends Wage and Hour Act protections to inmate employment, explicitly including offenders employed by the Department of Correctional Services (DOC) and local jails — wages must meet or exceed Wage and Hour Act minimums. This treats the State and its political subdivisions (including DOC) as employers for those purposes.

- Requires DOC to open deposit accounts for committed offenders earning wages (per §83-183.01) and directs that inmates be given full control of those accounts.

- Requires DOC and jails to follow Occupational Safety and Health Act requirements and related regulations even if the federal law may not formally apply.

- Establishes Jail Standards Board rules on hours, pay, due process, and restitution collection, plus annual electronic reporting to the Legislature; administrators must provide data to the board.

- No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
