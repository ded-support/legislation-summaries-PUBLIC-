Summary

LB578 amends multiple statutes affecting the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) and references the Department of Labor (U.S.). Key DCS provisions: committed offenders employed by DCS or others must be paid at least state Wage and Hour Act rates and DCS must open deposit accounts for wage earners; DCS must adopt rules on hours, labor conditions, compensation, due process, and restitution collection, may withhold/transfer wage funds to satisfy restitution, and must annually report restitution collection data to the Legislature; DCS must follow federal OSHA requirements. Fiscal provisions: section 33-157 directs initial Crime Victim Fund transfers to a DCS Facility Cash Fund (amounts certified by DCS) for LB510 implementation; thereafter 75% of remitted funds go to the Victim's Compensation Fund and 25% to the Reentry Cash Fund. Section 83-917 designates the Reentry Cash Fund to be administered by DCS for reentry program costs. The bill cites the U.S. Department of Labor’s Dictionary of Occupational Titles for occupational classification.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Extends Wage and Hour Act coverage to inmates and committed offenders, explicitly naming the State and its political subdivisions (including the Department of Correctional Services and jails) as employers subject to those wage requirements.

- Department of Correctional Services must pay committed offenders at least Wage and Hour Act rates, open deposit accounts for offender wages under section 83-183.01, and give inmates control of their deposit accounts as specified. The department must also act in accordance with federal OSHA requirements even where the federal law might not directly apply.

- The Jail Standards Board will set rules on hours, conditions, compensation, due process, restitution collection, and annual restitution reporting to the Legislature; administrators must report to the board.

- No new funding, appropriations, or specific budget allocations are included.
