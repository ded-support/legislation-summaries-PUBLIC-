Summary

LB67 creates the Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act and assigns complaint intake, review, investigation, enforcement, and adjudicatory authority to "the department" and its director (including use of contracted peer review organizations). Hospitals providing emergency care to sexual‑assault survivors must comply with the Act; the department may investigate complaints, hold informal conferences (within 30 days) or formal hearings under department rules, and decisions are appealable under the Administrative Procedure Act. Remedies: first substantiated complaint triggers a written warning and required corrective action; second and subsequent substantiated complaints carry fines of $1,000 either per affected survivor or per month until required training is provided. The bill authorizes department policies to address provider moral/religious objections and contract with peer review organizations; no other appropriations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a complaint-and-enforcement process administered by "the department" (and its director), including use of department representatives and contracted peer review organizations to review alleged failures to comply with the Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act. Complaints may trigger review/investigation; hospitals have 15 days to contest a notice and may request an informal conference (held within 30 days) or a hearing. Timelines: representative's report within 20 working days; department decision within 10 working days; hospitals may request a hearing within 5 working days; department hearings follow its rules and the Administrative Procedure Act and allow subpoenas. Remedies: first substantiated complaint = written warning and required correction; second/subsequent = $1,000 fines (per survivor found denied required info/contraception or $1,000 per month until required training is provided). No appropriations or budget allocations are specified.
