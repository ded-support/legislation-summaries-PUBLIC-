Summary

LB67 creates the Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act and assigns enforcement to "the department" (and its director). The department receives and reviews complaints about hospital compliance, may investigate, and considers factors when deciding to investigate. Hospitals get notice and 15 days to contest; they may request informal conferences with a department representative or a contracted peer review organization, or a hearing under department rules. The department issues decisions (appeals under the Administrative Procedure Act). Enforcement: first substantiated complaint = written warning and required correction; second and subsequent = $1,000 fines either per survivor denied information/contraception or per month until required training is provided. The bill contains a severability clause. No funding, appropriation, or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds enforcement procedures vesting complaint authority with "the department" (and the department director). Complaints about hospital compliance with the Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act are to be filed with the department, which may investigate using specified factors. The bill creates timelines and processes: 15-day response to department notices; option for an informal conference (department staff or a contracted peer review organization) to be held within 30 days; a written report within 20 working days; department decision within 10 working days; and a 5-working-day window to request a hearing. Hearings follow department rules; decisions are mailed and may be appealed under the Administrative Procedure Act. Remedies: first substantiated complaint yields a written warning and required corrective action; second and subsequent substantiated complaints carry a $1,000 fine either per affected survivor or per month until required training is provided. No appropriations or budget changes are included.
