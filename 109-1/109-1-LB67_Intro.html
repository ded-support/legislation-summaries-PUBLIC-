Summary

LB67 (Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act) charges "the department" and its director with complaint intake, investigation, and enforcement for hospital compliance. The department may investigate, convene informal conferences (using department staff or a contracted peer review organization), hold hearings under departmental rules, and issue decisions appealable under the Administrative Procedure Act. Remedial authority: a first substantiated complaint triggers a written warning and required correction; second and subsequent substantiated complaints carry a $1,000 fine either per affected sexual assault survivor or per month until mandated training is provided. Hospitals must provide personnel training, develop policies to address conscience objections, and comply with emergency contraception/forensic exam protocols. The bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions designate the (unnamed) department as the recipient and investigator of complaints under the Sexual Assault Emergency Care Act, authorize the director to issue notices and hold informal conferences, and permit the department to contract with peer review organizations to conduct conferences. Timelines and procedures are specified for hospital responses, assignment of non‑investigator representatives, reports, departmental decisions, hearings (per department rules), and appeals under the Administrative Procedure Act. Enforcement: first substantiated complaint yields a written warning and required correction; second and subsequent substantiated complaints carry a $1,000 fine either per survivor denied required information/offer of emergency contraception or per month until required staff training is provided. No appropriations or budget allocations are included.
