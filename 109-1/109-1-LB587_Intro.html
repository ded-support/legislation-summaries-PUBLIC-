Summary

LB587 amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act by revising sections 76-1425 and 76-1427. Key changes: tenants may terminate for landlord noncompliance with shorter notice for recurring breaches (now at least seven days' written notice for a repeat violation within six months); landlords have a seven-day cure period for material noncompliance before a termination date set not less than fourteen days after notice. Tenant remedies are expanded to explicitly include remediation of known mold problems and pest infestations (tenant may purchase supplies/services and deduct costs from rent), recovery for diminution in fair rental value, recovery for loss in value of the tenant's personal property, rent abatement for the period of noncompliance, and, for deliberate failures, recovery of actual/reasonable cost or fair value of substitute housing. Reasonable attorney’s fees are allowed. No state agencies, departments, or budget appropriations are referenced or affected.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds tenant remedies for landlord failure to remediate known mold or pest infestations: tenants may (a) purchase supplies or services to remediate during the landlord’s noncompliance and deduct actual, reasonable costs from rent, and (b) recover damages for diminution in value of the tenant’s personal property caused by the landlord’s deliberate or negligent noncompliance. Text also reorganizes/remarks subsections. No government agencies or funding/appropriations are mentioned or changed.
