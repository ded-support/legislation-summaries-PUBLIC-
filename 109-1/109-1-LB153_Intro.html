Summary

LB153 requires the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to take multiple Medicaid/CHIP actions: submit a state plan amendment to CMS by October 1, 2025 to implement at least six months of postpartum coverage under the Children's Health Insurance Program (with an option for 12 months); DHHS earlier must seek federal approval (by October 1, 2023) to extend Medicaid postpartum coverage from 60 days to at least six months (or 12 months). DHHS must designate two medical respite facilities (by October 1, 2025), report annually, and provide coverage for electric personal-use breast pumps, lactation consultations, translation/interpretation services, and continuous glucose monitors per specified timelines. Funding: these services are to be financed from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund (specified uses expanded), with investment earnings credited to the General Fund beginning October 1, 2024. The bill signals specific appropriation intents (e.g., up to $600,000 annually for CGM expansion; a 145% rate increase for lactation consultation).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services must, by October 1, 2025, apply to CMS via a CHIP state plan amendment for federal matching funds to create a health-services initiative providing at least six months of postpartum coverage for mothers whose child is enrolled under the unborn child option. Covered services must match Medicaid’s comprehensive postpartum benefits; the department may seek authorization for twelve months. The Legislature expresses intent to fund these CHIP postpartum services from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund (section 68‑996). No specific appropriation amounts are specified.
