Summary

Creates the Landlord and Tenant Radon Awareness Act. It requires lessors (which may include government agencies or subdivisions) to provide prospective tenants and tenants, on application or request, a radon-hazard disclosure form and any available radon test records; test results must be furnished within ten days of receipt. The bill references licensing under the Radiation Control Act for "radon measurement specialists" and "radon mitigation specialists," tying practitioner credentials to that regulatory framework. It bars lessors from withholding security deposits to pay for radon testing or mitigation, except where a tenant installed a mitigation system without the lessor’s consent and the system was not installed by a licensed radon mitigation specialist. Applies to leases executed on or after the act’s effective date. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Landlord and Tenant Radon Awareness Act," requiring lessors — expressly including any government agency or subdivision thereof — to provide prospective tenants (at application and on request) with radon information and any test results within 10 days of receipt. It mandates use of a specified radon-hazard disclosure form and recognizes radon measurement and mitigation specialists as individuals licensed under the Radiation Control Act (i.e., subject to the State's licensing regime). The State of Nebraska is cited as recommending testing and mitigation. It bars withholding security deposits to pay for radon testing/mitigation except where a tenant installed an unapproved or improperly installed mitigation system; itemized withholding must be provided. No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
