Summary

LB206 amends the Nebraska Criminal Code to create enhanced penalties for specified "covered offenses" (assaults, robbery, arson, burglary, theft, criminal mischief/trespass, and related inchoate and accessory conduct) when committed in a "disaster area" during an "emergency period." Disaster area is tied to: a gubernatorial emergency proclamation (section 81-829.40), a local emergency declaration (section 81-829.50), a Presidential major disaster/emergency declaration under 42 U.S.C. 5121 et seq. (Stafford Act), or a mandatory evacuation order by the appropriate local, state, or federal official. Penalties increase one classification (Class I misdemeanor elevated to Class IV felony). Prosecutors must allege enhancements in charging documents and prove them beyond a reasonable doubt to judge or jury. No funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a new crime-enhancement provision. Key points:

- Defines covered offenses (various assaults, robbery, arson, burglary, theft, criminal mischief/trespass, and related inchoate or accessory conduct).

- Defines "disaster area" to include: emergency proclamations by the Governor (sec. 81-829.40), local emergency declarations (sec. 81-829.50), Presidential major disaster/emergency declarations under the Stafford Act (42 U.S.C. 5121 et seq.), or mandatory evacuation orders by the appropriate local, state, or federal official.

- Defines "emergency period" as the duration of the proclamation/declaration/order (or 30 days after issuance if no end date).

- Penalty enhancement: offenses committed in a disaster area during the emergency period are punished at the next higher classification (Class I misdemeanor enhanced to Class IV felony).

- Enhancement allegations must appear in the charging document and be proved beyond a reasonable doubt by the prosecuting attorney.

No agencies are granted funding or appropriations; no budgetary changes are included.
