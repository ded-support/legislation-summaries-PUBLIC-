Summary

LB199 (2025) amends the Nonrecourse Civil Litigation Act and adds a two‑year statute of limitations for personal‑injury claims accruing on or after the bill’s effective date. Agency-related provisions:

- Secretary of State: issues, renews, may temporarily issue, refuse, suspend, revoke, or deny certificates of registration for civil litigation funding companies; may act for character/fitness/financial concerns or for violations (including the bill’s new disclosure requirement). Actions subject to the Administrative Procedure Act with notice and hearing.

- Reporting/recordkeeping: registered funding companies must annually submit prescribed data to the Secretary of State (number/amount of fundings, repayments, fees charged, shortfalls). The Secretary of State must prepare and electronically submit an annual aggregate report to the Clerk of the Legislature and the Legislature’s Judiciary Committee.

- Revisor of Statutes: directed to assign the new statute‑of‑limitations section to Chapter 25, article 2.

No appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a two‑year statute of limitations for personal injury claims, effective only for causes accruing on or after the act's effective date. Creates disclosure duties for consumers (or their attorneys) of any nonrecourse civil litigation funding: they must provide copies to each party/party’s attorney, "any court, agency, or tribunal" where the claim is pending, and any known indemnitor/insurer (the latter even before a suit or administrative proceeding is filed). The duty is ongoing; new or amended contracts must be disclosed within 30 days. Such funding agreements and participants are discoverable, and knowing violations are subject to penalties. No specific government departments are named and no changes to funding, appropriations, or budgets are included.
