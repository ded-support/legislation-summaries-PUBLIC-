Summary

- Defines "Department" to mean the Department of Labor (and "Commissioner" as Commissioner of Labor).

- Assigns the Nebraska Department of Labor to calculate and publish, by October 15 each year starting 2026, the minimum wage rate that will take effect the following January 1.

- Provides that employees may submit Wage and Hour Act complaints to the department; the department will forward credible complaints to the appropriate county attorney or pursue its own enforcement and must develop forms and promulgate rules to implement this process.

- States all records and information obtained by the department under the bill are confidential and disclosable only by court order.

- Requires employer posting to include advisement and instructions on submitting complaints to the department.

- No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions give the Department of Labor (Commissioner) expanded enforcement authority over employers of tipped employees: employers must retain for at least three years employee records (name, address, occupation, rate and amount of pay per period, daily and weekly hours), keep them on-site or at a suitable location, and make them available to the Commissioner by appointment (including on-demand if stored out-of-state). Employers must provide sworn statements on Department-prescribed forms and may not refuse access, falsify records, withhold information, retaliate against employees who complain or testify, or pay below required rates. Records obtained by the Department are confidential and disclosable only by court order. Employees with a reasonable belief of a Wage and Hour Act violation may submit a complaint to the Department “in a manner prescribed by the Department” (using Department forms); the Department will develop forms/rules and will forward credible complaints to county attorneys or pursue enforcement itself. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
