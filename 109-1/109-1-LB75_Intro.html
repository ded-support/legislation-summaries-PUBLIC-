Summary

This bill designates "Department" as the Department of Labor and "Commissioner" as the Commissioner of Labor and assigns the department/commissioner several specific duties and powers, including:

- The Nebraska Department of Labor must annually (by Oct. 15, beginning 2026) calculate and publish the minimum wage rate effective the following Jan. 1.

- The commissioner may approve certain training programs, subpoena records and witnesses, inspect records, and gather testimony for Wage and Hour Act enforcement.

- Employers must maintain records, provide sworn statements on forms prescribed by the commissioner, and make records available for inspection; records obtained by the department are confidential and disclosable only by court order.

- The department will accept employee complaints, forward credible complaints to county attorneys or pursue enforcement, and promulgate necessary forms and rules.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Labor):

- Employers of tipped/gratuity-paid workers must keep 3 years of records (name, address, occupation, rate, pay amounts, daily/week hours), kept on premises or made available on demand; records are open for inspection by the Commissioner by appointment; out-of-state records must be produced on demand.

- On demand, employers must provide sworn statements on forms and in the manner prescribed by the Commissioner.

- All records and information obtained by the Department are confidential, not public records, and may be disclosed only by court order.

- Employees with a reasonable belief of Wage and Hour Act violations may file a complaint with the Department in a manner prescribed by the Department; the Department will forward credible complaints to the county attorney or pursue its own enforcement and must adopt forms and rules to implement this.

- Employers are prohibited from denying Commissioner access, falsifying or refusing records, retaliating against employees, or underpaying wages; each day of violation is a separate offense.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
