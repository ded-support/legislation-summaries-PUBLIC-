Summary

- Department of Economic Development (the "department"): will accept applications (through Dec. 31, 2025) to create up to two "destination districts," review/approve applications, set the state occupation tax rate for transactions in approved districts after notice and hearing, designate trustees to hold occupation-tax proceeds, enter agreements using those proceeds, form and appoint members (including removal authority) of a community redevelopment authority for each district, and annually update workforce-housing construction cost thresholds based on the Producer Price Index.

- Department of Transportation: may enter into agreements with approved destination-district applicants to construct roads, bridges, and other infrastructure within a district (subject to applicable federal and state laws and codes).

- U.S. Department of Labor (Bureau of Labor Statistics): its Producer Price Index is specified as the basis for the Department of Economic Development's annual updates to housing cost limits.

No specific appropriations, budget allocations, or direct funding amounts are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Economic Development (DED)

- DED may accept applications (until Dec. 31, 2025) to create up to two "destination districts" (40‑year term, ≤5,000 acres) if projects exceed $3 billion in new development and 10 million annual visitors.

- DED will review/approve applications, keep most materials confidential, set and levy a new state occupation tax (in addition to sales tax) for transactions on applicant-owned property after notice/hearing, designate a trustee bank to hold occupation‑tax proceeds, and use those funds in agreements to advance projects.

- DED may form and appoint (and remove) a Community Redevelopment Authority for the district and treat the district as a "village" under Community Development Law.

Department of Transportation (DOT)

- DOT may enter agreements with applicants to construct roads, bridges, and related infrastructure within districts (must meet federal/state codes).

Department of Labor

- No provisions or funding changes affecting the Department of Labor.

Budget/funding effects

- Establishes a district‑level occupation tax and authorizes use of tax proceeds and applicant‑issued bonds (not state obligations). No explicit state appropriations or direct budget allocations to agencies are provided.
