Summary

LB477 amends the Workplace Privacy Act to prohibit employers from demanding access to employees’ or applicants’ personal Internet accounts or forcing login, contact-list changes, or settings changes. It generally bans requiring apps or wearable devices to track employee location or contacts. Exception: the Governor may authorize employer contact tracing during a Governor-declared pandemic state (per §81-829.40) only if the proclamation permits contact tracing, it occurs on employer premises during work hours, and collected data is destroyed within 48 hours after any Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommended tracing period. Data that links employees to tracked employer property must be stored by an independent third party and released only by warrant (court/law enforcement). Employers are prohibited from taking adverse actions for refusals or violations. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. Original sections are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions restrict employer-mandated location/tracking apps or wearable devices, except when the Governor issues a pandemic state-of-emergency under section 81-829.40. Under such a proclamation, employers may only perform contact tracing on their premises during work hours; collected data must not be placed in personnel files and must be destroyed within 48 hours after any tracing period recommended by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Employers may track employer-owned property or monitor employer property use so long as data do not associate employees with that property. Any data that does associate an employee must be held by an independent third party and accessed only by warrant. Employers are prohibited from adverse actions based on refusal to comply or on data obtained in violation of the rule. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
