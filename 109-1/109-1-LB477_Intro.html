Summary

LB477 amends the Workplace Privacy Act. It broadens the statutory definition of “employer” to expressly include subcontractors and customers. It prohibits employers from requiring employees or applicants to provide usernames/passwords, log into personal Internet accounts in the employer’s presence, add contacts, or change account settings. Employers also may not require use of apps or wearable devices to track location, travel patterns, or contacts, except during a Governor-declared pandemic state of emergency under section 81-829.40 and only if: (1) use is limited to on‑premises contact tracing during work hours; (2) data are excluded from personnel files and destroyed within 48 hours after any CDC-recommended tracing period; and (3) other privacy safeguards apply. Any employee-associated tracking data must be stored by an independent third party and accessed only by warrant. Employers are barred from adverse actions for refusal or for relying on improperly obtained data. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds conditional exceptions allowing employer use of electronic devices for contact tracing only when the Governor proclaims a pandemic state of emergency under section 81-829.40. Conditions: tracing limited to employer premises during work hours; collected data excluded from personnel files and destroyed within 48 hours after the end of any tracing period recommended by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Data linking employees to tracked property must be held by an independent third party and released only by warrant. No government funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
