Summary

The bill amends duties of the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) regarding children under its charge. DHHS must take custody/control of children’s assets, screen each child for Social Security eligibility within 60 days, submit applications and appeal denials, and, when serving as representative payee, manage, conserve, and hold benefit payments separate from department funds (including use of trusts and accounts to avoid federal asset/resource limits). It must conserve minimum percentages of benefits by age, provide accounting and written notice at review hearings, consult with beneficiaries 14+ at least semiannually, assist with benefit applications before discharge, and deliver an exit notice. By October 1, 2026, DHHS must create a beneficiary request form and adopt implementing rules. DHHS shall maximize federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds for implementation costs.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services must screen each child in its care for Social Security benefit eligibility within 60 days and apply (and appeal if needed). If DHHS is representative payee, it must manage payments per federal law: hold funds separate (trust account), conserve age-based minimum percentages (14+ 20%; 16+ 30%; 17+ 40%; 18+ 50%), avoid exceeding federal asset limits by using allowable accounts (e.g., PASS, ABLE, IDA, special‑needs trust), and send unspent funds to the child on exit. Children (14+) get semiannual consultation and may seek juvenile‑court orders on use. DHHS must provide notices, accounting on request, a public access form by Oct 1, 2026, and assist with benefit continuity before discharge. For implementation costs, DHHS must maximize federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds. No specific appropriations are included.
