Summary

The bill amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §43-907 to impose duties on the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) for children under its charge: DHHS must take custody/control of children’s assets; screen each child for Social Security benefit eligibility within 60 days of entry and apply/appeal on the child's behalf; serve as representative payee when appropriate and manage/payments consistent with federal law (holding funds separate, conserving specified minimum percentages by age, avoiding federal asset/resource limits, and using specified exempt accounts); provide accounting and written notices to children, guardians/attorneys, and the juvenile court (including itemized expenditures at review hearings); consult with beneficiaries 14+ every six months; assist with benefit continuation before discharge; create a public form and promulgate rules by October 1, 2026. DHHS must seek maximum federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds for implementation costs.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions require the Department of Health and Human Services to screen each child in its custody for Social Security benefit eligibility within 60 days, apply and appeal as needed, provide age‑appropriate written notices and accounting, and allow children (and certain representatives) access to records and requests to use/conserve funds. When the department is representative payee it must manage payments per federal law, hold funds separate (typically in a trust), use accounts that avoid exceeding federal asset limits (e.g., PASS, ABLE, IDAs, special‑needs trusts, dedicated back‑payment accounts), and conserve minimum percentages of benefits by age (14+: 20%; 16+: 30%; 17+: 40%; 18+: 50%). For beneficiaries 14+, the department must consult at least every six months and provide six‑month pre‑exit assistance and notice. By Oct. 1, 2026, DHHS must publish a form to request access. The department must seek federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds to implement these requirements. No specific appropriations are listed.
