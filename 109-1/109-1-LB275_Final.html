Summary

LB275 amends §43-907 to impose specific duties on the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) for children under its charge. DHHS must screen each child for Social Security benefit eligibility within 60 days, submit applications and appeals as needed, and serve as representative payee when appropriate. As payee, DHHS must manage, use, conserve, and hold benefit payments separately (including trust accounts), avoid exceeding federal asset/resource limits, conserve specified minimum percentages by age (20% at 14+, 30% at 16+, 40% at 17+, 50% at 18+), and maintain/produce accounting and notices to the juvenile court at each review. DHHS must publish a beneficiary request form and adopt rules by October 1, 2026, and must maximize federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds for implementation costs.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for the Department of Health and Human Services concerning children in its charge who receive Social Security benefits: screen each child within 60 days and apply/appeal for benefits as needed; provide age‑appropriate written notices and accounting (including total spent and itemized expenditures) and permit requests for access to funds. When DHHS is representative payee it must hold benefits separately (trust account), conserve specified minimum percentages by age (14+: 20%; 16+: 30%; 17+: 40%; 18+: 50%), avoid exceeding federal asset/resource limits using vehicles like PASS, ABLE, IDAs, special‑needs trusts, and dedicated back‑payment accounts, and consult beneficiaries at least every six months. Youth 14+ may petition juvenile court to direct use of funds. DHHS must provide exit notices and create a public request form by Oct 1, 2026. Funding: DHHS must seek federal Title IV‑E funds before using General Funds; no new appropriation is specified.
