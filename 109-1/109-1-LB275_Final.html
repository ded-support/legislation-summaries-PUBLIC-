Summary

The bill amends §43-907 to impose multiple duties on the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) regarding children under its charge. Key provisions require DHHS to: screen each child for Social Security eligibility within 60 days and apply/appeal on the child's behalf; serve as representative payee when appropriate and manage/hold benefit payments separate from department funds consistent with federal law; report receipt, conservation, and expenditures to the juvenile court at each review and provide accounting on request; consult beneficiaries age 14+ semiannually; conserve specified minimum percentages of benefits by age (14+: 20%; 16+: 30%; 17+: 40%; 18+: 50%); create a public form and adopt rules by Oct 1, 2026; and seek maximal federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds for implementation costs.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates duties for the Department of Health and Human Services to screen children in its care for Social Security benefit eligibility within 60 days, file and, if needed, appeal applications on their behalf, and provide age‑appropriate written notice and accounting to children and representatives. If the department acts as representative payee it must manage payments per federal law, keep funds separate (generally in trust), use accounts that avoid federal resource limits (e.g., ABLE, special‑needs trusts), and conserve minimum percentages of benefits by age (14+: 20%; 16+: 30%; 17+: 40%; 18+: 50%). For beneficiaries 14+, the department must consult at least semiannually, assist with benefit continuity six months before exit, and notify exiting children about retained funds and payee status. A public request form is due by Oct 1, 2026. Fiscal: department must maximize federal Title IV‑E funding before using General Funds; no specific appropriation amounts included.
