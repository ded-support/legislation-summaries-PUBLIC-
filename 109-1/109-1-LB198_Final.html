Summary

LB198 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act (Reissue §§ 44-4601, 44-4603, 44-4610) to tighten regulation of pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), health carriers, health benefit plans, plan sponsors, pharmacy services administrative organizations, and pharmacies. Key provisions: expanded definitions (including clinician-administered drugs), nondiscrimination and reporting limits for specialty pharmacies, delivery and appeal requirements for clinician-administered drugs, protections allowing network pharmacies to decline dispensing if paid below acquisition cost, prohibitions on steering or auto-transfers to mail-order affiliates (with limited exceptions), and rules allowing retail pharmacies to offer delivery ancillary services. The bill bans spread pricing in new PBM–health plan contracts after Jan 1, 2026 (fully prohibited by Jan 1, 2029). It references the federal Drug Supply Chain Security Act for track-and-trace. No state agency or department is specifically named, and the bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocations. The act becomes operative Jan. 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and regulatory limits on pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), health carriers, specialty pharmacies, retail/network pharmacies, and clinician‑administered drug dispensing. Key provisions require PBMs to treat unaffiliated specialty pharmacies comparably, limit reporting frequency, comply with federal Drug Supply Chain Security Act track‑and‑trace requirements, require advance delivery notices/signatures for clinician‑administered drugs, create appeal/exception processes, protect provider and patient choice (limits on forced mail‑order or PBM‑selected specialty pharmacies), allow pharmacies to refuse to dispense below acquisition cost without retaliation, and permit retail delivery as an ancillary service. It phases out PBM “spread pricing”: barred in new contracts after 1/1/2026 (with some extensions) and fully prohibited from 1/1/2029. No specific state or federal agencies/departments are created or assigned new duties and no funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included.
