Summary

LB198 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act to (1) define terms including clinician‑administered drug, specialty pharmacy, and spread pricing; (2) restrict pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) and health carriers/health benefit plans from excluding accredited Nebraska specialty pharmacies, imposing stricter terms on unaffiliated specialty pharmacies, demanding excessive reporting, or compelling clinician‑administered drug handling that undermines provider administration or timely delivery; (3) bar health plans/carriers/PBMs from forcing exclusive mail‑order use, transferring prescriptions without member consent, or auto‑enrolling members in mail order except limited maintenance‑medication exceptions; (4) permit network pharmacies to decline to fill if paid below acquisition cost without retaliation; (5) protect retail pharmacies offering ancillary delivery services; and (6) prohibit spread pricing in new PBM‑plan contracts after Jan. 1, 2026, with a full ban by Jan. 1, 2029. Operative date: Jan. 1, 2026. No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No state agencies or departments are newly created or referenced; the only government law cited is the federal Drug Supply Chain Security Act (21 U.S.C. 360eee et seq.) for track-and-trace compliance. The additions define "clinician‑administered drug" and set numerous limits and obligations on pharmacy benefit managers, health carriers, specialty and retail pharmacies (reporting frequency, nondiscrimination, delivery timing, appeal processes, prohibitions on forcing use of certain pharmacies, protections for providers who decline under‑reimbursed drugs, and rules on mail‑order auto‑enrollment). Contracts with PBMs may not start new spread‑pricing after Jan 1, 2026, with a complete ban by Jan 1, 2029. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
