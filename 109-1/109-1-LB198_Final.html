Summary

LB198 amends Nebraska’s Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act and governs pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), health carriers/health benefit plans, plan sponsors, pharmacy services administrative organizations, specialty and retail pharmacies, and network pharmacists. Major actions: require PBM inclusion of accredited specialty pharmacies and limit audit/reporting frequency; establish delivery, documentation, and appeal processes for clinician‑administered drugs and prohibit penalizing providers who decline PBM‑sourced drugs; bar PBMs/carriers from mail‑order exclusivity, involuntary prescription transfers, and financial incentives favoring PBM affiliate pharmacies (with narrow maintenance‑drug exceptions); permit network pharmacies to refuse to fill if paid below acquisition cost without retaliation; and phase out spread pricing in PBM‑plan contracts (no new contracts with spread pricing after Jan 1, 2026; banned by Jan 1, 2029). Operative Jan 1, 2026. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and regulatory limits on pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), health carriers, health benefit plans, plan sponsors, specialty and retail pharmacies, and providers. Key provisions require PBMs to: treat unaffiliated specialty pharmacies no more stringently than affiliates; limit reporting to quarterly unless necessary; comply with federal Drug Supply Chain Security Act track‑and‑trace requirements and provide data on request; meet delivery timing/signature rules for clinician‑administered drugs; establish appeal/exception processes; and refrain from forcing patient pickup, penalizing providers, or using exclusive mail‑order steering except narrow opt‑outs. Permits pharmacies to decline under‑cost dispensing without retaliation. bans spread pricing in new PBM–plan contracts after Jan 1, 2026 (with limited grandfathering) and fully bans it Jan 1, 2029. No government agencies/departments or funding/appropriation changes are specified.
