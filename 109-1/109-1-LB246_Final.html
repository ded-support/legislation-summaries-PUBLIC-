Summary

LB246 amends the Nebraska Pure Food Act to add a definition for "cultivated-protein food product" (foods resembling animal tissue grown from manufacturing cells) and declares such products to be "adulterated." It bars manufacturing, importing, distributing, promoting, offering, or selling adulterated food and makes violations a separate deceptive trade practice. The bill links these changes to the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act by adding a specific cross-reference. The bill also references existing statutory adoptions of the Food Code and Current Good Manufacturing Practice standards (sections 81-2,257.01 and 81-2,259). No specific state agencies or departments are named, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of “cultivated‑protein food product” as a food whose sensory attributes resemble animal tissue but is produced from manufacturing cells grown in vitro (including stem cells originally isolated from an agricultural food animal). Prohibits producing, importing, promoting, displaying for sale, or attempting to sell such cultivated‑protein products. States that any violation committed in the course of business also constitutes a deceptive trade practice and that this section does not alter liability under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Also references violations of section 81‑2,282. No state agencies or departments are named, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes included.
