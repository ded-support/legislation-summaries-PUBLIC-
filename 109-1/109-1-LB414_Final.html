Summary

LB414 establishes a state suicide mortality review team and requires it to enter a cooperative agreement with the Nebraska Violent Death Reporting System (NVDRS) to share and store review-related data. Upon request by the bill’s director, a county attorney or the Attorney General may issue subpoenas for records (to be entered into NVDRS and provided to the team as aggregate narratives). The team has five business days to obtain requested records and may access coroner/autopsy reports, law enforcement (including probation and corrections) reports, medical and mental‑health records, EMS and prescription‑monitoring data, school and employment records, toxicology, and next‑of‑kin interviews. Prosecuting attorneys may permit law enforcement to withhold records that would jeopardize prosecutions. All team materials are confidential and largely immune from subpoena or use in court. Annual reports are due April 1 to the Clerk of the Legislature and the Department of Health and Human Services. No funding or appropriations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority and procedures for suicide mortality review teams to establish a cooperative agreement with the Nebraska Violent Death Reporting System to share and store review-related data. At a director's request, a county attorney or the Attorney General may issue subpoenas for listed records (coroner/autopsy, law enforcement/probation/corrections, medical and mental‑health records, EMS, prescription monitoring, toxicology, employment, school, next‑of‑kin interviews); subpoenaed material must be entered into the Violent Death Reporting System and provided to the team as an aggregate narrative. Agencies must provide requested records within five business days unless law enforcement, with prosecuting attorney approval, withholds records that would impede a criminal investigation. Review team records are confidential and generally exempt from subpoena/discovery; team members have testimonial protections. Beginning April 1, 2026, and annually, the team must report to the Clerk of the Legislature, Department of Health and Human Services, and nonprofits with specified case data and recommendations. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
