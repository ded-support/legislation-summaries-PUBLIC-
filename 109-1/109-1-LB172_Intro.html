Summary

The bill directs the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) to order mental‑health evaluations to determine "dangerous sex‑offender" status for specified sexual‑offense convictions, with evaluations ordered at least 180 days before scheduled release and written findings provided by 150 days before release. Failure or refusal to participate in or complete sex‑offender treatment offered by DCS or the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) while incarcerated may trigger such evaluation; inability to complete treatment due to time constraints or unavailable programming is explicitly not treated as refusal. Victims under 18 must report sexual assault to DHHS, and confidentiality exceptions allow sharing victim identity/information with DHHS for child abuse/neglect investigations. The Attorney General’s Child Protection Division may investigate or prosecute upon written DHHS request if a county attorney declines. No appropriations or budget allocations for DCS or DHHS are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions expand and clarify child‑pornography offenses and definitions: they list covered statutes, define terms (electronic communication device, sexually explicit conduct, visual depiction, and “indistinguishable”), set affirmative defenses, and create age‑based and repeat‑offender sentencing tiers (under 19 = Class III felony; 19+ = Class ID; prior covered‑offense conviction = Class IC). The text also cross‑references the Child Pornography Prevention Act and related sections. There are no funding, appropriation, or explicit administrative changes naming the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services; DOC would implement any new sentences under existing authority, and DHHS is not assigned duties or budget changes.
