Summary

The bill directs reporting and investigatory roles for DHHS and new prosecutorial authority for the Attorney General’s Child Protection Division: allegations of sexual assault involving victims under 18 must be reported to the Department of Health and Human Services; criminal‑justice agencies may share victim identity and identifying information with DHHS for child abuse/neglect investigations; and the AG’s Child Protection Division may, on written request from DHHS (or others), investigate or initiate prosecution of crimes against children.

For the Department of Correctional Services, the bill requires DCS to order mental‑health evaluations to assess “dangerous sex offender” status for specified convictions. Incarcerated persons who refuse or fail sex‑offender treatment offered by DCS or DHHS are subject to evaluation, except when noncompletion is due to time constraints or lack of programming; evaluations must be ordered ≥180 days before release with written findings by 150 days prior.

No explicit appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations for DHHS or DCS are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds and clarifies definitions and scope for child‑pornography offenses: expands "covered offense" to list specific statute sections and new sections of this act; defines "electronic communication device," "sexually explicit conduct," "visual depiction," and "indistinguishable"; sets out affirmative defenses; and establishes sentencing classes by age and prior convictions (under 19 — Class III felony; 19+ — Class ID felony; prior covered‑offense conviction — Class IC felony). For sections 28‑1601 to 28‑1603 it aligns definitions with the Child Pornography Prevention Act.

Impact on agencies: no explicit changes, funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services are included.
