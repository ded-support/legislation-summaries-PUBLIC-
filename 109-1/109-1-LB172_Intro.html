Summary

The bill designates the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) as the recipient of sexual‑assault reports for victims under 18 and authorizes sharing victims' identities and personal information with DHHS and other agencies as necessary for child‑abuse or neglect investigations. At DHHS's written request, the Attorney General’s Child Protection Division may investigate or initiate prosecution. The Department of Correctional Services must order mental‑health evaluations to determine dangerous sex‑offender status for specified convictions and for inmates who refuse or fail sex‑offender treatment while incarcerated (excluding failures due to time constraints or program unavailability). No appropriations, budgetary allocations, or other funding changes for DHHS or the Department of Correctional Services are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds and clarifies definitions and elements for child‑pornography offenses: expands "covered offense" to list specific statutes (including sections 3–5 of the bill and the Child Pornography Prevention Act), adopts "electronic communication device" and "indistinguishable" definitions, specifies offense elements and affirmative defenses, and sets age‑based felony classifications (under 19 = Class III; 19+ = Class ID; prior conviction = Class IC). Also cross‑references related forfeiture/covered‑property provisions.

No language in the added text references the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services, and there are no appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations included.
