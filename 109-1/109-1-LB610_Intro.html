Summary

The bill amends multiple Medical Assistance Act provisions assigning responsibilities to the Department of Health and Human Services (the department). Key provisions:

- Supplemental reimbursement equals federal financial participation and must not, combined with other reimbursements, exceed 100% of actual costs; payments distributed to eligible providers based on actual allowable costs.

- Governmental entities seeking supplemental reimbursement must provide evidence, data, and records as specified by the department and certify eligibility per federal rules.

- The department must seek necessary federal approvals by January 1, 2026; may limit the program to Title XIX–allowable costs; and will implement intergovernmental transfers only if federal participation is available.

- The department will submit claims and annual assurances for federal financial participation; the chief executive officer may decline or adjust transfers/payments to maintain federal compliance.

- Increased capitation payments may begin for services on or after January 1, 2025, subject to federal approval.

No new state appropriations or specific budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the Department of Health and Human Services to seek any necessary federal approvals (by 2026) to implement subsection (2) of the Ground Emergency Medical Transport Act as that provision and related regulations existed on January 1, 2025. The directive includes assessing actual and allowable costs and ensuring compliance with other applicable federal requirements. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
