Summary

LB550 requires public school districts to adopt a released‑time policy excusing students to attend off‑campus religious instruction under specified conditions. Key provisions affecting government entities:

- School districts must treat excused students as attending for purposes of school funding and attendance counts.

- Released‑time courses generally may not be held on district property unless allowed under a neutral equal‑access policy that opens facilities to community groups.

- Districts may award academic credit for such courses only after secular evaluation using the same criteria as similar offerings; credit determinations must be neutral to religious content or denomination.

- Districts may not deny released‑time sponsors equal access to funds, benefits, or services available to community groups.

- Aggrieved persons/organizations may sue districts for damages, equitable/declaratory relief, and attorney fees.

The act takes effect immediately as an emergency measure.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules for "released time" courses administered around school districts: students excused to attend such courses are still considered enrolled in their school for attendance and school funding calculations; courses generally may not be held on district property unless allowed under a neutral equal‑access policy; districts may award academic credit using secular, neutral evaluation criteria; districts cannot deny released‑time providers equal access to funds, benefits, or services they provide to other community groups. Enforcement allows private suits against the school district for actual damages, equitable/declaratory relief, and reasonable attorney fees. No state agency or department is named and there are no new appropriations or explicit budget allocations.
