Summary

LB550 requires school districts to adopt a policy excusing students to attend released‑time courses for religious instruction, subject to parental consent and specified safeguards. School districts may not use public funds for the courses, and courses generally must be off district property unless the district has a neutral equal‑access policy opening facilities to community groups. Students excused to attend such courses "shall be considered as attending" their school for purposes of determining school funding and satisfying attendance requirements. Districts may award academic credit for released‑time courses only after secular evaluation criteria. The bill preserves equal access to district‑provided funds, benefits, or services for sponsoring entities and authorizes aggrieved persons or organizations to sue school districts for actual damages, equitable/declaratory relief, and attorney's fees. It takes effect immediately.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for school districts regarding "released time" courses: districts must allow such courses off campus under a neutral equal‑access policy, may grant secularly evaluated academic credit, and cannot base credit decisions on religious content. For funding, any student excused to attend a released time course is still counted as attending the home school for purposes of school funding and attendance calculations. Districts also may not deny released‑time sponsors equal access to funds, benefits, or services the district provides to community groups. Enforcement: individuals or organizations may sue the school district for violations and seek actual damages, equitable/declaratory relief, and reasonable attorney fees. No new appropriations or specific budget allocations are made.
