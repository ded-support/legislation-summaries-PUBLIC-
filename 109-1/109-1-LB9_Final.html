Summary

LB9 adds regulation and taxation of alternative nicotine products, electronic nicotine delivery systems (ENDS), and defined "nicotine analogues." Key state actors and fiscal provisions:

- Tax Commissioner: expanded enforcement and reporting powers under the cigarette tax division and Tobacco Products Tax Act — requires monthly manufacturer/importer/stamping-agent reports, examines returns, issues deficiency notices (3-year rule; 6 years for fraud), may suspend/revoke licenses, impose administrative penalties (up to $1,000 for cigarette-related violations; up to $5,000 under the Tobacco Products Tax Act), and direct seizure/forfeiture of contraband tobacco/ENDS without warrant.

- Attorney General: may seek injunctions on behalf of the Tax Commissioner and recover investigation/action costs and attorney fees.

- Governor: advises/consents to Tax Commissioner hiring; county attorneys, sheriffs, and peace officers may assist enforcement.

- Fiscal impacts: snuff taxed 44¢/oz; ENDS ≤3 ml at $0.05/ml, >3 ml at 10% of price; alternative nicotine and most other tobacco products taxed at 20% of price. A $500 stamping-agent license fee is deposited to the General Fund; administrative expenses may be paid by warrants against the General Fund, capped at 4% of collections. Effective January 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions for "nicotine analogue" and ties "alternative nicotine product" to section 28-1418.01. Assigns a 20% tax on alternative nicotine products based on the first owner's purchase price or resale price. Expands enforcement authority of the Nebraska Tax Commissioner: examine returns promptly, issue deficiency notices (generally within 3 years, 6 years for fraud/large omissions), and accept written extensions of the assessment period. Authorizes administrative sanctions—license suspension/revocation (under sections 28-1420 to 28-1429 and the Administrative Procedure Act) and penalties up to $5,000. Declares untaxed tobacco products contraband; the Tax Commissioner, the commissioner's agents/employees, or any state peace officer (when directed) may seize them without a warrant. Forfeited products are to be destroyed or used for law enforcement then destroyed; seizure costs charged to the seized person. No appropriations or budgetary allocations are included.
