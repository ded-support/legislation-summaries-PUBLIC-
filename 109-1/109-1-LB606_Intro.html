Summary

The bill consolidates parole functions into the Department of Correctional Services (DCS), abolishing the Division of Parole Supervision and assigning parole powers/duties to the DCS director. DCS will maintain electronic parole files, receive presentence reports, provide record access to oversight bodies, set performance metrics, direct risk-and-needs assessments and training, supervise parolees, run reentry programming (including a technical‑violation residential pilot), and establish sanction matrices and community supervision procedures. DCS may contract with private providers for community work‑release and reentry centers, use designated legislative funds for agreements, and is the intended recipient of a $1,000,000 General Fund appropriation to implement that Act. Parolees owe a $25 monthly programming fee remitted to the Parole Program Cash Fund. The director may certify overcrowding (140% capacity) to expedite releases. DHHS may subpoena records for licensing investigations. Department of Revenue employees are excluded from the “law enforcement officer” definition (sec. 81‑1401).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Correctional Services: Added provisions require the department to maintain all records and files for the Board of Parole, including records for individuals under lifetime community supervision (sec. 83-174.03), while not preventing the department from also keeping daily records for the Board of Pardons. Other additions clarify director/department implementation duties and authorize continued in‑facility correctional treatment, medical care, or vocational training when it will substantially enhance an offender’s ability to lead a law‑abiding life after release. References to the Community Corrections Act and board responsibilities were expanded.
