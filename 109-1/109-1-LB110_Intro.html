Summary

LB110 prohibits health care providers from performing pelvic examinations on anesthetized or unconscious patients in hospitals or medical clinics without prior written consent, except when an authorized decision-maker provides written consent, the exam is required for emergency diagnosis/treatment, or a court orders it for evidence collection. Violations are subject to discipline under the Uniform Credentialing Act. If such an exam is performed while the patient is unconscious or anesthetized, the patient must receive written notification before discharge. No agencies beyond the Uniform Credentialing Act reference or committee assignment (Health and Human Services) are specified; the bill contains no funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds criminal/disciplinary limits on pelvic exams: providers may not perform pelvic exams on anesthetized or unconscious patients without prior written consent, except if an authorized decision‑maker consents in writing, the exam is needed for emergency diagnosis/treatment, or a court orders the exam to collect evidence. Violations subject the provider to discipline under the Uniform Credentialing Act (i.e., action by the state licensing/credentialing authorities). If such an exam is performed while the patient is unconscious or anesthetized, the patient must be notified in writing before hospital or clinic discharge. No agencies are newly created and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
