Summary

LB205 restricts admissible evidence and damages for past and future medical expenses by using payer-based measures and statutory rate benchmarks. For claimants without private coverage (or on Medicare/Medicaid), recoverable medical damages are capped at 120% of the applicable Medicare reimbursement rate or, where no Medicare rate exists, 170% of the applicable state Medicaid rate. The bill requires disclosure of health coverage, letters of protection, and any sale of medical accounts receivable (including purchaser identity and purchase price). It limits total recoverable medical damages to amounts paid, amounts due, and reasonable necessary future care. The bill also imposes a $1,000,000 cap on noneconomic damages per plaintiff in actions involving commercial motor vehicles requiring a commercial driver's license; courts must first determine full noneconomic damages and then reduce awards to the cap. Medicare and state Medicaid rates are expressly used as benchmarks. No funding, appropriations, or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules governing admissible evidence and caps for medical damages in personal injury/wrongful death cases. It explicitly references Medicare and Medicaid (including state Medicaid rates and federal Social Security Act §1396d(l)(2)(B) as of Jan 1, 2025) as the pricing benchmarks: unpaid past or future medical charges are limited to 120% of applicable Medicare rates (or, if no Medicare rate, 170% of the applicable state Medicaid rate). It requires disclosure when medical receivables are sold to factoring companies. Also imposes a $1,000,000 cap on noneconomic damages in actions involving commercial motor vehicles. No direct changes to government appropriations, budgets, or new funding allocations are made.
