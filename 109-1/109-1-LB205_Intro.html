Summary

LB205 tightens evidentiary and disclosure rules in Nebraska personal injury and wrongful death cases and sets a statutory noneconomic‑damages cap for commercial motor vehicle cases.

- Medicare and Medicaid: admissible medical‑expense evidence is limited to amounts actually paid, insurer‑obligated payments, or benchmarks tied to Medicare rates (120%) or state Medicaid rates (170%) for uninsured or Medicaid/Medicare patients; future charges use Medicare/Medicaid rates in effect at trial.

- State Medicaid program rates are expressly used as benchmarks where Medicare rates do not apply.

- Claimant disclosures required: payments made, factoring‑company purchases of accounts receivable (name and purchase price), health coverage status/identity, and any letter‑of‑protection referrals (attorney referrals waive privilege for that disclosure).

- Courts: must first determine noneconomic damages and then reduce awards to a $1,000,000 cap per plaintiff for actions involving commercial motor vehicle carriers; jury and bench trial procedures specified.

No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules limiting admissible evidence and recoverable medical damages in personal injury/wrongful-death cases, including a definition of "letter of protection." For unpaid charges, it ties allowable amounts to payer status: insurer obligations if non‑Medicare/Medicaid coverage; otherwise 120% of the applicable Medicare reimbursement rate or, if none, 170% of the applicable state Medicaid rate. Requires disclosure if medical accounts receivable were sold, the claimant's health coverage identity, and referrals (including attorney referrals and relevant financial ties). Imposes a $1,000,000 cap on noneconomic damages in actions involving commercial motor vehicles (jury/court must first find uncapped amount, then reduce to the cap). Government programs referenced: Medicare and state Medicaid; no agencies, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed or created.
