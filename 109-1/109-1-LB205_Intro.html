Summary

LB205 revises evidentiary rules and damage limits in Nebraska personal injury and wrongful death cases.

Agencies/departments referenced:

- Medicare and Medicaid: Recoverable medical damages are tied to Medicare reimbursement rates (generally 120% for past/future calculations) or, where no Medicare rate exists, 170% of the applicable state Medicaid rate. If private coverage exists, amounts are limited to what the coverage would pay plus the claimant’s share.

- State courts (Judiciary): Courts/juries must first find noneconomic damages without regard to limits, then reduce awards to statutory caps; courts must apply the statute’s disclosure and admissibility rules.

- Statutory reference to section 60-316 (commercial driver licensing) ties the commercial motor vehicle definition to motor-vehicle licensing law.

Financial impact: No appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions reference Medicare and Medicaid rates for calculating admissible medical damages: unpaid past or future medical charges are limited to amounts actually paid, insurer-allowed amounts, or—when uninsured or covered by Medicare/Medicaid—100%–120% of applicable Medicare reimbursement rates (and 170% of applicable state Medicaid rates when no Medicare rate applies). The text also invokes 42 U.S.C. section 1396d(l)(2)(B) (as of Jan 1, 2025) and requires disclosure of factoring-company purchases of medical receivables, the claimant's health coverage, and letters of protection (including attorney referrals). A $1,000,000 cap on noneconomic damages is added for claims involving commercial motor vehicles. No changes to agency budgets, appropriations, or funding allocations are made.
