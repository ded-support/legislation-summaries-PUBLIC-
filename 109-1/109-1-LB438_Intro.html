Summary

LB438 requires authorized gaming operators to check the statewide collection system (effective on an implementation date set by the Tax Commissioner) before paying sports-wagering winnings. Operators must deduct any identified debts or outstanding state tax liabilities and remit those deducted amounts to the Department of Revenue to be credited against the debtor’s liability per section 9-1306 and the Gambling Winnings Setoff for Outstanding Debt Act.

The bill also revises tax distributions. For non-sports gaming revenue: 75% to the State Treasurer (2.5% Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund; 2.5% General Fund; 70% Property Tax Credit Cash Fund) and 25% to local government (county or county/city split). For sports-wagering revenue: 2.5% Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund; 5% Racing and Gaming Commission’s Racetrack Gaming Fund; 27.5% Property Tax Credit Cash Fund; 20% General Fund; 45% Education Future Fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added language permits online sports wagering (through authorized racetrack-based operators or their contracted platform providers) and subjects gross gaming revenue from sports wagering to the tax in section 9-1203. The Department of Revenue’s role is to collect/remit that tax to the State Treasurer, to be credited as follows: 2.5% to the Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund; 5% to the Racing and Gaming Commission’s Racetrack Gaming Fund; 27.5% to the Property Tax Credit Cash Fund; 20% to the General Fund; and 45% to the Education Future Fund. No additional appropriations to the Department of Revenue are specified.
