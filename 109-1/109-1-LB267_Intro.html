Summary

LB267 amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to allow a tenant who is a victim of domestic violence to remove a cotenant/occupant perpetrator from the rental agreement (upon a protective order or certified domestic-violence verification). Procedural duties for landlords: serve the required five‑day notice to the perpetrator, file suit for possession against only the perpetrator if they do not vacate, and—if judgment is entered—recover court costs and reasonable attorney’s fees, rekey/install new locks, and deny the perpetrator access unless escorted by law enforcement. Where the perpetrator is not a cotenant, landlords must change locks within 24 hours on tenant request; tenants may change locks if landlords fail to do so and must provide the landlord a key or code. Landlords acting in good faith are shielded from liability. No state agency appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The additions reference courts and law enforcement but no specific agencies or budget changes. Protective or restraining orders (court-issued) or certified domestic-violence documentation permit a tenant to (a) remove a cotenant-perpetrator from the lease and trigger landlord eviction actions under section 76-1431 (with courts able to award court costs and reasonable attorney fees to the landlord if judgment is entered) and (b) require a landlord to rekey or replace locks within 24 hours when the perpetrator is not a cotenant. A law enforcement officer may be required to escort a perpetrator reclaiming property. Landlords are protected from liability for good-faith actions and may require tenants to pay actual, reasonable lock-change costs. No appropriations, funding, or departmental budget allocations are included.
