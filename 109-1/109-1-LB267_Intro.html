Summary

Bill amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to allow tenants to remove a cotenant or occupant who commits domestic violence (with protective order or certified verification) and to require landlords to change locks when the perpetrator is non-cotenant. Relevant government actors and duties:

- Courts: landlords may file suit for recovery of possession against the perpetrator; if judgment favors the landlord, the landlord may recover court costs and reasonable attorney’s fees from the perpetrator.

- Law enforcement: landlords must refuse perpetrator access to reclaim property unless escorted by a law enforcement officer.

No state agency appropriations or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds tenant protections allowing removal of a cotenant perpetrator of domestic violence (with a protective order or certified domestic-violence documentation) by notification to the landlord and triggering the five‑day removal process under section 76‑1431. Landlord obligations include serving the five‑day notice, pursuing possession suit against the perpetrator if they fail to vacate, installing new locks (per section 3), notifying the tenant how to regain access, and refusing perpetrator access unless escorted by a law enforcement officer. If judgment favors the landlord, the court may award court costs and reasonable attorney fees to the landlord. For non‑cotenant perpetrators, a tenant with a protective order/certification may require the landlord to change or rekey locks within 24 hours; if the landlord fails to act, the tenant may change locks and must provide the landlord access. No state agencies beyond courts and law enforcement are named; no funding or appropriations are provided.
