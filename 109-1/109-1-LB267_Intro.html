Summary

LB267 amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to allow tenants who are victims of domestic violence to remove a cotenant/occupant perpetrator from a rental agreement (upon providing a protective/ restraining order or certified documentation) and to require landlords to pursue possession against that perpetrator in court. Courts may award landlord court costs and reasonable attorney fees if judgment is entered. For non‑cotenant perpetrators, landlords must change unit locks within 24 hours upon tenant request and documentation; landlords may charge actual reasonable costs and may retain a key/code. Law enforcement may be required to escort a perpetrator reclaiming property. No state agency appropriations or other funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No state agencies or departments are newly created or directed; the only government actor mentioned is a "law enforcement officer" (to escort a perpetrator reclaiming property). The additions let tenants with a protective order or certified domestic-violence documentation: (1) remove a cotenant perpetrator from the rental agreement (tenant provides documentation and written notice; landlord pursues eviction/possession actions only against the perpetrator; landlord must re-key and notify the tenant; landlord may recover court costs and attorney fees from the perpetrator; landlord not liable for good-faith actions); and (2) require immediate lock changes when the perpetrator is not a cotenant (landlord must change locks within 24 hours or tenant may do so and must provide the landlord a key/code). No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.
