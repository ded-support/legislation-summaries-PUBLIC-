Summary

LB267 amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to allow tenants who have a protective/ restraining order or certified domestic-violence documentation to remove a cotenant or occupant who perpetrated the violence from the rental agreement. It directs landlords to serve eviction notices and, if necessary, file suit in court for possession solely against the perpetrator; courts may award the landlord court costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. Landlords must install new locks (or rekey/reset codes) and notify the tenant how to obtain access; law enforcement may be required to escort a perpetrator reclaiming property. No state agency or department is specifically created or referenced beyond courts and law enforcement, and the bill contains no appropriations or funding changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds tenant protections when domestic violence occurs: a tenant with a protective order or certified domestic-violence documentation can (1) have a cotenant/perpetrator removed from the rental agreement (landlord must pursue possession actions in court against the perpetrator only; landlord may recover court costs and reasonable attorney’s fees if judgment is entered) and (2) require a landlord to change or rekey locks within 24 hours when the perpetrator is not a cotenant. Landlords may require reimbursement for actual lock-change costs; tenants may change locks if landlord fails. The bill references courts (judicial proceedings) and law enforcement officers (required to escort a perpetrator reclaiming property). No agencies, departments, or budget/appropriation changes are specified.
