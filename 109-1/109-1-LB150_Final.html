Summary

LB150 centralizes parole functions in the Department of Correctional Services (DCS), eliminating the Division of Parole Supervision and transferring related duties to the DCS Director. DCS assumes expanded responsibilities for incarceration (sentences >1 year), parole administration and records, validated risk-and-needs assessments (initial and semiannual), performance metrics, staff training, sanction/reward matrices, a technical‑violation residential pilot, lifetime supervision procedures, and development/oversight of community correctional, work‑release and reentry centers (including contracting and use of designated legislative funds). DCS will collect parole programming fees credited to a new Parole Program Cash Fund; the Legislature intends a $1,000,000 General Fund appropriation to implement the Community Work Release and Reentry Centers Act. DCS must certify overcrowding emergencies (140% capacity) and expedite releases; its Director joins the Reentry Continuity Advisory Board. DHHS gains data access for treatment evaluation, subpoena access for licensing investigations, authority on paternity genetic testing, and firearm‑disability notification duties. The Department of Banking and Finance must collect/post in‑state garnishment agents and is immune from liability. The Department of Revenue is excluded from the law‑enforcement definition; Transportation may require anti‑discrimination assurance for directional sign eligibility.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Banking and Finance: Adds clarifying garnishment service rules for corporate entities (excludes certain financial institutions). Service may be by section 25-509.01 or mutual agreement; if not served on the registered agent a copy must be sent to the agent’s location unless requested otherwise; proof of that mailing is not required to continue garnishment.

- Department of Correctional Services: Allows jails or DOC facilities to host expanded mental‑health beds under the new Regional Mental Health Expansion Pilot Program; permits mental‑health board hearings by videoconference from DOC facilities; includes exclusions for convicted sex offenders. No direct appropriation to DOC is specified.

- Department of Health and Human Services: Pilot promotes expanded regional mental‑health beds and cooperation; the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement will administer grants and seek federal funding for collaborative emergency response. DHHS is implicated in mental‑health board/hearing changes but receives no explicit funding in the text.

- Department of Revenue: No substantive changes affecting the department appear in the added text.

- Department of Transportation: Adds criminal‑statute definitions for “tamper” and “rail infrastructure” (affecting rail assets); DOT not directly addressed and no funding changes.
