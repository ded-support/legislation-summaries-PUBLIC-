Summary

LB634 creates the Legislative Sunset Review Act and establishes a Legislative Sunset Review Committee (members: chair of the Appropriations Committee, Executive Board appointees including five legislators with geographic balance, plus others). It abolishes the Legislature’s Planning Committee. Reviewable entities (state agencies, reviewable entities and their advisory committees) must file detailed electronic reports to the Clerk of the Legislature; reviews begin in 2027 and recur every five years. The committee may access agency records, request assistance from state agencies and officers, and exercise standing‑committee powers. Reports must address Auditor of Public Accounts findings, public records/Open Meetings compliance, and program consolidation/privatization options. The act is operative Jan. 1, 2026, and repeals specified statutes. No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are provided in the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a Legislative Sunset Review Committee composed of the chair of the Appropriations Committee, five legislators appointed by the Executive Board of the Legislative Council (with geographic balance), and other elected committee officers. Initial appointments occur soon after Jan. 1, 2026; the committee gets staff support from legislative divisions. The committee must publish a sunset review schedule on the Legislature’s website, beginning reviews in 2027 and reviewing each “reviewable entity” every five years. Reviewable entities and state agencies must electronically submit validated reports to the Clerk of the Legislature before their review year, including performance measures, three years of data, compliance (e.g., Open Meetings, public records, small/minority purchasing), audit responses (Auditor of Public Accounts), duplication analyses, privatization alternatives, advisory committee inventories, and recommendations (including statutory or budgetary changes). The committee may use standing-committee powers, request assistance from agencies/officers, and inspect records. No direct funding or appropriation changes are specified.
