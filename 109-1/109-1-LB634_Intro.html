Summary

LB634 establishes the Legislative Sunset Review Act, creating a Legislative Sunset Review Committee within the Legislature (members appointed by the Executive Board of the Legislative Council and including the Appropriations Committee chair). The committee receives staff support from legislative divisions and staff, is chaired by elected committee members, and begins reviews in 2027 on a five‑year cycle. Reviewable entities must file detailed electronic reports with the Clerk of the Legislature (performance data, compliance, advisory committees, overlap/duplication, corrective actions including Auditor of Public Accounts findings, etc.). The committee may access records of reviewable entities, state agencies, and officers and request their assistance. The bill abolishes the Legislature’s Planning Committee, becomes operative January 1, 2026, and repeals specified obsolete statutes. No new appropriations or funding allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a Legislative Sunset Review Committee composed of the chair of the Appropriations Committee, five legislators appointed by the Executive Board of the Legislative Council (with geographic representation), and others; chair/vice-chair elected by the committee. Initial appointments begin as soon as possible after Jan 1, 2026; staff support comes from legislative divisions and staff. The committee must publish a review schedule on the Legislature's website, begin reviews in 2027, and review each "reviewable entity" every five years. Reviewable entities must electronically file detailed performance, budgetary, procurement (including small/minority business goals), advisory‑committee and compliance information with the Clerk of the Legislature; data must be validated by the entity's administrator. The committee may review costs, alternatives (including privatization), request assistance and records from state agencies/officers, and inspect files. References include the Auditor of Public Accounts, public records statutes (secs. 84‑712 to 84‑712.09) and the Open Meetings Act. No funding or appropriation changes are specified.
