Summary

LB367 amends the Uniform Credentialing Act to prohibit licensed providers under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology practice acts from providing conversion therapy (including gender-identity and sexual-orientation conversion therapy) to persons under 19. The bill defines conversion therapy, exempts pastoral/religious counseling by clergy, and makes violations both a disciplinary ground under the Uniform Credentialing Act (enforced by the department) and a deceptive trade practice. It also adds the prohibition to the list of bases for action against pharmacy technician registrations, allowing the department, with the recommendation of the board, to deny or discipline registrations. Sec. 3(5) bars the use of state funds or funds of political subdivisions to provide, refer for, or cover conversion therapy.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a ban on licensed health professionals (those credentialed under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology Practice Acts) from providing conversion therapy to anyone under 19. Defines conversion, gender-identity conversion, and sexual-orientation conversion therapy. Violations are classified as deceptive trade practices under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Exempts clergy/religious counselors acting in a pastoral (non-healthcare) capacity. Fiscal language prohibits any state or political‑subdivision (local) funds from being spent to conduct, refer for, or provide health‑benefit coverage for conversion therapy. No specific appropriations, dollar amounts, or new agency budget allocations are included.
