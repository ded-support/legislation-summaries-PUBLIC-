Summary

LB367 amends the Uniform Credentialing Act to (1) add a new section prohibiting licensed providers under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology Practice Acts from providing conversion therapy to persons under 19, (2) define conversion-, gender-identity-, and sexual-orientation-conversion therapy and carve out clergy/religious-capacity exceptions, and (3) make violations both a ground for discipline by the Department (with board recommendation) and a deceptive trade practice under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. The bill also clarifies that Department disciplinary procedures and notice/hearing processes apply, and extends these disciplinary grounds to pharmacy technician registrations overseen by the Department and board. It prohibits expenditure of state or political-subdivision funds for providing, referring, or insuring conversion therapy.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition barring credentialed providers under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology Practice Acts from providing conversion, gender identity, or sexual‑orientation conversion therapy to anyone under 19. Defines those therapies and exempts clergy acting in a purely religious/pastoral (non‑healthcare) capacity. Violations are designated deceptive trade practices under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act (subject to its remedies/penalties). Crucially, the state and its political subdivisions are forbidden from expending funds to conduct or refer for conversion therapy or to provide health‑benefit coverage for it.
