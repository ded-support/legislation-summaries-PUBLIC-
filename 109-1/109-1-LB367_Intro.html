Summary

LB367 amends the Uniform Credentialing Act to (1) prohibit credentialed providers under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology practice acts from delivering conversion therapy to persons under 19; (2) add provision of conversion therapy as a disciplinary ground under section 38-178; and (3) declare such conduct a deceptive trade practice under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. The bill authorizes the department, with recommendation of the board, to deny, suspend, or revoke pharmacy technician registrations for violations now including conversion-therapy-related grounds, and preserves existing department procedures (notice, hearings, Licensee Assistance Program participation). It also bars expenditure of state or political-subdivision funds for conducting, referring for, or providing health-benefit coverage of conversion therapy. No specific appropriations or new budget allocations are made.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a ban on licensed health professionals (under the Medicine and Surgery, Mental Health, Nurse, Pharmacy, and Psychology Practice Acts) from providing "conversion therapy" to anyone under 19. Defines conversion, gender‑identity conversion, and sexual‑orientation conversion therapy. Violations are treated as deceptive trade practices under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Exempts clergy/religious counselors acting in a pastoral (non‑healthcare) capacity. Prohibits use of state funds or funds of any political subdivision to conduct, refer for, or provide health‑benefit coverage for conversion therapy. No new appropriations or allocations are made; instead the text forbids government spending for these services.
