Summary

LB300 amends multiple school statutes to expand and clarify the Superintendent Pay Transparency Act. It requires boards of Class I–V school districts and boards of educational service units (ESUs) to ensure superintendent and ESU administrator compensation complies with the Act. Key provisions: (1) defines salary, benefits, and compensation (including employer contributions under the School Employees Retirement Act and Class V SERA); (2) caps total compensation for a superintendent or ESU administrator at five times the district’s/ESU’s beginning-teacher compensation for the same contract year; (3) declares contracts violating the cap invalid, prohibits expenditure of funds on them, requires forfeiture and return of excess pay, and mandates filing notice with the Commissioner of Education within 30 days; and (4) mandates pre-approval and post-approval publication of proposed and approved contracts and cost estimates (website publication permitted). No new appropriations or budget allocations are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds compliance with a new Superintendent Pay Transparency Act: boards must set superintendent/administrator pay consistent with that Act and related sections. Defines Benefit (wide range of employer-paid items including retirement contributions, early-retirement inducements, annuities, insurance premiums, severance, cash awards, payment for unused leave), Compensation (salary plus benefits), Beginning‑teacher compensation (first‑year pay with maximum typical benefits), and Salary (gross wages including overtime and certain retirement/deferral contributions).

Imposes a cap: no contract may require paying a superintendent/administrator more than five times the beginning‑teacher compensation in the same school district or educational service unit. Violating contracts are invalid; excess pay must be forfeited and returned. Boards/officials must notify the Commissioner of Education within 30 days of learning of a violation and provide related compensation and cap calculations.

Agencies mentioned: school districts, educational service units, boards of education, Commissioner of Education, and references to state School Employees Retirement Acts and federal tax code sections. No direct changes to appropriations or new funding allocations are included.
