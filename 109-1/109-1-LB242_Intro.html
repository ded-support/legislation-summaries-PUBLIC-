Summary

LB242 amends Nebraska tax law and reporting requirements, affecting county and municipal officials, the Property Tax Administrator, State Treasurer, Tax Commissioner, county treasurers, county boards of equalization, and county clerks/election commissioners. Key fiscal changes: a multi‑year School District Property Tax Relief schedule ($750M in 2024 up to $902M in 2029, then +3% annually), with counties retaining 1% of each county allocation for costs; the Property Tax Administrator certifies county disbursements to the State Treasurer by Sept. 15, paid in two equal installments (by Jan. 31 and Apr. 1). The bill revises property tax request authority calculations to add growth‑ and inflation‑based increases, expands allowable exceptions (including county attorneys/public defenders, behavioral‑health/substance‑abuse programs, matching grants, revenue replacement), caps unused carryforward at 5%, and allows limited conversion of prior restricted‑fund authority. Municipalities imposing or raising occupation taxes must post annual collection/use reports.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules affecting political subdivisions' property-tax calculations and uses. Key changes:

- Includes accumulated "excess valuation" existing July 1, 2025, in the increase-in-excess-valuation calculation for the governmental unit's first fiscal year on/after July 1, 2025 (see §18-2147).

- Explicitly allows property-tax budgeting for substance-abuse prevention, behavioral health, opioid-use prevention, matching state and federal grants, and to replace prior-year revenue streams eliminated or reduced by legislation.

- Permits political subdivisions to convert unused restricted funds authority (as of June 30, 2025, per §13-521) into unused property-tax request authority for fiscal years beginning on/after July 1, 2025, capped at 5% of the subdivision's total 2024 property tax levy.

- Directs the county treasurer to retain 1% for costs and disburse remaining collected funds.
