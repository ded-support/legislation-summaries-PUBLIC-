Summary

LB242 amends Nebraska tax law affecting multiple state and local officials and funds. Key agency changes:

- Property Tax Administration: revises the Property Tax Growth Limitation Act (affecting political subdivisions, community colleges, governing bodies) to change how property tax request authority is calculated (adds growth-percentage and inflation-based increases), expands allowable exceptions (including county attorneys/public defenders, substance-abuse/behavioral-health, matching grants, revenue replacement), and limits carryforward unused authority to 5%; permits conversion of certain unused restricted-fund authority (capped at 5% of 2024 levies).

- County treasurers/collectors and county boards: must itemize public-safety tax amounts on tax statements; county treasurers may retain 1% of School District Property Tax Relief disbursements for costs and must disburse the remainder to school districts per certification by the Property Tax Administrator and State Treasurer.

- School District Property Tax Relief Act: specifies annual statewide relief amounts for 2024–2030 (rising from $750M to $902M) and thereafter increases by 3% annually; procedures for returning unused homestead credits to the Property Tax Administrator and crediting the School District Property Tax Relief Credit Fund.

- Municipalities/city councils: tighter occupation-tax classification rules and mandatory annual public reporting of new/increased occupation taxes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions affecting local governments and the county treasurer. Key changes:

- Political subdivisions may convert unused restricted‑funds authority (as of June 30, 2025, per §13‑521) into unused property‑tax request authority for FYs beginning on/after July 1, 2025, capped at 5% of that subdivision’s 2024 property taxes.

- Accumulated excess valuation existing July 1, 2025 must be included when calculating increases in excess valuation for the first fiscal year on/after July 1, 2025.

- Explicitly allows property‑tax budgeting for substance‑abuse prevention/behavioral health/opioid prevention, matching state and federal grants, and to replace revenue streams reduced or eliminated by legislative action (references §13‑3404 for exceptions and growth calculations).

- County treasurer may retain 1% for costs and must disburse remaining funds.
