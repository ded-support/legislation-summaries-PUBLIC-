Summary

LB242 amends Nebraska property tax law and municipal occupation tax reporting. Key agency impacts and funding changes:

- County treasurers: must itemize on tax statements amounts funding public safety services and separately show bond principal/interest; may retain 1% of School District Property Tax Relief disbursements for county costs and must file electronic reports to the Property Tax Administrator on returned unused homestead credits (form prescribed by the Tax Commissioner).

- Property Tax Administrator: certifies county disbursements, credits returned funds to the School District Property Tax Relief Credit Fund.

- State Treasurer: receives certified disbursement amounts.

- Municipalities: required to post annual reports (within 90 days after fiscal year end) on new or increased occupation taxes, detailing collections, fund deposits, dedicated uses, and termination dates.

- School District Property Tax Relief Act: establishes specific statewide relief totals for 2024–2030 and a 3% annual growth thereafter; disbursements to counties occur in two payments (by Jan 31 and Apr 1).

- Property Tax Growth Limitation Act: revises calculation of property tax request authority, adds eligible exceptions (including county attorneys/public defenders, behavioral health, matching grants, revenue replacement), and limits unused authority carryforward and conversion caps.

Effective on enactment (emergency clause).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates new property-tax rules affecting governmental units and political subdivisions. Key agency mentions: county treasurer (retains 1% for costs, then disburses remaining funds), political subdivisions, and governmental units. Changes include: accumulated excess valuation as of July 1, 2025 must be included in redevelopment project valuation calculations for the first fiscal year beginning on/after that date; political subdivisions may convert unused restricted-fund authority (per §13-521) into unused property-tax request authority for fiscal years beginning on/after July 1, 2025, capped at 5% of the subdivision’s 2024 property tax levy; new permitted property-tax budget categories include substance-abuse/behavioral-health/opioid prevention, matching state and federal grants, and replacement of revenue streams cut by legislation.
