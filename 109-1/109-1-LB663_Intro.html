Summary

LB663 amends county planning law (section 23-114.01). Key agency roles and changes:

- Requires each county planning commission member and each county board member to complete two hours of role-specific education per term, developed and presented by the respective county attorney.

- Requires county planning commissions and county boards to issue a statement of factual findings when granting or denying conditional-use/special-exception requests.

- Establishes processing timelines: the commission has 30 days to assess completeness (10‑day notice for deficiencies), 30 days to reevaluate after additional information, and 90 days to decide or recommend; the board has analogous 30/10/30/90-day steps after receiving the commission’s recommendation.

- Creates a presumption that applicants will comply with local, state, and federal requirements; challengers bear the burden of proof.

Administrative finance notes: members remain eligible for expense reimbursement, mileage per section 81-1176, and per diem up to $15/day not to exceed $1,000/year. No new appropriations specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a two-hour-per-term training requirement for each commission member and county board member on commissioner/board roles, with the training developed and presented by the respective county attorney. Establishes procedural rules for planning commissions and county boards: 30 days to declare an application complete (10‑day written notice if incomplete), 30 days after supplemental information to re-evaluate, and a 90‑day deadline to grant/deny or recommend action. Creates a presumption that applicants will comply with local, state, and federal requirements (burden on challengers to prove otherwise) and bars commissions/boards from conditioning a conditional use permit or special exception on obtaining other agency permits (though applicants still must secure required local, state, or federal permits). No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
