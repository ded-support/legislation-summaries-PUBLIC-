Summary

LB663 amends county planning law (section 23‑114.01). Key agency-related changes:

- Requires each county planning commission member and each county board member to complete two hours of role‑specific education per term, developed and presented by the respective county attorney.

- Establishes procedural deadlines for the county planning commission and county board to determine application completeness (30 days), to notify applicants of deficiencies (within 10 days), to re‑determine completeness after additional materials (30 days), and to grant/deny or recommend decisions within 90 days of a complete application.

- Requires commissions/boards to issue written factual findings supporting conditional use/special exception decisions and presumes applicants will comply with applicable local, state, and federal requirements (burden of proof on challengers).

- Retains appeal routes to the county board or district court as presently structured.

No changes to state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates procedures involving county-level bodies and other agencies: requires each county commissioner and county board member to complete two hours per term of role-specific education developed and presented by the county attorney. Clarifies that planning commissions and county boards must base conditional use permits/special exceptions solely on county zoning, may not condition approval on obtaining other federal, state, or local permits, and do not relieve applicants of separate permit obligations to those agencies. Establishes a presumption the applicant will comply with local, state, and federal requirements (burden on challenger). Sets deadlines: planning commission — 30 days to check completeness, 10-day notice if incomplete, 30 days after supplemental info, and 90 days to decide or recommend; county board follows analogous 30/10/30/90 timelines. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
