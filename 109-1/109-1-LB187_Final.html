Summary

LB187 amends Nebraska real estate licensing law and affects the Nebraska Real Estate Commission, Nebraska State Patrol, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, with a reference to the State Department of Education for school-exemption context. Key agency-related changes: clarifies written agency agreement requirements and enforcement authority of the commission (rulemaking, school monitoring, disciplinary actions and civil fines under section 81-885.10); requires original-license fingerprinting and FBI national-criminal-history checks processed through the Nebraska State Patrol (applicant pays costs and authorizes release to the commission); adjusts prelicense/postlicense coursework and exam provisions; revises allowable payments to unlicensed foreign or nonresident persons as referral fees; adds prohibitions (including right-to-list agreements). Operative dates: sections 1 and 6 — July 1, 2025; sections 3 and 8 — January 1, 2026. No appropriations or funding changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements to real estate practice: buyers of residential property with one to four dwelling units must sign a written agency agreement; designated brokers may act as single agents for property types not covered by the one-to-four‑unit rule. The state "commission" is expressly authorized to adopt and promulgate rules specifying mandatory course subject matter. It also establishes that satisfactory evidence of course completion is valid for four years for license applications. No specific state agencies beyond the commission are named, and no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
