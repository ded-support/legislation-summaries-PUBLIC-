Summary

The bill designates "Department" as the Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) for the Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act and assigns multiple DMV duties: require title certificates (including all‑terrain and utility‑type vehicle titles) on department‑selected security paper labeled “manufactured for off‑road use”; prescribe/contract for secure power‑of‑attorney forms; competitively bid license‑plate materials and furnish new plate types (including ATV/UTV plates); accept and record motorcycle safety course completion on DMV records (system change by Jan 1, 2024); and collect specified fees/surcharges from successful school or learner‑permit applicants. The bill sets DMV to annually set motor vehicle fees, with proceeds deposited to a Motor Vehicle Fee Fund split 50/50 to counties and municipalities for roads. Funding changes: special‑interest plate $50 fee split $25 to the DMV Cash Fund and $25 to the Highway Trust Fund; certain plate fees/handling credited to DMV Cash Fund or first to the Highway Trust Fund; and sales/use tax proceeds from aircraft sales/leases credited to the Department of Transportation Aeronautics Capital Improvement Fund while motor vehicle sale proceeds (excl. ATVs/UTVs) are credited to the Highway Trust Fund with specified transfers.

Summary (agencies: DMV, DOT)

- Department of Motor Vehicles: New language treats all-terrain vehicles (ATVs) and utility‑type vehicles (UTVs) as "motor vehicles" when registered under the Motor Vehicle Registration Act, requires titling/registration and issuance of ATV/UTV license plates. The bill specifies registration fees (text includes $3 for ATVs and $3 for UTVs and also references $25 and $5 line items in fee schedules). Utility‑type vehicle is newly defined by size/tires and expressly excludes ATVs, golf cars, and low‑speed vehicles.

- Department of Transportation: Clarifies operation of registered ATVs/UTVs on highways per section 60‑6,356, but bars them from the Interstate system, state highways, expressways, and freeways; counties/cities/villages may further prohibit. Safety exemptions noted for occupants wearing required protection or three‑point belts.

No explicit funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
