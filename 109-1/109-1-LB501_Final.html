Summary

LB501 amends statutes governing assessment and post-damage valuation procedures, reallocating duties and timelines among local and state tax authorities. Key agencies/departments affected:

- County assessors: must inspect reported damaged property and submit a prescribed report to the county board of equalization by July 20; preliminary valuation notice timing retained for large counties.

- County clerks: receive owner damage reports (due by July 15) on Tax Commissioner forms.

- County boards of equalization: must adjust assessments to the value on the damage date, may meet June 1–July 25 (or through Aug 10 with an extension), and handle notices/protests and appeals to the Tax Equalization and Review Commission.

- County treasurers: collection/enforcement duties (distress warrants) do not apply to property classified as damaged.

- Tax Commissioner: prescribes reporting forms.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations. The bill repeals prior sections and takes effect immediately as an emergency measure.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a requirement that the county assessor inspect and review all properties for which a report is filed under this section and submit a comprehensive report to the county board of equalization by July 20 of the current assessment year. The report must use a form prescribed by the Tax Commissioner and include all property-owner reports filed under this section. Agencies mentioned: county assessor, county board of equalization, and the Tax Commissioner. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
