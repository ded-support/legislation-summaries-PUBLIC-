Summary

The bill creates and reorganizes legislative oversight offices and sets agency duties and information‑sharing rules:

- Department of Correctional Services: establishes an Office of Inspector General within the Division of Legislative Oversight (appointment with legislative committee approval; ineligible if a recent DCS executive), requires prompt notification of deaths/serious injuries/hospitalizations, access to records (medical/mental‑health records generally require consent except death investigations), subpoena authority, confidentiality and annual reporting; inmate/parole files are confidential with limited public release. Deputy public counsel for corrections has authority over DCS and local jails.

- Department of Health and Human Services and Department of Veterans' Affairs: deputy public counsel for institutions has authority over DHHS mental‑health institutions/regional behavioral health authorities and veterans institutions; DHHS must report/cooperate with child‑welfare Inspector General investigations.

- Department of Administrative Services: Accounting Administrator must furnish information to the Legislative Fiscal Analyst; building division must prepare cost analyses for projects ≥ $400,000 (threshold reset every four years); DAS employees prohibited from impermissible tax‑record disclosure.

- Department of Revenue: Tax Commissioner retains return confidentiality but must provide returns/secures audit workpapers on request to the Auditor/Legislative Audit Office for audits.

- Department of Labor, Department of Insurance, Department of Motor Vehicles: limited, specified tax‑return disclosures permitted — Labor for employment‑law administration and tax‑incentive audits (using Labor data/NAICS), DMV for IFTA enforcement, Insurance for tax‑credit administration.

Staffing for new oversight offices is authorized "within the amount available by appropriation" or "within available appropriations"; no specific appropriations or budget increases are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added text creates expanded legislative oversight: establishes Offices (Public Counsel; Inspectors General for Child Welfare and the Correctional System) and a Legislative Audit Office with broad investigatory and subpoena powers over state agencies (including DAS, DOI, Labor, Corrections, DHHS, DMV, Revenue, Veterans’ Affairs). Agencies must provide records/personnel (30-day production), may negotiate privilege objections, and face expedited litigation in Lancaster County to compel or quash subpoenas. DHHS and the Department responsible for corrections are specifically required to notify the respective Inspector General when criminal investigations involving death or serious injury begin and end; agencies may enter secure information‑sharing agreements. The Legislative Auditor may hire staff “within the amount available by appropriation through the Division of Legislative Oversight” (no specific dollar amounts added). Any fees for counsel present during questioning are not charged to the offices or Legislative Council.
