Summary

Bill LB383 affects the three agencies as follows:

- Department of Correctional Services (DCS): DCS must order court‑admissible mental‑health evaluations for specified sex‑offenders (certain sex‑offense convictions, repeat registrants, prior registration violators, or those who refused/failed sex‑offender treatment) to determine dangerous‑sex‑offender status. Evaluations must be ordered ≥180 days before scheduled release; findings and an affidavit must be provided to the Attorney General and relevant county attorneys no later than 150 days before release. Failure to complete treatment because of time constraints or unavailable programming is not treated as refusal.

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): Expands racketeering list to include assaults (first, second, third degree and vehicular) on DHHS employees. Requires sexual‑assault reports for victims under 18 to DHHS, permits criminal‑justice agencies to share child‑abuse investigation information with DHHS and assisting agencies, and authorizes the Attorney General’s Child Protection Division to act on DHHS’s written requests.

- Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV): Forfeiture provisions require DMV title inquiries to identify owners and liens on seized in‑state vehicles/vessels; for out‑of‑state vehicles the bill requires attempts to identify licensed owners and contact the appropriate state title agency.

No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations for these agencies are included.
