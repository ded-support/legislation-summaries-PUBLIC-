Summary

The bill amends multiple statutes affecting the three agencies as follows:

- Department of Motor Vehicles: Sec. 8 (amending 25-21,302) requires inquiry of DMV records when seized motor vehicles or titled vessels may show recorded owners or liens; responses must be used to identify and name owners or lienholders in forfeiture petitions.

- Department of Health and Human Services: Adds “a Department of Health and Human Services employee” to the list of protected persons under the racketeering/predicate-offense statute (making assaults on DHHS employees prosecutable under that provision). DHHS is named as the reporting recipient for sexual-assault victims under 18, may receive information from criminal-justice agencies for child-abuse/neglect investigations, and is identified as a provider of sex-offender treatment during incarceration.

- Department of Correctional Services: DCS must order mental-health evaluations at least 180 days before scheduled release to assess whether certain offenders qualify as dangerous sex offenders; results and an affidavit must be sent to the Attorney General and relevant county attorneys. Evaluations may be required where inmates refused or failed in-prison sex-offender treatment.

No appropriations, funding increases, or agency-specific budget allocations for DMV, DHHS, or DCS are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No specific new duties, appropriations, or budget allocations are directed to the Department of Correctional Services, Department of Health and Human Services, or the Department of Motor Vehicles.

- Department of Correctional Services: The bill creates/clarifies offenses and penalties for child sexual abuse material (Class III felony for offenders under 19, Class ID for adults, Class IC for repeat offenders) and references forfeiture statutes—these could increase incarceration demand but include no funding or appropriation language.

- Department of Health and Human Services: No substantive provisions or funding changes affecting DHHS in the added text.

- Department of Motor Vehicles: A “digitized identification card” is defined (mirrors data on an operator’s license), but the bill does not assign duties to DMV or provide funding.

Attorney General enforcement and civil penalties (up to $2,500 per violation) are included; penalties go to the State Treasurer.
