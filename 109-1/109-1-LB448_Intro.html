Summary

LB448 (Postconviction Relief Act) expands postconviction relief procedures and identifies the following government actors and fiscal effects:

- District courts: receive and adjudicate verified postconviction motions, may appoint up to two attorneys, order disclosure of prior counsel files, conduct hearings, and enter findings; proceedings are civil.

- County attorney: must be served notice of postconviction motions.

- Appellate courts: hear appeals from district court orders; appellate release discretionary.

- Nebraska Supreme Court and U.S. Supreme Court: referenced as triggers for filing deadlines and retroactive rule application.

- County boards: required to pay court-fixed fees and expenses to appointed counsel (county-level budgetary obligation).

The bill creates an actual-innocence claim, time-limit exceptions, and limits the court’s inquiry when excusing procedural defaults. No statewide appropriations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Postconviction Relief Act," establishing grounds for relief as constitutional violations or actual innocence. Defines actual innocence standards: newly discovered evidence (e.g., exculpatory DNA, third‑party confessions, impeachment of state witnesses, or forensic scientific evidence undermined by new research) and a clear‑and‑convincing showing that the new evidence likely would have changed the outcome. Defines "applied" and "foundational" validity for forensic methods. Allows district courts to excuse procedural defaults or time limits to prevent manifest injustice if the prisoner makes a colorable actual‑innocence claim; failing to raise ineffective trial‑counsel claims on direct appeal no longer automatically bars them. Permits district courts, on prisoner request, to order prior trial or appellate counsel to produce complete case files. No specific state agencies or departments beyond "district court" and references to "state's" witnesses are named; no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are provided.
