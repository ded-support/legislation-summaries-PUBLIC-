Summary

LB448 renames and codifies the Postconviction Relief Act and revises postconviction procedures. Key government actors: district courts (may appoint up to two attorneys, conduct hearings, order disclosure), county attorneys (must be served notice), appellate courts (appeals treated as civil), the Nebraska Supreme Court and U.S. Supreme Court (timing and retroactivity rules referenced), county boards (required to pay appointed counsel's fees and expenses in full as fixed by the court), prosecutors, and law enforcement (subject to new disclosure and discovery duties). The bill makes proceedings civil, taxes costs as in habeas cases, and expressly allows district courts to order prior trial/appellate counsel to produce complete case files. No statewide appropriations are created; the only funding directive requires county boards to pay appointed counsel.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a Postconviction Relief Act establishing relief for constitutional violations or actual innocence. Key provisions: standards for proving actual innocence (new evidence such as exculpatory DNA, alternative-suspect evidence, impeachment or undermining forensic science) and definitions for foundational and applied validity of forensic methods. Courts may excuse procedural defaults or timing limits to prevent manifest injustice, limited to whether the prisoner makes a colorable actual-innocence claim; failure to raise ineffective-assistance-at-trial on direct appeal is not a procedural default. The district court may order prior trial or appellate counsel to produce complete case files to the prisoner. No executive agencies or departments are newly created or mentioned, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.
