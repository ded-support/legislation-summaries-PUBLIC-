Summary

LB448 renames and revises Nebraska's postconviction relief framework as the Postconviction Relief Act. Key agency/office impacts:

- District courts: retain original jurisdiction, may appoint up to two attorneys for postconviction proceedings, order disclosure of prior trial/appellate counsel files, and grant hearings or relief. Proceedings are civil; orders on motions are final judgments appealable to appellate courts.

- County attorneys: must be served notice of postconviction motions.

- County boards: required to pay appointed counsel's fees and reasonable expenses in the full amount fixed by the district court.

- Appellate courts, Nebraska Supreme Court, and U.S. Supreme Court: referenced for finality, timelines, and retroactivity of newly recognized constitutional claims.

- Law enforcement and prosecutors: bill provides for disclosure and discovery obligations (details in act).

Substantive changes include adding actual innocence as a statutory ground, a one-year filing limitation with enumerated tolling exceptions, and narrow excusal of defaults for manifest injustice.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Postconviction Relief Act," creating statutory postconviction relief grounds: (1) constitutional denial/infringement rendering a judgment void or voidable under the Nebraska or U.S. Constitution, or (2) actual innocence. Defines actual innocence standards: new evidence (e.g., exculpatory DNA, alternative-suspect evidence, impeachment of state's witnesses, or undermining forensic science) and a clear-and-convincing showing that the new evidence likely would have changed the outcome. Specifies when forensic evidence is "undermined" and defines foundational and applied validity. Allows courts to excuse procedural defaults or time limits to prevent manifest injustice if a colorable actual-innocence claim exists. Preserves ineffective-assistance-of-trial-counsel claims not raised on direct appeal. Permits district courts to order prior trial/appellate counsel to provide complete case files to the prisoner. No funding, appropriations, or budget changes are included.
