Summary

LB448 renames and revises Nebraska’s Postconviction Relief Act to allow postconviction relief for claims of actual innocence, set standards for newly discovered evidence, and permit courts to excuse procedural defaults to prevent manifest injustice. Agencies and actors referenced include: district courts (jurisdiction, hearings, appointment of up to two attorneys, authority to order disclosure of trial/appellate counsel files), county attorneys (to receive notice), appellate courts (appeals and interim release), the Nebraska Supreme Court and U.S. Supreme Court (timing rules), and county boards. Fiscal provision: when the district court appoints counsel, the court fixes fees and the county board is required to pay those fees in full. Proceedings are civil in nature.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Postconviction Relief Act," defining new statutory grounds and procedures for postconviction relief. Key additions: actual innocence is a ground for relief and must be shown with new evidence (e.g., exculpatory DNA, third‑party confessions, impeachment or relevant forensic evidence) by clear and convincing proof that the outcome likely would have differed. Establishes standards for undermining forensic scientific evidence and defines "foundational" and "applied" validity. Courts may excuse procedural defaults or time limits to prevent manifest injustice if the prisoner makes a colorable actual‑innocence claim. Failure to raise ineffective assistance of trial counsel on direct appeal is not a procedural default. Prisoners may request their prior counsel's complete case files.
