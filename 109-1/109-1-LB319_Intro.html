Summary

LB319 directs the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to maximize use of federal SNAP options and funding, minimize use of state General Funds, hire necessary personnel, and file an annual report to the Legislature detailing options, actions, caseloads, and program costs. DHHS must develop a state outreach plan meeting Food and Nutrition Service (U.S. Department of Agriculture) criteria, submit it to USDA FNS for approval and federal matching funds, and may accept gifts, grants, and donations; implementation may be deferred if private or federal funds are insufficient. DHHS shall create a TANF-funded categorical eligibility policy (raising gross income eligibility to 165% until Oct 1, 2025) and annually report administrative costs and federal funding availability. The Department of Labor may administer the SNAP Next Step Program only to the extent federal funds are available and is not to use General Funds for those processes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a SNAP eligibility restriction: under 21 U.S.C. 862 (as of Jan 1, 2025), persons convicted of a felony involving possession, use, or distribution of a controlled substance are eligible for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits under this subsection only if they (i) have completed their sentence or (ii) are serving parole, probation, or post‑release supervision.

Agency effects: USDA/FNS must implement and enforce the restriction. No provisions change Department of Labor or HHS responsibilities. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
