Summary

LB412 amends paternity statutes to authorize alleged biological fathers to intervene in juvenile-court paternity proceedings. Key government entities mentioned: district courts (where child is domiciled or found), juvenile courts, county courts, county sheriffs (service of process), counties, and the State of Nebraska. Procedural changes: the juvenile court may order genetic testing and require the juvenile be produced; no filing fee may be charged for the complaint and supporting affidavit. Funding changes: the juvenile court may allocate payment for genetic testing to the complainant, the county, or the state at its discretion. The bill also expands the statutory definition of child (under 18) and adds “reasonable education” to the definition of support.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of "child" as a person under 18 (regardless of whether born out of wedlock) and authorizes the juvenile court, when ordering genetic testing or establishing paternity, to consider: the child's age; the child's relationship with any presumptive parent, any parent whose rights were established by acknowledgment or court finding, and the complainant; whether establishing paternity would benefit or harm the child; and any other factor the court deems relevant. No other government agencies or departments are mentioned, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
