Summary

LB412 amends Nebraska paternity law. Key agency/department effects:

- Juvenile courts: Authorizes a person claiming to be a child's biological father to file a complaint to intervene in juvenile-court proceedings (affidavit required; no filing fee). Juvenile courts may order genetic testing, require the child be made available, and consider specified factors when deciding intervention. Intervention is barred if parental rights were previously terminated.

- County courts/sheriffs: Summons in paternity matters may be directed to a county sheriff and served in any county; county courts retain existing jurisdictional limits.

- State and counties: Genetic-testing costs may be allocated to the complainant, the county, or the state at the juvenile court’s discretion — creating potential county or state fiscal exposure (no specific appropriation included).

- Definitions: Clarifies “child” (under 18) and adds that “support includes reasonable education.”

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds standards for juvenile courts (per sections 43-1401 to 43-1418 and subsection (3) of §43-1411) when ordering genetic testing or establishing paternity: the court may consider the child's age; the child's relationship to any presumptive parent, any parent whose rights were established, and the complainant; whether establishing paternity would benefit or harm the child; and any other relevant factor. It also defines "child" as a person under 18, regardless of birth status. No other government agencies or departments are named, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
