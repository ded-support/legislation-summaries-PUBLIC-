Summary

LB412 amends Nebraska paternity law to (1) expand definitions (child under 18; support includes reasonable education) and (2) allow a person claiming to be a biological father to file a complaint to intervene in juvenile-court proceedings where the juvenile court has jurisdiction. Procedural provisions reference district courts (including cases under the Uniform Interstate Family Support Act), county courts, separate juvenile courts, and sheriffs for service. The bill: waives filing fees for the intervention complaint; authorizes juvenile courts to order genetic testing and require the child’s availability for testing; and permits the juvenile court to allocate genetic-testing costs to the complainant, the county, or the state at its discretion. It bars intervention by persons whose parental rights have been terminated.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and decision-making guidance for juvenile courts in paternity/genetic-testing matters. Key points:

- "Child" is defined as a person under 18 years, regardless of being born out of wedlock.

- The juvenile court may order genetic testing or establish paternity and may consider: the child's age; the child's relationship with any presumptive parent; the child's relationship with any parent whose rights are established; the child's relationship with the complainant; whether establishing paternity would benefit or harm the child; and any other relevant factor the court deems appropriate.

- Only the juvenile court (a government judicial body) is implicated; no other agencies or departments are mentioned.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
