Summary

LB111 regulates insurer practices for motor vehicle repairs involving nonoriginal equipment manufacturer (non‑OEM/aftermarket) parts. It (1) defines terms (referencing consumer definitions in sections 60‑2701 and 60‑1401.10); (2) bars insurers from requiring non‑OEM parts that lack permanent manufacturer identification or that are not equal in fit and performance to OEM parts (including consideration of modification costs); (3) requires written consumer consent before using non‑OEM parts, clear identification of such parts on estimates, and a prescribed ten‑point notice to consumers; (4) prohibits insurers from charging consumers the price difference if the consumer elects OEM parts; (5) forbids requiring aftermarket parts for vehicles manufactured within 36 months of damage; and (6) permits a specific policy notice for older vehicles. Violations are Class I misdemeanors. No state agencies are named and there are no funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions (consumer; consumer care; insurer; nonoriginal equipment manufacturer; part) and prohibits insurers from requiring non‑OEM parts that lack permanent manufacturer ID or that are inferior in fit/performance. Insurers must notify consumers in writing (≥10‑point type) and obtain written consent before using non‑OEM parts; identify any such parts in the repair estimate; and may not require aftermarket parts for vehicles <36 months old. Policies for vehicles >36 months may include a capitalized 10‑point notice allowing aftermarket parts. Violations are a Class I misdemeanor. No government agencies, departments, or funding/appropriations are mentioned.
