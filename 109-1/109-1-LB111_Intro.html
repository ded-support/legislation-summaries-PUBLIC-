Summary

LB111 regulates insurer practices for motor vehicle repairs. It cross-references statutory definitions in section 60-2701 (consumer) and section 60-1401.10 (consumer care) but does not name or alter any specific state agency or department. Key provisions: insurers may not require non‑OEM parts lacking permanent manufacturer identification or that are inferior in fit/performance; insurers must obtain written consumer consent before using non‑OEM parts and may not charge consumers the price difference for choosing OEM parts; estimates must disclose non‑OEM parts and include prescribed notice language; vehicles manufactured within 36 months cannot be required to receive aftermarket parts; insurers may include a standardized aftermarket‑parts notice in policies for older vehicles. Penalty: each violation is a Class I misdemeanor. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No government agencies or departments are referenced, and there are no funding, appropriations, or budget changes.

Summary of additions: The bill defines key terms (consumer, consumer care, insurer, nonoriginal equipment manufacturer, part) and imposes consumer protections governing use of aftermarket/non‑OEM parts. Insurers must ensure non‑OEM parts carry permanent identification, be equal in fit and performance to OEM parts (including modification costs), and disclose any non‑OEM part in the repair estimate. Insurers must obtain written consumer consent before using non‑OEM parts, may not charge consumers a price difference if they choose OEM parts, and must provide a 10‑point font notice on estimates. Insurers cannot require aftermarket parts for vehicles manufactured within 36 months of damage. Violations are Class I misdemeanors.
