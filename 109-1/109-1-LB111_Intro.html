Summary

LB111 regulates insurers' use of nonoriginal equipment manufacturer (non‑OEM/aftermarket) parts in motor vehicle repairs. It (1) defines insurer, consumer, consumer care, non‑OEM, and part (excluding tires/windshields/windows) with cross‑references to sections 60‑2701 and 60‑1401.10; (2) bars insurers from requiring non‑OEM parts that lack permanent manufacturer identification or that are inferior in fit/performance (including modification costs); (3) requires written consumer consent before using non‑OEM parts and forbids charging consumers the price difference to use OEM parts; (4) mandates a specified notice in repair estimates and clear identification of any non‑OEM parts; (5) prohibits requiring aftermarket parts for vehicles manufactured within 36 months of damage; and (6) makes violations Class I misdemeanors. No state agencies or budgetary appropriations are named or affected.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions define terms and impose consumer-protection rules on insurers regarding non‑original equipment manufacturer (non‑OEM/aftermarket) parts: insurers may not require use of non‑OEM parts that lack permanent manufacturer identification or are inferior in fit/performance; must notify consumers in ≥10‑point font and obtain written consent before using non‑OEM parts; must identify any non‑OEM part in repair estimates; may not force aftermarket parts for vehicles manufactured within 36 months of damage; may include a standard policy notice for older vehicles. Violations are a Class I misdemeanor. No specific government agencies or departments are referenced, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.
