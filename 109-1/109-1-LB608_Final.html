Summary

- The bill defines a correctional officer as someone employed full-time by a jail or by the Department of Correctional Services.

- The Department of Revenue is assigned administration and enforcement of the First Responder Recruitment and Retention Act, including rulemaking authority. Applicants and tuition-waiver recipients must file documentation with the Department of Revenue proving employment, eligible-disabled status, or Nebraska residency (via annual tax returns). Institutions must provide lists of tuition-waiver recipients to the Department of Revenue, which will maintain records and, by August 1 each year, report recipients who failed to file residency tax returns.

- Funding: Beginning July 1, 2028, subject to available appropriations, the Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education shall reimburse state universities for 50% of tuition waivers granted the prior year for correctional officers, youth detention officers, formerly employed eligible-disabled persons, and their qualifying children. Reimbursements require commission-approved documentation and will be prorated if appropriations are insufficient.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Correctional Services: The bill adds/defines "correctional officer" as a full‑time employee of a jail or the Department of Correctional Services and explicitly includes correctional officers (and youth detention officers) among eligible first responders for tuition waivers. Tuition waivers for current or formerly employed correctional/youth detention officers and their qualifying children are limited to state university attendance beginning July 1, 2027.

- Department of Revenue: Applicants (first responders, eligible disabled persons, and qualifying children) must annually file documentation with the Department of Revenue proving employment/status and Nebraska residency to receive/renew waivers.

- Funding: Beginning July 1, 2028, subject to appropriations, the Coordinating Commission will reimburse state universities 50% of those waivers (prior year), with claims prorated if appropriations are insufficient.
