Summary

- The bill defines "correctional officer" to include individuals employed full-time by a jail or by the Department of Correctional Services.

- The Department of Revenue is assigned administration and enforcement authority for the First Responder Recruitment and Retention Act, including rulemaking authority.

- Applicants/recipients must file annually with the Department of Revenue: proof of employment and Nebraska residency (first responders), proof of eligible disabled status and residency (eligible disabled persons), and qualifying-child documentation. Tuition-waiver recipients must file Nebraska tax returns annually; the Department will maintain records of waiver recipients and, by each August 1, report to institutions which recipients failed to file required tax returns. Institutions must provide lists of recipients to the Department by December 31.

- Funding: Beginning July 1, 2028, subject to available appropriations, the Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education shall reimburse state universities for 50% of tuition waivers for correctional/youth detention officers, formerly such officers who are eligible disabled persons, and their qualifying children; reimbursements require commission-approved documentation and will be prorated if appropriations are insufficient.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Correctional Services: "Correctional officer" is defined to include full‑time employees of a jail or the Department of Correctional Services. Correctional officers (and formerly employed correctional officers who are eligible disabled persons) and their qualifying children are covered by the tuition‑waiver program; for eligible disabled former correctional/youth detention officers and their qualifying children, waiver eligibility at state universities begins July 1, 2027.

- Department of Revenue: applicants/recipients must annually file with the Department of Revenue proof of employment/status and Nebraska residence (first responders) or proof of disabled status and residence (eligible disabled persons); qualifying children similarly must file documentation.

- Funding/appropriations: beginning July 1, 2028, subject to available appropriations, the Coordinating Commission will reimburse state universities for 50% of waivers for correctional/youth detention officers, specified eligible disabled persons, and their qualifying children (prior‑year grants). Reimbursements are claim‑based and prorated if appropriations are insufficient; no direct appropriations to the Departments are added.
