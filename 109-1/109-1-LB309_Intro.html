Summary

This bill designates the Department of Environment and Energy as the lead agency to implement the Safe Battery Collection and Recycling Act. The department must review and approve battery stewardship plans (plans due Jan 1, 2027), act on plan amendments within set timelines, publish approved plans and annual reports on its website (excluding proprietary data), require producer notifications, commission or receive audits, review out‑of‑state studies, and report findings to the Legislature's Natural Resources Committee by May 30, 2028. The department may adopt rules to implement the Act. Enforcement roles include the Attorney General and county attorneys, with civil penalties ($10,000 per violation) remitted to the State Treasurer for constitutional distribution; knowingly false statements to the department are a Class IV felony. Financially, the Battery Stewardship Cash Fund is created to hold fees collected under section 4, administered by the department and invested by the state investment officer under existing investment statutes; funds are restricted to carrying out the Act.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The additions designate the Department of Environment and Energy as the lead agency to administer the Safe Battery Collection and Recycling Act: approving/denying battery stewardship plans (120-day review), requiring plan amendments, posting approved plans (excluding proprietary data), and adopting rules. The department must review out‑of‑state studies and report to the Legislature’s Natural Resources Committee by May 30, 2028. The Attorney General or county attorneys may seek injunctions for violations. A Battery Stewardship Cash Fund is created, funded by fees collected under section 4, administered by the department and restricted to implementing the Act; funds may be invested by the state investment officer under state investment statutes. Civil penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer for constitutional distribution. Appeals follow the Administrative Procedure Act.
