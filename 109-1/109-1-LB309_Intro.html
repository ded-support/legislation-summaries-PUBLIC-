Summary

LB309 designates the Department of Environment and Energy as the lead agency to administer the Safe Battery Collection and Recycling Act. The department must review and approve battery stewardship plans (plans due by Jan. 1, 2027), act on plans/amendments within statutory timeframes, host approved plans and annual reports on its website (excluding proprietary information), require notice of producer changes, receive a one-time audit after five years, and review out‑of‑state studies with a report to the Legislature’s Natural Resources Committee by May 30, 2028. The department may adopt rules and enforce plan compliance (denials/appeals under the Administrative Procedure Act). The Battery Stewardship Cash Fund is created, comprised of fees collected under section 4, administered by the department and invested by the state investment officer. Civil penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the Department of Environment and Energy as the lead agency to administer the Safe Battery Collection and Recycling Act: review/approve battery stewardship plans (120‑day review; denial/resubmission timelines), require producer membership notifications, publish approved plans/producer lists/annual reports on the department website (excluding proprietary info), and adopt rules. The department must review out‑of‑state studies and submit findings to the Legislature's Natural Resources Committee by May 30, 2028. Creates the Battery Stewardship Cash Fund, made up of fees under section 4, administered by the department and restricted to Act implementation; funds may be invested by the state investment officer under state investment laws. Civil penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer; Attorney General or county attorneys may seek injunctions.
