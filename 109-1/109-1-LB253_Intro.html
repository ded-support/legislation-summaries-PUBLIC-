Summary

LB253 requires coverage of biomarker testing by health insurers, nonprofit health service plans, health maintenance organizations, and the Nebraska medical assistance program (Medicaid, administered by the Department of Health and Human Services). Coverage must be provided for diagnosis, treatment, management, or monitoring when supported by medical/scientific evidence, including FDA approvals/labels, CMS national or local coverage determinations, Medicare administrative contractor determinations, and nationally recognized guidelines. Insurers must minimize care disruptions (e.g., multiple biopsies). Prior authorization decisions are required within 72 hours for nonurgent and 24 hours for urgent requests for both commercial plans and Medicaid; appeal/exception processes must be posted on insurer websites and DHHS's website. Contracted Medicaid providers must offer testing at the same scope, duration, and frequency as the program. The bill sets a Medicaid implementation date of January 1, 2026. No explicit appropriations or funding changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the state medical assistance program (and private health insurers, nonprofit plans, HMOs) to cover biomarker testing — by Jan 1, 2026 for the medical assistance program — when supported by medical/scientific evidence, specifically citing: FDA-approved/cleared test indications, FDA‑indicated tests for FDA‑approved drugs, FDA drug label warnings/precautions, CMS national or Medicare administrative contractor local coverage determinations, and nationally recognized clinical guidelines. Entities contracting with the medical assistance program must provide equivalent scope, duration, and frequency. Prior‑authorization decisions must be communicated within 72 hours (nonurgent) or 24 hours (urgent). Insurers and the medical assistance program (or their third‑party agents) must provide clear, accessible exception and adverse‑determination appeal processes on their websites; the medical assistance program must post its process on the Department of Health and Human Services website. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
