Summary

LB123 amends enforcement of budget, audit, and property-tax filing requirements. The Auditor of Public Accounts (APA) must prepare budget documents, calculate restricted funds authority, and notify a governmental unit and the State Treasurer of noncompliance. The State Treasurer is directed to suspend or withhold state aid to noncompliant political subdivisions or municipalities until compliance. Suspended funds are held for six months; if noncompliance continues they are forfeited and redistributed to other eligible recipients in the county or returned to the Highway Allocation Fund (or to the General Fund for homestead-exemption reimbursements). If noncompliance persists for 12 months after APA notice, the subdivision becomes ineligible for future state-aid distributions until compliance. The bill also updates audit-filing enforcement for cities and villages and repeals the original cited statutes (including section 13-3407).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Agencies mentioned: Auditor of Public Accounts and the State Treasurer.

- Added provisions: The Auditor notifies the State Treasurer of a governmental unit's delinquency and later of compliance; state aid is forfeited until the Auditor reports compliance to the State Treasurer.

- Funding effects: Forfeited state aid remains forfeited and will be redistributed per the bill; if there is no eligible recipient the funds return to the Highway Allocation Fund.

- Penalties: Any political subdivision failing to comply within 12 months of the Auditor's notice is ineligible for future state aid distributions (cities/villages losing entitlement under sections 39-2511–39-2520); eligibility is restored upon compliance.
