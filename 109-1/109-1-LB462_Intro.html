Summary

The bill repeatedly designates the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) as the lead agency for child-protection functions: it defines "Department" and describes investigations as actions by the department; requires DHHS to establish a 24/7 statewide toll-free reporting number; mandates DHHS create free online mandatory-reporter training and promulgate implementing rules by Oct. 1, 2027; directs DHHS to retain report records in a child-protection tracking system; assigns counties to child advocacy centers via DHHS; and requires DHHS Division of Children and Family Services representation on county child abuse/neglect investigation and treatment teams. Protocols must allocate initial-response roles between DHHS and law enforcement and use DHHS risk determinations (high/very high) for team review. Juvenile-court jurisdiction expressly includes juveniles voluntarily relinquished to DHHS or agencies licensed by DHHS. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to oversee mandatory-reporter training and rulemaking. DHHS must: (1) provide or approve training for all persons required to report suspected child abuse/neglect, to be completed within 90 days of starting and every two years thereafter; (2) create a free, publicly available online training by October 1, 2027, covering identification, reporting, trauma‑informed response, implicit bias/cultural competency/race‑conscious reporting, and alternatives/preventive community resources, developed in consultation with relevant expertise and updated at least every five years; and (3) adopt and promulgate rules to implement the section by October 1, 2027. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
