Summary

The bill explicitly references the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) as follows: it defines “Department” to mean DHHS; requires DHHS to establish a statewide toll-free child abuse/neglect reporting number; requires DHHS to provide or approve mandatory-reporter training (with a free online training to be created by Oct. 1, 2027 and updated at least every five years) and to adopt implementing rules by Oct. 1, 2027; directs DHHS to retain all reports and related records in a child protection tracking system for statistics and future investigations; assigns DHHS responsibility to assign counties to child advocacy centers and to share/coordinate initial responses and information with law enforcement; and provides for DHHS (Division of Children and Family Services) representation on county child abuse investigation and treatment teams. Juvenile-court jurisdiction includes juveniles voluntarily relinquished to DHHS or DHHS-licensed placement agencies. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): by October 1, 2027 DHHS must provide or approve mandatory reporter training on child abuse/neglect (to be completed within 90 days of starting and every two years thereafter), create a free publicly available online version of that training (developed in consultation with experts on implicit bias and culturally diverse families), update it at least every five years, and adopt rules and regulations to implement the section. Training topics include identification, reporting, trauma‑informed responses, implicit bias/cultural competency/race‑conscious reporting, and alternatives/community resources. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are specified.
