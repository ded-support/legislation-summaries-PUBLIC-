Summary

This bill defines "Department" as the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) and assigns several explicit DHHS roles:

- DHHS is responsible for assigning each county or contiguous group of counties to a child advocacy center and is a named participant in multidisciplinary child abuse investigation and treatment teams (via the Division of Children and Family Services).

- Team protocols must designate DHHS responsibilities, including initial-response role assignment and information-sharing with law enforcement.

- DHHS must retain all report information and related records in a child protection tracking system.

- DHHS must provide or approve mandatory-reporter training (required beginning Oct. 1, 2027), create a free online training by that date, update it at least every five years, and adopt implementing rules and regulations by Oct. 1, 2027.

- Juvenile-court jurisdiction explicitly covers juveniles voluntarily relinquished to DHHS.

No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions assign specific duties to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS):

- By October 1, 2027 DHHS must create and publish a free, publicly accessible online training for all mandatory reporters of child abuse/neglect, and adopt implementing rules and regulations by that date.

- DHHS will provide or approve training that mandatory reporters must complete within 90 days of beginning employment/service and at least every two years thereafter.

- Required training topics: identification and reporting of abuse/neglect; trauma‑informed responses; implicit bias, cultural competency, and race‑conscious reporting; and alternatives to reporting including community resources/preventative services. DHHS must consult experts in implicit bias and culturally diverse family systems and update the training at least every five years.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified in the added text.
