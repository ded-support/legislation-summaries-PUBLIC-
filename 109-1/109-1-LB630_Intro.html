Summary

LB630 amends the Occupational Therapy Practice Act and repeatedly references two state entities: "the board" (which approves training and examinations, receives national licensure exam results, and verifies entry‑level competency) and "the department" (which issues certificates authorizing occupational therapists and assistants to use specified physical‑agent and instrument‑assisted modalities). The bill also refers to "Affairs or other federal agencies" in exemption language. Key agency actions: temporary licenses for exam applicants (practice only in association with a licensed occupational therapist); board‑approved training/exams for modality certification; department issuance of modality certificates; and verification/reporting duties tied to the national licensure exam entity. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. The bill repeals several prior statutory sections.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions for occupational therapy licensing: temporary licenses expire upon issuance/denial of a permanent license, 180 days after issuance, or notification of exam failure. Applicants must take the national licensure examination per the rules of the entity administering it, with results sent to the (licensing) board for verification of entry‑level competency. Expands and clarifies evaluation domains, intervention methods, prevention/health‑promotion activities, group interventions, and practitioner proficiency documentation. Defines supervision as a collaborative review process where the occupational therapist is accountable for services by assistants and aides, and the occupational therapy assistant is accountable for services they provide. No specific government agencies beyond the licensing board and the entity administering the national exam are named, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
