Summary

Bill LB630 amends the Occupational Therapy Practice Act, altering roles for the occupational therapy board and an unnamed state department (the department). Key agency actions: the board approves training courses and examinations and receives national licensure exam results from the entity administering that exam to verify entry‑level competency; the department issues certificates for physical‑agent and instrument‑assisted modalities and for occupational therapy assistants to set up/implement superficial thermal modalities (with onsite OT supervision). The Hand Therapy Certification Commission (or equivalent bodies recognized by the board) is explicitly recognized for certification equivalency. The bill also references federal agencies in exemption language. Several statutory sections are repealed. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements that applicants must take the national licensure examination as administered by the entity that runs that exam, follow that entity's rules, and meet its minimum requirements; examination results are sent to "the board" for verification of entry‑level competency. Specifies when a temporary license expires (earlier of issuance of permanent license, board denial, 180 days after issuance, or notification of exam failure). Clarifies documentation of modality proficiency, expanded elements of occupational therapy evaluation and interventions, and defines supervision/accountability: the occupational therapist is accountable for services by assistants and aides, and the assistant is accountable for services they provide. No specific state agencies or departments beyond "the board" and the exam‑administering entity are named, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
