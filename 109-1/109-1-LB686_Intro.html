Summary

LB686 prohibits government entities and officials from knowingly maintaining lists, records, or registries of privately owned firearms or their owners, except records kept in the regular course of a criminal investigation, prosecution, or as otherwise required by law. It bars payment card networks from requiring or incentivizing merchant category codes (MCCs) that distinguish firearm retailers, and prohibits covered entities from assigning such distinguishing MCCs. The Attorney General is given exclusive authority to investigate alleged violations, issue written notices, and, if violations persist after 30 business days, file for injunctions; continued noncompliance may lead to civil fines up to $1,000 per violation (stayed pending appeal). Compliance with a statutory MCC requirement is an affirmative defense. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds prohibitions and an enforcement scheme centered on the state Attorney General. Key points:

- Government entities/officials may not maintain lists/registries of privately owned firearms or owners, except records kept in regular criminal investigations/prosecutions or as otherwise required by law.

- Payment card networks and covered entities may not require, incentivize, or assign merchant category codes that distinguish firearm retailers from other retailers; a statutory defense exists if a code was required by law.

- The Attorney General has exclusive authority to investigate alleged violations, issue written notices, seek injunctions if violations persist after 30 business days, and pursue civil fines up to $1,000 per violation for noncompliance 30 business days after an injunction. Fines are stayed pending appeal. Remedies are exclusive to this section.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
