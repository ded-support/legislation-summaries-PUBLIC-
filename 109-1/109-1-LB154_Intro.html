Summary

LB154 amends the Hearing Instrument Specialists Practice Act and shifts administrative responsibilities to the state credentialing apparatus. The Department (per the Uniform Credentialing Act) and the Hearing Instrument Specialists board jointly: define licensure standards and exams; issue and renew licenses and temporary training permits; require annual employer filings listing licensed specialists; set record-retention (seven years); and may waive exam components. The board’s composition is specified (three licensed hearing instrument specialists, one otolaryngologist, one audiologist, plus one public member). The Board of Medicine and Surgery must be consulted for rules on cerumen-removal training courses. The State Department of Education is retained in an existing exemption. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and regulatory duties involving the state "department" and the licensing "board." Key agency actions: organizations must file annually with the department (lists of licensed hearing instrument specialists and statements of compliance on department forms); the department, with board recommendation, may evaluate foreign programs, waive exam components, determine exam eligibility, and accept receipts as verification. The board sets exam structure, practical/competency standards, retake rules, and may adopt cerumen‑removal course requirements only after consulting the Board of Medicine and Surgery. The bill references federal rule 21 C.F.R. 801.109 for device ordering. It also requires seven‑year record retention and professional liability insurance for cerumen removal. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are added.
