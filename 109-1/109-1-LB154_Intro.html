Summary

LB154 amends the Hearing Instrument Specialists Practice Act and related credentialing provisions. Key government entities affected: the Department (under the Uniform Credentialing Act) retains authority to issue, renew, and discipline hearing-instrument specialist licenses and temporary training permits; the Hearing Instrument Specialists Board’s composition is specified (three licensed hearing instrument specialists, one otolaryngologist, one audiologist, plus one public member); and the Board of Medicine and Surgery must be consulted before the specialists’ board adopts cerumen-removal rules. The bill requires organizations selling hearing instruments to file annually with the Department a list of employed licensed specialists and a compliance statement. The Department, with board recommendation, manages exam eligibility, waivers, and temporary license issuance/renewal. No appropriations or budgetary changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds administrative duties for the state "department" and the hearing instrument specialist "board" and involves the Board of Medicine and Surgery. Key agency actions:

- Organizations that employ licensed hearing instrument specialists must annually file lists and a compliance statement on department forms.

- The department (with board recommendation) will: verify receipts/forms, determine foreign licensure program equivalency, waive exam components, and decide eligibility to take the licensure exam.

- The board may adopt rules under §38-126 and, after consulting the Board of Medicine and Surgery, set initial cerumen‑removal course requirements.

- Licensed specialists must maintain patient and training records for seven years and comply with department/board record requests.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
