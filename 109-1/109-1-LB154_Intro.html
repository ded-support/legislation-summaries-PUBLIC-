Summary

LB154 amends the Hearing Instrument Specialists Practice Act and related credentialing provisions. Key agency/departments actions and roles:

- The Department (licensing authority) issues hearing instrument specialist licenses and temporary training licenses, determines exam eligibility, accepts verification forms, and receives annual employer filings of licensed specialists.

- The Hearing Instrument Specialists Board (appointments per §38-158) is restructured to five professional members (three licensed hearing instrument specialists, one otolaryngologist, one audiologist) plus one public member; it recommends exams, rulemaking, training approvals, and student practicum requirements to the Department.

- The Board of Medicine and Surgery must be consulted before the Board adopts cerumen-removal course rules.

- The State Department of Education remains referenced for audiology/speech practice exemptions.

- Licensees must retain records seven years; employers must file annually with the Department.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds repeated roles for the state "department" (forms, verification, licensure determinations, record retention) and the specialty "board" (exam approval, competency standards, credential determinations, rulemaking recommendations). The Board of Medicine and Surgery must be consulted for cerumen‑removal course rules; the department may accept waivers, determine foreign program eligibility, and require annual filings from organizations listing employed licensed hearing instrument specialists and statements of compliance. References to the Audiology and Speech‑Language Pathology Interstate Compact and federal regulation 21 C.F.R. 801.109 are included. Requires seven‑year record retention and department‑provided forms. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
