Summary

LB488 amends probation and juvenile supervision procedures. Key agencies: the Office of Probation Administration (OPA), probation officers and chief probation officers, county attorneys, peace officers, and the sentencing/adjudicating (juvenile) courts. Major provisions: a peace officer must assume custody of an adult probationer or temporarily take a juvenile into custody when requested by a probation officer; probation officers may impose administrative/custodial sanctions or seek revocation and must report violations to county attorneys; OPA may establish a statewide graduated-response matrix, provide implementation and training, and the probation administrator/administrator must adopt rules (including prompt court-review procedures). The bill repeals the original sections. No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds mandatory duties for peace officers: upon request by a probation officer, a peace officer must arrest and assume custody of the probationer; likewise, upon a probation officer’s request (per subsection (6) of §43‑286.01), a peace officer must take a juvenile into temporary custody. No state agencies beyond probation and peace officers are newly created or referenced, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
