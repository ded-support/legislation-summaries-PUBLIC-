Summary

LB488 amends probation and juvenile custody statutes to clarify roles and procedures for the Office of Probation Administration, probation officers, peace officers, county attorneys, and courts. Key points:

- Probation officers may arrest or take juveniles into temporary custody when there is risk of flight or danger; if they request assistance, a peace officer must assume custody of the probationer or juvenile.

- County attorneys receive written reports after such detentions and must promptly decide whether to seek revocation or authorize release; sentencing/adjudicating courts handle revocation proceedings.

- The Office of Probation Administration is authorized to adopt rules, promulgate a statewide standardized graduated‑response matrix for juveniles, and provide implementation training; the probation administrator also must promulgate rules.

- No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that, upon a probation officer’s request (per subsection (6) of §43‑286.01), a peace officer must take a juvenile into temporary custody. It clarifies that when called on by a probation officer, a peace officer is required to assume temporary custody of the juvenile. Agencies referenced: peace officers and probation officers. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
