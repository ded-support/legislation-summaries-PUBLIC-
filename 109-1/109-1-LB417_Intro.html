Summary

LB417 assigns and clarifies responsibilities across multiple state education entities. Key provisions:

- Board of Regents, University of Nebraska: given powers and duties to administer the Nebraska Promise Program.

- Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education: to administer the College Promise Fund, distribute funds to community and state colleges, develop an appeals process, and implement intent to fully fund annual tuition waivers equal to the total value of waivers provided. Fund receipts may include legislative transfers; the state investment officer will invest fund balances under existing state investment law.

- Community colleges and state colleges: must waive remaining tuition (after federal grants and state scholarships) for eligible Nebraska residents (income < $65,000) per program rules.

- State Board of Education and State Department of Education: update statewide assessment/reporting systems; the board to recommend national and portable career‑readiness assessments (including National Career Readiness Certificates) and ensure no‑cost administration; the department to use the student identifier system to track and annually report achievement and discipline data to the Governor, Legislature, districts, ESUs, and learning communities.

- Governor: appoints a technical advisory committee (appointments confirmed by the Legislature).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds administration and funding mechanics and assessment duties to state education entities. Key points:

- Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education will administer a new "College Promise Fund" to reimburse community colleges and state colleges for tuition waivers; the fund consists of legislative transfers and is intended to be fully funded each year equal to the total value of waivers. The state investment officer may invest fund balances under the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and Nebraska State Funds Investment Act. Colleges must waive 100% of remaining tuition after federal grants and state scholarships; Cooperating Commission will provide an appeals process.

- The State Board (and "the department") will recommend nationally recognized career‑readiness assessments (leading to a National Career Readiness Certificate) beginning 2025–26. School districts must report individual student scores per state procedures; assessments are provided at no cost to twelfth‑grade public students.
