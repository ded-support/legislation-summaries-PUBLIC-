Summary

LB417 assigns administration of the Nebraska Promise Program to the Board of Regents of the University of Nebraska and establishes the College Promise Act. The Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education will administer a College Promise Fund to distribute payments to community and state colleges, with the Legislature authorized to transfer funds; the bill declares the Legislature's intent to fully fund the Fund annually for the total value of tuition waivers. The state investment officer will invest any available fund balances. The Coordinating Commission must also develop an appeals process. The State Board of Education and the State Department of Education retain roles in statewide assessments, student data tracking and reporting, and the State Board must recommend career‑readiness assessments. The act takes effect immediately under an emergency clause.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds administration and funding structure for a “College Promise” tuition-waiver program and new school assessment duties. Key agencies/departments:

- Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education: administers the College Promise Fund, develops the appeals process for waiver denials, and oversees distributions to community colleges and state colleges.

- Community colleges and state colleges: must verify eligibility, notify applicants within 45 days, waive remaining tuition after federal grants/state scholarships, and report eligibility decisions.

- Legislature: authorizes transfers into the College Promise Fund; intent language directs full annual funding equal to the total value of waivers.

- State investment officer: may invest fund balances per state investment statutes.

- State board (and the department referenced, i.e., Dept. of Education): beginning 2025–26 to recommend nationally recognized career‑readiness assessments; school districts must report individual student scores per procedures and provide assessments at no cost.
