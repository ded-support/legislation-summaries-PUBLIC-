Summary

LB417 assigns and clarifies responsibilities across state education agencies and postsecondary authorities. Key provisions:

- Board of Regents of the University of Nebraska: given powers and duties to administer the Nebraska Promise Program.

- Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education: to administer the College Promise Fund, develop an appeals process, and distribute funds to community and state colleges. Legislature intends to fully fund the Fund annually equal to the total value of tuition waivers; funds may be invested by the state investment officer.

- Community colleges/state colleges: must waive remaining tuition (after federal grants/state scholarships) for eligible students and notify applicants within 45 days.

- State Board of Education: adopt and maintain statewide assessment/reporting plans, recommend national college- and career-readiness assessments.

- State Department of Education: receive plans, operate the student identifier system, collect individual student achievement and discipline data, and report analyses annually to the Governor, Legislature, districts, ESUs, and learning communities.

- Governor: appoints a technical advisory committee (Legislative confirmation required).

The act contains operative-date and emergency-effect provisions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Agencies/departments: Coordinating Commission for Postsecondary Education administers the College Promise Fund and must develop an appeals process (per sec. 6). The state investment officer will invest fund balances under the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and Nebraska State Funds Investment Act. The state board (of education) and the department (education) set assessment procedures and recommendations; school districts must report individual student assessment scores per procedures and section 79-760.05.

- Funding/appropriations: Establishes a College Promise Fund made of legislative transfers and directs the Legislature’s intent to fully fund the Fund each year equal to the total value of tuition waivers awarded by community and state colleges.

- Other operational changes: Colleges must verify eligibility and notify applicants within 45 days; eligible waivers cover 100% of remaining tuition after federal grants and state scholarships. Beginning 2025–26 the state board will recommend portable career-readiness assessments (free to 12th-graders) and permit retakes.
