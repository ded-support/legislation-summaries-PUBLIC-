Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): required to pursue all SNAP options to maximize federal funding and minimize General Fund use, hire necessary personnel, and submit an annual report to the Health and Human Services Committee (due Dec. 1) detailing options, actions, caseload, costs and expenditures. DHHS must develop a state outreach plan meeting Food and Nutrition Service (USDA) criteria, may accept gifts/grants/donations, partner with counties/nonprofits, contract to allow costs to be paid from private or federal funds, submit the plan to FNS and request federal matching funds, and may be exempt from implementing the plan if insufficient private/federal funds. DHHS shall create a TANF-funded policy to establish categorical eligibility and temporarily raise gross income limits to 165% of OMB poverty guidelines until Oct. 1, 2025, and must annually report evaluations, administrative cost impacts, and federal funding availability to the HHS Committee and Legislative Fiscal Analyst. DHHS also must not seek SNAP work‑requirement waivers and must assign eligible 16–59 year olds to employment and training programs.

- Department of Agriculture (federal USDA): DHHS outreach plan must meet Food and Nutrition Service (USDA) criteria and be submitted to FNS; DHHS may request federal matching funds.

- Department of Labor: SNAP Next Step Program participation is encouraged for SNAP households with income between 131%–165% of OMB poverty guidelines (subject to program availability); the Legislature intends that the Department of Labor not use General Funds for these processes and that participation depend on available federal funds.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (impacts by agency):

- Department of Agriculture: Prohibited from seeking, applying for, accepting, or renewing any SNAP work‑requirement waivers (7 U.S.C. 2015(o)) and from using the federal exemption option in 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(6)(E), except where federal law expressly requires.

- Department of Labor: Likely to see increased referrals/coordination obligations to deliver employment and training services for all SNAP recipients ages 16–59 under the state's general work requirement (7 U.S.C. 2015(d)(4)). Will need to align services with work‑registration and unemployment systems.

- Department of Health and Human Services (or designated state agency administering SNAP): Must assign eligible 16–59 year‑olds to employment/training and administer exempt categories (caregivers of young/disabled persons, half‑time students, inpatient substance‑use treatment, employed ≥30 hours, certain 16–17 year‑olds), coordinating with Title IV and treatment providers.

No appropriations, funding increases, or budget allocations are included.
