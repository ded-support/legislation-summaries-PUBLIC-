Summary

The bill assigns primary responsibility to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to maximize federal SNAP options and federal funding, minimize use of state General Funds, employ necessary personnel, and submit annual and evaluation reports (including administrative costs and federal funding availability) to the Legislature. DHHS must develop a USDA Food and Nutrition Service–approved state outreach plan, may accept gifts/grants/donations and seek federal matching funds, and may decline implementation if private or federal funds are insufficient. DHHS shall create a TANF‑funded policy temporarily increasing gross income eligibility to 165% of OMB poverty guidelines (returning Oct 1, 2025) and must assign most 16–59-year-olds to employment and training programs. The Department of Labor is referenced as administrator of a SNAP Next Step partnership with DHHS; the Legislature intends no General Funds be used by Labor for that program. The bill also bars DHHS from seeking certain federal SNAP work‑requirement waivers.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Agriculture

- Prohibits the department from seeking, applying for, accepting, or renewing SNAP work‑requirement waivers (7 U.S.C. 2015(o)) unless federally required, and bars use of the state's discretionary exemption under 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(6)(E).

- Requires the department to assign all people ages 16–59 to SNAP employment & training programs under 7 U.S.C. 2015(d), subject to enumerated exemptions (e.g., parents/caregivers of children under 6, incapacitated persons, half‑time students, certain employed individuals, inpatient rehab participants, those already under Title IV/unemployment work registration, and some 16–17 year‑olds).

Department of Health and Human Services
