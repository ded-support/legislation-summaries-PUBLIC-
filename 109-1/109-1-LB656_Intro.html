Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): required to apply for SNAP options to maximize federal funding and minimize state General Funds; hire staff to assess options; submit an annual report (by Dec. 1) to the Health and Human Services Committee with program options, actions, caseload, costs and expenditures; develop a USDA Food and Nutrition Service (FNS)-compliant state outreach plan, accept gifts/grants/donations, partner with counties/nonprofits, and submit the plan to USDA FNS for approval and matching funds; may be exempt from implementing the plan if private or federal funds are insufficient; create a TANF-funded categorical eligibility policy raising gross income eligibility to 165% of OMB poverty guidelines through Oct. 1, 2025 and report annually (by Dec. 15) to the committee and Legislative Fiscal Analyst on costs and federal funding availability; prohibited from seeking SNAP work‑requirement waivers and from providing certain federal exemptions; must assign eligible individuals to employment and training programs.

- Department of Agriculture (USDA/FNS): DHHS must submit the state outreach plan to the USDA Food and Nutrition Service for approval and request federal matching funds.

- Department of Labor: referenced as administrator of the SNAP Next Step Program (a DHHS–Department of Labor partnership); eligible SNAP households are encouraged to participate where available; intent that the Department of Labor use no General Funds for these processes and to utilize federal funds if available.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the state administering department to not seek or grant SNAP work‑requirement waivers beyond federal law and to assign all persons aged 16–59 to federally defined employment & training (E&T) programs, with listed exemptions (parents of young children/incapacitated persons, half‑time students, certain inpatients, those working ≥30 hrs/week, and some 16–17 year‑olds).

- Department of Agriculture (USDA/FNS interactions): Limits state waiver requests to USDA; no funding or federal appropriation changes in the text.
