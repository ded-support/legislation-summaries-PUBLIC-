Summary

LB573 creates the Meat and Poultry Workers and Contractors Protection Act. Key agency roles and actions:

- The bill repeatedly tasks the "commissioner" (as used in the bill) to receive and investigate complaints, develop a compliance monitoring process within 90 days, assess civil penalties up to $15,000 for violations, and bring civil actions on behalf of aggrieved workers or contract growers.

- Covered industrial operators must submit disaster mitigation plans to the commissioner and, after a disaster-mitigation event, provide workers and affected contract growers workers' compensation coverage, severance, and lost-revenue payments; violations trigger commissioner enforcement and private suits (unpaid compensation plus liquidated damages and attorney fees).

- The commissioner must require operators with ≥25 employees to provide employees information about federal collective-bargaining/FLSA rights (29 C.F.R.; 29 U.S.C.).

- The commissioner shall appoint a Workers and Contractors Task Force (specified membership including two nonvoting members from the Legislature’s Agriculture Committee) that reports annually to the commissioner and Legislature.

No appropriations or agency budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates enforcement and oversight duties for a state "commissioner" (investigate complaints, develop a compliance-monitoring process within 90 days, assess civil penalties up to $15,000, and bring court actions). References federal OSHA and the Nebraska Workers' Compensation Act for worker protections and compensation. Requires covered operators to submit disaster mitigation plans and provide worker severance/compensation and contract-grower lost‑revenue payments (private obligations). Requires employers with ≥25 employees to provide workers information on collective‑bargaining rights under 29 C.F.R. and the Fair Labor Standards Act. Establishes a Commissioner‑appointed Workers and Contractors Task Force (includes union and agricultural representatives, an attorney, and two nonvoting members from the Legislature’s Agriculture Committee) to meet regularly and report annually to the commissioner and Legislature. No state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
