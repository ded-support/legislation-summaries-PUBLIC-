Summary

- Department of Administrative Services: The bill retains a Class I misdemeanor prohibition on disclosure of taxpayer information by employees of the Tax Commissioner, the State Treasurer, or the Department of Administrative Services.

- Department of Labor: The Tax Commissioner may disclose tax information to the Department of Labor as necessary to administer the Employment Security Law, the Contractor Registration Act, or the Employee Classification Act.

- Department of Revenue: The bill (amending §77-2711) authorizes the Auditor of Public Accounts to audit the Department of Revenue (including in response to suspected municipal-level reporting irregularities identified by certified municipal reviewers). Confidential tax returns provided for audit must be examined on Department of Revenue premises and audit work papers stored securely in the Department. The Department of Revenue is not liable for impermissible disclosures by municipalities or their agents.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a new subsection permitting a municipality‑certified individual who, after reviewing municipal documents, suspects an irregularity or discrepancy to notify the Auditor of Public Accounts. The Auditor may then perform an audit under existing subsection (11). No provisions in the added text reference the Department of Administrative Services, Department of Labor, or Department of Revenue, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes included.
