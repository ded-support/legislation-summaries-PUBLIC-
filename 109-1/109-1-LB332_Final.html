Summary

LB332 assigns and clarifies regulatory responsibilities for state agencies and professional boards and includes targeted Medicaid funding intents.

- Department/Boards: The department (per the Uniform Credentialing Act) and relevant boards are directed to administer licensure, temporary training licenses, examinations, rulemaking, practitioner lists, recordkeeping, and enforcement for hearing instrument specialists and funeral service collaborative agreements. The Board of Pharmacy is reconstituted to eight members: five practicing pharmacists (at least one hospital), one pharmacy technician, and two public members.

- Public health clinics/pharmacy: Delegated dispensing permits must meet labeling, staffing, and pharmacist-availability requirements; emergency pharmacy refill limits are specified.

- Medicaid/Department of Health: Requires coverage expansions (continuous glucose monitors, medical-respite waiver submissions to CMS, electric personal-use breast pumps, and expanded lactation consultation). Fiscal notes/intent: no more than $600,000 annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund for CGM expansion (excess from the same fund); medical respite services to be funded from that fund; intent that lactation consultation reimbursement be increased 145% and tuition-waiver funding be considered at half cost in the biennial budget.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds regulatory duties for the state "department" and the professional "board" over assistant funeral directors and licensed hearing instrument specialists: the department must provide forms, accept annual employer filings of licensed hearing instrument specialists, adopt rules and regulations, verify receipts (per section 38‑1514), and — with the board’s recommendation — determine eligibility of foreign-trained applicants and waive exam components. The board approves examinations, practical/jurisprudence components and establishes training/practicum requirements. The federal Food and Drug Administration is cited for applicable device regulations. Record‑keeping requirements (seven years) and various licensing, supervision, and scope‑of‑practice controls are added. Funding: the Legislature expresses intent to consider continued funding in the biennial budget equal to at least one‑half of tuition waivers/fees for a related UNMC transfer program; no specific appropriation amounts included.
