Summary

LB332 directs multiple state agencies and boards, primarily the Department (credentialing functions under the Uniform Credentialing Act, i.e., Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services), the State Department of Education, the Board of Pharmacy, and the Hearing Instrument Specialists board.

Key agency actions and changes:

- DHHS: expanded licensure, supervision, collaborative‑agreement and discipline provisions for funeral directing; issues and oversees hearing‑instrument licensure, temporary training licenses, exams, records, and rulemaking; must accept annual employer lists for organizations selling hearing instruments.

- State Department of Education: retains limited credential exemption for employees providing audiology/speech services.

- Board of Pharmacy: membership reconfigured to eight members (five pharmacists, one pharmacy technician, two public members).

- Public health clinics/delegating pharmacists: new delegated‑dispensing conditions and pharmacist availability/reporting requirements.

- Medicaid (DHHS): mandates coverage expansions (continuous glucose monitors, postpartum extension waiver efforts, breast pumps and lactation visits) with funding provisions — intent that lactation visit appropriations equal a 145% rate increase; CGM expansion capped at $600,000 annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund (excess funded by that fund); medical respite services to be funded from the same fund. The act takes effect immediately (emergency clause).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds regulatory provisions governing assistant funeral directors and establishes detailed scope, practice, licensure, examination, recordkeeping (7-year retention), consumer‑receipt and sale requirements, and cerumen‑removal limits for hearing instrument specialists. It repeatedly vests authority in "the department," often acting "with the recommendation of the board," to adopt rules, approve/waive exam components, determine foreign‑trained eligibility, and require annual employer filings. Federal and credentialing bodies referenced include the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (21 C.F.R.), the National Board for Certification in Hearing Instrument Sciences, and the International Hearing Society. University of Nebraska Medical Center and the Legislature are named regarding an early‑admission tuition waiver program; the Legislature intends to consider biennial funding equal to at least one‑half of those waiver/fee costs.
