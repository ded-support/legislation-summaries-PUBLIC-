Summary

LB332 makes multiple regulatory changes affecting state licensing boards and the Department of Health and Human Services (the department). Key agency actions:

- Funeral Directing and Embalming: creates an assistant funeral director role subject to collaborative agreements, supervision and recordkeeping under department oversight and adds discipline for noncompliance.

- Hearing Instrument Specialists Board/Department: revises definitions, scope (including cerumen removal and required face‑to‑face or reviewed hearing assessments within six months), licensure pathways, temporary training licenses, exam standards, record retention (7 years), employer reporting, and board rule‑making authority.

- Board of Pharmacy/public health clinics: modifies board composition; authorizes delegated dispensing at public health clinics with specified practitioner, labeling, pharmacist‑on‑call and refill limits (contraceptives only).

- Medicaid/DHHS: mandates coverage expansions (continuous glucose monitors, extended postpartum coverage, electric personal breast pumps and lactation visits, medical respite waivers) and directs funding sources. Fiscal notes: up to $600,000/year from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund for CGM expansion; medical respite and lactation consultation funding to be supported as specified; legislative intent to consider state funding of tuition waivers (≥50% of cost) in the biennial budget.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds regulatory requirements and oversight by "the department" and the licensing "board" for assistant funeral directors and hearing instrument specialists: the board may set exam, practicum and credential criteria; the department may adopt rules, receive annual lists of employed hearing instrument specialists from organizations, determine licensure eligibility/waivers, and require recordkeeping. The text incorporates federal FDA rules (21 C.F.R. 801.422 and 801.109) as standards and references credentialing bodies (National Board for Certification in Hearing Instrument Sciences; International Hearing Society). It also requires consumer disclosures, documentation, and limits (e.g., cerumen removal scope, face‑to‑face assessments). Fiscal language: the Legislature expresses intent to consider continued funding—at least one‑half of the cost of tuition waivers/fees for a state‑college to University of Nebraska Medical Center pathway—through the biennial budget process; no specific appropriation is made.
