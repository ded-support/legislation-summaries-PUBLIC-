Summary

This bill assigns implementation and oversight roles primarily to the Department (DHHS under the Uniform Credentialing Act) and several licensing boards. Key agency actions:

- Department/boards: regulate new assistant funeral director credentialing, supervision, collaborative agreements, and disciplinary grounds under the Funeral Directing and Embalming Practice Act.

- Department and the Hearing Instrument Specialists board: revise licensure, examinations, temporary training licenses, scope (including cerumen removal, hearing assessment requirements), recordkeeping, and corporate reporting duties.

- Board of Pharmacy: membership revised to eight members (five pharmacists — at least one hospital pharmacist — one pharmacy technician, two public members).

- DHHS/director: authorize delegated dispensing at public health clinics with pharmacist availability requirements.

- DHHS (Medicaid): must pursue state plan amendments/waivers (postpartum coverage, CGMs, medical respite). Funding intents: up to $600,000/year from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund for continuous glucose monitor expansion; medical respite services funded from that fund; lactation consultation appropriation targeted at a 145% rate increase; Legislature intends to consider at least half of tuition-waiver costs in the biennial budget. Annual reporting to the Health and Human Services Committee required for medical respite. The act takes effect immediately (emergency).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds multiple regulatory duties for the state "department" and its licensing "board" (no agency renamed): the department may adopt rules, must accept annual lists from organizations employing licensed hearing instrument specialists, receives verification forms, and — with board recommendations — determines eligibility to sit for or waive components of licensure examinations and approves examination standards and retake rules. Federal agency referenced: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (21 C.F.R. citations) for clinical warnings and device classifications. The University of Nebraska Medical Center and state colleges are named regarding an early-admission tuition-waiver program; the Legislature declares intent to consider continued funding in the biennial budget equal to at least one-half of the cost of those tuition waivers or fees (no specific appropriation specified).
