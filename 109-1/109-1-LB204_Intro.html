Summary

LB204 adopts the "Biometric Autonomy Liberty Law," imposing limits on collection, possession, use, sale, retention, portability, and destruction of biometric data. It defines "public entity" to include the State of Nebraska, state and local agencies, political subdivisions, courts, and the Legislative Council. The State Department of Education sets the required seventh-grade lexile for consent language. The bill exempts law enforcement uses, health information covered by HIPAA, emergency medical services, DMV facial-recognition fraud detection, FERPA-protected education records, and certain financial data; sections limiting device/collection requirements do not apply to biometric data collected for a "security purpose." The Attorney General may issue subpoenas, seek injunctive relief, and bring civil actions (treated as Consumer Protection Act violations) with a 60-day cure notice. Operative date: January 1, 2026. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions define "public entity" to include the State of Nebraska, state and local agencies, political subdivisions, Nebraska courts (and judges/justices), and the Legislative Council. The State Department of Education sets the seventh‑grade lexile standard for written consent language. The Attorney General is empowered to subpoena, seek injunctive relief, and bring civil actions (with a 60‑day cure period) for violations, which are treated as Consumer Protection Act violations. Law enforcement agencies have multiple disclosure and use exceptions (including for investigations, missing persons, and public health). The Department of Motor Vehicles is exempt for facial recognition fraud detection. HIPAA, FERPA, EMS Practice Act, and GLBA-covered financial institutions are excluded. No funding or appropriations changes are included.
