Summary

LB204 (2025) enacts the "Biometric Autonomy Liberty Law" regulating biometric data. It defines "public entity" to include the State of Nebraska, state and local agencies, political subdivisions, courts, and the Legislative Council. The Attorney General is empowered to issue subpoenas, pursue civil damages and injunctive relief, and enforce violations under the Consumer Protection Act (with a 60‑day cure notice). The bill references the State Department of Education for consent‑language lexile standards. Exemptions include law enforcement uses, security purposes, HIPAA‑covered health information, emergency medical services, DMV facial recognition for fraud detection, FERPA‑protected education records, and GLBA‑covered financial data. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. The act is operative January 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a Biometric Autonomy Liberty Law defining public entities to include the State of Nebraska (and its agents), state/local agencies, political subdivisions, Nebraska courts, and the Legislative Council. The State Department of Education sets the seventh‑grade lexile standard for consent language. The Attorney General is given enforcement authority — subpoena power, civil actions for damages, and injunctive relief — with a 60‑day cure notice requirement before suit. Law enforcement agencies (and cooperating processors) have specified disclosure/use exceptions; the Department of Motor Vehicles is explicitly exempted for facial‑recognition fraud detection. Other statutory exceptions reference HIPAA, FERPA, the Emergency Medical Services Practice Act, the Data Privacy and Genetic Information Privacy Acts, and Gramm‑Leach‑Bliley. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
