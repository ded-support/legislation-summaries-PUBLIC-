Summary

LB504 (2025) enacts the Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act. Key government entities referenced:

- Attorney General: Enforcement authority for violations (also treated as deceptive trade practices). The AG may not seek civil penalties under the Act until July 1, 2026. Beginning July 1, 2026, covered services may face civil penalties up to $50,000 per violation.

- State Treasurer: All penalties collected must be remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution pursuant to Article VII, §5 of the Nebraska Constitution.

Federal agencies cited for definitions/standards include the Federal Trade Commission (dark patterns) and the Bureau of Labor Statistics (CPI adjustments); federal health and privacy laws/agencies (HITECH, HIPAA, FDA-related human subject protections) are noted as exceptions. The Act becomes operative January 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds an Age-Appropriate Online Design Code with multiple federal and state agency references and limited fiscal actions. Key agencies mentioned: Federal Bureau of Labor Statistics (CPI used to adjust a $25M revenue threshold), Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — including FTC definitions of “dark pattern” and COPPA/FTC rules, Attorney General (enforcement; cannot seek civil penalties until July 1, 2026), State Treasurer (all collected civil penalties remitted for distribution per Neb. Const. Art. VII, §5), Health & Human Services–related federal statutes/regulators via references to GLBA (Title V), HITECH, HIPAA (section 264 regs), and the Food and Drug Administration and International Council for Harmonisation for clinical-trial exemptions. Fiscal provisions: civil penalties up to $50,000 per violation; no new appropriations or budget line-items are created—penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer for constitutional distribution.
