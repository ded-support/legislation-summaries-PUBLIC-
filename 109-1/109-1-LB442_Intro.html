Summary

The bill directs the Department of Health and Human Services to administer the state social‑services program and authorizes the department to contract with other social agencies for services. The department must provide child‑care assistance to families with incomes from 130% to 400% of federal poverty level beginning October 1, 2026, and must adopt rules and regulations implementing program requirements. Funding is provided by a 0.52% payroll tax (employer 0.39%, employee 0.13%); General Funds are prohibited from covering state costs, including administration, arising from the income‑eligibility changes beginning October 1, 2026. The department shall set and annually revise a fixed-rate schedule for child‑care payments, not to exceed providers’ private rates, with possible separate rates for infants and children with special needs.

DHHS duties and funding changes — summary:

- DHHS will administer statewide social services and may contract for service delivery; ADC statutory maximums apply only to public assistance grants, not social services.

- Beginning Oct 1, 2026, DHHS must extend child‑care assistance to families with incomes from 130% to 400% of FPL and adopt rules implementing eligibility and provider requirements.

- Eligibility details: after 12 continuous months, 10% of household gross earned income is disregarded at redetermination; assistance is cost‑shared on a sliding scale with family contributions capped at existing transitional child‑care cost‑sharing rates (as of Jan 1, 2025).

- Operational rules (effective Oct 1, 2025): employer/employee accommodation and enrollment flexibilities for households with provider employees.

- Funding: a 0.52% payroll tax (0.39% employer / 0.13% employee) will fund the program. General Funds are prohibited from covering state costs (including administration) arising from the 2026 income‑eligibility expansion.

- DHHS must set annual fixed reimbursement rate schedules for providers and prioritize highest assistance for qualified apprentices.
