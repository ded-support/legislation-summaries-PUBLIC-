Summary

The bill requires the Department of Health and Human Services to administer a new state child care subsidy program and authorizes contracting with other social agencies. Beginning October 1, 2026, the department must provide child care assistance to families with incomes from 130%–400% of the federal poverty level, adopt rules implementing eligibility (including a 10% gross-earned-income disregard after 12 continuous months, sliding-scale cost-sharing, and criminal-history checks), and establish an annual fixed-rate reimbursement schedule effective each October 1. Beginning October 1, 2025, the department must allow certain accommodations and enrollment flexibilities for household members. Funding is provided by a 0.52% payroll tax (0.39% employer / 0.13% employee); General Funds may not be used to cover state costs, including administrative costs, attributable to the income-eligibility changes beginning October 1, 2026. The program must prioritize apprentices for highest assistance.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

DHHS must administer expanded social services and implement a state child-care subsidy program. Key DHHS duties: provide child care assistance to families earning 130%–400% of FPL effective Oct 1, 2026; require providers to meet Child Care Licensing Act criminal-history checks; apply a sliding‑scale, cost‑share for benefits with a 10% gross‑earned‑income disregard after 12 continuous months and at each redetermination; adopt rules for reasonable‑accommodation and enrollment exceptions (effective Oct 1, 2025); set an annual fixed-rate reimbursement schedule (effective Oct 1 each year) that may include higher rates for infants or children with special needs but not exceed providers’ private charges; and prioritize highest assistance for qualified apprentice workers. Funding: a 0.52% payroll tax (0.39% employer / 0.13% employee) funds the program; General Funds cannot pay state costs from the eligibility expansion beginning Oct 1, 2026.
