Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): Peace officers may deliver juveniles taken into temporary custody to DHHS for temporary placement in the least restrictive environment; DHHS shall supervise such placements, may consent to emergency medical/psychological/psychiatric treatment, and temporary custody by DHHS terminates if no court order is entered within 48 hours. For mental‑health detentions, officers may deliver juveniles to DHHS and must file prescribed certificates and notify county attorneys and parents. DHHS, with the Office of Probation Administration, shall use day‑reporting and evening‑reporting centers for juveniles and may adopt rules to select, oversee, and reimburse such centers (no specific appropriation included).

- Department of Correctional Services (DCS): Courts may commit offenders to DCS for a complete study prior to sentencing. The definition of a staff‑secure juvenile facility expressly excludes institutions operated by DCS.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Correctional Services: The bill limits sentences for Class IB felonies committed by persons under 22, capping maximum imprisonment at 60 years and placing an upper limit on minimum terms (not greater than 20 years). This changes potential incarceration exposure and will affect DOCS caseloads and sentence administration. No specific appropriations or funding increases for DOCS are included.

- Department of Health and Human Services: DHHS (with the Office of Probation Administration) is required to use day‑reporting and evening‑reporting centers for juveniles under the Nebraska Juvenile Code, may adopt rules to select/oversee those centers, and may reimburse them. The bill also prohibits transferring adjudicated juveniles out of state except for emergency medical or mental‑health treatment, and restricts dissemination of confidential juvenile information to media/social media. No explicit appropriation or budget allocation for these duties is specified.
