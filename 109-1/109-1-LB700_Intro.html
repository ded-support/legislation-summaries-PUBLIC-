Summary

- Department of Correctional Services (DCS): The bill preserves court authority to commit an offender to DCS for a comprehensive study prior to sentencing (amend. to 29-2204). It also specifies that a "staff secure juvenile facility" excludes any institution operated by DCS (amend. to 43-245).

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): Peace officers delivering certain juveniles in temporary custody must transfer custody to DHHS, which must place juveniles in the least restrictive environment, supervise placements, and may consent to necessary emergency medical, psychological, or psychiatric treatment; such temporary custody terminates if no court order is issued within 48 hours (amend. to 43-250, 43-253). DHHS may also receive juveniles for mental-health evaluation. Separately, DHHS and the Office of Probation Administration are directed to use day‑reporting and evening‑reporting centers for juveniles and may adopt rules to select, oversee, and reimburse those centers (sec. 12). No specific appropriations or dollar amounts are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Correctional Services: adds a sentencing limit for persons convicted of a Class IB felony who were under 22 at the time of the offense — maximum not greater than 60 years and minimum not greater than 20 years (except as otherwise provided). This alters potential DCS caseload/duration exposure but contains no direct funding or appropriation language.

- Department of Health and Human Services: required, together with the Office of Probation Administration, to use day‑reporting and evening‑reporting centers for juveniles under the Nebraska Juvenile Code; DHHS may adopt rules to select, oversee, and reimburse such centers. Also bars out‑of‑state transfer of adjudicated juveniles by counties or state agencies except for emergency medical or mental‑health treatment. No specific appropriations, budget allocations, or funding amounts are included.
