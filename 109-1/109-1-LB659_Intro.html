Summary

Bill LB659 amends the Election Act to impose procedural and reporting duties on election commissioners and county clerks using vote-counting devices. They must run three independent pre-count accuracy tests (by the commissioner/clerk, a chief deputy or opposite‑party registered voter, and the device installer/operator). Commissioners/clerks must certify test completion dates and provide anticipated completion dates and a written election‑day procedures plan to the Secretary of State (SoS); the SoS must post certifications online. County central committees supply watcher credentials (one per party); watchers/observers must comply with section 32‑1525. The bill also clarifies device security, ballot handling, storage of device media, and procedural limits on early tabulation. No funding or appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds pre-count testing and reporting requirements involving local election officials and the Secretary of State. Election commissioners or county clerks must run at least three independent tests of vote‑counting devices (including the installed counting program) before counting begins, conducted by: (a) the election commissioner/county clerk; (b) the chief deputy or a registered voter of a different party; and (c) the installer or device operator. Each political party may appoint one watcher via the county central committee; other observers only as authorized. Watchers/observers must meet section 32‑1525 rules and cannot be excluded unless an unobstructed CCTV (or similar) view is provided. County officials must certify testing completion (and provide an anticipated completion date) to the Secretary of State, who will post the certification on the Secretary of State website. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
