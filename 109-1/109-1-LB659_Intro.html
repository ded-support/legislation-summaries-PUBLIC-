Summary

LB659 requires election commissioners and county clerks to follow new testing, reporting, and security duties when using vote‑counting devices. Agencies/actors and key duties:

- Election commissioners/county clerks: must run at least three independent pre‑count accuracy tests (by the official, a chief deputy or a registered voter of a different party, and the installer/operator); ensure sealing, transport, storage of ballots and device media; adopt procedures for overvotes/damaged ballots (without judging intent), write‑in counting, ballot pickup/transport from drop boxes, and election‑day counting (no tabulation from picked‑up ballots before one hour prior to poll closing); provide appropriate security personnel.

- County central committees: appoint one watcher per political party; watchers/observers must comply with watcher rules (sec. 32‑1525).

- Secretary of State: receive and post certification of test completion before statewide primaries/general elections, receive anticipated testing completion dates and written election‑day plans (submitted ≥25 days before each election).

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires election commissioners or county clerks to run at least three independent pre-count tests of vote‑counting devices (including the counting software), conducted by: the commissioner/clerk; the chief deputy or a registered voter of a different party; and the installer/operator. County central committees may appoint one watcher per political party; other observers only if authorized; all must meet watcher/observer rules and not be excluded unless an unobstructed CCTV view is provided. Election officials must certify testing completion to the Secretary of State (who must post it online) and notify the Secretary of State of the anticipated completion date. No funding or appropriation changes.
