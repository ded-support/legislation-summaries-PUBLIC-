Summary

LB659 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §§32-101 and 32-1049 to impose new testing, reporting, and procedural duties on election commissioners and county clerks and reporting obligations for the Secretary of State. Key agency provisions:

- Election commissioners/county clerks must run at least three independent pre-count tests of vote-counting devices (by the commissioner/clerk; the chief deputy or a registered voter of a different party; and the program installer/operator). They must secure ballot transport, retain precinct containers after counting, safeguard device media, handle overvotes/damaged ballots without judging intent, count write-ins, and adopt procedures for ballot pickup, drop-box transport, and same-day counting (no tabulation before one hour prior to poll closing).

- Each political party’s county central committee may appoint one watcher; watchers/observers must meet §32-1525 rules and cannot be excluded unless an unobstructed CCTV view is provided.

- Commissioners/clerks must certify and submit testing dates and an anticipated completion date to the Secretary of State; the Secretary of State must post certifications on its website and receive a written election‑day plan 25 days before each election.

No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires election commissioners or county clerks who use vote‑counting devices to run at least three independent pre‑count tests (by the commissioner/clerk, a chief deputy or registered voter of a different party, and the device installer/operator). Allows one party watcher per political party appointed and credentialed by each county central committee; watchers/observers must follow section 32‑1525 and may only be excluded if an unobstructed CCTV (or similar) view is provided. Prior to any statewide primary or general election, the election commissioner or county clerk must certify the testing completion date to the Secretary of State, who must post that certification (and be notified of the anticipated completion date). No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
