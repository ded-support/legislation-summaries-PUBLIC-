Summary

LB533 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act. It defines “clinician‑administered drug” and requires specialty pharmacies that ship such drugs to comply with federal shipping standards (including USP general chapter 800), Drug Supply Chain Security Act track‑and‑trace rules, provide 24/7 access to an employed pharmacist or nurse, require five business‑day delivery notice to providers, and obtain provider signatures on delivery. The bill requires pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) and health carriers to establish appeal/exception processes for mandated specialty‑pharmacy dispensing and prohibits requiring patient pickup for provider administration. The statute identifies “Director” as the Director of Insurance (Nebraska Department of Insurance). Operative date: January 1, 2026. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for specialty pharmacies and obligations for pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) and health carriers. Key government references: requires compliance with federal drug-shipment law including United States Pharmacopeia general chapter 800 and the federal Drug Supply Chain Security Act (21 U.S.C. 360eee et seq.) (track-and-trace). Requirements include 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse, honoring refill requests per PBM/health carrier utilization procedures, five business‑day advance delivery notice and signature on receipt for shipments to providers, and adherence to DSCSA for compounded/manipulated clinician‑administered drugs. PBMs/health carriers must provide an appeal/exception process for delivery failures or urgent one‑time use. Prohibits forcing pharmacies to send clinician‑administered drugs to patients for patient transport to providers. No funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
