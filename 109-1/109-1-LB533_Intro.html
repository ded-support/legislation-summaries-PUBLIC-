Summary

LB533 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act. It defines "clinician-administered drug" and requires specialty pharmacies that ship such drugs to comply with federal requirements (including USP general chapter 800 and the Drug Supply Chain Security Act), provide 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse, accept refill requests per PBM/health carrier utilization procedures, and obtain signature on deliveries to health care providers. Requesting providers must give five business days' notice of delivery. PBMs and health carriers must establish an appeal/exception process when delivery commitments fail or immediate one-time use is needed. The bill designates "Director" as the Director of Insurance and becomes operative January 1, 2026. No funding or appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and rules for "clinician‑administered drug" and "specialty pharmacy." Specialty pharmacies that ship these drugs must follow federal drug‑shipment rules (including USP chapter 800), provide 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse, accept refill requests per PBM/health carrier procedures, and follow DSCSA track‑and‑trace rules when compounding. Providers must give delivery date/time/place at least five business days ahead; shipments to providers require signature. PBMs and health carriers that mandate use of a specialty pharmacy must provide an appeal/exception process when delivery fails or immediate, irreparable harm would result without a one‑time use of an in‑stock drug. PBMs/health carriers may not require specialty pharmacies to dispense clinician‑administered drugs to patients for patient transport to providers. No specific government agencies are newly created or funded; federal statutes (USP standards and the Drug Supply Chain Security Act) are referenced. No funding or appropriation changes.
