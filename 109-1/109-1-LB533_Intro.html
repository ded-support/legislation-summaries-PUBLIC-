Summary

LB533 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act (sections 44-4601 and 44-4603) and adds operational rules for specialty pharmacies handling clinician‑administered drugs. It defines “clinician‑administered drug” and “specialty pharmacy,” and assigns regulatory terms (including Director meaning the Director of Insurance). Requirements: specialty pharmacies must follow federal shipping laws (including USP general chapter 800), provide 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse, accept refill requests per PBM/health carrier procedures, and follow DSCSA track‑and‑trace for compounded/manipulated drugs. When a PBM or health carrier selects a specialty pharmacy, providers must give five business days’ delivery notice; shipments to providers require signature. PBMs/health carriers must maintain appeal/exception processes and may not force patient pickup for provider administration. Operative January 1, 2026. No funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds rules for specialty pharmacies dispensing clinician‑administered outpatient drugs, requiring compliance with applicable federal drug‑shipment laws (including United States Pharmacopeia General Chapter 800) and the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (21 U.S.C. 360eee et seq.) for track‑and‑trace of compounded or manipulated products. Specialty pharmacies must provide 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse, accept refill requests per payer utilization procedures, obtain five business days' notice of delivery details and a signature on delivery to providers, and not ship drugs to patients for them to transport to providers. Pharmacy benefit managers/health carriers must publish an appeal/exception process for delivery failures or urgent one‑time use. No agencies are newly created and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
