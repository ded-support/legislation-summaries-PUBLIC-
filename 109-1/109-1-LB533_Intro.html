Summary

Bill LB533 amends the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Licensure and Regulation Act (effective Jan. 1, 2026). It: (1) adds a defined term "clinician‑administered drug"; (2) identifies the Director of Insurance as the statute's regulatory officer; (3) imposes requirements on specialty pharmacies that ship clinician‑administered drugs — compliance with federal law including USP <800>, Drug Supply Chain Security Act trace/track rules for compounded drugs, 24/7 access to an employed pharmacist or nurse, allowance for PBM/insurer refill requests, five-business-day provider delivery notice, and signature upon provider receipt; (4) requires PBMs and health carriers to establish an appeal/exception process when mandated specialty pharmacy dispensing fails or immediate one‑time administration is needed; and (5) prohibits directing specialty pharmacies to dispense to patients for patient transport to providers. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and rules for "clinician‑administered drugs" and "specialty pharmacies." Key federal references: requires compliance with federal drug‑shipment standards including United States Pharmacopeia General Chapter 800 and the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (21 U.S.C. 360eee et seq.). Requirements imposed on specialty pharmacies: 24/7 access to a pharmacist or nurse; compliance with federal track‑and‑trace; ability to accept refill requests per payer utilization procedures; require five business days' advance delivery notice from provider and signature on receipt. Requires pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) and health carriers to publish an appeal/exception process when specialty pharmacy dispensing fails delivery timelines or immediate one‑time use of an in‑stock drug is needed. Prohibits requiring specialty pharmacies to ship clinician‑administered drugs to patients for patient transport to a provider. No government agencies are newly created or funding, appropriations, or budget allocations altered.
