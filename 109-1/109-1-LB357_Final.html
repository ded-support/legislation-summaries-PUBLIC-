Summary

LB357 creates and consolidates regulatory authority in a seven‑member State Racing and Gaming Commission (Governor‑appointed) that selects a Governor‑approved executive director, may hire staff, license racetrack enclosures and gaming operators, adopt rules, levy fines, and require market and socioeconomic studies to be filed with the Governor and the Legislature’s General Affairs Committee. Background checks are conducted by the Nebraska State Patrol with FBI checks at the applicant’s expense. Fiscal changes: establishes/renames the Racetrack Gaming Fund (State Treasurer to transfer the Racing Cash Fund balance); investment by the State Investment Officer with investment earnings credited to the General Fund beginning Oct. 1, 2024; imposes a one‑time $5 million operator license fee (payable over five years), a $50,000 annual review fee, and a 20% annual tax on gross gaming revenue. The bill also narrows equine‑activity immunity, creates liability exceptions for certain negligent or intentional acts, and repeals numerous related statutes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission: defines "licensed racetrack enclosure," will issue racetrack enclosure licenses (separate from horseracing-meet licenses), sets application and renewal fees of $10,000, and three-year license terms; existing horseracing licensees are deemed to hold racetrack enclosure licenses until the commission rules on applications.

- State Treasurer: directed to transfer any money in the Racing and Gaming Commission's Racing Cash Fund on the act's effective date to a new Racetrack Gaming Fund.

- Funding/payment terms: an initial $5 million license fee is referenced with $1 million due each year thereafter until paid; language ties certain license fees and gross tax receipts under specified horseracing statutes to the scheme while excluding taxes under section 2-1208.01 and horseracing administration receipts.

- Nebraska State Patrol and FBI: applicants must provide fingerprints to the Nebraska State Patrol, which submits them to the FBI for national criminal-history checks and reports results to the commission.
