Summary

The bill substantially restructures oversight of racetrack gaming by expanding the State Racing and Gaming Commission’s licensing, enforcement, staffing, and rulemaking authority (including licensing “racetrack enclosures”), requiring fingerprint/FBI checks via the Nebraska State Patrol, mandating six annual Commission meetings, and directing statewide market and socioeconomic studies reported to the General Affairs Committee and the Governor. It creates a $5 million one-time authorized gaming operator license fee per racetrack enclosure (payable $1M/year over five years), allows a $50,000 annual review fee for 20‑year operator licenses, and permits oversight fees to fund regulation. The Racetrack Gaming Fund is expanded to receive license/application fees and certain horseracing receipts; the Racing Cash Fund is eliminated and its balance transferred into the Racetrack Gaming Fund on enactment. The State Treasurer will collect/remit gaming taxes and fines, and investment earnings are credited to the General Fund beginning October 1, 2024.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and licensing rules for "licensed racetrack enclosure," allowing the Nebraska State Fair Board, county fair boards, county agricultural societies, and qualifying non‑profit civic/livestock organizations to apply to the Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission. New application and renewal fees are $10,000; licenses last three years and are distinct from horseracing meet licenses (existing meet licensees are temporarily deemed licensed until the commission acts). Financial changes: the State Treasurer must transfer the Racing and Gaming Commission's Racing Cash Fund balance to a new Racetrack Gaming Fund; language references a multi‑year initial $5 million license payment schedule (with $1 million due annually until paid) and preserves commission collection of specified license fees and gross tax receipts (excluding certain taxes). Background checks: applicants must submit fingerprints to the Nebraska State Patrol, which forwards them to the FBI and reports results to the commission. Definitions for "dollar amount collected" and "gross gaming revenue" are clarified.
