Summary

LB357 significantly expands the State Racing and Gaming Commission's regulatory, licensing, enforcement, and fiscal role. The Commission may issue a new 3‑year "racetrack enclosure" license ($10,000 application/renewal), authorize 20‑year operator licenses subject to a one‑time $5 million fee per enclosure (payable over five years) and an annual $50,000 review fee, levy oversight fees, increase meetings to at least six per year, exercise subpoena/enforcement powers, adopt rules, and commission market/casino/socioeconomic studies (due Jan 1, 2025 and every five years). A 20% annual gaming tax on gross gaming revenue is remitted monthly; delinquency penalties apply and certain reporting failures can be felonies. Nebraska State Patrol and FBI will perform fingerprinting and national background checks (applicant‑paid). Financial flows: license fees/fines go to a Racetrack Gaming Fund, Racing Cash Fund monies transfer into that fund, and investment earnings are credited to the General Fund beginning Oct. 1, 2024. The bill also narrows equine‑activity immunity by enumerating five exceptions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds licensing and regulatory provisions involving the Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission, Nebraska State Fair Board, county fair boards, county agricultural societies, and civic corporations that may apply for a racetrack enclosure license. Key funding/fee changes: $10,000 application and $10,000 renewal fees for a three-year racetrack enclosure license; an initial $5 million license fee is referenced with $1 million due each year thereafter until paid. The State Treasurer must transfer any money in the Racing and Gaming Commission’s Racing Cash Fund to a new Racetrack Gaming Fund. Definitions added for “dollar amount collected,” “gross gaming revenue,” and “promotional gaming credit.” Background checks: applicants must provide fingerprints to the Nebraska State Patrol, which will submit them to the FBI and report results to the commission.
