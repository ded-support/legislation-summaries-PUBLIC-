Summary

Creates an Office of Grants in the executive branch presided over by a Governor‑appointed Director of Grants (serve at the Governor’s pleasure; nationwide search; minimum ten years’ federal grant experience). The Director may hire staff as funding allows. The office will identify national (including federal) grant programs and assist state agencies, political subdivisions, and in‑state private organizations with identification, applications, and compliance. By July 1, 2026, and annually thereafter, the office must produce and update a statewide plan to maximize national grant funding and submit an annual electronic report to the Legislature summarizing the plan, legislative impacts on grantability, entities served, and awards and dollar totals secured. No specific appropriations or funding amounts are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates an Office of Grants in the executive branch, led by a Director appointed by the Governor (serving at pleasure) after a nationwide search; Director must have ≥10 years’ federal grant experience. The Director may appoint staff “for which adequate funding is available.” The Office will identify national (including federal) grant programs and assist state agencies, political subdivisions, and in‑state private organizations with identification, applications, and compliance. By July 1, 2026, it must produce (and update annually) a statewide plan to maximize grant receipts. Annual electronic reports to the Legislature (due July 1) must summarize the plan, analyze impacts of recent state legislation on grant eligibility, and report entities served and awards secured (number and dollar amounts). No specific appropriations or budget allocations are included.
