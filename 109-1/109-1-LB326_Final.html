Summary

LB326 explicitly defines "Department" as the Department of Insurance and "Director" as the Director of Insurance. It expands the Director's investigatory and enforcement role (including hearings, orders, penalties) and makes failure to respond to a written inquiry from the department within 15 working days an unfair trade practice. Health insurance lead generators and certain insurers must reimburse the Department for examination expenses per the Insurers Examination Act. The Director must transmit covered insolvency claims to the Nebraska Property and Liability Insurance Guaranty Association and will receive recovered sums from the association to handle per Chapter 77 procedures. Mutual insurance holding companies must file annual statements (by March 1) and audits (by June 1) with the Director. The bill preserves a 20% premium-tax-offset mechanism tied to director-prescribed certificates of contribution.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions expand definitions and regulatory authority overseen by the Department of Insurance (the director): they define “customer,” “health insurance lead generator,” “lead generating device,” and “recording,” require health insurance lead generators to keep books, records and complete call recordings accessible for director examination (retain current year plus two prior years), and authorize enforcement by the director. Changes to the insurer/association law: broaden the definition of covered claims to include claims after policy transfers/assumptions (with conditions), limit association liability for cybersecurity coverage to $300,000 per single insured event, and give the guaranty association rights to review/contest pre‑liquidation settlements, assert statutory defenses/offsets, and seek to set aside judgments entered by default or failure to defend. Confidentiality agreements with rehabilitators/liquidators are required. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
