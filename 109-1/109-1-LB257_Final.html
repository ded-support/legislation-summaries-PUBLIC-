Summary

LB257 makes credentialing and licensing changes and assigns duties to several state agencies and boards.

- Department (with board recommendation): May issue licenses/certificates to marriage and family therapists based on out-of-state licensure (requires Nebraska jurisprudence exam); expands and clarifies occupational therapy definitions, scope (new modality definitions), supervision rules, certification for dry needling, and training/examination authority for modalities administered by therapists and assistants.

- Department of Health and Human Services (referred to as department in Child Care Licensing Act): Requires written proof of liability insurance ($100,000 per occurrence) before licensing and continuous maintenance; inspectors must verify insurance at unannounced inspections and may suspend licenses if coverage lapses (3 business-day cure). Department may seek federal funds to subsidize fingerprinting/national criminal history checks; otherwise applicants/staff bear costs. Background checks (FBI via Nebraska State Patrol, NSOPR, state registries) are required and portable; employers with disqualified staff are ineligible for licensing or subsidy.

- Nebraska State Patrol: Conducts fingerprint transmission to FBI.

- State Fire Marshal: May inspect programs for fire safety at the department's request.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures for licensing and certification administered by "the department, with the recommendation of the board": recognition of out‑of‑state marriage and family therapist licensure (requiring a valid unrestricted license, application/fees, and passing the Nebraska jurisprudence exam); issuance of certificates for occupational therapists to use dry needling after board‑approved training; and board approval of examinations and training for physical‑agent and instrument‑assisted modalities. Defines supervision responsibilities for occupational therapists, assistants, and aides, and restricts delegation of evaluations, treatment planning, and certain modalities. Strengthens enforcement: inspectors must verify liability insurance, the department must notify licensees of lapses, and may suspend licenses if proof is not provided within three business days (see §71‑1911.03). No funding or appropriation changes.
