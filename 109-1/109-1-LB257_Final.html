Summary

This bill amends licensing and oversight duties for the department (licensing authority) and various credentialing boards, and involves the State Fire Marshal, Nebraska State Patrol, the FBI, and local environmental health personnel. Key agency provisions:

- Department and boards: expand licensure-by-credential to allow marriage and family therapists licensure/certification based on other-jurisdiction licenses; revise occupational therapy definitions, scope (new modality definitions), supervision rules, and board authority to set training/exam requirements (including dry needling certification).

- Child Care Licensing Act: requires written proof of liability insurance ($100,000 per occurrence) before licensure; inspectors (department or delegated local/city/county personnel) must verify coverage at unannounced inspections and may suspend licenses if coverage lapses (three business‑day cure). Department may request State Fire Marshal fire inspections.

- Background checks: Nebraska State Patrol transmits fingerprints to the FBI for national checks; department may seek federal funds to offset costs; checks are portable. No appropriations or budget allocations are changed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds multiple licensing and oversight duties for "the department" (with the recommendation of "the board"): it must issue mental health practitioner or independent mental health practitioner licenses and certificates to marriage and family therapists licensed in other jurisdictions who meet qualifications (valid unrestricted out‑of‑state license, application/fees, Nebraska jurisprudence exam). It requires the department to issue a certificate for occupational therapists to perform dry needling after board‑approved training, and sets board approval/ examination requirements for physical‑agent and instrument‑assisted modalities and assistant supervision. Inspectors acting for the department must verify required liability insurance at unannounced inspections and investigations; failure to provide proof within three business days triggers license suspension until the department verifies coverage. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
