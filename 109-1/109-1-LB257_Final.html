Summary

LB257 directs multiple state agencies and clarifies departmental duties:

- Department (with the appropriate professional board) must issue licenses/certificates based on licensure in other jurisdictions for marriage and family therapists and continues oversight of occupational therapy licensure, scope (including new modality definitions), training/certification (including dry needling), and supervision standards.

- Department of Health and Human Services–type responsibilities expanded for child care: applicants must provide proof of liability insurance to the department before licensure; inspectors must verify insurance at unannounced inspections and may suspend licenses if coverage is not reinstated within three business days.

- Department may request State Fire Marshal fire-safety inspections, delegate environmental health inspections to local personnel, and rely on city/village/county inspections per statute.

- Nebraska State Patrol must transmit fingerprints to the FBI and report results to the department. The department may seek federal funds to offset fingerprinting costs and adopt implementing rules. No direct appropriations or new budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds multiple licensure and practice provisions directing "the department," acting with "the board," to: issue marriage-and-family-therapist licenses or certificates based on valid out‑of‑state licensure (app, fees, Nebraska jurisprudence exam); grant occupational therapist certification for dry needling after board‑approved training; and set competency, supervision, and delegation limits for occupational therapists, assistants, and aides (including allowed physical‑agent/instrument‑assisted modalities and prohibitions on assistants using dry needling/diathermy). Inspectors (within the department) must verify required liability insurance at unannounced inspections and investigations; failure to supply proof within three business days results in license suspension until verified under section 71‑1911.03. No specific funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are mentioned.
