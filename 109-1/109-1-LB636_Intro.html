Summary

LB636 allows counties to seek state reimbursement for medical services provided to county jail inmates. It assigns the Jail Standards Board authority to receive and determine eligibility of county reimbursement claims (certified by county boards), require supporting information, audit county records (at least every two years), promulgate rules, and request warrants from the State Treasurer to pay approved amounts to county treasurers (credited to county general funds). Claims must be filed within 45 days after each quarter-end; claims caused by jail or employee negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct are ineligible. The Legislature caps annual appropriations for the Jail Standards Board’s eligible criminal court cost reimbursement at $21 million beginning FY2025-26. The bill also amends sheriffs’ fee provisions (33-117) and distress-warrant fee/commission rules (77-1720), including mileage and minimum charges, and takes effect immediately due to an emergency clause.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural and funding rules centering on the Jail Standards Board and county officials. Key points:

- Counties must submit certified reimbursement claims to the Jail Standards Board within 45 days after each quarterly period; late claims are ineligible.

- If approved, the Jail Standards Board requests a warrant from the State Treasurer to pay the county treasurer; amounts credit the county general fund.

- The Jail Standards Board has sole authority to determine eligible medical costs, may obtain additional information, audit county records at least biennially, promulgate rules, and will not reimburse costs caused by jail/county negligence or misconduct.

- Beginning FY2025‑26, legislative appropriations for the Jail Standards Board’s eligible criminal court cost reimbursements are capped at $25 million annually.

- County sheriffs may charge fees for additional officers serving a warrant (minimum $10), not to exceed existing rate limits.
