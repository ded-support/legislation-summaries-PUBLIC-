Summary

LB636 authorizes counties to seek state reimbursement for medical services provided to county jail inmates. The Jail Standards Board is designated to administer the program: it will set eligibility, accept county board‑certified quarterly claims (within 45 days of quarter end), audit county records (at least biennially), request additional information, adopt rules, and deny reimbursement for costs resulting from county/jail employee negligence or misconduct. If approved, the board requests a warrant from the State Treasurer payable to the county treasurer and credited to the county general fund. The Legislature caps annual appropriations for the Jail Standards Board's eligible criminal court cost reimbursements at $21 million beginning FY2025‑26. The bill also revises sheriffs’ fee/mileage provisions (minimum $10 charges, prorated mileage, ability to charge for additional officers) and updates collection/reporting duties to county treasurers and county boards. An emergency clause makes the act effective on passage.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures and limits for county reimbursement claims administered by the Jail Standards Board. Key agencies: Jail Standards Board, State Treasurer, county boards/treasurers, county sheriffs, and the Legislature. New/changed provisions:

- Claims must be certified by county boards and filed with the Jail Standards Board within 45 days after each quarterly period (Mar 31, Jun 30, Sep 30, Dec 31); late claims are ineligible.

- If approved, the Board requests a warrant from the State Treasurer to pay the county treasurer, credited to the county general fund.

- The Board has sole authority to determine reimbursable medical costs, may audit county records (at least once every two years), may request additional information, and may adopt rules; it will not reimburse costs caused by jail or staff negligence.

- Funding cap: beginning FY2025–26 and thereafter, legislative appropriations to the Jail Standards Board for eligible criminal court cost reimbursement shall not exceed $21,000,000 annually.

- Allows county sheriffs to charge fees for additional officers when safety requires (subject to existing rate limits).
