Summary

LB636 authorizes counties to seek state reimbursement for medical services provided to county jail inmates and assigns administration to the Jail Standards Board. The Board must: receive certified quarterly claims (within 45 days after each quarter), determine eligible medical costs, request warrants from the State Treasurer to pay county treasurers (credited to county general funds), audit county records (at least biennially or as needed), request additional information, adopt rules, and deny reimbursement for costs caused by jail or employee negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct. The bill revises sheriff fee and mileage provisions (fees remitted to county treasurers and certain constable fees to county court clerks) and updates distress-warrant fees/commissions for sheriffs. It caps annual Legislature-approved appropriations to the Jail Standards Board for eligible criminal court cost reimbursement at $21 million beginning FY2025‑26. The act takes effect immediately under an emergency clause.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Agencies/departments: Jail Standards Board, State Treasurer, county board, county treasurer, county sheriff, Legislature.

- Funding/appropriations: Beginning FY2025‑26, the Legislature’s total annual appropriations to the Jail Standards Board for eligible criminal court cost reimbursements are capped at $21 million per year.

- Administrative/financial process and authorities: County boards certify claims; the Jail Standards Board reviews, may request additional information, has sole authority to determine eligible medical costs, may audit county records (at least once every two years), adopt rules, and deny reimbursement for costs caused by negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct. If approved, the Board requests a warrant from the State Treasurer and payment is credited to the county general fund.

- Other: County sheriffs may charge a fee for additional officers when safety requires (not to exceed specified rates).
