Summary

Bill LB661 assigns and clarifies duties for state investment bodies and officials:

- Nebraska Investment Council: appointed members are fiduciaries for investments of Public Employees Retirement Board systems, the Nebraska College Savings Plan, the ABLE program, and state funds; prohibited from investing retirement-system assets in defined "restricted entities" (largely specified China-related lists) and must divest holdings tied to such entities once the State Treasurer publishes the list.

- State investment officer: subject to the same prohibition and divestment duty; must file monthly pooled-account operation reports to the Governor, State Treasurer, Auditor of Public Accounts, Department of Administrative Services, and the council.

- State Treasurer: must create and publish the restricted-entity list by Oct. 1, 2025, and update it at least semiannually.

- Legislature: semiannual electronic reports to the Nebraska Retirement Systems Committee and Clerk identifying each restricted entity holding, asset class, and amount; first due Jan. 15, 2026.

No funding or appropriations are changed; original statutory sections are repealed. An emergency clause makes the act effective on passage.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds investment prohibitions, reporting, and list‑creation duties affecting state retirement investments. Key agencies/departments:

- State Treasurer (or designee): must create and publish a “restricted entities” list by Oct 1, 2025, and update it at least semiannually.

- Public Employees Retirement Board / state investment officer: prohibited from investing in restricted entities; must identify and divest retirement holdings in such entities as soon as practicable.

- Nebraska Retirement Systems Committee of the Legislature and Clerk of the Legislature: receive semiannual electronic reports from the state investment officer (first due Jan 15, 2026, then semiannually).

- Federal agencies and lists referenced as bases for restriction: Office of Foreign Assets Control (U.S. Dept. of the Treasury), U.S. Dept. of Defense, Federal Communications Commission, U.S. Dept. of Homeland Security, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (plus referenced Presidential orders).

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are added.
