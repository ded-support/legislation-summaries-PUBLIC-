Summary

LB352 amends §79‑760.05, directing the State Board of Education and the State Department of Education to implement statewide student‑achievement and student‑discipline tracking systems using the department’s student identifier. The bill requires a public, de‑identified, searchable discipline database and obligates school districts to report individual‑student incidents (suspensions, expulsions, alternative placements, restraints/seclusion, offenses triggering long‑term discipline, and law‑enforcement/SRO involvement). Districts must designate discipline data coordinators and publish contact information. The Department of Education must annually analyze and electronically report aggregated and disaggregated data to the Governor, Legislature, school districts, ESUs, and learning communities; reports must include progress indicators for achievement and reduced discipline for vulnerable groups. The Commissioner of Education may enforce noncompliance under accreditation rules. No appropriations or funding changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for the State Board of Education and the Department of Education to create and publish a de‑identified, searchable public database of student discipline data (searchable by district, school, offense, discipline type, school resource officer/law‑enforcement involvement, restraints, seclusion, etc.). The Board must require each school district to designate a discipline‑data coordinator and publish that contact on the district website; the department must post the report electronically on its website. Data must be disaggregated by demographics, discipline types, and academic/progress indicators and must not identify individual students. The Commissioner of Education may notify noncompliant districts, allow time to comply, and impose remedial action (including accrediting sanctions) for intentional, material refusal. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes are specified.
