Summary

The bill assigns several adoption-related duties to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): it requires preplacement/postplacement adoptive home studies to be completed by DHHS or a licensed child-placement agency (with DHHS authority to set and, in individual cases, waive fees); directs petitioners to request DHHS to check the central child-abuse registry as part of background checks; requires adoptive-home-study rules and inclusion of national criminal-history and central-registry checks per DHHS regulations; mandates that original medical histories be forwarded to DHHS after an adoption decree; and requires DHHS to provide adoptive petitioners access to the department’s case file for children committed to DHHS and to file verification with the court. No new appropriations, budget allocations, or other funding changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Health and Human Services focus):

- Adds/clarifies a "sole legal parent" definition and permits a second adult to adopt if the sole legal parent consents, the parent-child relationship exists, and an adoptive home study is completed.

- Provides that if the sole legal parent adopted the child less than six months before the second adult's petition and the second adult was included in the prior home study, a new adoptive home study is not required unless the court orders one; the court may still order a home study or background check if in the child's best interest.

- Clarifies consent form content and confirms both parents remain liable for support after such adoptions.

No provisions alter DHHS statutory duties, nor are there any new funding, appropriation, or budget allocations for DHHS in the added text.
