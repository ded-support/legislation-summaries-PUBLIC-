Summary

LB70 assigns several procedural and administrative responsibilities to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) in adoption proceedings. DHHS (or a licensed child‑placement agency) must conduct required preplacement or postplacement adoptive home studies per department rules, including national criminal history and central‑registry checks; the department sets fees for home studies and may waive costs. Judges may direct petitioners to request DHHS to check the child‑abuse central registry. DHHS provides the medical history form to be filed with adoption reports and must be sent the original medical history after decree. For children committed to DHHS, prospective adoptive parents must be given the opportunity to read the department’s case file; DHHS must exclude legally protected material, provide a signed verification of access for filing with the court, and allow postfinalization review upon written request. No appropriations or budget allocations are specified.

Summary (Department of Health and Human Services relevance):

The underlined additions clarify who may be adopted by a "second adult person" (minors and adult children, including those with a sole legal parent), set consent and petition requirements, and require an adoptive home study except in limited circumstances (no new study if the sole legal parent adopted the child under six months earlier and the second adult was included in that prior study, unless the court orders otherwise). Courts may order new home studies or background checks as needed. The text limits adoptions so a person cannot have more than two legal parents and requires a spouse to join when applicable. It also addresses parental support liability.
