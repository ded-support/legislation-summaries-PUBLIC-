Summary

LB302 ceases Nebraska’s observance of daylight saving time (effective only after Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming enact similar laws) and harmonizes statutory time references. Key agency/departments and administrative effects:

- Governor: retains discretion to extend annual leave in special cases.

- Legislative Council / Executive Board: exercises that discretion for legislative employees.

- Director of Personnel: required to adopt and promulgate rules to administer vacation leave provisions.

- Employing agencies: must provide reasonable opportunity to use accrued vacation; if a reasonable written request to use leave is denied and the leave is forfeited, the agency must pay the cash equivalent within 30 days.

- Public Employees Retirement Board: cash payments for denied-forfeited leave count as compensation only for defined contribution or cash-balance retirement plans.

No appropriations, budget allocations, or other funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a provision that Nebraska’s standard time follows the Uniform Time Act of 1966 for both central and mountain zones but that the state will not observe daylight saving time and is exempt from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a) (the federal DST advancement provision). The subsection becomes operative on January 1 following the date when Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming each enact similar laws. The provision is void if subsection (2) of §49-1301 becomes operative. No government agencies or departments are newly created or mentioned, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
