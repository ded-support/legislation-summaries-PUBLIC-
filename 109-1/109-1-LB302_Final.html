Summary

LB302 removes Nebraska's observation of daylight saving time (seeking exemption from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a)) but delays that change until Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming enact similar laws. It clarifies election administration times (polls and ballot receipt deadlines) by time zone and reiterates responsibilities of election judges and clerks.

Agency-specific effects:

- Director of Personnel: required to adopt rules to administer state vacation leave.

- Employing agencies and agency heads: must provide opportunity to use accrued vacation; if a reasonable request to avoid forfeiture is denied, the agency must pay cash equivalent within 30 days.

- Governor (or Executive Board of the Legislative Council for legislative employees): may extend leave in special cases.

- Public Employees Retirement Board: cash payments for denied/forfeited leave count as compensation for defined contribution or cash-balance plans only.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds language making Nebraska’s official time follow the Uniform Time Act of 1966 in both its central and mountain time zones but exempting the state from observing daylight saving time (referencing 15 U.S.C. 260a(a)). The exemption becomes operative on the January 1 after Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota and Wyoming each enact laws to likewise not observe daylight saving time and claim the same federal exemption. The provision is nullified if subsection (2) of Nebraska §49-1301 becomes operative. References are to the State of Nebraska and federal statute; no state agencies or departments are newly created or mentioned, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
