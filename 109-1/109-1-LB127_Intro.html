Summary

LB127 expands remedies for persons against whom criminal charges are dismissed, result in acquittal, or whose convictions are overturned by adding statutory damages and procedural rules. It defines "prosecutor" to include the Attorney General, deputy and assistant attorneys general, county attorneys and deputies, city attorneys, and others with authority to prosecute. Trial courts must hold hearings to determine damages and may award reasonable attorney fees, litigation expenses (including appeals and postconviction actions), and actual damages (e.g., lost wages). The bill excludes recovery when the defendant is convicted of another offense arising from substantially the same misconduct, allows defendants to waive damages in dismissal agreements with prosecutors, and retains absolute discharge under timed trials (amending and repealing the prior §29-1208). No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a wrongful-prosecution remedy naming who counts as a “prosecutor”: the Attorney General and deputy/assistant AGs, county attorneys and deputies, city attorneys, and any other persons authorized to prosecute. It requires trial courts, after specified dismissals/acquittals or postconviction relief, to hold hearings and award reasonable attorney fees and litigation expenses (including appeals/postconviction work) and actual damages (e.g., lost wages). It permits defendants to waive damages in a dismissal agreement with a prosecutor and excludes relief when the defendant is convicted of a separate, same-or-higher-level offense arising from substantially the same misconduct. No changes to agency funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
