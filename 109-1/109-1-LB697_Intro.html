Summary

LB697 amends the Pharmacy Practice Act. Key agency-related changes:

- Board of Pharmacy: composition set at eight members — five actively practicing pharmacists (at least one hospital pharmacist), one pharmacy technician, and two public members.

- Compounding: persons authorized to compound must follow USP–NF chapters 795 and 797 as of Jan 1, 2023, and may compound only for a practitioner’s medical order, research/teaching (not for sale), or office use by an outsourcing facility operating under 21 U.S.C. §353b or Neb. §71-470. Hospital compounding permitted across health system affiliates. Reconstitution and beyond-use dating must follow manufacturer labeling or USP–NF. Compounding prohibited for FDA-withdrawn drugs, essentially copied drugs absent shortage/allergy, or products designated by FDA/Board as noncompoundable.

- Delegated dispensing permits for public health clinics: allow non-pharmacist dispensing without onsite pharmacist under detailed conditions (initial dispensing rules, formulary limits, labeling and documentation requirements, director-approved patient info, and mandatory availability of a delegating/on-call pharmacist).

No funding or appropriation changes. Original sections repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that two members must be either from an "outsourcing facility" operating under federal law (21 U.S.C. §353b) or under state §71‑470, or be Nebraska‑licensed practitioners affiliated with a public health clinic holding a delegated dispensing permit. Agencies implicated: federal oversight of outsourcing facilities under 21 U.S.C. §353b, state regulatory authority tied to §71‑470, and public health clinics subject to state permitting for delegated dispensing.
