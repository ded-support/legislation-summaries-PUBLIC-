Summary

The bill makes the Department of Revenue (via the Tax Commissioner) administrator of a new statewide consumption tax: registering sellers; collecting, auditing and enforcing liabilities (summonses, liens, seizures, garnishments); designating tax collectors and requiring surety bonds for large sellers; developing tax‑exempt certificates (rules by 9/1/2025; electronic and paper applications available by 10/1/2027); publishing forms and operating a fraud hotline/award program; promulgating remittance, recordkeeping and transitional inventory-credit rules. The Department must maintain employee continuing education, produce an annual report to the Legislature beginning 2029, and may not use assessed tax amounts to evaluate staff. All tax proceeds are remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund. Tax‑collection agreements are limited to finally assessed (not appealed) taxes and 12‑month terms; interest accrues from entry of the final assessment. Disclosure of tax information outside the Department is criminalized (Class IV felony) with limited enumerated exceptions. The Department of Health and Human Services must certify developmental‑disability eligibility for homestead exemptions on forms prescribed by the Department of Revenue. No explicit appropriations or new budget allocations to either agency are specified.

Department of Revenue

- Becomes the primary collector/administrator of a new 7.5% statewide consumption tax effective 1/1/2028 (replacing income, sales, property, motor vehicle, and inheritance taxes).

- Required to collect proceeds and remit them to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund; State Treasurer disburses per the state's budget; Department of Administrative Services will administer agency-level consumption tax receipts.

- New operational duties: create online forms and a tax‑exempt certificate program (rules by 9/1/2025; application available by 10/1/2027); maintain continuing education; publish applicable interest rates; run hotline/whistleblower award program; require receipts; conduct audits, summonses, liens, garnishments and criminal/ civil penalties; establish large‑seller weekly remittance and security rules; permit limited installment agreements; annual report to the Legislature starting 2029.

- Administrative limits/benefits: cannot use assessed tax amounts to evaluate employees; specific penalty‑abatement provisions and confidentiality/disclosure rules.
