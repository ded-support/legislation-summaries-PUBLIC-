Summary

The bill assigns administration of the new Nebraska EPIC consumption tax to the Department of Revenue (via the Tax Commissioner), including registration, collection, reporting, audits, enforcement (seizure, garnishment, liens), penalty assessments, and rulemaking. Income, sales/use, property, motor vehicle, and inheritance taxes are repealed effective Dec. 31, 2027, though the Department may still collect taxes due from 2027 during calendar year 2028. The Department must adopt tax‑exempt certificate rules (by Sept. 1, 2025), provide electronic/paper applications (by Oct. 1, 2027), post forms online, remit proceeds to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund, and produce annual reports to the Legislature beginning 2029. Short‑term collection agreements, strict confidentiality (unauthorized disclosure a Class IV felony), specified disclosure exceptions, de‑identified research reporting, and interest calculation rules are established. DHHS must certify homestead exemption eligibility for persons with developmental disabilities using forms prescribed by the Department of Revenue. No direct appropriations or budget reallocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Revenue (Tax Commissioner)

- Major new role: implement and administer a statewide 7.5% consumption tax beginning Jan 1, 2028 (collection, registration of sellers, audits, enforcement, penalties, liens, seizures, criminal referrals).

- Revenue flow: DOR collects consumption taxes and remits proceeds to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund. State Treasurer disburses revenue; Department of Administrative Services administers/disburses consumption-tax revenue to state agencies. Political‑subdivision distributions handled by the State Treasurer per sections 41–57.

- Operational duties: create tax‑exempt certificate rules (by 9/1/2025) and application (by 10/1/2027), provide forms online, maintain recordkeeping, hotline, whistleblower award program, continuing education for staff, weekly remittance rules for large sellers, and transitional inventory credit administration.

- Limits/controls: DOR may not use assessed tax amounts to evaluate employee performance; must abate certain penalties for agency errors; confidentiality and disclosure rules updated.
