Summary

LB493 adopts the Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (an anti‑SLAPP framework), eliminates prior provisions relating to actions involving public petition and participation, and repeals Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 25-21,241 through 25-21,246. The bill vests Nebraska courts with new procedures: a defendant may file a special motion to dismiss within 60 days of service; filing triggers an automatic stay of related proceedings (with limited discovery allowed for good cause); courts must hear the motion within 60 days (or later for permitted discovery/good cause) and rule within 60 days after the hearing. A party may appeal an adverse order as of right within 21 days. Courts must award costs, reasonable attorney fees, and litigation expenses to a prevailing moving party. The act is declared an emergency. No appropriations or agency budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural rules for special motions under the Uniform Public Expression Protection Act: filing such a motion stays other proceedings (including discovery and hearings) with narrow exceptions (limited discovery where necessary, injunctions for imminent public health/safety, motions for fees). Courts must hear the motion within 60 days (subject to limited extensions). Establishes standards for dismissal with prejudice, effects of voluntary dismissals on fee awards, a 21‑day appeal filing deadline, and mandatory award of costs, reasonable attorney fees, and litigation expenses to a prevailing moving party. No government agencies, departments, or changes to funding/appropriations are mentioned.
