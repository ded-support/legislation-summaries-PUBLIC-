Summary

LB256 adopts the Midwest Interstate Passenger Rail Compact and creates a multistate commission to coordinate and advocate for intercity and high‑speed passenger rail. Commission membership involves each Governor (or designee), two state legislators, and a private‑sector appointee; commissioners serve without commission compensation. The commission may hire staff, establish an office, assess dues, accept federal/state/local grants, gifts, and appropriations, and must submit budgets to the officer designated by each party state and report annually to member states’ Governors and legislatures. Financing of commission operations is by equal apportionment among compacting states when legislatures authorize appropriations; the commission may not issue debt. Nebraska is prohibited from participating in or appropriating funds for any compact rail project (including planning) unless authorized by a legislative bill. LB256 creates the Midwest Interstate Passenger Rail Compact Cash Fund to hold gifts, grants, bequests, or legislative transfers; investments managed by the state investment officer under Nebraska investment law.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions limit Nebraska’s authority and create a dedicated fund. Nebraska may not participate in or appropriate funds for any Midwest Interstate Passenger Rail Compact project — including planning — unless the Legislature enacts a specific bill authorizing that project. A "Midwest Interstate Passenger Rail Compact Cash Fund" is created to hold gifts, grants, bequests (federal, state, public, or private) and any transfers by the Legislature. Fund proceeds are to be used for compact passenger-rail initiatives. The state investment officer will invest any available fund balances under the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and the Nebraska State Funds Investment Act. The text also contemplates receipts from federal government, party states, and state or local departments, agencies, or municipalities.
