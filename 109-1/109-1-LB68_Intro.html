Summary

LB68 amends the Medical Assistance Act to require coverage and reimbursement rules for FDA‑approved self‑administered hormonal contraceptives. Insurers (individual/group sickness and accident, hospital/medical/surgical, and self‑funded employer plans to the extent not federally preempted) must reimburse in‑network health care providers or dispensing entities per unit: up to a three‑month supply on the first prescription and up to a twelve‑month supply for subsequent refills. Under the Medical Assistance Program, "the department" is directed to ensure a prescription for a one‑time twelve‑month dispensation. The bill references the federal Food and Drug Administration as the approval authority. No changes to appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds coverage and dispensing requirements for FDA‑approved self‑administered hormonal contraceptives: insurers, hospital/medical policies, and non‑preempted self‑funded employer plans must reimburse in‑network providers per unit — first prescription up to a three‑month supply, subsequent refills up to a twelve‑month supply. It clarifies providers are not required to prescribe 12‑month supplies and plans may not impose cost‑sharing for an alternative method if a patient switches before using the previously dispensed supply. The federal Food and Drug Administration is referenced for product approval. The bill also directs "the department" administering the medical assistance (Medicaid) family planning program to ensure a one‑time dispensation of a 12‑month supply. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are specified.
