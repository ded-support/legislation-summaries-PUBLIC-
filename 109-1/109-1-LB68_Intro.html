Summary

LB68 requires insurers and applicable self‑funded employer plans that cover FDA‑approved, self‑administered hormonal contraceptives to reimburse in‑network providers or dispensing entities on a per‑unit basis: first prescription up to a three‑month supply (if prescribed) and subsequent refills up to a twelve‑month supply (even if the enrollee was not covered at first fill). The bill amends the Medical Assistance Act (section 68‑901) to direct the department administering the medical assistance program to ensure family‑planning services include a one‑time dispensation of a twelve‑month supply when prescribed. It also clarifies that providers are not required to prescribe twelve‑month supplies and plans may not impose cost‑sharing for switching methods before using a dispensed supply. No appropriations or budgetary changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds insurance and Medicaid-family-planning requirements for self‑administered, FDA‑approved hormonal contraceptives. Mentions the federal Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as the approving authority and notes self‑funded employee plans are subject to federal preemption. Insurers must reimburse in‑network providers on a per‑unit basis: first prescription up to a three‑month supply; subsequent refills up to a twelve‑month supply even if the enrollee wasn’t on the plan at first fill. The bill directs “the department” that runs the state medical assistance (Medicaid) program to ensure a one‑time prescription dispensing a twelve‑month supply. No changes to appropriations or funding are included.
