Summary

LB609 creates the Controllable Electronic Record Fraud Prevention Act and assigns enforcement and oversight roles to state entities. The Consumer Protection Division of the Office of the Attorney General must publish model notice language for gift‑card fraud warnings and may issue written citations; repeat violators face civil penalties up to $250 (first citation is a warning). The bill requires controllable electronic record kiosks and receipts to display contact information for relevant state or local law enforcement agencies and government agencies for reporting fraud. An unspecified “department” is given authority to require additional disclosures, request evidence of blockchain‑analytics use, and set other information requirements. The bill also amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §29‑817 to include “controllable electronic records” as property for search‑warrant purposes. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements that controllable electronic record kiosk operators must display and provide contact information for the operator and "relevant state or local law enforcement agency or government agency" for reporting fraud, and that the (unnamed) department may require additional disclosures and request evidence of operators' use of blockchain analytics. Operators must maintain toll-free live customer service hours and a daily-monitored law‑enforcement contact method. Also requires a full‑time compliance officer and written antifraud policies; sets transaction limits and fee caps. The Consumer Protection Division of the Office of the Attorney General must release model notice language for gift‑card fraud warnings, and the Attorney General may issue citations and civil penalties (up to $250 after multiple citations). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
