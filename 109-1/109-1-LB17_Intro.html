Summary

This bill amends the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act to restrict landlord fees and require disclosures related to tenant screening. Government entities and authorities referenced:

- Consumer reporting agencies: landlords must provide copies of any consumer/tenant screening reports obtained, plus the name, address, and phone number of each consumer reporting agency that furnished a report, and notify tenants of their right to access and dispute information held by those agencies.

- Attorney General: enforcement authority alongside private civil actions; violations are treated as violations of section 59-1602 (Consumer Protection Act).

- State and federal credit reporting law references: the Credit Report Protection Act and federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (including cited FCRA provisions) are preserved for limiting rights/duties and liability.

Civil penalties: landlords who knowingly violate the section may incur up to $300 per violation, plus court costs and reasonable attorney fees. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds comprehensive limits on landlord fees and new disclosure and enforcement rules. Key government-related items:

- Requires landlords to provide prospective tenants any consumer or tenant background reports obtained and to identify the consumer reporting agency (name, address, phone).

- Advises tenants of rights to access and dispute information with the consumer reporting/credit reporting agency and references the state Credit Report Protection Act and the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. provisions cited).

- Permits enforcement under the Consumer Protection Act by an aggrieved person or the Attorney General; knowing violations may incur civil penalties up to $300 per violation plus costs and attorney fees.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
