Summary

LB93 amends Nebraska discovery statutes to expand obligations for prosecuting authorities, law enforcement agencies, and courts in criminal cases. Prosecuting attorneys must permit defendants to inspect and copy: law‑enforcement reports related to an investigation; electronic communication devices, computers, and digital information; and disclosure of any prosecution expert’s identity, qualifications, opinions, and bases. Courts may order access and specify time/place/manner for defense testing of physical or digital evidence; they may accept sealed written submissions from prosecutors if witness safety is at risk, preserved for appellate review. The continuing duty to disclose is retained. No agencies receive new funding or appropriations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds disclosure and discovery requirements for prosecutors and courts in criminal (and ordinance) cases where imprisonment is possible. Key points:

- Prosecuting authority must disclose expert witnesses’ names, qualifications, and complete opinions (including bases/reasons).

- Section made subject to the continuing duty of disclosure under §29-1918.

- Courts may order prosecutors to make electronic evidence (devices, computers, digital information) available or allow defendants to perform equivalent testing with their experts.

- Defines “computer” (refers to §28-1343), “digital information” (examples listed), and “electronic communication device” (refers to §28-833).

- Parties must promptly disclose additional discoverable evidence previously requested or court-ordered (per §§29-1912–29-1921).

No government agencies beyond “prosecuting authority” and courts are referenced, and there are no funding or appropriation changes.
