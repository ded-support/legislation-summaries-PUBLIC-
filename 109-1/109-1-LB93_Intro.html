Summary

LB93 amends Nebraska discovery law to expand what prosecuting authorities and law enforcement must disclose to defendants. Key agency-related changes:

- Prosecuting attorneys must disclose expert witnesses’ names, qualifications, and complete opinions (including bases/reasons) (amend. to §29-1912).

- Courts may order prosecuting attorneys to provide access to electronic communication devices, computers, and digital information (or permit defense testing/analysis) when such items or their analyses constitute prosecution evidence; definitions for computer, digital information, and electronic communication device are added (amend. to §29-1913).

- Law enforcement agencies’ reports that directly relate to investigations remain discoverable; newly discovered material subject to prior request or court order must be promptly disclosed under the continuing duty to disclose (§29-1918).

The bill references trial and appellate courts and excludes jailhouse informants (see §§29-4701–29-4706). No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds disclosure and discovery rules focused on digital evidence. Key points:

- Requires the prosecuting authority to disclose any expert witness’s name, qualifications, and a full statement of opinions and bases.

- States the section is subject to the continuing duty of disclosure (sec. 29‑1918).

- Authorizes the trial court to order the prosecuting attorney to make electronic devices, computers, digital information, or related scientific analyses available to the defendant or allow the defendant comparable testing.

- Defines “computer,” “digital information,” and “electronic communication device” (by cross‑reference).

- Requires prompt disclosure of newly discovered evidence if previously requested or court‑ordered.

Agencies mentioned: prosecuting authority/prosecuting attorney and the courts. No funding or appropriation changes.
