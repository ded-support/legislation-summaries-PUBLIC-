Summary

LB616 authorizes local authorities and law enforcement to enforce red‑light violations using automatic license plate reader (ALPR) systems, and expands permitted ALPR uses (outstanding parking/traffic violations, unregistered/uninsured vehicles, equipment or registration violations, vehicles linked to warrants/missing/stolen persons, ongoing criminal investigations). The Department of Motor Vehicles (department/director) retains approval authority over driver's safety training curricula and fees and over registration renewal procedures. For ALPR red‑light enforcement, jurisdictions must offer violators a choice to pay a fee or enter a department‑approved driver's safety training program (the program fee is waived if the violator participates), no points are assessed, and no warrants may issue for nonpayment/nonparticipation. Unpaid fees become a county‑treasurer collection requirement prior to registration renewal; proceeds are allocated to the county, local school system/school district, city, and village in the vehicle’s tax district. Evidence from ALPR use is limited to enforcement proceedings under the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority for local authorities to use automatic license plate reader (ALPR) systems to enforce red‑light violations, subject to specified safeguards: the motorist may pay a fee or enter a driver's safety training program under the pretrial diversion provisions (secs. 29‑3605 to 29‑3609); fees are waived if the motorist completes the program; no points assessed (sec. 60‑4,182); no warrant for nonpayment/nonparticipation. The county treasurer collects unpaid fees as a condition of vehicle registration renewal (sec. 60‑392) and must allocate proceeds to the county, local school system/school district, city, and village in the vehicle's tax district. Evidence from ALPRs is limited to proceedings enforcing this section. Agencies/departments: local authorities, county treasurer, and local government entities/school districts.
