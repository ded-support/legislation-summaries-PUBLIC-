Summary

LB616 authorizes local authorities and law enforcement to enforce red‑light violations using automatic license plate reader (ALPR) systems subject to conditions. The Department (DMV) must approve driver's safety training curricula and fees and promulgate rules. Local authorities must offer violators a choice to pay a civil fee or attend an approved driver's safety training program; if the violator chooses training the program fee is waived. Red‑light violations enforced via ALPR will not generate points under the DMV point system and no warrant may issue for failure to pay or attend. Unpaid fees become a registration renewal block; the county treasurer collects and allocates fee proceeds to the county, local school system, school district, city, and village in the vehicle's tax district. The bill also expands statutorily permitted ALPR uses (e.g., identifying unregistered/uninsured or stolen vehicles) and limits admissibility of ALPR‑derived evidence to enforcement proceedings under the section.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions authorize local authorities to enforce red‑light violations using automatic license plate reader systems if: the alleged violator may pay a fee or enter a driver's safety training program under pretrial diversion (sections 29‑3605 to 29‑3609); the training fee is waived; no DMV points (per §60‑4,182) are assessed; and no warrant may issue for failure to pay or participate. Unpaid fees become a registration renewal hold—must be paid to the county treasurer per §60‑392. The county treasurer must allocate fee proceeds to the county, local school system/school district, city, and village in the vehicle’s tax district. Evidence from ALPRs is limited to enforcement of this section. No statewide appropriation or new agency created.
