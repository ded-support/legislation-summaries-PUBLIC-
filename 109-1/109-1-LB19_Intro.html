Summary

LB19 moves elections for mayor and seven council members in Nebraska cities of the metropolitan class to the statewide election calendar. Beginning with the 2028 statewide primary and general elections, candidates will be nominated at the statewide primary and elected at the statewide general; terms begin the fourth Monday after the election. Officials whose terms began after the May 2025 general election serve until the fourth Monday after the 2028 statewide general and may run in 2028.

The bill harmonizes numerous municipal election provisions to reference statewide primaries/general elections and the Election Act. It repeatedly references local officials and election authorities: city councils, city clerks, county clerks or election commissioners (available to verify petitions), and the canvassing authorities that handle general-election canvass duties. Financial provisions retained/clarified: park bonds—$100,000/year without voter approval; bridge acquisition/revenue bonds—authorization up to $2 million (and additional revenue bonds up to 15% of purchase price for repairs) with limited circumstances allowing the council to dispense with an election. No state agency appropriations or statewide budget allocations are changed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that, notwithstanding local charters or ordinances, cities of the metropolitan class will use the statewide election cycle beginning with the 2028 statewide primary and every four years thereafter: candidates are nominated at the statewide primary and elected at the statewide general; terms begin the fourth Monday after the election. Transitional rule: officers whose term began the fourth Monday after the May 2025 general election will serve until the fourth Monday after the 2028 statewide general and may run for reelection in 2028. Each metropolitan-class city must elect seven city council members per section 32-536, with their terms coinciding with concurrently elected officers. No state agencies or departments are newly created or mentioned, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
