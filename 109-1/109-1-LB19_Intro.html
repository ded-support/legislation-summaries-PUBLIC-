Summary

LB19 amends multiple statutes governing cities of the metropolitan class to shift candidate nomination to the statewide primary and election of the mayor and seven city council members to the statewide general election beginning with the 2028 statewide primary/general cycle. Terms continue to commence the fourth Monday after election; incumbents whose terms began after the May 2025 general election serve until the fourth Monday after the 2028 statewide general and may run in 2028.

The bill harmonizes references throughout law to the statewide primary/general election (Election Act) and updates procedural roles for municipal entities: city councils, mayors, city clerks, county clerks or election commissioners, and canvassing authorities for special, franchise, ordinance, bond, and condemnation questions. It retains existing local bond provisions (annual $100,000 park bonds without voter approval; bridge acquisition/revenue bond limits including a $2 million threshold and up to 15% for repairs). No state appropriations are made.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that, beginning with the 2028 statewide primary and every four years thereafter, candidates for elective office in "cities of the metropolitan class" will be nominated and elected at the statewide primary and general elections, with terms commencing the fourth Monday after the general election. A transitional clause extends terms that began the fourth Monday after the May 2025 general election until the fourth Monday after the 2028 statewide general, and allows those officers to seek reelection in 2028. It also specifies each metropolitan class city shall elect seven council members per section 32-536. Affected entities: city governments and statewide election authorities. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are mentioned.
