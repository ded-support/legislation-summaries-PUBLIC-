Summary

LB222 amends duties and limits on law-enforcement authority under the Nebraska Rules of the Road. It explicitly involves the Superintendent of Law Enforcement and Public Safety and officers of the Nebraska State Patrol (with carrier enforcement officers excepted) and other peace officers. Key changes: peace officers generally may not stop or detain vehicle operators for nonmoving violations, for driving with a suspended/revoked license under specified statutes, or on most misdemeanor warrants (with narrow enumerated exceptions). Stops for equipment violations are limited to defects that relate to the vehicle and pose an objectively reasonable immediate, serious safety threat; officers must log or notify dispatch of the primary reason before stopping and inform the operator on contact. Vehicle searches require a warrant or written, bilingual consent form (explained orally, revocable, right to counsel/interpreter); evidence seized in violation is inadmissible. The act takes effect immediately (emergency clause). No appropriations or funding changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds limits on vehicle stops, detentions, and searches by peace officers. Key agency references: peace officers and dispatch; interpreter services are required where needed. Major provisions:

- Defines “immediate, serious threat” for equipment-failure stops.

- Prohibits stops/detentions for nonmoving violations, certain license suspensions, and many misdemeanor warrants (with listed exceptions), and bars stops where equipment violations do not pose an immediate serious threat to safety.

- Requires officers to log digitally or notify dispatch the primary reason for an equipment-related stop before initiating it, inform the operator on first contact, and restrict questioning to the equipment issue unless plain-view evidence creates separate reasonable suspicion.

- Establishes that an “authorized person” is the vehicle operator and requires a warrant or written consent before vehicle searches. Consent must be preceded by oral explanation, given on a written form (English and Spanish), state voluntariness and right to counsel, allow revocation, and note no punishment for refusal. Evidence obtained in violation is inadmissible.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
