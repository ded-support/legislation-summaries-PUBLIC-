Summary

LB24 amends probation fee and revocation statutes. District and county courts must charge a $30 one‑time administrative enrollment fee and monthly programming fees ($25 for standard probation; $35 for intensive supervision and for participants in non‑probation programs using probation personnel). Clerks remit collected fees to the State Treasurer for credit to the Probation Program Cash Fund. Probationers (including those received under section 29‑2637 or the Interstate Compact for Adult Offender Supervision and participants in qualifying interlocal non‑probation programs) are eligible for fee waivers if previously found indigent or, after hearing, if payment would be an undue hardship. Courts may revoke probation for nonpayment except where the probationer is financially unable. Revocation procedures require clear-and-convincing proof; felony probationers face revocation for substance‑abuse/noncriminal violations only after 90 cumulative days of custodial sanctions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added text lets courts waive fees under §29‑2262(2)(m) and (o) in whole or part for defendants previously found indigent in the case or when payment would cause undue hardship; waivers apply only while the probationer/participant cannot pay monthly probation-programming fees. It also permits extending a probation term, at the joint request of the probationer and the prosecutor, while a revocation motion is pending or until the statutory maximum, so long as the probationer has counsel or makes the request knowingly on the record. Agencies implicated: the courts (judiciary), probation/community corrections, and prosecutors. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
