Summary

LB24 amends two probation statutes. Courts (district/county/sentencing courts) must assess a $30 one-time administrative enrollment fee and monthly programming fees ($25 for standard probation, $35 for intensive supervision or participants in non‑probation programs using probation resources via interlocal agreement). Fees are paid to the clerk of the court and remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the Probation Program Cash Fund; applicable costs may be covered by funds under §29‑2262.07. Courts may waive monthly fees if the offender was previously found indigent or, after hearing, if payment would be an undue hardship. Probation may be revoked for nonpayment except where the offender is financially unable and so found after hearing. For felony probationers, revocation for substance‑abuse or noncriminal violations may be instituted only after 90 days of cumulative custodial sanctions. Probation terms may be jointly extended by probationer and prosecutor pending resolution.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that courts must waive, in whole or in part, the fees listed in subdivisions (2)(m) and (o) of §29‑2262 if the offender was previously found indigent in the same case or, after hearing, if payment would be an undue hardship due to limited income, employment/school status, or physical/mental handicap. The waiver lasts only while the probationer or participant in a non‑probation program is unable to pay the monthly probation programming fee. Also permits the probation term to be extended, at the joint request of the probationer and the prosecutor, while a motion or information to revoke probation is pending (or until the statutory maximum probation period), provided the probationer has counsel or knowingly and voluntarily requests the extension in open court. Agencies referenced: courts, probation (probation departments/programs) and prosecutors. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
