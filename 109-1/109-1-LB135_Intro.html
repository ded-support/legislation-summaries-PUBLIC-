Summary

LB135 standardizes election procedures for school-district and related local fiscal questions and reallocates certain administrative responsibilities among election officials and state fiscal offices.

Key agencies and changes

- Election commissioners / county clerks: must conduct school bond, levy, and budget-exceeding elections under the uniform procedures of §32-559(3); prepare ballots, provide early-voting ballots, count ballots, certify results, and may bill school districts directly for special-election costs. Timing and notice requirements are clarified (e.g., ballots/notice windows).

- School boards / political subdivisions: must file orders/resolutions with the appropriate election commissioner or county clerk; joint entities (and joint public agencies that include school districts or ESUs) cannot issue bonds without voter approval at elections held per §32-559(3); joint entities must give at least 50 days’ notice for such elections.

- County boards / county clerks / election commissioners: retain authority to place levy-exceeding questions on ballots and implement levy changes consistent with election results.

- Property Tax Administrator: receives reports of levy-allocation amounts for joint public agencies.

- Auditor of Public Accounts: receives records when resident meetings approve one-year budget-growth exceptions.

Budget/funding effects

- Clarifies transfer and calculation rules for restricted funds and allowable growth in governmental budgets (§13-519).

- Political subdivisions remain responsible for election costs; allocation and minimum charges are specified; school districts can be billed directly for special elections. No new appropriations are made.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds detailed election procedures for school-district questions, emphasizing roles of local officials. Key provisions:

- Requires school board elections to comply with subsection (3) of §32-559 and sets filing deadlines for ballot certification (March 1 for statewide primary, Sept. 1 for statewide general, eighth Friday before a political‑subdivision general).

- Assigns administration and ballot counting to the election commissioner or county clerk, assisted by two disinterested persons; receiving boards deliver ballots to that official for counting.

- Requires 30‑day first‑class mail notice, publication of a sample ballot 7–15 days before the election, early‑voting ballots available 21 days prior, and cross‑county certification/transfer of registration lists on request.

- Two appointed counters must be paid at least the minimum hourly rate under §48‑1203.

No new appropriations or other funding changes are specified.
