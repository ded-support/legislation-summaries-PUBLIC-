Summary

LB607 designates the Nebraska Department of Environment and Energy as lead agency to implement and enforce extended‑producer‑responsibility and waste‑reduction provisions. The department will promulgate rules (including fees), approve battery stewardship plans, maintain a public program website, audit compliance (appeals to the Environmental Quality Council), and may treat submitted data as confidential or join multistate clearinghouses. Reporting by manufacturers (resin‑by‑resin virgin vs. postconsumer plastic) begins Jan 1, 2027. Civil penalties (initial recycled‑content penalty $0.20/lb; potential price‑differential formula after Jan 1, 2029) are remitted to the State Treasurer; noncompliance can incur up to $1,000/day. Landfill/solid‑waste fees rise to $2.25/ton (2026), $3.25 (2027), $4.25 (2028); revenues split 50/50 between the Integrated Solid Waste Management Cash Fund (spill response, regulation, remediation, monitoring) and the Waste Reduction and Recycling Incentive Fund (department‑administered grants/reimbursements, admin deductions, $1.5M/year scrap‑tire cap through 6/30/2029). The department may set program fees and manufacturer registration fees; act operative Jan 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key agencies: the Department of Environment and Energy (the Department), the Director of Environment and Energy, the Environmental Quality Council, and the State Treasurer.

Major agency roles and funding/budget items:

- The Department is the primary implementing, rulemaking, enforcement, and reporting authority for the battery stewardship, minimum recycled content, and related programs. It must adopt rules, set/allocate fees, host an electronic registration system, post data online, and may join a multistate clearinghouse.

- Fees: battery stewardship organizations pay annual fees set by the Department to cover the Department’s full costs and to allocate costs among organizations. Manufacturers must register annually and pay a $1,000 registration fee (modifiable by the Department).

- Penalties and remittances: civil penalties (various caps, including $1,000/day and up to $10,000/day for repeat violations) and per‑pound recycled‑content penalties (initially $0.20/lb) are collectible and remitted to the State Treasurer per constitutional distribution. Appeals go to the Environmental Quality Council.

- Timelines: Dept. must complete and report an assessment of noncovered batteries (due Oct. 1, 2027).

- Disposal fee schedule added: $2.25 (2026), $3.25 (2027), $4.25 (2028).
