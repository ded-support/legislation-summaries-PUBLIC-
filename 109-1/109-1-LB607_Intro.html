Summary

The bill places primary implementation and enforcement with the Department of Environment and Energy. It establishes the Environmental Stewardship of Batteries Act, Extended Producer Responsibility Data Collection Act, and Minimum Recycled Content Act; the department will adopt rules, set annual fees (including a $1,000 baseline manufacturer registration fee and fees for battery stewardship organizations sufficient to cover department costs), certify third‑party compliance, and impose civil penalties appealable to the Environmental Quality Council (fines remitted to the State Treasurer). Disposal fees at regulated landfills increase to $2.25/ton (1/1/26), $3.25/ton (1/1/27) and $4.25/ton (1/1/28); receipts split 50/50 between the Integrated Solid Waste Management Cash Fund and the Waste Reduction and Recycling Incentive Fund, with up to 5% of prior‑year disposal‑fee revenue available to reimburse political subdivisions for remediation. Manufacturers must report resin‑specific virgin/postconsumer plastic data annually beginning 1/1/27; initial recycled‑content shortfall penalties are $0.20/lb (with a post‑2029 pricing option). The department may join a multistate clearinghouse, administer grants (including up to $1.5M/year for scrap tire projects through 6/30/29), and reimburse its administrative costs from the Waste Reduction and Recycling Incentive Fund; legislative transfers to the General Fund are permitted. The act is operative 1/1/26.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds and centers the Department of Environment and Energy as the implementing agency for multiple new programs. Key duties: adopt rules, approve battery stewardship plans, establish and collect fees to cover the department’s full implementation/enforcement costs, host an online registry of approved producers, create an electronic registration system, and produce reports/assessments (battery end‑of‑life assessment due July 1, 2027; report to Legislature by Oct. 1, 2027). Financial provisions: a graduated disposal fee ($2.25 in 2026; $3.25 in 2027; $4.25 in 2028); annual manufacturer registration fee ($1,000, adjustable); battery stewardship organizations pay annual department fees and must reimburse local governments for demonstrable collection costs; civil penalties specified (e.g., up to $1,000/day, up to $10,000/day for repeated violations; 20¢/lb penalty for recycled‑content shortfalls initially). Penalties remit to the State Treasurer; confidentiality requests go to the Director of Environment and Energy.
