Summary

LB607 makes the Department of Environment and Energy (DENE) the primary implementing, oversight, and enforcement agency for battery stewardship, disposal‑fee changes, and the Minimum Recycled Content/Extended Producer Responsibility Data Collection Acts. DENE may adopt rules, approve stewardship plans, maintain a public producer list, set program fees to cover implementation, assess and collect penalties (battery: up to $1,000/day, $10,000/day for repeat violations; recycled‑content: initial $0.20/lb with a price‑based formula after 1/1/2029), and oversee manufacturer registration/reporting (reports due 1/1/2027; third‑party recycled‑content certification by 2028). Appeals go to the Environmental Quality Council; DENE may recover cleanup costs via civil action in Lancaster County District Court. Disposal fees rise (2026–2028) with revenues split 50/50 to the Integrated Solid Waste Management Cash Fund and the Waste Reduction and Recycling Incentive Fund; political subdivision reimbursements capped at 5% of prior‑year disposal‑fee revenue. DENE may deduct admin costs, award grants (including up to $1.5M/year for scrap tire projects through 6/30/2029), accept gifts, and remit penalties to the State Treasurer. Act operative Jan 1, 2026.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds repeated central roles for the Department of Environment and Energy (and its Director): rulemaking, plan approval, enforcement, fee-setting, reporting, assessments, and posting data online. The department must establish annual fees charged to battery stewardship organizations to fully cover its implementation/enforcement costs and may set/modify a $1,000 annual manufacturer registration fee (waivers for de minimis manufacturers). Battery stewardship organizations must reimburse local governments for demonstrable collection costs and provide free collection materials/training. A disposal-fee schedule is added ($2.25 in 2026; $3.25 in 2027; $4.25 in 2028). Civil penalties (various caps) are assessed by the department and remitted to the State Treasurer; appeals go to the Environmental Quality Council. EPA nonwaste determinations are referenced.
