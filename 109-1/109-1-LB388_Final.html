Summary

LB388 revises procedures for judicial nominating commissions and assigns specific duties to state actors. Key agencies/departments and changes:

- Nebraska Supreme Court / Clerk of the Supreme Court: Supreme Court judge chairs commissions (nonvoting); Clerk will solicit lawyer nominations (including electronically via the Court’s attorney licensure portal), mail or electronically deliver ballots, and oversee elections. Ballots are counted by a board of the Clerk, Secretary of State, and Attorney General (or their alternates).

- Governor: appoints Supreme Court judge members, citizen members, and fills governor-appointed vacancies.

- Nebraska State Bar Association (Executive Council and Executive Director): solicits/adds candidates when nominations are insufficient, designates alternate lawyer members, and fills alternate vacancies upon Clerk notification.

- Secretary of State and Attorney General: serve on the ballot-counting board.

The bill also addresses residency/party-registration requirements, vacancy procedures, and automatic termination for changed residency or party registration. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural rules involving the Nebraska Supreme Court and the judicial nominating process. Key agency mentions and roles:

- Supreme Court: provides an attorney licensure portal for electronic submissions (deadline Oct. 1 of even-numbered years).

- Clerk of the Supreme Court: authorized to fill vacancies by promoting alternates using certified election results when same‑party alternates are unavailable and to notify the Nebraska State Bar Association of needed vacancies/party designations.

- Judicial nominating commissions: vacancy and temporary‑vacancy procedures clarified; service ends once candidate names are sent to the Governor.

- Executive Council of the Nebraska State Bar Association: must designate lawyers to fill vacancies upon Clerk notification.

Also requires lawyer members/alternates to resign before applying for a judgeship and addresses residency-based releases. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
