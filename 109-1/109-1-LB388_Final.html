Summary

LB388 amends statutes governing judicial nominating commissions and assigns specific roles to state officials and agencies. Key agency responsibilities and changes:

- Governor: appoints successor Supreme Court judges on commissions, citizen members, and fills citizen vacancies promptly; notified when additional citizen appointments are needed for quorum.

- Nebraska Supreme Court / Clerk of the Supreme Court: Clerk solicits lawyer nominations (now may use the Court’s attorney licensure portal), mails or electronically delivers ballots, ensures secrecy/validity, and sets ballot counting date; Supreme Court justice chairs each commission (nonvoting) and the designated justice may not be one originally selected from that district.

- Nebraska State Bar Association (Executive Council/Executive Director): nominates additional lawyer candidates if party-specific nominations are insufficient, designates alternate lawyer members upon Clerk notification, and fills alternate vacancies.

- Secretary of State and Attorney General: serve on the ballot-counting board (or their alternates).

Other changes: residency and voter-registration requirements for lawyer members; four-year terms and alternate-member rules; caps on consecutive service (maximum 12 years; over 10 years disqualifies reelection); electronic nomination deadlines (Oct. 1) and Executive Council deadline (Nov. 1); hearing and submission timelines (first public hearing within 60 days; names to Governor within 90–120 days). A change in residency or party registration automatically terminates commission membership.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures involving the Nebraska Supreme Court and the judicial nominating commission, and assigns roles to the Clerk of the Supreme Court and the Executive Council of the Nebraska State Bar Association (NSBA). Key points:

- Notices may be delivered electronically via the Supreme Court’s attorney licensure portal by Oct. 1 of even-numbered years; nominations due by Nov. 1.

- Vacancies on judicial nominating commissions: an alternate lawyer of the same political party fills the unexpired term; if none, a nonpartisan alternate may fill it. If no suitable alternates exist, the Clerk of the Supreme Court uses prior certified election results to promote the highest‑vote alternate who meets constitutional requirements to preserve party balance.

- For unexpired‑term vacancies the Clerk notifies the NSBA Executive Council, which must designate lawyers meeting statutory qualifications.

- Temporary vacancies for meetings are filled under the same rules; interested lawyer members must resign before notice publication to be eligible for nomination.

No funding, appropriations, or budget changes are included.
