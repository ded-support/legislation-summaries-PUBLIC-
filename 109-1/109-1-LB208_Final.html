Summary

The bill centralizes administration with the Department of Revenue (DOR): DOR (and the Tax Commissioner/designee) is defined as "the Department" and charged to prescribe application forms, determine eligibility, approve/certify tax credits, adopt implementing rules, enforce sales/use tax law, participate in the Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement (including certifying service providers/automated systems and joint procurement), notify retailers of local option tax changes, and maintain/post local boundary, rate, and ZIP‑to‑rate/jurisdiction mappings. DOR will host confidential audit work papers on‑premises, act as custodian for secure records, and may provide confidential returns for on‑site municipal review. Funding/credit caps set: food‑donation credits up to $500,000 FY2025‑26 and thereafter; a tax‑credit program $1,000,000 FY2024‑25 and $1,500,000 thereafter; child‑care credits until annual total reaches $15,000,000. The United States Department of Agriculture is referenced for Emergency Food Assistance Program eligibility. The Department of Administrative Services is included among officials covered by taxpayer confidentiality (unauthorized disclosure a Class I misdemeanor). The Department of Motor Vehicles or county treasurer may collect a $5 penalty for specified late filings/remittances.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Revenue

- Model 1 sellers whose sales/use tax functions are performed by a certified service provider (and that provider is paid under the Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement) will not receive collection fees.

- Creates a refundable Child Care Tax Credit per section 77‑7203 for taxpayers who qualify under the federal Child Care Tax Credit rules, effective for taxable years beginning or deemed to begin on or after Jan 1, 2024.

- Tax rate lookup/database language: when a five‑digit ZIP spans multiple taxing jurisdictions, the database must apply the highest combined tax rate (mention of nine‑digit ZIP handling included).

- Annual credit caps: once an annual limit is reached no further credits that fiscal year; claims received the day the limit is exceeded are prorated. Residency requirement affirmed.
