Summary

The bill assigns several duties and funding changes across agencies. Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) is authorized as the Title IV‑E single state agency to contract with the new Juvenile Probation Agency, must apply for IV‑E reimbursement (remitting recoveries to the Juvenile Probation Program Cash Fund), arrange/pay court‑ordered evaluations/placements when insurance is unavailable, accept short‑term juvenile placements, access and (in limited circumstances) release sealed juvenile records, develop transition plans and provide documents/fee waivers for exiting youth, report serious incidents at youth facilities to the Inspector General, and participate in Medicaid waiver priorities. Department of Administrative Services (DAS) will receive semiannual budget reports and may process certified unpaid obligations against the Juvenile Probation Agency; certain FY2025‑26 Supreme Court appropriations/salary limits are nullified and redirected to the new agency. Courts must notify the Department of Motor Vehicles when adjudication or sealing affects licensing; Department of Correctional Services may access sealed records for committed youth. The Departments of Labor, Economic Development, and Correctional Services are named in intent language to coordinate integrated programs with DHHS; legislative intent creates a Juvenile Probation Contractual Services Cash Fund and funds for seven additional intake officers.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (focus on listed agencies):

- Department of Administrative Services (DAS): The Juvenile Probation Administrator must submit electronic reports to the DAS budget division on Jan. 15 and July 15 each fiscal year. The accounting division of DAS will receive certifications of valid encumbrances; any unpaid obligations certified to DAS as of June 30, 2026, shall be paid by the new Juvenile Probation Agency from unexpended program balances. The administrator must prepare and submit budget/appropriation requests.

- Funding/appropriations: State pays agency salaries, travel, drug testing, and electronic-surveillance equipment. Counties initially fund district office maintenance (with inter-county reimbursement rules). A Juvenile Probation Contractual Services Cash Fund is created for payments from local political subdivisions. FY2025‑26 appropriations/salary limits previously assigned to the Supreme Court for certain programs are voided and reallocated to the new Office of Probation Administration.

- Department of Economic Development, Department of Labor, Department of Correctional Services, Department of Health and Human Services, Department of Motor Vehicles: No changes or funding actions referenced.
