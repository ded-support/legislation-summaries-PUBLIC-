Summary

The bill creates a statewide Juvenile Probation Agency and imposes reporting and budget actions involving state agencies. The Juvenile Probation Administrator must submit semiannual reports to the budget division of the Department of Administrative Services; valid encumbrances certified to DAS’s accounting division as of June 30, 2026, will be paid by the Juvenile Probation Agency from existing unexpended appropriations. Certain FY2025–26 appropriations and salary limits enacted for the Supreme Court are nullified and reappropriated to the Office of Probation Administration/Juvenile Probation Agency. DHHS remains the Title IV‑E single state agency, must seek IV‑E reimbursements (remitted to the Juvenile Probation Program Cash Fund), may receive sealed juvenile records for case purposes, provide foster‑care oversight and transition supports, and will transfer some community‑services/parole funds to the new agency. Courts must send adjudication abstracts to the DMV; the DMV, DOL, DCS, and DED are named in related records, employment‑access, and interagency integration provisions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key points affecting agencies and funding:

- Reporting: The Juvenile Probation Administrator must electronically report to the budget division of the Department of Administrative Services (DAS), the Legislative Fiscal Analyst, and the Supreme Court on Jan. 15 and July 15 each fiscal year (case and intake/detention counts).

- Appropriations/Transfers: On and after July 1, 2026, probation positions transfer to the new Juvenile Probation Agency; transferred employees retain state personnel/bargaining rights. Any FY2025‑26 appropriations and salary limits previously enacted to the Supreme Court for specified program classifications are voided and reappropriated to the Office of Probation Administration (new agency).

- Encumbrances/accounting: Valid unpaid obligations certified to the accounting division of DAS will be paid by the Juvenile Probation Agency from unexpended balances in those program classifications.

- Funds/costs: A Juvenile Probation Contractual Services Cash Fund is created; the state pays salaries, travel, drug testing, electronic surveillance equipment, and interpreter costs (via Supreme Court appropriations or other funds).

- Departments of Economic Development, Labor, Correctional Services, Health & Human Services, and Motor Vehicles are not addressed or changed in the added text.
