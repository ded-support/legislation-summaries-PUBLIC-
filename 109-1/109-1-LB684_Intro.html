Summary

The bill establishes a new Juvenile Probation Agency that must prepare a budget, submit appropriation requests, and provide semiannual reports to the Budget Division of the Department of Administrative Services (DAS). Any outstanding Office of Probation Administration encumbrances certified to DAS as of June 30, 2026, shall be paid by the Juvenile Probation Agency from unexpended appropriation balances. The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) retains placement/supervisory responsibility for juveniles, must pay costs for court‑ordered evaluations/placements (unless court orders otherwise), may access sealed juvenile records for statutory purposes, and may contract with the Juvenile Probation Agency as a Title IV‑E surrogate. DHHS must remit Title IV‑E reimbursements to the State Treasurer for credit to the Juvenile Probation Program Cash Fund; expansion of the pilot is funded by transfers from DHHS and the Office of Juvenile Services. Courts must send adjudication abstracts to the Department of Motor Vehicles and notify DMV when sealing affects impoundment or licensing. The Department of Labor must provide an employer-facing link about sealed juvenile records. The bill expresses legislative intent that DHHS, Labor, Correctional Services, and Economic Development coordinate programs; Department of Correctional Services may access sealed juvenile records for committed individuals.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Administrative Services (DAS): The Juvenile Probation Administrator must submit electronic reports to DAS’s budget division on Jan. 15 and July 15 each fiscal year. Any FY2025-26 appropriations/salary limits previously enacted for Supreme Court program classifications are voided and reallocated to the new Office of Probation Administration (Agency No. XXX). Unpaid fiscal 2025-26 obligations certified to the DAS accounting division will be paid by the Juvenile Probation Agency from existing unexpended balances. State pays agency salaries, travel, drug testing, electronic-surveillance equipment, and certain interpreter costs; the administrator prepares the agency budget/appropriation requests.
