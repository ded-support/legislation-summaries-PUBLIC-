Summary

LB615 (Introduced by Sen. J. Cavanaugh) amends Nebraska’s election statute (rev. §49-1401) to define and prohibit deceptive, fraudulent deepfakes of candidates and to require conspicuous disclosures for synthetic media. It authorizes candidates to seek injunctive or other equitable relief through the courts to prohibit publication of prohibited deepfakes. Specified exceptions reference radio/television broadcasters, cable/satellite operators, internet news outlets and periodicals when material is bona fide news, paid broadcasts with good-faith verification, federally required candidate advertising, or satire/parody. The bill cites “federal law” where applicable but does not name or create any state agency or department, nor does it include any changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations. The original §49-1401 is repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions require deceptive audiovisual material to be labeled as "Image," "Video," or "Audio"; visual disclosures must be legible and for videos remain on screen the entire duration; audio disclosures must be clearly read at start, end, and at least every two minutes for recordings longer than two minutes. Candidates harmed by deceptive deepfakes may seek injunctive or equitable relief. Exemptions cover radio/TV broadcasters (including cable/satellite operators, programmers, producers) airing such material as bona fide news coverage or when federal law requires airing candidate ads, paid broadcasts where the broadcaster made a good‑faith authenticity check, regularly published news outlets that disclose inaccuracies, and satire/parody. No specific government agencies or departments are named, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
