Summary

LB368 creates the Nebraska Youth in Care Bill of Rights and prescribes duties for the state child‑welfare "department" (the responsible agency under existing statutes), caseworkers, guardians ad litem, and the courts. Key agency obligations include facilitating sibling placements/visitation, reunification and visitation with biological family, transition planning (per §43‑1311.03), ensuring confidentiality consistent with law, and providing access to birth certificates, SSN, medical/educational records and related documents. Caseworkers must be trained on the rights, provide paper notices and periodic discussions with youth, and explain a grievance process; the department must acknowledge grievances within three business days and inform youth of Administrative Procedure Act review. Guardians ad litem must discuss rights with youth and raise infringements to the court. The Nebraska Strengthening Families Act Committee (§43‑4716) will monitor implementation. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for "the department" (child welfare agency) and related actors: caseworkers must be trained on and provide paper copies of youth rights, discuss them with children (age 14+) at specified intervals (within 72 hours and two weeks of placement, then quarterly), obtain acknowledgments, and offer private, regular grievance instruction. Guardians ad litem must discuss rights within two weeks of appointment and every six months and must raise alleged infringements with the court. The department must facilitate sibling placement/visitation, reunification/visitation as required by law, ensure confidentiality and access to vital records, and acknowledge grievances within three business days with info on the grievance and Administrative Procedure Act review. The Nebraska Strengthening Families Act Committee (per §43-4716) will monitor implementation. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
