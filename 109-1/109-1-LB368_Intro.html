Summary

LB368 creates the Nebraska Youth in Care Bill of Rights and assigns implementation duties to state child‑welfare entities. The bill requires "the department" to: facilitate joint‑sibling placement and visitation, inform children of placement/visitation rights, facilitate reunification and family visitation consistent with law, provide transition planning and access to vital records and medical/educational files, ensure confidentiality, and explain reasons for denied visits/placements. Caseworkers must be trained on and provide written notice of the rights (with specified timing and acknowledgments) and explain a grievance process; the department must acknowledge grievances within three business days. Court‑appointed guardians ad litem must review and discuss rights with children and escalate alleged infringements to the court. The Nebraska Strengthening Families Act Committee will monitor implementation. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for "the department" (child welfare) to facilitate sibling placement/visitation, reunification and family visitation, ensure confidentiality, provide key documents and transition plans, and inform children of their rights. Requires appointment and responsibilities for guardians ad litem (per §§43-272/43-272.01) to discuss rights with children (within two weeks and every six months) and to raise alleged infringements to the court. Requires caseworkers to be trained on and to provide/discuss written youth-in-care rights on specified timelines and to explain a grievance process; the department must acknowledge grievances within three business days and provide Administrative Procedure Act review information. Directs courts to discuss these rights at dispositional, review, and permanency hearings and assigns monitoring to the Nebraska Strengthening Families Act Committee (§43-4716). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
