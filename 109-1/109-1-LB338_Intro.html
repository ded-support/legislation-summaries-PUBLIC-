Summary

LB338 (2025) prohibits life insurers, disability insurers, and long-term care insurers in Nebraska from using genetic information — or soliciting genetic test results or actions related to genetic testing — to deny, limit, or rate coverage or impose preexisting-condition exclusions, except when there is a clinical diagnosis. The bill allows insurers to review medical records as part of an application exam, ask about family history, and consider clinical diagnoses. It cites the federal Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (29 U.S.C. §1182) and relies on Nebraska’s statutory definitions in section 48-236. Referred to the Legislature’s Banking, Commerce and Insurance Committee. No state agencies are newly created or assigned duties, and the bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added findings and protections direct that the Legislature endorses genetic testing benefits and, consistent with the federal Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (29 U.S.C. 1182), bars life, disability, and long‑term care insurers — absent a clinical diagnosis — from canceling, limiting, denying coverage, setting different premium rates, soliciting genetic information, or using genetic test results or testing decisions. Exceptions allow insurers to review medical records, ask about family history, and consider clinical diagnoses. Genetic terms adopt definitions in section 48‑236. No state agencies or departments beyond "the Legislature" are newly created or referenced, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
