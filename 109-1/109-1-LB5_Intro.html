Summary

LB5 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §28-470 to provide immunity related to opioid antagonists. It shields health professionals licensed under the Uniform Credentialing Act (physicians, physician assistants, nurse practitioners, pharmacists) from administrative or criminal action when, with reasonable care, they prescribe, administer, or dispense opioid antagonists. Emergency responders licensed under the Emergency Medical Services Practice Act or via the EMS Licensure Interstate Compact and peace officers, as well as law enforcement employees, are protected from administrative action, criminal prosecution, and personal civil liability (except for willful, wanton, or grossly negligent acts) when they obtain and administer opioid antagonists from their EMS organization or law enforcement agency. Law enforcement agencies and EMS organizations remain potentially liable for their personnel’s acts. Defined agencies include police departments, town marshals, sheriffs’ offices, and the Nebraska State Patrol. No funding, appropriation, or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of "opioid antagonist" to include naloxone hydrochloride or any opioid antagonist approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration for emergency reversal of known or suspected opioid overdose. Mentions the FDA; no agencies are assigned new duties and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
