Summary

LB381 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §68-974 (Medical Assistance Act) to tighten program-integrity requirements for the Department (DHHS) and its contractors and to formalize coordination with the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) and Medicaid managed care organizations. Key provisions: limit claim review to one year from date of payment (except fraud); require written justification to providers to commence audits, 180-day determination letters, 10 business days' notice for onsite audits, and minimum response windows for records; exclude capitated managed‑care claims from recovery-audit scope and require coordination among DHHS, contractors, and MCOs; ban most extrapolated overpayments; require annual contractor performance metrics posted on the department website and a December 1 annual report to the Legislature. No appropriation or budget allocation changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds audit and notice protections tied to "the department" and its contractors (program integrity contractor; audit/recovery contractor). Key agency/contractor requirements:

- The audit-initiating entity must provide clear written justification to the provider before commencing an audit.

- Adjustments to payments barred after one year from payment except for provider fraud.

- Records requests must be limited to documents relevant and proportional to the services audited (per subsection (12)).

- Onsite audits must be scheduled with at least ten business days' advance notice; documentation cannot be required sooner than 90 days after a provider's request.

- Written adverse findings/recoupment notices from the department/contractor must include ten specified items (beneficiary, dates, amount, claim number, detailed basis/evidence, payment method, claims-adjustment procedure, appeal right, recovery method, and a 60-day wait before recoupment).

- Program integrity contractors may not recoup until appeals are exhausted (except credible fraud) and must provide appeal procedures, contact info, and respond to provider inquiries within ten business days.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
