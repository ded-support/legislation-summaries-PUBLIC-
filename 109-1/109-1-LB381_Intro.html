Summary

LB381 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §68-974 to tighten requirements for program integrity and recovery audit contractors working under the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (the department) and in coordination with the federal Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). Contractors must review applicable state statutes/policies with the department at audit commencement, follow department audit procedures, and coordinate audits (scope, method, timing) with the department and managed care organizations for capitated Medicaid claims (which are excluded from recovery-audit scope). The department must publish annual contractor performance metrics on its website and submit an electronic report to the Legislature by December 1 detailing contracts, parties, programs, estimated cost recovery, and savings. Provider appeal and notification timelines are specified. No changes to appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural limits and provider protections for audits involving "the department," "program integrity contractor," and "audit recovery contractor" (and references third‑party payers). Key requirements: a written justification to the provider when commencing an audit; a one‑year lookback for payment adjustments except for fraud; records requests must be relevant and proportional; onsite audits require at least ten business days' notice and a good‑faith scheduling effort; providers get at least 90 days after an onsite audit to furnish requested documents; overpayment notices must list beneficiary, service dates, overpayment amount, claim identifiers, detailed basis and evidence, payment method/date, claims‑adjustment procedure, appeal rights, recovery method, and a 60‑day delay before recoupment. Contracts that conflict with these limits are unenforceable. Program integrity contractors may not recoup until appeals are exhausted (unless credible fraud) and must provide appeal procedures, contact info, and answer provider questions within ten business days. The bill also directs the department to submit an electronic filing (clause incomplete). No funding or appropriation changes are included.
