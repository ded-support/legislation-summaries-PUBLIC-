Summary

- The Department may require an affidavit and any other documentation it deems necessary to substantiate eligibility for the Preceptorship Tax Credit.

- The Department determines application completeness, approves qualifying applications, and certifies the amount of credit approved to the licensed physician.

- The Department may approve up to $1,000,000 in Preceptorship Tax Credits in any single fiscal year.

- The Department may adopt and promulgate rules and regulations to implement the Preceptorship Tax Credit Act.

- Separately, the bill specifies that the $5,000 home-purchase tax credit (extremely blighted areas) is subject to recapture by the Department of Revenue.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added text directs the Department of Revenue to administer the Preceptorship Tax Credit Act: require an affidavit and any other documentation, determine application completeness, approve and certify qualifying credits, and promulgate rules to implement the program. It establishes an annual cap of $1 million in credits the department may approve, gives approval priority to rural preceptors, prorates credits when the cap is reached on the same day, and bars transfer or sale of credits. The change is a tax-credit allocation (a $1 million per‑year limit), not a direct appropriation or new cash funding line.
