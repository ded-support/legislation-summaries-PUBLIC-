Summary

LB216 centralizes district-court clerical functions under the judicial branch and shifts related funding to the State.

Key agency actions and fiscal changes:

- Beginning January 7, 2027, elected clerks of the district court and their employees transfer to state employment under the Nebraska Supreme Court; salaries, benefits, and expenses to be paid by the State with funds appropriated to the Supreme Court. Transferred staff become subject to Supreme Court personnel rules.

- Counties must provide accrual data; counties reimburse the State (and employees) per statutory formulas for transferred sick leave (25% of value) and vacation leave (100% for transferred hours); counties remain owners of furniture/equipment while books/files transfer to the State Court Administrator.

- Retirement transfers handled under existing systems (Retirement System for Nebraska Counties or State Employees Retirement System) with cost‑sharing intent to prevent benefit loss.

- The State will fund specified district/separate juvenile court IT and communications operational costs via the Supreme Court.

- Federal reimbursements for child/spousal/medical support costs received on behalf of clerks are to be remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to Agency 5 – Supreme Court.

- Department of Health and Human Services will receive required court statistical reports; the State Court Administrator negotiates reimbursement agreements on behalf of clerks.

- Oaths filed with the Secretary of State; clerks are prohibited from serving on mental-health boards.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions:

- Effective Jan 7, 2027, elected clerks of the district court and their employees transfer to state employment under the Nebraska Supreme Court; State Court Administrator handles records transfer and appointment/management rules apply for vacancies.

- The State of Nebraska, using funds appropriated to the Supreme Court, will pay all salaries, benefits, and expenses of transferred clerks/employees. Transferred staff keep employment unless removed for cause under Supreme Court personnel rules.

- Counties retain furniture/equipment but must transfer books/files to the State Court Administrator.

- Leave and retirement: accrued sick leave (up to 1,440 hours) transfers; county reimburses state 25% of its value (excess sick reimbursed 25% to employee). Vacation transfer cap 280 hours; county reimburses state 100% of transferred vacation value (excess reimbursed 100% to employee). Retirement-cost sharing and SERS/County Retirement System provisions govern benefit continuity.

- State will pay certain court IT and communications operational costs from Supreme Court appropriations.

- Federal reimbursements for direct costs are to the State Treasurer for credit to Agency 5 – Supreme Court.
