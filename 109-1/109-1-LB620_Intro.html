Summary

LB620 (2025) creates the Neighborhood Revitalization Act, authorizing district courts to bring in rem proceedings to declare deteriorated real property public nuisances, appoint receivers, and enforce abatement plans. Key government actors: district courts (jurisdiction, plan approval, lien establishment, auction oversight), municipal corporations (inspection duties, may be petitioner, authorized to access and secure parcels and charge owners for costs, required to provide periodic updates), county registers' offices/registers of deeds and clerks (recording petitions, liens, and receiver deeds), and taxing authorities (receiver must satisfy delinquent property tax liens within 30 days; receiver's lien is first priority except for general property tax liens per §§77-203, 77-207). Receivers may pay outstanding municipal fines, penalties, and taxes. No line-item appropriations, funding changes, or new state budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures giving courts (district courts) authority to proceed in rem against a “subject parcel” and to appoint receivers. Identifies municipal corporations (cities, towns, villages), county clerks, county register/register of deeds, and courts as key actors: municipal corporations must be notified, may inspect parcels, may access and secure parcels (costs charged to the owner), and must provide periodic updates to the court. Receiver powers include taking possession, paying operating costs, insurance, pre‑receivership mortgages, outstanding municipal fines/assessments and state/local taxes (delinquent property tax liens must be fully satisfied within 30 days). Receiver’s liens are first priority except for general property tax liens (refs. to sections 77‑203, 77‑207). Certification of prospective receivers occurs through the applicable court with financial and organizational documentation. No new appropriations or budget allocations are specified.
