Summary

LB620 creates the Neighborhood Revitalization Act and assigns primary implementation and enforcement roles to Nebraska district courts and local municipal corporations (counties, cities, villages, townships). District courts hear in rem actions, may issue orders of compliance, appoint receivers, establish and enforce a first-priority receiver's lien (subject only to general property tax liens), and supervise receiver auctions consistent with existing sale procedures (sec. 25-1516). Municipal corporations must be notified, may inspect parcels, may secure and maintain properties at their discretion and charge owners for costs, and must provide periodic updates to the court. Receivers must satisfy delinquent property tax liens within 30 days and may pay outstanding municipal fines, taxes, and assessments. Applications to serve as certified receivers are filed with the applicable court. No state appropriations or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds court-led Neighborhood Revitalization procedures that involve local governments and court offices. Key agency/office roles:

- Municipal corporations (cities, counties, townships, villages) must be notified, may inspect parcels, may secure/maintain nuisance properties, and must provide periodic updates to the court; costs incurred may be charged to the property owner.

- District courts have in-rem jurisdiction, may appoint receivers, approve plans, record orders, and supervise auctions.

- County offices (clerk; register's office/register of deeds) must record notices, liens, and orders and handle filings.

- References to state/local tax law (sections 77-203, 77-207) make receiver liens subordinate only to general property tax liens; receivers must satisfy delinquent property tax liens within 30 days.

Funding/appropriations: no new government appropriations or budget allocations are created. The bill shifts costs to owners (municipal cost recovery, receiver liens/fees — 10% of receiver costs, minimum $2,500).
