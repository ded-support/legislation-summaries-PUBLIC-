Summary

LB69 revises statutes governing the Commission on African American Affairs. The Commission (14 members appointed by the Governor) must include an immigrant/new American and a young professional among initial appointees; members may serve no more than two full four‑year terms and must have names posted on the Commission website. The bill clarifies the Commission’s purpose (serving African/Black Americans across the diaspora), authorizes it to receive and administer state, federal, and other funds, employ its own personnel, and requires an office for executive management. Duties explicitly include coordinating programs with other state and federal agencies, keeping the Governor’s office apprised, and expanded reporting items — including the Commission’s budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants received, and public funds allocated. An executive board (chair plus four members) may contract for goods/services up to $2,000 per contract and supervise Commission initiatives.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds initial-appointee requirements: one commissioner must identify as an immigrant/new American and one must be a young professional (18–24). Requires the commission to list all commissioners on its website. Expands the commission's required content/activities to include workforce-development initiatives (scholarships, mentoring, school partnerships), health and wellness programs, criminal justice/public-safety efforts (including police‑community relation improvements and services for formerly incarcerated people), and housing/community development in partnership with housing agencies. Requires metrics/KPIs (employment, graduation, health outcomes, recidivism), public feedback and a community needs assessment, and published future goals/strategic plans. Financial transparency provisions require the commission to disclose its budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants received, and public funds allocated. No new appropriations or agency funding changes are specified.
