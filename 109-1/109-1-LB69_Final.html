Summary

LB69 amends statutes governing the Commission on African American Affairs. Key agency-related changes:

- Commission composition: 14 Governor‑appointed members of African ancestry; one initial appointee must identify as an immigrant or new American and one as a young professional (18–24). Commissioners limited to two full four‑year terms. Names must appear on the commission website.

- Governance: Commission elects its chair; an executive board (chair + four members) may enter contracts up to $2,000 each and supervise commission initiatives.

- Funding/administration: Commission may receive and administer state, federal, and other funds, employ staff and set compensation, and must be provided an office for executive management.

- Duties: Work with state and federal agencies and keep the Governor’s office apprised. Reporting requirements now must include budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants, and public funds allocated.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions require initial commission appointments to include one immigrant/new American and one young professional (18–24). The commission must list all commissioners on its website. New required public content covers program areas (health and wellness; criminal justice/public safety, including police-community relations and services for formerly incarcerated individuals; housing and community development, including partnerships with housing agencies; education/career partnerships with schools), metrics/KPIs, public feedback and community needs assessments, and future strategic goals. Importantly, the commission must publicly disclose financial information — budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants received, and public funds allocated — enhancing transparency but not specifying new appropriations or budget increases.
