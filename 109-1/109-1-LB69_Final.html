Summary

LB69 revises statutes governing the Commission on African American Affairs. The commission is set at 14 governor-appointed members (all of African ancestry), with one initial appointee identifying as an immigrant/new American and one as a young professional (18–24). Members may serve no more than two full four-year terms; commissioners’ names must appear on the commission website. The commission may receive and administer state, federal, and other funds, employ personnel of its choosing, and shall be provided an office for executive management. Duties emphasize coordination with state and federal agencies and elected officials and reporting to the Governor’s office. Reporting requirements must include financial information (budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants, public funds allocated). An executive board (chair + four members) may contract up to $2,000 per contract. Original sections are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds composition and transparency requirements for the commission: one initial appointee must be an immigrant/new American and one a young professional (18–24); all commissioners' names must be posted on the commission website. Expands required public-facing content to include program areas and partnerships (scholarships, mentoring, school partnerships, career advancement), health and wellness programs, criminal justice/public safety initiatives (including police-community relations and services for formerly incarcerated persons), and housing/community development (affordable housing, eviction prevention, partnerships with housing agencies). Requires metrics/KPIs (employment, graduation, health, recidivism), public feedback/community needs assessments, and publication of the commission’s financial information (budget, funding sources, expenditures, grants, public funds). No new appropriations or dollar allocations specified.
