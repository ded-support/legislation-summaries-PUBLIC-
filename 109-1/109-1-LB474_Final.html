Summary

LB474 substantially expands the Department of Banking and Finance’s regulatory, licensing, examination, enforcement, and fee authorities for money transmitters, digital asset depositories, installment loans/sales, and residential mortgages (including use of NMLS, background checks, privacy/breach rules, rulemaking, cease‑and‑desist, fines, and charging examination/investigation costs to licensees). The bill prescribes new fee and bond requirements (including a $50,000 digital‑asset depository charter fee and higher money‑transmitter application/renewal fees), permits annual assessments on regulated institutions, and directs fees and fines to the State Treasurer (crediting the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund or otherwise as required). The Department of Insurance gains flexibility to approve nonstandard policy provisions and must establish, by Jan 1, 2026, a tax (up to 6%) on non‑Medicare HMO direct‑writing premiums, remitted to the Medicaid Access and Quality Fund. DHHS is exempted from offering corrective action plans when a provider is formally restricted and must seek federal approval by Aug 1, 2025 for Medicaid Access and Quality Act funding.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Banking and Finance (DBF)

- Expands DBF authority under a new Nebraska Money Transmitters Act: defines terms, establishes licensing, examination, background-check, net‑worth and surety bond (≥$100k or 100% of average daily liability, cap $500k) requirements, permissible investments, reporting, enforcement (fines up to $5,000/day), multistate supervisory participation, use of Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry, and acceptance of third‑party audits/exams.

- Sets money transmitter fees: $1,500 application; $750 annual renewal.

- Implements parallel provisions for the Nebraska Installment Loan and Sales Act: DBF is the regulator; license/application/branch/renewal fees and surety bond ($50,000) schedules and enforcement powers spelled out (e.g., application fees: sales $150, loan $500; renewal/branch fees listed).

- All license fees/charges credited to the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund; fines remitted to the State Treasurer per Article VII, §5.

Department of Insurance

- Requires policyholders (policies issued/delivered on/after Jan 1, 2026) receive electronic or mailed notice at least 15 days before termination/lapse for nonpayment; assignees get equivalent standing.

Department of Health and Human Services

- No substantive budget, appropriation, or funding changes; only a brief definitional reference to "provider"/visiting faculty permits (section 38‑2045) without funding effects.
