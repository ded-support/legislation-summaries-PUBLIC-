Summary

The bill broadens regulatory, licensing, enforcement, and fiscal authorities across three agencies:

- Department of Banking and Finance: defines the Department/Director, authorizes hiring staff, participation in NMLS, background checks, rules, exams, investigations, cease‑and‑desist orders, and administrative fines (up to $5,000/violation). Establishes extensive license/branch/application/renewal fee schedules (including a $50,000 digital‑asset depository charter fee and various mortgage, installment, and branch fees), surety bond minima ($50,000) with volume‑based increases, cost recovery for exams/investigations, and limits loans under Dept. licenses from statutory interest caps. Collected fees/fines are remitted to the State Treasurer and credited/distributed to the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund or per constitutional distribution rules.

- Department of Insurance: retains Director authority on contestability limits; requires 15‑day electronic/mail notice before lapse for policies issued/delivered on/after Jan 1, 2026; caps retail‑installment insurance charges to filed premium rates and mandates issuance of policy/certificate within 30 days; establishes, by Jan 1, 2026, a tax schedule up to 6% on non‑Medicare HMO direct‑writing premiums, proceeds credited to the Medicaid Access and Quality Fund.

- Department of Health and Human Services: allows immediate termination without a corrective action plan when a provider’s licensing/operations are restricted by DHHS or another agency, and must amend the Medicaid state plan or file federal authorizations by Aug 1, 2025 to obtain federal financial participation for the Medicaid Access and Quality Act.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Banking and Finance — Expands the Nebraska Money Transmitters Act: new/clarified definitions; director authority to use the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System/Registry, multistate supervisory processes, third‑party audits, and shared examinations; background checks/fingerprints; application fee $1,500 and annual renewal $750; acquisition‑of‑control fee $1,500; net‑worth and surety‑bond requirements (bond = greater of $100,000 or 100% of average daily liability up to $500,000); reporting, examination, investment and trust requirements; civil penalties (up to $5,000 per violation/day) and enforcement powers. All fees collected by the department are remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund; fines remitted per the state constitution.

Department of Insurance — New requirement for policies issued/delivered on/after Jan 1, 2026: at least 15‑day electronic or mailed notice to owner and any assignee before termination/lapse for nonpayment; assignees have same notice standing as owners.

Department of Health and Human Services — No substantive programmatic, funding, or appropriation changes identified in the added text.
