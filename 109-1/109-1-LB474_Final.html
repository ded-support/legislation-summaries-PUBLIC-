Summary

LB474 sharply expands roles for three agencies:

- Department of Banking and Finance: centralizes regulation of money transmitters, installment‑loan/sales and digital‑asset depositories—definitional changes; mandatory use of NMLS; background checks/fingerprints; acceptance of third‑party/out‑of‑state exams; rulemaking, cease‑and‑desist, fines (up to $5,000/violation), lien authority, and billing of exam/investigation costs to licensees. Sets new fees/assessments (examples: $50,000 digital‑asset depository charter; $1,500 money‑transmitter application/acquisition fee; $750 annual renewal; $150–$500 installment filing fees) and minimum surety bonds (baseline $50,000). All collected fees/fines remit to the State Treasurer for credit to the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund (fines follow constitutional distribution).

- Department of Insurance: preserves director authority over life/endowment limits; requires ≥15 days’ notice before termination of policies issued/ delivered on/after Jan 1, 2026; limits insurance charges in installment contracts and mandates delivery of policy/certificate within 30 days when holder purchases dual‑interest insurance. Director must set (by Jan 1, 2026) a tax schedule up to 6% on non‑Medicare HMO direct‑writing premiums; proceeds go to the Medicaid Access and Quality Fund.

- Department of Health and Human Services: provider‑corrective‑action exceptions for DHHS‑restricted providers; must amend the Medicaid state plan or seek federal authorization by Aug 1, 2025 to obtain federal financial participation for the Medicaid Access and Quality Act.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Banking and Finance (DBF)

- Expands/clarifies Nebraska Money Transmitters Act and Nebraska Installment Loan & Sales Act: definitions, licensing, examinations, multistate coordination, use of the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry, fingerprint/background checks, surety bond and minimum net‑worth rules, reporting, enforcement (including cease‑and‑desist) and administrative fines (up to $5,000 per violation/day).

- New fees: money transmitter application $1,500; renewal $750. Installment license/application/branch and renewal fees specified (e.g., sales $150, loan $500; branch fees $100–$250; renewal amounts listed). Installment license surety bond $50,000; money‑transmitter bond tied to average daily liability ($100K minimum; up to $500K).

- All department fees and license revenues remit to the State Treasurer for credit to the Financial Institution Assessment Cash Fund; fines remit to the State Treasurer for constitutionally required distribution.

Department of Insurance

- Adds notice requirement for policies issued/delivered on/after Jan 1, 2026: at least 15‑day electronic or mailed notice to owner and assignee before termination/lapse for nonpayment; assignee given owner’s standing for that notice.

Department of Health and Human Services
