Summary

LB604 assigns and clarifies duties and court procedures involving the Secretary of State, the Revisor of Statutes, and Nebraska courts for initiative and referendum petitions. Key provisions:

- Secretary of State: receives filings (sponsor names/addresses), transmits measure to the Revisor, keeps filings confidential five days, posts text/notice when a sample petition is filed, prepares petition form within five business days, and must announce and state reasons if refusing placement on the ballot for legal insufficiency (excluding signature insufficiency); failure to state reasons waives those defenses.

- Revisor of Statutes: reviews and suggests form/draftsmanship changes within ten business days.

- Courts: mandamus suits filed in Lancaster County District Court within ten days of refusal; suits asserting insufficiency advanced expeditiously, with appeal to the Court of Appeals within ten days. If a suit does not involve signature insufficiency, the Nebraska Supreme Court may hear the case in original jurisdiction if practicable. Petition sponsor of record is a required defendant. Parties may raise substantive facial insufficiency.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for the Secretary of State to post the measure text and a notice that it is in circulation on the Secretary of State’s website upon receipt of a sample petition, and to keep that posting up until the petition-filing deadline. If the Secretary of State refuses to place a measure on the ballot for legal insufficiency other than lack of signatures, the Secretary must announce the refusal on the same date as the circulation posting and state the reasons; failing to state reasons waives those defenses. Courts or officials may have a measure declared legally removed if preventing certification/printing is no longer practicable. Lawsuits challenging legal sufficiency (except signature insufficiency) must be filed within 60 days of the circulation posting (or by the petition filing deadline if the 60th day is later); the Nebraska Supreme Court shall hear non‑signature challenges in original jurisdiction if practicable. Parties may raise facial substantive insufficiency at any time; ripeness is not required. No funding or appropriation changes.
