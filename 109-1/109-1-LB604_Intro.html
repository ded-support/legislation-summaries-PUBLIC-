Summary

LB604 amends initiative and referendum procedures, assigning specific duties to state officers and clarifying judicial review.

Key agency actions:

- Secretary of State: must transmit proposed measure to the Revisor of Statutes for a form/draftsmanship review (completed within 10 business days), keep filings confidential for five days, prepare petition forms, post measure text and circulation notice on the Secretary of State website upon receipt of a sample petition, and—if refusing placement for legal insufficiency other than lack of signatures—announce the refusal and state reasons at the time of posting (failure to state reasons waives those defenses).

- Revisor of Statutes: review and recommend form/draftsmanship changes.

Judicial process and jurisdiction:

- Residents may seek mandamus in Lancaster County District Court within 10 days of a refusal announcement.

- Sponsor of record must be named as a defendant in suits to enjoin placement.

- Challenges to legal sufficiency (except signature insufficiency) must be filed within 60 days after posting (or by petition-filing deadline if earlier).

- Lancaster County District Court has original jurisdiction; if the suit does not assert signature insufficiency, the Nebraska Supreme Court may hear the case under original jurisdiction if practicable and on petition. Appeals to the Court of Appeals must be filed within ten days; Administrative Procedure Act appeal procedures do not apply.

Budget/funding changes: none.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties and timelines for the Secretary of State and the Supreme Court: the Secretary of State must post a measure’s text and a circulation notice on the Secretary of State website until the petition-filing deadline and, if refusing to place a measure on the ballot for any legal insufficiency other than lack of signatures, must announce that refusal the same day and state the reason (failure to state a reason waives those defenses). Lawsuits challenging non‑signature legal insufficiencies must be filed within 60 days of the circulation posting (or by close of business on the last petition‑filing day if the 60th day falls after that deadline). If practicable, the Supreme Court shall hear such non‑signature challenges under its original jurisdiction upon petition. Officials or residents may raise substantive facial insufficiency without ripeness dismissal; the section does not limit other post‑enactment suits. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
