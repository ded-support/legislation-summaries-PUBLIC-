Summary

LB604 amends initiative and referendum procedures in Nebraska statutes, reallocating duties among state officers and clarifying judicial review. Key agency provisions:

- Secretary of State: receives sponsor filings and sworn statements; transmits proposed text to the Revisor of Statutes; keeps filings confidential for five days after revisor review; prepares petition form within five business days of final language; posts measure text and circulation notice on the Secretary of State website until the filing deadline; must announce and state reasons when refusing placement on the ballot for non-signature legal insufficiencies.

- Revisor of Statutes: reviews proposed measures and suggests form/draftsmanship changes within ten business days.

- Courts: Lancaster County district court has primary jurisdiction for related litigation; suits challenging placement (non-signature issues) have tightened timing and party-join rules; the Supreme Court may exercise original jurisdiction when practicable. Administrative Procedure Act appeal procedures do not apply.

No funding, appropriation, or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural requirements for the Secretary of State and court review:

- Secretary of State must post the measure text and a notice that it is in circulation on the Secretary of State's website until the petition-filing deadline. If the Secretary of State refuses to place a measure on the ballot for any legal insufficiency other than lack of signatures, the refusal and its reason must be announced the same day as the circulation posting; failure to state the reason waives those defenses.

- Suits alleging legal insufficiency (other than signature insufficiency) must be filed within 60 days of the circulation posting (or by the petition filing deadline if the 60th day falls after that deadline). The Nebraska Supreme Court shall, if practicable and upon petition, hear non‑signature insufficiency cases under its original jurisdiction (see section 24‑204).

- Parties and officials may raise substantive facial insufficiency claims without dismissal for lack of ripeness. The section does not limit other post‑enactment suits.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
