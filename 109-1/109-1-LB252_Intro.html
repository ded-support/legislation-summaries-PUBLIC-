Summary

The bill amends the Medical Assistance Act (section 68-901) to add a new requirement for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). DHHS must ensure its Medicaid formulary and preferred drug list do not disadvantage or discourage FDA‑approved non‑opioid pain drugs relative to opioids — including prohibiting designation of a non‑opioid as nonpreferred when an opioid is preferred and barring more restrictive utilization controls (e.g., prior authorization, step therapy) for non‑opioids than the least restrictive controls for opioids. The requirement applies immediately upon FDA approval and to drugs provided under DHHS contracts with managed care organizations. The bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the Department of Health and Human Services, when setting and maintaining the Medicaid formulary and preferred drug list, to ensure any FDA‑approved non‑opioid pain treatment is not disadvantaged versus opioids. Impermissible actions include designating a non‑opioid as nonpreferred while an opioid is preferred and imposing more restrictive utilization controls (e.g., prior authorization, step therapy) on a non‑opioid than the least restrictive controls applied to an opioid. The rule takes effect for a non‑opioid immediately upon FDA approval and covers drugs provided under DHHS contracts with managed care organizations. It does not prevent preferring one opioid over another or one non‑opioid over another. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
