Summary

LB252 directs the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), when establishing and maintaining the Medicaid formulary and preferred drug list, to ensure FDA‑approved non‑opioid pain drugs are not disadvantaged or discouraged relative to opioids. Prohibited actions include designating a non‑opioid as nonpreferred if an opioid is preferred and imposing more restrictive utilization controls (e.g., prior authorization or step therapy) on non‑opioids than on opioids. The requirement applies immediately upon FDA approval and covers drugs provided under DHHS contracts with managed care organizations. No changes to DHHS funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are contained in the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the Department of Health and Human Services, when setting and maintaining the Medicaid formulary and preferred drug list, to ensure FDA‑approved non‑opioid pain treatments are not disadvantaged or discouraged compared with opioids. Prohibited actions include labeling a non‑opioid as nonpreferred when an opioid is preferred and imposing more restrictive utilization controls (e.g., prior authorization, step therapy) on non‑opioids than the least restrictive controls applied to opioids. The rule allows intra‑class preferences (opioid vs opioid, non‑opioid vs non‑opioid). It takes effect for a non‑opioid immediately upon FDA approval and applies to drugs provided under DHHS contracts with managed care organizations. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
