Summary

The bill makes multiple provisions referencing the Department of Economic Development (DED) and (federally) the Department of Labor's Bureau of Labor Statistics:

- DED: may not impose additional program requirements on Affordable Housing Trust Fund recipients; will fund applications in priority order using available funds less administrative costs; may contract or provide funds to other governmental entities; may recapture misused or unspent funds and credit recaptures to the Trust Fund; must include detailed Affordable Housing Trust Fund project, budget, and planned-distribution information in its annual status report; establish and administer the Middle Income Workforce Housing Investment Fund (including a subaccount for Economic Recovery Act land parcel preparation); grant caps ($10M per grantee over two years), matching-fund requirements (reduced from 50% to 25% for awards on/after July 19, 2024), return of unengaged grants after 24 months, and transfer of fund balances to the General Fund beginning July 1, 2029; nonprofits must file annual reports to DED.

- Department of Labor (BLS): DED shall annually update certain housing cost/value limits using the Producer Price Index published by the U.S. Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Economic Development

- New Affordable Housing Trust Fund provisions: DED cannot force new construction or rental-conversion projects receiving Trust Fund dollars to meet requirements/applications for other DED programs or unduly restrict construction/sales on certain funded parcels.

- Enforcement tools expanded: DED may disqualify applicants (up to 24 months) or take other remedial actions for misuse of funds (excluding criminal fraud/theft which remain prosecutable).

- Recapture rules: upon project noncompletion DED must recapture a pro rata share of awards (credited back to the Affordable Housing Trust Fund). Recapture also applies to homebuyer assistance when a home is sold (from net proceeds).

- New program definitions and rent‑to‑own rules (homeownership incentive reserve account, project reserve account, purchase-option timing and tenant protections).
