Summary

LB440 creates and funds the Education Leave and Support Act. Key agencies and fiscal actions:

- State Department of Education: administers the State Education Leave Fund and Education Retention Fund, develops application/review/disbursement procedures, and must submit an annual report on expenditures, utilization, and balances.

- State Treasurer: receives quarterly remittances of a 0.35% payroll fee on taxable wages of certificated teachers (beginning Jan 1, 2026) collected by districts and an employer match equal to total employee-collected fees; credits receipts to the State Education Leave Fund and transfers any surplus over 20% of projected annual needs to the Education Retention Fund on department notification.

- State Investment Officer: invests fund balances per state law.

- Auditor of Public Accounts: conducts annual audit of the State Education Leave Fund.

Funding use: reimburse districts (beginning July 1, 2026) for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of an employee's FMLA leave (capped at actual daily cost). Misused funds must be repaid and incur department penalties.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a State Education Leave Fund, administered by the State Department of Education, funded by a new 0.35% payroll fee on covered teachers' taxable wages effective Jan. 1, 2026. Employers must collect the fee from employees, remit it quarterly to the State Treasurer, and remit an equal employer match; funds are invested by the state investment officer per existing state investment law. Beginning July 1, 2026, the fund reimburses school districts for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of a teacher's FMLA leave (not to exceed actual daily substitute cost as determined by the department); reimbursed teachers need not use accrued personal/sick leave for that period. The department will set application, review, and disbursement procedures and file annual reports to the Legislature. Surplus (>20% of projected needs) may be transferred to a new Education Retention Fund for shortages and professional development. Misuse requires repayment and department-prescribed penalties; the fund is audited annually by the Auditor of Public Accounts.
