Summary

LB440 (Education Leave and Support Act) assigns administration to the State Department of Education and creates two state funds managed with investments by the state investment officer under applicable state investment acts. Beginning Jan. 1, 2026, a 0.35% payroll fee on certificated teachers’ taxable wages (collected quarterly from employees) plus an equal employer match is remitted to the State Treasurer and credited to the State Education Leave Fund. The department will reimburse school districts (starting July 1, 2026) for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of a teacher’s FMLA leave, up to actual daily costs as determined by the department; reimbursed teachers need not use accrued leave during that period. Excess (>20% of projected needs) is transferred by the State Treasurer to a new Education Retention Fund for shortages and professional development. The department must adopt procedures, report annually to the Legislature, and funds are audited by the Auditor of Public Accounts.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a new payroll-funded State Education Leave Fund administered by the State Department of Education (the department). Beginning Jan 1, 2026, a 0.35% payroll fee will be withheld from covered teachers’ taxable wages; school districts must collect it and remit quarterly to the State Treasurer and also remit an equal employer match. Receipts are credited to the State Education Leave Fund, invested by the state investment officer under existing state investment acts. Funds reimburse districts (beginning July 1, 2026) for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of a teacher’s FMLA leave (capped at actual daily cost as set by the department); affected teachers need not use accrued leave for those six weeks. The department must adopt application/disbursement procedures and report annually to the Legislature. Surplus funds (>20% of projected needs) are transferred by the State Treasurer to a new Education Retention Fund, also administered by the department, to address shortages and professional development. Misuse requires repayment and penalties; the Auditor of Public Accounts must audit the Leave Fund annually.
