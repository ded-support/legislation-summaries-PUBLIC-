Summary

Creates the Education Leave and Support Act and establishes the State Education Leave Fund and Education Retention Fund, both administered by the State Department of Education. Beginning Jan. 1, 2026, a 0.35% payroll fee on certificated teachers' taxable wages is collected from employees, remitted quarterly to the State Treasurer, and matched by employers; all receipts are credited to the State Education Leave Fund. The fund, investable by the state investment officer under existing state investment law, reimburses school districts (beginning July 1, 2026) for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of a teacher's FMLA leave; reimbursements capped at actual daily substitute cost as determined by the department. Surplus (>20% of projected annual needs) may be transferred by the State Treasurer to the Education Retention Fund for shortages and professional development. The department must report annually to the Legislature; funds are auditable by the Auditor of Public Accounts; misuse triggers repayment and departmental penalties.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a new revenue stream and two state funds administered by the State Department of Education. Beginning Jan 1, 2026, a 0.35% payroll fee on certificated teachers' wages is collected by school districts and remitted quarterly to the State Treasurer; employers must remit an equal matching amount. Proceeds are credited to the newly created State Education Leave Fund, administered by the Department of Education and invested by the state investment officer under existing investment laws. The fund (reimbursements begin July 1, 2026) reimburses districts for substitute-teacher costs for the first six weeks of a teacher's FMLA leave, up to actual daily cost as set by the department; reimbursed teachers need not use sick/personal leave. The Department must report annually to the Legislature. Excess (>20% of projected needs) may be transferred by the Treasurer to an Education Retention Fund (also DOE‑administered) for addressing shortages and professional development. The Auditor of Public Accounts will audit the Education Leave Fund; misuse by districts triggers repayment and departmental penalties.
