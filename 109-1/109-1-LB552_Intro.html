Summary

LB552 prohibits public postsecondary educational institutions governed by the Board of Regents of the University of Nebraska, the Board of Trustees of the Nebraska State Colleges, and community college governing boards from maintaining a “diversity, equity, and inclusion” (DEI) office or requiring or spending public money on DEI programs as defined in the bill. The measure defines prohibited activities (hiring influence, differential treatment, race-/gender‑referenced policies/programs) and allows exceptions: sexual‑harassment training; attorney‑staffed compliance offices; grant/accreditation statements highlighting first‑generation, low‑income, or underserved student support or certifying nondiscrimination; demographic data collection; scholarly research/art; recognized student organization activities; and short‑term guest speakers. Employees compelled to attend DEI programs may sue for injunctive relief. No new appropriations or positive budget allocations are included; the bill restricts use of public funds for DEI.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions and restrictions applying to public postsecondary institutions governed by the Board of Regents (University of Nebraska), the Board of Trustees (Nebraska State Colleges), and community college governing boards. Defines "diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) office" and "DEI program" broadly to include units or trainings that influence hiring, provide differential treatment, implement race/gender‑referenced policies, or require employee participation. Prohibits requiring DEI participation as a condition of enrollment or institutional functions and bars spending public money on DEI programs or offices. Carves out exceptions (e.g., sexual‑harassment training; legal compliance offices staffed by attorneys; grant/accreditation statements; demographic data collection; scholarly research; student organization activities; short‑term guest speakers). Allows employees compelled to attend a DEI program to sue the institution for injunctive relief. No specific new appropriations or budget allocations are made; the change limits use of public funds for DEI.
