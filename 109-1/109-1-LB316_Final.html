Summary

The bill references the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) in definitions (including "USDA-licensed hemp producer"). It defines "Department" in the Uniform Controlled Substances Act as the Department of Health and Human Services. The Department of Revenue is assigned administration of a new excise tax: beginning January 1, 2026, a 10% excise tax on retail cannabidiol products; retail sellers must maintain electronic sales records as prescribed by the Department of Revenue, file monthly returns and remit tax by the 20th day following each reporting month, and may be required to file and pay electronically. Collected tax receipts are to be remitted monthly to the State Treasurer for credit to the Property Tax Credit Cash Fund. No appropriations or agency budget increases are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions:

- Definitions: "Cannabidiol product" defined as a finished hemp consumer product with CBD as the primary ingredient, meeting THC limits and excluding cannabinoids produced by chemical conversion/synthesis (e.g., hexahydrocannabinol).

- Excise tax (Dept. of Revenue): Beginning Jan 1, 2026 a 10% excise tax on retail sales of cannabidiol products. Tax is additional to other state/local taxes.

- Compliance/administration (Dept. of Revenue): Retailers must keep electronic sales records as prescribed by the Department of Revenue, provide them on request, file monthly returns by the 20th day following the month, and remit tax. The Department of Revenue will collect and remit receipts at least monthly to the State Treasurer for deposit to the Property Tax Credit Cash Fund.

Agency impacts/funding: No explicit changes to appropriations or funding for the Department of Agriculture or Department of Health and Human Services are included; duties and revenue collection are assigned to the Department of Revenue.
