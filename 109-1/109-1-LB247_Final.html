Summary

LB247 amends statutes administered by the Department of Environment and Energy. It prescribes disposal-fee distribution: 65% to the Integrated Solid Waste Management Cash Fund (for the department’s spill/ emergency response, regulation, investigation, remediation and CERCLA nonfederal cost‑share/operation and maintenance) and 35% to the Waste Reduction and Recycling Incentive Fund (for grants to counties/municipalities and reimbursements to political subdivisions for roadside/dump-site remediation). Reimbursement to political subdivisions is capped at 5% of prior-year disposal-fee revenue; the department director must consider geographic distribution. The bill clarifies that the State Treasurer credits these funds and allows the department to seek cost recovery in Lancaster County. It revises the Petroleum Release Remedial Action Cash Fund administration (revenues: statutory fees, settlements, gifts/grants) and authorized expenditures, including State Fire Marshal fee‑collection costs, claims approved by the State Claims Board, spill response, and up to $1.5 million per fiscal year through June 30, 2025 for CERCLA cost‑share/O&M. Operative July 1, 2025; emergency clause included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority to provide cost‑share, operation, and maintenance support for remediation of Superfund sites to cover nonfederal costs (including in‑kind services) under the federal Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (42 U.S.C. 9601 et seq.). Effective through June 30, 2025. No specific agency is named in the added text (it invokes the federal CERCLA statute, under which EPA typically acts), and no explicit appropriation or dollar amount is specified.
