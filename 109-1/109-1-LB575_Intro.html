Summary

LB575 amends procedures under the Property Tax Request Act (section 77‑1633) and property tax levy limits (section 77‑3442). Key agency duties and fiscal effects:

- County clerks must organize a joint public hearing (held 7:00 p.m., on/after July 14 and before July 24) for political subdivisions seeking increases above allowable growth; county clerks must notify county assessors and participants by July 4. Political subdivisions must electronically send required information to county assessors by July 4. Postcards with tax estimates must be printed and initially paid from the county general fund and mailed at least seven days before the hearing; mailing costs are later charged proportionately to participating subdivisions based on parcel counts.

- Adds a new statutory levy constraint: when a political subdivision’s total taxable valuation rises, its levy is limited to the rate that would raise the identical dollar amount of property tax as the prior year; exceeding that limit requires a two‑thirds affirmative vote of the governing body.

- References affected: county assessor, county clerk, county board, county general fund, Department of Natural Resources, Division of Aeronautics (Dept. of Transportation), school districts, community colleges, natural resources districts, educational service units, regional metropolitan transit authority.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds subsection (12), which limits property tax levies for political subdivisions: if a political subdivision's total taxable valuation rises year‑to‑year, its levy may not be increased beyond the amount needed to raise the identical dollar amount of property tax revenue as the prior year. An override is allowed only by an affirmative two‑thirds vote of the subdivision's governing body. The provision is subject to an exception in section 77‑3444. No specific state agencies or departments are named, and there are no direct appropriations or line‑item funding changes; the change constrains local governments' ability to increase property tax revenue when valuations grow.
