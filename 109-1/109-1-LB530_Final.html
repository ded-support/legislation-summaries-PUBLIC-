Summary

The bill makes the following agency-specific changes:

- Department of Correctional Services (DOCS): prohibits placing juveniles in DOCS secure youth confinement facilities and excludes DOCS institutions from the “staff secure juvenile facility” definition; requires inmates sentenced to DOCS convicted under controlled-substance provisions to receive drug treatment/counseling; DOCS may access sealed juvenile records for individuals committed to or under its care.

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): permits peace officers to deliver juveniles taken into custody for certain noncriminal matters to DHHS for temporary placement, supervision, and emergency medical/behavioral consent (custody ends if no court order within 48 hours); courts must notify DHHS when DHHS was a party or the juvenile was a ward; DHHS may access and disclose sealed juvenile records as needed for intake, case planning, evaluations, reporting, supervision, and licensing.

- Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV): courts must transmit abstracts of juvenile convictions/adjudications affecting driving privileges and notify the DMV when sealed records include impoundment or prohibitions on obtaining licenses/permits.

- Department of Transportation (DOT): authorized to temporarily reduce posted maximum speed limits under specified conditions, require electronic/digital signage and notices, adopt rules, and maintain related policies and records.

- Department of Labor: must provide a website link informing employers they may not ask about sealed records and ensure employment applications state applicants need not disclose sealed records.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Labor: No substantive changes or funding/appropriation items affecting the department.

Department of Correctional Services: Electronic monitoring tampering is added as a Class I misdemeanor and applies to devices required by court order or as a condition of parole — DOCS roles are implicated but no new duties or funding are specified.

Department of Health and Human Services: No substantive changes or funding/appropriation items affecting the department.

Department of Motor Vehicles: Courts may order driver bans and suspend (up to 2 years) or revoke (15 years) operator's licenses for convictions involving tampering with electronic monitoring devices; orders are to be administered at sentencing/final judgment/probation revocation. No new DMV funding is specified.

Department of Transportation: New authority to temporarily reduce posted speed limits for weather, traction, emergencies or congestion, effective only when shown on electronic/digital signs; reductions in 5 mph increments (policy may allow different increments), no minimum speed while reduced, recordkeeping and a requirement to develop policy and adopt rules. No appropriations or budget allocations included.
