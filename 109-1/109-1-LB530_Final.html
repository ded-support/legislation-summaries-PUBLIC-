Summary

LB530 makes these agency-specific changes:

- Department of Correctional Services: excludes DOC institutions from the definition of “staff secure juvenile facility,” bars placement/assignment of specified juveniles in DOC secure youth confinement, allows DOC access to sealed records for committed/placed individuals, and requires defendants sentenced to DOC to attend substance‑abuse treatment.

- Department of Health and Human Services: authorizes peace officers to deliver certain juveniles to DHHS for temporary placement, supervision, and consent to emergency medical/psychological/psychiatric care (custody ends if no court order within 48 hours); requires DHHS notification for sealed records involving a ward or DHHS and permits DHHS access/release of sealed juvenile records as needed for intake, case plans, evaluations, federal reporting, supervision/protection, and licensing/certification.

- Department of Motor Vehicles: courts must transmit conviction abstracts and notify DMV when juvenile sealing orders include impoundment or prohibitions on obtaining licenses.

- Department of Labor: must provide a website link advising employers not to inquire about sealed records and ensure employment applications include required disclosure language.

- Department of Transportation: expands DOT authority to set and temporarily reduce speed limits, use electronic/digital speed signage, erect/maintain stopped‑vehicle signage, be listed among road assistance personnel, and retain implementation records.

No appropriations or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Transportation: Authorizes DOT to temporarily reduce posted speed limits for weather, traction, emergencies or congestion; requires electronic/digital signs for reduced limits, 5 mph increment changes, no minimum speed during temporary reductions, recordkeeping, policy development, and rulemaking authority. Also adds safe-passing rules for stopped vehicles and vulnerable road users with penalties (repeat violations elevating to Class IIIA misdemeanor). No funding or appropriation changes specified.

Department of Motor Vehicles: Courts may order operator's license suspension (up to 2 years) or revocation (15 years) as part of convictions for intentionally tampering with court-ordered electronic monitoring devices; orders take effect on sentencing/final judgment/probation revocation. No funding changes specified.

Department of Correctional Services (and probation offices): Creates a Class I misdemeanor for intentional tampering with electronic monitoring devices, allows enhanced penalties if tampering leads to death/serious injury, and affects parole/probation administration and juvenile supervision (including probation officer access to electronic monitoring data). No appropriations or budget allocations included.

Department of Health and Human Services and Department of Labor: No substantive additions or funding changes affecting these agencies in the added text.
