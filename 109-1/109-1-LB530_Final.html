Summary

The bill affects five agencies as follows:

- DHHS: Peace officers delivering certain juveniles must deliver them to DHHS, which may place/supervise temporary placements, consent to emergency medical/behavioral treatment, receive a written county-attorney report within 24 hours, and whose temporary custody ends if no court order within 48 hours. DHHS must also receive sealing notices when it was a party or the youth was a state ward and may access/release sealed juvenile records as needed for intake, case planning, evaluations, federal reporting, supervision/protection, and licensing/certification duties.

- Department of Correctional Services: Prohibits specified juveniles from confinement in adult facilities or the DOC-operated secure youth facility; DOCS may access sealed records for youth under its care.

- DMV: Courts must transmit abstracts/sealing notices when licenses are impounded or prohibited for controlled-substance convictions.

- DOT: Authorizes temporary reductions of posted speed limits (with electronic signage, 5 mph increments, recordkeeping, policy/rule authority) and allows signage for yielding/passing stopped vehicles.

- Department of Labor: Must add a website link informing employers not to ask about sealed records and that applicants need not disclose them.

No funding or appropriation changes.
