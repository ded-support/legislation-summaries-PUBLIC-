Summary

The bill affects five agencies as follows:

- DHHS: Peace officers delivering juveniles under custody or mental‑health holds must turn custody to DHHS, which must make and supervise temporary placements (ending after 48 hours if no court order) and may consent to emergency medical/psychological treatment; officers must forward mental‑health certificates to county attorneys and notify parents. DHHS must also receive sealing notices when it was a party or the juvenile was a ward and may access/release sealed juvenile records for intake, case plans, evaluations, federal reporting, supervision/protection, and licensing/certification duties.

- DCS: Bars placement/transfer of specified juveniles to adult correctional facilities or the secure youth confinement facility and excludes DCS institutions from the definition of staff‑secure juvenile facilities; DCS may access sealed juvenile records for individuals under its care for supervision and investigations.

- DMV: Courts must transmit abstracts/notice to the DMV when juvenile licenses are impounded, juveniles are prohibited from obtaining permits, or a sealing order includes such restrictions.

- DOT: Authorizes temporary reductions of posted highway speeds for visibility, traction, emergencies or congestion with required electronic signage, policy, records, 5‑mph increment guidance, and rulemaking; DOT must erect/maintain Move‑Over/stopped‑vehicle signs and may treat DOT agents as road‑assistance personnel.

- Department of Labor: Must provide a website link informing employers they may not ask about sealed records and that applications should state applicants need not disclose sealed records.

Funding: Probation fees are remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the Probation Program Cash Fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Transportation: Expanded authority to temporarily reduce posted maximum speeds for weather, traction, emergencies or congestion; required use of electronic/digital signs, five‑mph increment changes, no minimum speed during reductions, recordkeeping and policy development duties, and rulemaking authority. Also adds safe‑passing rules for stopped vehicles and vulnerable road users, and a prima facie reckless‑driving standard for speeds over double the limit; repeat violations carry Class IIIA misdemeanor exposure.

- Department of Motor Vehicles: Courts may suspend (up to 2 years) or revoke (15 years for more serious offenses) operator’s licenses as part of convictions for intentionally tampering with electronic monitoring devices.

- Department of Correctional Services, Department of Health and Human Services, Department of Labor: No substantive agency‑specific duties or budgetary/funding or appropriation changes identified in the added text.
