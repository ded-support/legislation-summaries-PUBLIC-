Summary

The bill defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and assigns it primary regulatory authority over fantasy contest licensing and enforcement. The department may adopt rules (subject to specified limits), conduct background investigations, issue or deny licenses, require geolocation and anti-fraud controls, incorporate and operate a voluntary self‑exclusion program, receive operator reports of prohibited participants, and direct forfeited prizes to problem‑gaming charities. Application and license administration duties and investigations are vested in the department. Fiscal provisions create the Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund, to be administered by the department and credited with all license and application fees; monies in the fund may be invested by the state investment officer. Civil penalties collected by the department are remitted to the State Treasurer for constitutionally prescribed distribution. The bill sets specific fee structures (including a $5,000 application fee and percentage‑based license fees) to fund administration.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and gives it primary regulatory, licensing, investigatory, and enforcement authority over fantasy contest operators (rulemaking re: consumer protection, geolocation, anti‑fraud/AML measures, background checks, investigations, referrals to law enforcement), while prohibiting rulemaking that governs individual contest mechanics. The department must process applications, conduct background investigations, issue/deny licenses, and integrate fantasy self‑exclusion into existing programs. Fee/funding provisions: nonrefundable application fee $5,000; initial license fees (existing operators: 10% of prior year adjusted gross receipts or $5,000, whichever is less; new operators: $5,000 plus a supplemental 10% fee on adjusted gross receipts). The Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund is created, administered by the department, to receive license/application fees (invested per state law). Civil penalties accrue to the state; forfeited prizes are directed by the department to problem‑gaming programs.
