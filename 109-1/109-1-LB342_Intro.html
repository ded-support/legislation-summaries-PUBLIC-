Summary

"Department" is defined as the Department of Revenue. The department is the licensing and regulatory authority for fantasy contest operators: it receives applications (including a $5,000 nonrefundable application fee), conducts background investigations, issues or denies licenses, and may adopt rules (with limits — it may not regulate individual contest rules or reclassify contests as sports wagering). Initial license fees are set (existing in‑state operators: 10% of adjusted gross fantasy contest receipts from the prior year or $5,000, whichever is less; new operators: $5,000 plus a supplemental 10% remittance). The Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund is created and administered by the department; license and application fees are credited to it and invested per state law. The department administers self‑exclusion, maintains a prohibited‑participant list, directs forfeited prizes to problem‑gaming charities, may enforce civil penalties (collected penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Article VII, §5), and may cooperate with law enforcement.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions designate the "Department" as the Department of Revenue and assign it primary regulatory, licensing, enforcement, and rulemaking duties for fantasy contest operators (with limits on regulating contest rules or platforms). The department must: process applications and background checks, issue/deny licenses, adopt rules (including geolocation, anti‑fraud/AML, and problem‑gaming standards), operate a self‑exclusion list, receive operator reports, refer investigations to law enforcement, and assess civil penalties (up to $1,000 per violation, $10,000 max per transaction).

Financially, a $5,000 nonrefundable application fee is required; initial license fees are 10% of prior-year adjusted gross receipts or $5,000 (whichever is less) with supplemental 10% obligations for some new entrants. The Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund is created, administered by the department, to receive all license/application fees and fund program administration; funds may be invested per state law.
