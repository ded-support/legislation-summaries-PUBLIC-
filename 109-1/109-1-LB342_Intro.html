Summary

The bill defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and makes the department the primary regulator for fantasy contest licensing and oversight. The Department of Revenue is authorized to adopt rules, conduct background investigations, issue or deny licenses, investigate prohibited conduct, require geolocation and anti‑fraud measures, incorporate fantasy self‑exclusion into existing programs, and bring civil actions to recover penalties. Operators must report self‑excluded participants and related forfeitures to the department. The Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund is created and administered by the Department of Revenue; all license and application fees collected by the department are credited to this fund and used for program administration. Civil penalties recovered by the department are remitted to the State Treasurer for constitutionally prescribed distribution. Application and license fee amounts and structures are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Defines "Department" as the Department of Revenue and makes it the licensing, regulatory, investigatory, and enforcement authority for fantasy contests. Responsibilities include background checks, issuing/denying licenses, rulemaking (compulsive‑gaming standards, investigations), requiring geolocation and anti‑fraud/AML measures, and operating self‑exclusion programs. Application must include specified ownership, criminal, and financial information and a $5,000 nonrefundable application fee. Initial license fees: operators already in-state pay 10% of prior-year adjusted gross fantasy receipts or $5,000 (whichever is less); new operators pay $5,000 plus a supplemental 10% fee on adjusted gross receipts (as required). Civil penalties (up to $1,000 per violation, $10,000 cap) accrue to the state. The Fantasy Sports Consumer Protection Fund is created, administered by the Department, and credited with license/application fees; funds may be invested by the state investment officer and used to administer the Act.
