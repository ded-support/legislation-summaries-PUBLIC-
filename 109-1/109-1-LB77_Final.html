Summary

- The bill defines "Department" as the Department of Insurance and requires the Department of Insurance to approve uniform prior authorization request forms; utilization review agents must use forms consistent with those approved by the department and may request exemptions from department requirements if they implement an approved electronic prior authorization interface. Utilization review agents must notify providers before implementing such systems.

- The medical assistance program must cover biomarker testing no later than January 1, 2028, and entities contracting with the program must provide testing at equivalent scope, duration, and frequency. The process to request exceptions to coverage must be readily accessible on the Department of Health and Human Services' website.

- No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Insurance (the "Department"): defined as the Dept. of Insurance and given oversight duties for prior authorization transparency — approving uniform prior-authorization request forms; requiring utilization review agents to post new/amended PA requirements on their websites and give contracted providers 60 days' notice; mandating that adverse determinations and appeals be made/reviewed by licensed physicians or appropriate clinical peers under medical director supervision; setting PA decision timelines (before Jan 1, 2028: urgent = 72 hours, nonurgent = 7 days) and creating a post‑2027 schedule; allowing utilization review agents to seek exemptions if they implement electronic/API PA systems per federal rule (with 90 days' provider notice). Provisions also require honoring prior authorizations during plan changes and continuity for inpatient care if reviews are untimely.

- Department of Health and Human Services: required to have the medical assistance program (Medicaid) cover biomarker testing no later than Jan 1, 2028, with specified clinical indications, minimal care disruption, equal scope/frequency for contractors, and a clear exception request process posted on DHHS's website.

No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
