Summary

This bill defines "Department" as the Department of Insurance and assigns it authority to approve uniform prior‑authorization request forms and to receive exemption requests from utilization review agents that implement standardized electronic prior‑authorization methods (including APIs meeting 45 C.F.R. 156.223(b)). It also requires the medical assistance program (administered by the Department of Health and Human Services) to cover biomarker testing no later than January 1, 2028, with specified clinical criteria, requires contractors to provide testing at equivalent scope/duration/frequency, and mandates that DHHS post a clear exception‑request process on its website. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Insurance

- Sets Department = Department of Insurance and strengthens prior-authorization oversight: utilization review agents must post new/amended requirements online and give contracted providers 60 days' notice.

- Adverse determinations and appeals must be made/reviewed by physicians or appropriate clinical peers; medical directors must be physicians licensed in the U.S.

- Notices of denials must cite written clinical review criteria and allow provider discussions (requestable within 3 business days) with expedited re-notice (1 business day urgent, 2 nonurgent).

- Establishes timeframes for prior-authorization decisions (shorter deadlines before and after Jan 1, 2028), honors prior authorizations for 60 days after plan changes, and permits API-based exemptions with 90 days' provider notice.

Department of Health and Human Services

- Requires the medical assistance program to cover biomarker testing by Jan 1, 2028 under specified clinical criteria, minimize care disruption, and obligates contractors to match scope/duration/frequency.

- DHHS must publish a clear exception-request process on its website.

No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
