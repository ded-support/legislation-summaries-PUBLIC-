Summary

LB276 requires multiple executive and local agencies to implement new oversight, reporting, and public-disclosure duties:

- Cities (metropolitan and primary) must create citizen police oversight boards by Jan 1, 2028; city councils appoint seven members. Boards may subpoena, inspect police records, investigate complaints and officer-involved shootings, consult the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and the Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council, and publish reports to police departments, mayors, city councils, and county attorneys. Police departments must respond to recommendations; labor contracts cannot negate board authority.

- City attorneys, county attorneys, and the Attorney General must maintain and post Brady/Giglio lists online (updated monthly). The Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice will adopt implementing rules by Sept 1, 2025.

- Chiefs/sheriffs/head administrators must file personnel changes with the training center within seven days, permanently retain misconduct records, and notify the Commission within 30 days of terminations/resignations for serious misconduct. The Commission must publish a public registry of decertified/convicted/misconduct findings and provide a challenge process.

- Judges/magistrates are barred from issuing no‑knock warrants. No appropriations or funding changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates required citizen police oversight boards in all primary/metropolitan cities (by Jan 1, 2028). Boards (seven public members appointed by city council) may investigate complaints, discrimination/harassment, officer-involved shootings, and misconduct; inspect police records (including personnel files); issue subpoenas; and consult standards from the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and the Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council. Boards must report findings to the city police department, mayor, city council, county attorney (for possible criminal conduct), and federal/state misconduct registries; reports and responses are public records and meetings fall under the Open Meetings Act. City/county attorneys and the Attorney General must maintain and post Brady/Giglio lists (updated monthly). The Nebraska Commission must promulgate implementing rules by Sept. 1, 2025. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
