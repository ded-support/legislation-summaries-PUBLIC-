Summary

LB276 creates the Municipal Police Oversight Act and amends law enforcement reporting and public-disclosure duties.

Key agencies/departments and requirements:

- Cities (metropolitan and primary class): must establish seven-member citizen police oversight boards by Jan 1, 2028 (appointment by city council). Boards may subpoena, inspect police records (including personnel), investigate complaints/shootings/misconduct, consult Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council, and report findings to police departments, mayors, city councils and county attorneys for criminal referral. Reports and agency responses are public; meetings subject to the Open Meetings Act.

- City attorneys, county attorneys, and the Attorney General: must maintain and publicly post Brady/Giglio lists (updated at least monthly). Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice to adopt rules by Sept 1, 2025.

- Chiefs, sheriffs, Superintendent of Law Enforcement and Public Safety, and agency heads: must file personnel changes with the training-center director within seven days; retain separation records five years; permanently retain records relevant to certification revocation and report terminations/resignations for serious conduct to the commission within 30 days.

- Nebraska Commission: must post a public registry of officers with surrendered/revoked certifications, convictions, or adjudicated serious misconduct (with agency affirmation and status); council to allow challenge procedure and notify commission within 30 days of actions.

- Revisor of Statutes: assigned to place the new act in chapter 18.

Also prohibits issuance of no‑knock warrants by judges or magistrates. No funding or appropriation changes included. The act takes effect immediately as an emergency measure.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a Municipal Police Oversight Act requiring each metropolitan/primary class city to establish a seven-member citizen oversight board by Jan 1, 2028, appointed by the city council. Boards may subpoena, inspect police records (including personnel files), investigate misconduct/shootings, publish reports, submit evidence to county attorneys, and consult standards from the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and the Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council. City police departments must respond to board recommendations. City, county attorneys, and the Attorney General must each maintain and publicly post monthly-updated Brady/Giglio lists on their websites beginning Sept 1, 2025; the Nebraska Commission must adopt rules by that date. Oversight boards are immune from good-faith liability; conflicting employment or bargaining provisions are void. No appropriations or funding changes are included.
