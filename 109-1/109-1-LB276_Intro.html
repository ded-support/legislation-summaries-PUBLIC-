Summary

LB276 creates the Municipal Police Oversight Act and revises duties for multiple state and local law-enforcement entities.

Key agency responsibilities:

- Cities (metropolitan/primary class) must establish seven-member citizen police oversight boards by Jan 1, 2028; boards may investigate complaints and shootings, inspect police records, issue subpoenas, consult standards from the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and the Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council, and publish findings. Police departments must respond to recommendations; oversight records are public and meetings follow the Open Meetings Act.

- City attorneys, county attorneys, and the Attorney General must maintain and post monthly Brady/Giglio lists; the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice must adopt implementing rules by Sept 1, 2025.

- Judges/magistrates are prohibited from issuing no‑knock warrants.

- Chiefs/sheriffs/agency heads must file personnel-status forms with the training‑center director within seven days, retain separation records, permanently retain misconduct records subject to commission review, and report terminations/resignations in lieu to the commission within 30 days. The commission must publicly post officer decertification/conviction/misconduct lists with details and a challenge procedure.

No funding or appropriation changes are included. The act takes effect immediately.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates mandatory municipal citizen police oversight boards (seven public members, appointed by city council) by Jan 1, 2028, with powers to investigate shootings, misconduct, discrimination, and employee complaints; inspect all police records (including personnel files) notwithstanding other law; issue subpoenas (court-enforceable); refer suspected criminal conduct to the county attorney; and report findings to the city police department, mayor, city council and federal/state registries. Oversight boards and investigators must consult standards from the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice and the Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council. All reports are public records; meetings fall under the Open Meetings Act. Cities may not contract or bargain away board authority. Beginning Sept 1, 2025, city attorneys, county attorneys, and the Attorney General must maintain and publicly post monthly Brady/Giglio officer lists; the Commission must adopt implementing rules by that date. No funding or appropriations are specified.
