Summary

LB181 amends the Young Adult Bridge to Independence Act to (1) exempt program participation from lawful‑presence verification and (2) extend program eligibility to young adults not lawfully present in the United States. It directs the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to: submit a state plan amendment to seek federal Title IV‑E funding (deadline listed Oct. 1, 2023); submit a Medicaid state plan amendment to the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) by Oct. 1, 2025 to provide Title XIX/XX (Medicaid/CHIP) coverage to program participants (including under‑21, not lawfully present); and adopt implementing rules by Oct. 1, 2025. DHHS must pursue other coverage options for uninsured participants, “including, but not limited to, payment from the General Fund.” The bill also repeals the original cited statutory sections. No specific appropriation amounts are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that young adults in the "bridge to independence" program (per the Young Adult Bridge to Independence Act) are eligible for medical care under federal Title XIX (Medicaid) or Title XXI (CHIP), regardless of lawful presence. If they are not eligible under those programs, "the department" must seek other coverage options, including payment from the state's General Fund. The department is required to submit a Medicaid state plan amendment to the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) by October 1, 2025 to provide medical assistance to participating young adults — including those under 21 who are not lawfully present.
