Summary

LB327 centralizes funding and administrative responsibility for probation with the State and the Nebraska Supreme Court. Key provisions:

- Supreme Court and State Court Administrator: set interpreter standards; State Court Administrator selects an implementation date between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027; Supreme Court may adopt implementing rules.

- State financial obligations (to be paid from funds appropriated to the Supreme Court): salaries and travel for probation staff; expenses for district principal offices; office space and facilities; costs of drug testing and electronic-surveillance equipment (except as otherwise provided); procurement, management and support of IT (hardware, software, network, Internet, technical support); interpreter services (including auxiliary aids) for limited-English and deaf/hard-of-hearing persons, and may use other funds/grants.

- Counties must cooperate during transition; probation administrator must prepare a budget and submit appropriation requests through the Supreme Court.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a State Court Administrator–selected implementation date between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027, and makes the state (rather than counties) responsible, beginning on that date, for probation service expenses. Covered agencies/actors: the State Court Administrator, the Oregon Supreme Court (or “Supreme Court”), and county officials. State payments must cover district probation office operations, office space and facilities, probation staff salaries and travel, and comprehensive IT (support, hardware, software, networks, Internet). Funding is to come from money appropriated to the Supreme Court. Counties must coordinate with the State Court Administrator on the transition. The Supreme Court may adopt implementing rules.
