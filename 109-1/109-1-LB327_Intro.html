Summary

LB327 shifts fiscal and administrative responsibility for probation services from counties to the state. Beginning on an implementation date set by the State Court Administrator between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027, the state (via appropriations to the Supreme Court) will pay: district probation office operation expenses; office space and facility costs for probation staff; probation staff salaries and travel; IT procurement/management/support; and interpreter services (including auxiliary aids) for non‑English and deaf/hard‑of‑hearing persons. The state already pays drug‑testing and electronic‑surveillance equipment costs except as otherwise provided. Counties must coordinate with the State Court Administrator on the transition. The probation administrator must prepare budget requests for Supreme Court approval and submission for appropriations. The Supreme Court may adopt rules and sets interpreter standards, requiring licensed interpreters for deaf/hard‑of‑hearing persons.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Effective between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027 (date chosen by the State Court Administrator), the state will assume payment for probation services. Covered costs include district office operations, office space/facilities for probation staff, probation staff salaries and travel, and comprehensive IT procurement/management/support (hardware, peripherals, phones/mobile devices, technical support, software licensing, network and Internet services). These costs are to be paid from funds appropriated to the Supreme Court. County officials must coordinate with the State Court Administrator to ensure a smooth transition of payment responsibilities. The Supreme Court may adopt rules to implement the section.
