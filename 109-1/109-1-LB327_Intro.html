Summary

LB327 shifts financial and administrative responsibility for probation from counties to the State. Key agency roles and budgetary changes:

- Supreme Court: adopts interpreter standards; may adopt rules to implement the act; will receive appropriations to pay probation-related expenses and interpreter services.

- State Court Administrator: selects the implementation date between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027 and coordinates transition with counties.

- State: becomes responsible for salaries and travel of probation staff; expenses for district principal offices; office space/facilities; drug testing (with existing exceptions); and comprehensive IT procurement/support. These costs are to be paid from funds appropriated to the Supreme Court.

- Counties: must collaborate on a coordinated transition.

- Probation administrator: prepares budget and appropriation requests for Supreme Court submission.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds an implementation date, set by the State Court Administrator between July 1, 2026 and July 1, 2027. Beginning on that date, the state (via funds appropriated to the Supreme Court) will assume payment for probation services — including district principal office operations, office space and facilities, probation staff salaries and travel, and comprehensive IT (technical support; hardware like printers/scanners/phones/mobile devices; software licensing; network and Internet services). County officials must coordinate with the State Court Administrator to ensure a smooth transition. The Supreme Court may adopt rules to implement the section.
