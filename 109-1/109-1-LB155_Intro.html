Summary

LB155 amends Nebraska’s justifiable-force statutes (28-1406, 28-1409, 28-1410). It clarifies definitions and treatment of public officers and law enforcement: "actor" excludes duly authorized law enforcement officers, and "public officer" is defined as any elected or appointed officer or employee of the State of Nebraska or its political subdivisions (excluding law enforcement). The bill explicitly states a person lawfully present in a motor vehicle (same definition as section 28-109) has no duty to retreat before using deadly force; it similarly extends no-retreat language to defense of others. Original sections are repealed and harmonized. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added language clarifies definitions and self‑defense rules and expressly excludes "any duly authorized law enforcement officer of the State of Nebraska or its political subdivisions" from the definition of "Actor." It also ties "motor vehicle" to the statutory definition in section 28‑109 and defines "unlawful force." New provisions state a person lawfully present in a motor vehicle is not required to retreat from it unless the person was the initial aggressor; the text also references officers and assisting officers in related provisions. No state agencies, departments beyond Nebraska law enforcement and its political subdivisions are created or changed, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.
