Summary

LB210 designates the Department of Health and Human Services (department) as the lead agency under the Population Health Information Act. Key department actions: impose fees on every pharmacy benefit manager for operational, non‑treatment use of the prescription drug monitoring program and remit those fees to a newly created Population Health Information Assessment Fund (fee amount left unspecified). The Fund is established to cover nonfederal costs and to reimburse the department for assessment collection costs (up to $250,000 annually); transfers to the General Fund are prohibited and investment earnings accrue to the Fund. The department must share vital‑statistics data quarterly with the designated health information exchange and report managed‑care organization compliance to the Health Information Technology Board by June 30 each fiscal year (beginning 2025‑26); the Board must notify the Department of Insurance of MCO noncompliance within 60 days.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): New authority to impose and remit fees from pharmacy benefit managers to the State Treasurer for credit to a newly created Population Health Information Assessment Fund (fee amount shown as "XXX"). The Fund is dedicated to nonfederal costs of the designated health information exchange and prescription drug monitoring program and may reimburse the department up to $250,000 annually for assessment collection costs; transfers to the General Fund are prohibited and investment earnings are credited to the Fund. DHHS must annually report (by June 30, beginning FY2025‑26) each managed care organization's compliance with claims‑sharing to the Health Information Technology Board and must share vital‑statistics data files quarterly with the designated HIE/operator.

- Department of Insurance (DOI): The Health Information Technology Board must report MCO noncompliance in the medical assistance program to DOI within 60 days after receiving DHHS’s status report. Insurers/health plans may also be subject to charges by the operator as a condition of doing business in Nebraska.
