Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (department) is defined as DHHS and is referenced throughout for operation, oversight, data sharing, and access to the prescription drug monitoring program and designated statewide health information exchange (HIE). DHHS (including its Division of Medicaid and Long‑Term Care medical and pharmacy directors) may receive and release PDMP data under HIPAA-consistent policies and must collaborate with the designated HIE on safeguards, technology, and data‑use agreements.

- A Population Health Information Assessment Fund is created to pay nonfederal costs and to reimburse DHHS for assessment collection costs (capped at $250,000 annually). The bill authorizes DHHS to impose fees on pharmacy benefit managers and requires remittance of those fees to the fund; fund earnings are credited to the fund and transfers to the General Fund are prohibited.

- DHHS must annually report managed care organizations' compliance in sharing claims data to the Health Information Technology Board (by June 30 beginning FY2025‑26); the board must report MCO noncompliance to the Department of Insurance within 60 days.

- DHHS must share vital statistics files quarterly with the designated HIE (first business day of Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct); the HIE must file an annual ROI report to the Legislature's Health and Human Services Committee by Sept. 1.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS)

- DHHS is authorized to impose fees on pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) for non‑treatment use of the prescription drug monitoring system and must remit those fees to the State Treasurer for credit to a newly created Population Health Information Assessment Fund (fee amount left as XXX).

- The Fund is designated to cover nonfederal costs of the designated health information exchange and PDMP and to reimburse DHHS up to $250,000 annually for assessment collection. No transfers to the General Fund; investment earnings credit back to the Fund.

- DHHS must report managed care organizations’ compliance on claims‑sharing to the Health Information Technology Board annually (by June 30, beginning FY2025‑26), share vital statistics with the designated HIE/operator quarterly, and enable an ROI report to the Legislature by Sept. 1.

- Department of Insurance

- Will receive the Health Information Technology Board’s report (within 60 days of DHHS’s report) identifying MCO noncompliance for those in the medical assistance program.
