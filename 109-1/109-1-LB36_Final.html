Summary

LB36 consolidates and clarifies the statutory authority of the Department of Transportation (the Department), reaffirming its power to plan, construct, maintain, operate and enter agreements (including with the United States and adjoining states) for the state highway system and interstate bridges. The bill expressly authorizes the Department to establish and operate mitigation banks and in‑lieu‑fee programs for transportation projects (threatened/endangered species, wetlands, habitat), adopt implementing rules, and enter cooperative agreements. The Department may acquire real property—including purchase, donation or condemnation—and redesignate the state highway system; it may establish wetlands banks sized up to 150% of projected mitigation needs. State agencies must give priority to the Department’s mitigation resources. Owners of mitigation bank property acquired after July 1, 2026 must pay sums in lieu of lost ad valorem taxes to affected counties. The bill contains no explicit appropriations or new Department budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates authority for the Department of Transportation to establish, operate, and use mitigation banks and in‑lieu‑fee programs to provide compensatory mitigation for DOT-administered transportation projects (impacts to threatened/endangered species/habitat, wetland dredge/fill, and habitat restoration/creation/enhancement/preservation). State agencies are urged to give priority to these DOT programs when reviewing mitigation plans. The department may enter cooperative agreements with public or private parties, acquire real property (purchase, donation, bequest, or eminent domain) for mitigation purposes, and adopt rules to implement the program. Properties acquired after July 1, 2026, must pay a sum in lieu of lost ad valorem taxes to the county. No specific new appropriations, budget allocations, or funding amounts for DOT implementation are specified.
