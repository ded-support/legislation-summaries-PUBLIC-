Summary

LB642 (Artificial Intelligence Consumer Protection Act) vests investigative and enforcement-related authority primarily with the Nebraska Attorney General. The AG may, during investigations, compel deployers or contracted third parties to produce risk‑management policies, impact assessments, and related records within 90 days, may evaluate those materials for compliance, and may treat disclosures as nonpublic and potentially trade secret–protected. The bill references cooperation with law enforcement agencies and federal guidance (including 45 C.F.R. part 46 and FDA requirements) for research exceptions. It exempts insurers and related entities subject to the Unfair Insurance Trade Practices Act and exempts banks, credit unions, affiliates or service providers subject to state or federal prudential regulators if those regulators’ rules are deemed substantially equivalent by the Attorney General. Effective date: February 1, 2026. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds enforcement and disclosure roles for the state Attorney General (may demand deployer/deployer-contracted third parties produce risk management policies, impact assessments, and related records within 90 days; such disclosures are not public records and may be marked proprietary). Carves out exemptions: insurers, certain fraternal benefit societies, and developers of insurer-used AI (per Unfair Insurance Trade Practices Act); and banks, credit unions, their affiliates/subsidiaries or service providers subject to examination by state or federal prudential regulators — if those regulators’ guidance is deemed by the Attorney General to be substantially equivalent and requires regular audits and mitigation of algorithmic discrimination. References federal authorities/regulations (FDA and 45 C.F.R. part 46) as permissible research standards and preserves cooperation with law enforcement and compliance with legal process. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
