Summary

LB642 (Artificial Intelligence Consumer Protection Act) empowers the Nebraska Attorney General to investigate compliance: during an investigation the AG may require deployers or contracted third parties to produce risk‑management policies, impact assessments, and related records within 90 days, review them for compliance, and treat disclosed materials as nonpublic (allowing trade‑secret designation). The bill permits cooperation with law enforcement and requires compliance with subpoenas and government inquiries. It exempts insurers and systems subject to the Unfair Insurance Trade Practices Act, and excludes banks, credit unions, affiliates, or service providers that are examined by a state or federal prudential regulator whose guidance the Attorney General deems substantially equivalent or more stringent. The federal Food and Drug Administration is referenced for research‑ethics compliance. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds detailed requirements for developers/deployers of "high‑risk" AI: documentation/model cards, pre‑deployment and post‑modification impact assessments, consumer notices and appeal/correction rights, retention of records for at least three years, and deadlines tied to February 1, 2026. The Attorney General may, in ongoing investigations, compel deployers or contracted third parties to produce risk‑management policies, impact assessments, or related records within 90 days, may evaluate compliance, and such disclosures are exempt from public‑record release; deployers may designate trade secrets. Exemptions cover insurers (and related fraternal benefit societies), banks/credit unions and affiliates subject to state or federal prudential regulators whose rules are deemed substantially equivalent or stricter, and references FDA and 45 C.F.R. part 46 for research. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
