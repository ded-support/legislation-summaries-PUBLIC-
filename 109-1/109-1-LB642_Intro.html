Summary

LB642 creates the "Artificial Intelligence Consumer Protection Act" regulating high‑risk AI deployments. Key government roles:

- Nebraska Attorney General: may, in ongoing investigations, compel deployers or contracted third parties to produce risk‑management policies, impact assessments, and related records within 90 days; may evaluate compliance; disclosed materials are exempt from public‑record release and may be designated proprietary. The Attorney General also determines whether federal/state prudential regulator guidance for banks/credit unions is "substantially equivalent" for exemption purposes.

- Federal references: research exceptions must comply with 45 C.F.R. part 46 and applicable FDA requirements; law enforcement and other federal/state/municipal authorities are recognized for compliance and investigatory actions.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are contained in the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds roles for multiple government actors but no funding changes. Key points:

- The state Attorney General may, during investigations, compel deployers or contracted third parties to produce risk‑management policies, impact assessments, and related records within 90 days, may evaluate compliance, and may designate disclosures as nonpublic or trade secrets.

- Exempts entities subject to examination by state or federal prudential regulators (banks, credit unions, affiliates, service providers) if the regulator’s guidance is deemed by the Attorney General to be substantially equivalent or stricter and requires regular audits and mitigation of algorithmic discrimination.

- Permits cooperation with law enforcement and compliance with federal, state, or municipal authorities and subpoenas.

- References federal authorities: FDA requirements and HHS Common Rule (45 C.F.R. part 46) for research exceptions.

- No appropriations, budget, or funding provisions are added.
