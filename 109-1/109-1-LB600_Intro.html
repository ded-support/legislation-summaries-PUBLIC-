Summary

LB600 grants several powers and duties to the Department of Transportation (DOT). DOT (or its agents) may remove vehicles or other objects from highways or shoulders without owner consent and is immune from liability for damage. DOT may temporarily reduce maximum lawful speeds for specified conditions, must display electronic/digital signage when doing so, develop/implement policy for such reductions, keep records of changes and highway speed limits, and adopt rules to implement the process. DOT may enforce school-crossing and highway-work-zone speed limits using speed control enforcement systems and may contract with operators. DOT must create a guidance document to calculate administrative fees related to system-issued warnings/citations and must erect and maintain signage notifying motorists of the move-over requirement on controlled-access highways. Civil penalties collected are remitted to the State Treasurer. No appropriations or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority and procedures for the Department of Transportation (DOT) to: remove vehicles/objects impeding traffic (with immunity from damage liability); temporarily lower posted highway speed limits for defined conditions (weather, traction, emergencies, congestion) using electronic/digital signage in 5 mph increments, with required policy, recordkeeping, and rulemaking; and enforce school-crossing and highway work-zone speed limits using speed control enforcement systems (automated devices), including contracting operators. Sets operation standards (active warning lights/workers present, signage), speed thresholds for automated citations (school: >6 mph; work zone: >11 mph), owner notice, appeal process, civil-penalty schedule, confidentiality and calibration/logging requirements, and guidance on administrative fees. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
