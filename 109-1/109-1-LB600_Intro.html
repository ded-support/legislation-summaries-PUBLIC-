Summary

- Authority to remove: The Department of Transportation (DOT) or its agents may remove vehicles or objects from a highway or highway shoulder without owner consent if they are or could impede traffic; DOT and its agents are immune from liability for damage caused by such removal (sec. 7).

- Temporary speed reductions: DOT may temporarily reduce maximum lawful speeds for weather, traction, emergencies, congestion, etc., must display electronic/digital signs showing the reduced limit and restoration instructions, make reductions in 5 mph increments, keep records of changes and each highway's speed limit, develop/implement policy, and adopt rules (sec. 9).

- Automated enforcement: DOT may enforce school crossing‑zone and highway work‑zone speed limits using speed control enforcement systems, may contract with certified operators, assess civil penalties, and must create guidance to calculate administrative fees (secs. 10, 12, 14).

- Signage and calibration: DOT must erect/maintain signs for the moved‑over law and systems’ images/data are restricted to DOT/local authority use; calibration must be performed by laboratories independent of DOT (secs. 17, 19, 16).

No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions give the Department of Transportation expanded operational and enforcement authority. Key DOT actions authorized: remove vehicles/objects from highways or shoulders that impede traffic (DOT/agents immune from damage liability); temporarily reduce posted maximum speeds for weather, traction, emergency, or congestion (must display electronic/digital signage, reductions in 5 mph increments, no minimum speed, DOT must adopt policy and keep records); and enforce school-crossing and highway work-zone speeds using speed-control enforcement systems (definition, operator training, annual independent calibration, daily operator logs, confidentiality rules). Enforcement conditions, recording thresholds (school: >6 mph; work zone: >11 mph), owner notice, civil penalties, appeal procedures, and an administrative fee methodology (DOT to publish guidance) are specified. No direct appropriations, budget allocations, or specific funding changes are included.
