Summary

Key Department of Transportation (DOT) provisions in LB600:

- DOT (or its agents) may remove vehicles/objects from highways/shoulders without owner consent when impeding traffic and is immune from liability for damage.

- DOT may reduce maximum lawful speed limits (temporarily) for weather, traction, emergencies, or congestion; such reductions require electronic/digital signage, five-mph increments, no minimum speed during reductions, and DOT must adopt policy, keep records (including per-highway limits and changes), and may promulgate rules.

- DOT may enforce school-crossing and highway-work-zone speed limits using speed control enforcement systems and may contract with certified operators.

- DOT (or local authorities) shall assess civil penalties for camera-enforced violations; DOT must create guidance for administrative fee calculation.

- DOT must erect/maintain signage on controlled-access highways regarding yielding to stopped vehicles.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (Department of Transportation):

- DOT may remove vehicles/objects obstructing highways or shoulders and is not liable for removal damage.

- DOT may temporarily lower posted speed limits for fog, precipitation, smoke, dust, traction issues, emergencies, or congestion; reductions must be displayed on electronic/digital signs, set in 5 mph increments, with no minimum speed, and DOT must adopt policy, keep records, and may adopt rules.

- DOT may use (or authorize/contract for) photographic/radar/laser speed-control systems to enforce school crossing and highway work-zone speeds subject to conditions (active school warning lights or presence of workers, signage). Thresholds: citations for >6 mph over in school zones, >11 mph over in work zones.

- Systems require independent annual calibration and operator daily logs. Civil penalties are assessed and remitted to the State Treasurer.

- No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for DOT are specified.
