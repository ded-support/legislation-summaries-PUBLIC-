Summary

LB90 revises municipal improvement-district procedures for cities of the first class, assigning responsibilities to the city clerk and the mayor and city council. Key agency actions:

- City clerk must publish district-creation notice weekly for at least 20 days. Property owners representing >50% of front footage who file written objections within 20 days require the mayor and city council to repeal the ordinance.

- Mayor and city council must solicit bids (and allow material requests via a petition by owners representing 25% of abutting property), postpone bid award at least 10 days, and allow a majority of abutting owners to select a received bid material.

- Costs are assessed by the mayor and city council as special assessments payable in up to 20 annual installments (interest limited by section 45-104.01). Three or more delinquent installments permit the council to declare all future installments due (with one-time 20‑day publication).

- City may issue street‑improvement bonds (≤20‑year maturities); special assessments serve as the sinking fund. The council may deny district formation if basic infrastructure (water, sewer, grading) is lacking and must notify interested parties in writing. Original sections are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority and procedural detail for the mayor and city council (specifically of a city of the first class) in creating and administering improvement districts (see secs. 16-619, 16-624, 16-617, 16-622). The council is directed to publish required notice, advertise bids for district improvements, and may fund improvements by levying special assessments on specially benefited property. Property owners may pay the entire cost chargeable to their lots within 50 days after the assessment to avoid liens. The bill also contemplates issuance of street improvement bonds per the authorizing ordinance.
