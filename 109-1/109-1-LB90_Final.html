Summary

LB90 amends statutes governing municipal improvement districts in cities of the first class. Key agency actions:

- City clerk must publish notice of district creation in a legal newspaper weekly for at least 20 days. Property owners representing >50% of front footage have 20 days to file written objections; if filed the ordinance is repealed by the mayor and city council.

- The mayor and city council control bidding/material selection: owners representing 25% may petition for materials; bids are postponed 10 days to allow majority selection.

- The mayor and city council determine special assessments, may spread assessments up to 20 annual installments, set interest (not exceeding the statutory rate), and may declare future installments due after three or more delinquencies (with one-time 20-day publication).

- The mayor and city council may issue city bonds (max 20‑year maturity) to fund improvements; special assessments constitute the sinking fund. They may deny district formation where basic infrastructure (water, sewer, street grading) is absent and must notify interested parties in writing. The Legislature’s statutory interest rate (section 45‑104.01) governs limits.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds that the mayor and city council (specifically of a city of the first class) are responsible for actions relating to creating improvement districts, advertising bids, and authorizing street‑improvement bonds under the cited statutory provisions (secs. 16‑617, 16‑619, 16‑622, 16‑624). Funding language specifies improvements may be paid by levying special assessments on specially benefited property; property owners may prepay the entire assessment within 50 days to avoid liens. The ordinance authorizing bonds governs original bond issuance for district improvements. No new appropriations to other agencies are created.
