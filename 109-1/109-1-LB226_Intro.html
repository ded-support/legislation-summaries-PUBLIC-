Summary

LB226 allows courts to set aside convictions or juvenile adjudications for defined "concealed weapon" offenses and directs that, once set aside, movants may seek sealing of criminal history records. Courts must notify the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice, the Nebraska State Patrol, and relevant law enforcement agencies, county attorneys, and city attorneys and order those entities to seal records. The bill expands private causes of action under the Security, Privacy, and Dissemination of Criminal History Information Act, permitting suits (including damages, equitable relief, or mandamus) against the state, state agencies, political subdivisions, and their officers/employees; it waives sovereign immunity for such suits, removes them from the State and Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Acts, and sets a limitations period (generally four years, with a special January 1, 2029 cutoff). The Attorney General may be asked by the Commission to bring actions. No appropriations or funding changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a post-conviction process letting people convicted or adjudicated for "concealed weapon offenses" move to set aside convictions after completion of sentence/disposition. Courts must consider post‑conviction behavior and recidivism risk, may grant relief if in the public interest, and there is a rebuttable presumption in favor of relief when the underlying conduct would not now violate current section 28‑1202. If granted, the court must allow sealing of the related criminal history per the cited statutes.

Creates a private right of action against the state, any state agency or political subdivision, and their officers/employees for violations of rules under the Security, Privacy, and Dissemination of Criminal History Information Act, waiving sovereign immunity and permitting damages and equitable relief. Such suits are excluded from the State Tort Claims Act and Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act. Provides filing deadlines (four years after accrual or by Jan 1, 2029 for certain older claims). No appropriations or funding changes.
