Summary

LB226 permits persons convicted or adjudicated for defined "concealed weapon" offenses to file motions in the county, district, or separate juvenile court that entered the conviction to set aside the record and (upon grant) to seek sealing of related criminal-history records. Courts granting relief must notify the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice, the Nebraska State Patrol, and relevant law enforcement agencies, county attorneys, and city attorneys; sealed records are restricted from public dissemination except to criminal justice agencies or limited research uses.

The bill amends the Security, Privacy, and Dissemination of Criminal History Information Act to create a private cause of action against state agencies, political subdivisions, and their officers/employees for violations, waiving sovereign immunity for such suits. Remedies include actual damages, equitable relief, and mandamus; actions are excluded from the State Tort Claims Act and Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act. Limitations: generally four years after accrual, with a special January 1, 2029 deadline for certain claims accrued on or after January 1, 2015. The Attorney General may be asked by the commission to bring suit. No funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures allowing persons convicted or adjudicated for certain concealed-weapon offenses to move to set aside the conviction and then seek sealing of criminal history records in the county, district, or juvenile court that entered the conviction. Creates private liability for a state agency, political subdivision, or their officers/employees who violate rules under the Security, Privacy, and Dissemination of Criminal History Information Act, and expressly permits plaintiffs to join the state, its agencies, or subdivisions as defendants (waiving sovereign immunity). Actions under the provision are excluded from the State Tort Claims Act and the Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act and are subject to specified limitations periods (generally four years, with a special Jan 1, 2029 deadline for certain older claims). No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
