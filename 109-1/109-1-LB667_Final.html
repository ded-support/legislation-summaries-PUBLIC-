Summary

LB667 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §60‑1438 (Motor Vehicle Industry Regulation Act) to revise warranty compensation and dispute procedures. It creates a formula and audit process for determining dealer compensation for parts (dealer-submitted repair orders, manufacturer audit, 45-day effective period), defines "fair wholesale value," limits burdensome documentation requirements, and allows manufacturers/distributors to request up to 100 additional repair orders to verify declared rates. The bill expressly assigns the manufacturer the burden to justify denials and authorizes dealer complaints to the board under the Act; the board may find unreasonable denials to violate the Act and enforce remedies. It also clarifies recall, claim‑submission, payment, audit, chargeback, and appeal timelines, extends coverage to separately warranted components, and excludes recreational vehicles. No appropriations or funding changes are made.

Summary of added provisions:

- When calculating parts compensation, only retail sales unrelated to warranty repairs may be used; markups for parts on recommended maintenance (oil, filters, fluids, brakes, spark plugs, wipers, tires, etc.) cannot be used. The Motor Vehicle Industry Regulation Act is cited as governing denials.

- If a franchisor supplies a part free or at reduced cost for warranty work, the franchisor must pay the dealer the dealer’s cost plus the dealer’s markup, computed using the part’s “fair wholesale value” (the greatest of dealer cost, franchisor price-schedule cost, or cost of a substantially identical franchisor-listed part).

- Manufacturers/distributors may request up to 100 additional repair orders (from a dealer-selected 90‑day period within the prior 12 months) to review labor/markup rates, may adjust warranty payment rates if nonwarranty rates are lower, and dealers have 30 days to rebut. Such requests cannot occur more than once per 12 months.

- Parties may mutually agree on rates.
