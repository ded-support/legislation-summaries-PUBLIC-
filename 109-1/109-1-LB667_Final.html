Summary

LB667 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §60-1438 (Motor Vehicle Industry Regulation Act). Key agency/process changes: dealers may establish an average parts markup by submitting 100 sequential or 90 days of customer-paid repair orders; manufacturers have 30 days to audit and 45 days after approval for the markup to take effect. If a manufacturer denies the declared markup, the dealer may complain to the board, which can find the denial an Act violation. Franchisors who supply parts at no or reduced cost must compensate dealers based on dealer cost plus markup multiplied by the part’s fair wholesale value (defined by three benchmarks). Manufacturers/distributors may request up to 100 additional repair orders (with specified timeframes and limitations) to reassess dealer rates and may adjust payments accordingly. The bill also sets timelines for claim submission, payment, audits, recall parts availability notices, and dealer compensation for recalls. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Summary of substantive changes:

- When calculating parts compensation, retail sales tied to warranty repairs cannot be used; markups for recommended maintenance parts (oil, filters, fluids, brake components, spark plugs, wiper blades, tire repair/replacement, etc.) cannot be used.

- If a franchisor supplies a part free or at reduced cost for warranty work, the franchisor must pay the dealer the dealer's cost plus the dealer’s markup, multiplied by the part’s “fair wholesale value.”

- “Fair wholesale value” = greatest of: dealer’s paid amount; franchisor’s price-schedule cost at time furnished; cost of a substantially identical part in franchisor’s price schedule.

- Manufacturers/distributors may request up to 100 additional dealer repair orders (from a dealer-selected 90‑day period within the past 12 months) to verify markup/labor rates, may adjust warranty payments if nonwarranty rates are lower, and dealers have 30 days to rebut. Requests cannot be made more than once in a 12‑month period.

- Parties may mutually agree on retail labor or markup rates.
