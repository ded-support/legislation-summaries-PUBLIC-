Summary

LB667 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-1438 (Motor Vehicle Industry Regulation Act). It revises dealer compensation rules for diagnostic work and parts: dealers may establish an average parts markup by submitting sequential customer-paid repair orders (or 90 days of orders) for manufacturer audit; manufacturers have 30 days to approve/deny and the board may adjudicate denials (manufacturer bears burden to justify denial under the Act). Manufacturers/distributors may request up to 100 additional repair orders to verify declared rates and may adjust warranty payments if dealer retail rates differ. The bill preserves timelines for dealer warranty claims (file within six months; manufacturers must approve/deny and pay within 30 days), specifies audit and chargeback rights, and reiterates manufacturer recall and warranty obligations. The Legislature repeals the original section. No state agency appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions govern franchisor/dealer compensation and rate review under the Motor Vehicle Industry Regulation Act. Key points:

- Denies use of retail-markup or retail sales data when computing compensation for recommended maintenance services.

- If a franchisor furnishes parts for warranty work at no/reduced cost, the franchisor must compensate the dealer for dealer cost plus the dealer’s markup, multiplied by the part’s “fair wholesale value,” defined as the greatest of dealer-paid cost, franchisor price-schedule cost, or substantially identical franchisor price-schedule cost.

- Manufacturers/distributors may request up to 100 additional dealer repair orders (from a 90‑day period within the prior 12 months) to verify declared rates, may adjust warranty payments accordingly, and must allow dealers 30 days to rebut; such requests cannot occur more often than yearly.

- Parties may mutually agree on rates.

No government agencies or departments are named, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
