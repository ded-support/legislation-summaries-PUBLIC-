Summary

LB92 (Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act) requires Nebraska trial courts to issue and enter orders sealing (clean slate relief for) eviction records when an eviction proceeding is dismissed. Tenants may also petition the trial court for sealing when records remain public despite dismissal, a writ of restitution is reversed/vacated or never executed, eviction was for nonpayment during the COVID‑19 emergency (3/13/2020–6/30/2021), a named defendant was a minor, or a judgment violated §76‑1431; otherwise a petition is available after three years from final judgment or writ issuance. Petitions are filed in the trial court, served on all parties, allow 30 days for objections (no objection ⇒ grant), and require a hearing if opposed (objector bears the burden). Orders are final and appealable under §25‑1902. References include eviction statutes ( §§25‑21,219–25‑21,235) and landlord definitions (§§76‑1410, 76‑1462). No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act." Key government actors: trial courts must immediately issue clean-slate orders (per section 5) when an eviction is dismissed and hear or process petitions for relief. Tenants may petition trial court for sealing of eviction records when proceedings meet specified grounds (e.g., dismissal, reversal/vacatur, writ never executed, eviction for nonpayment during the COVID-19 emergency Mar 13, 2020–Jun 30, 2021, a defendant was a minor, or other statutory violation), or after three years from writ/final judgment. Petitions are filed in trial court, notice served on parties, 30-day window for objections; no objection ⇒ grant without hearing; objection ⇒ hearing where objector bears burden to oppose relief. Orders granting/denying relief are final and appealable under section 25-1902. No agencies beyond the courts are created or funding/appropriation changes specified.
