Summary

LB92 adopts the "Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act." It requires trial courts to issue orders sealing (granting "clean slate relief" for) eviction records when an eviction proceeding is dismissed. Tenants may petition the trial court to seal records in specified circumstances (including dismissed cases that remain public; reversed, vacated, or unexecuted writs of restitution; nonpayment evictions during the COVID-19 emergency from March 13, 2020–June 30, 2021; cases naming a minor; or judgments violating existing statutory protections), or after three years from a writ or final judgment. Petitions are filed in the trial court, notice is required, parties have 30 days to object, and objections trigger a hearing with the objector bearing the burden. Orders are final and appealable. No state agencies, appropriations, or funding changes are specified. Operative date not provided in excerpt.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates the Residential Tenant Clean Slate Act (sections 1–6). Defines terms (clean slate relief = sealing under section 5; eviction proceeding; landlord; tenant; residential tenancy; trial court) and incorporates definitions from the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act and the Mobile Home Landlord and Tenant Act. Requires the trial court to immediately issue a sealing order under section 5 when an eviction is dismissed. Allows tenants to petition the trial court for sealing immediately in specified situations (e.g., vacated/reversed judgments, writ never executed, COVID-era nonpayment evictions Mar 13, 2020–Jun 30, 2021, named minor defendants, certain statutory violations) or otherwise after three years. Prescribes notice, a 30‑day objection period, hearings if objected (objector bears burden), and makes orders final and appealable. No state agencies or departments are added, and no funding or appropriations are included.
