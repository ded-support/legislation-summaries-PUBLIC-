Summary

LB559 creates new criminal offenses for skimmer devices and a continuing criminal enterprise involving financial transaction offenses and amends forfeiture procedures. Key government roles and fiscal provisions:

- Attorney General: administers the newly created Financial Fraud Victims' Reimbursement Fund, determines eligibility and reimbursement amounts, prescribes application form, and may distribute funds pro rata.

- State Investment Officer: invests fund assets pursuant to the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and the Nebraska State Funds Investment Act.

- State Treasurer: receives funds remitted after a retention period for distribution under Article VII, §5 of the Nebraska Constitution.

- Prosecuting authorities must specifically plead forfeiture in charging instruments; district courts handle pretrial/evidentiary forfeiture hearings and related procedures.

No direct appropriations are specified; the bill directs forfeited assets into the new fund with a 36-month retention/claims period before remittance.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds criminal provisions for skimmer devices and defines "financial transaction offense," and establishes the Financial Fraud Victims' Reimbursement Fund. Key agency roles and funding mechanics:

- The Attorney General will administer the Fund, determine victim eligibility, set reimbursement amounts, and prescribe application forms; distributions are pro rata if claims exceed available funds.

- The Fund consists of assets forfeited from financial transaction offenses (per §28-1601) and any investable money shall be invested by the state investment officer under the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and Nebraska State Funds Investment Act.

- Funds transferred into the Fund remain for at least 36 months (or until pending claims resolve); thereafter unclaimed funds are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Article VII, §5 of the Nebraska Constitution.

No direct appropriations are created; funding is from forfeited assets.
