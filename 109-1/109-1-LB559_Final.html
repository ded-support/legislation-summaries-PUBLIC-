Summary

LB559 adds financial transaction offenses (including unlawful installation/use of skimmer devices and a continuing criminal‑enterprise enhancement) to Nebraska’s forfeiture statute and creates a Financial Fraud Victims' Reimbursement Fund. The Attorney General will administer the Fund, which consists of assets forfeited from financial transaction offenses (per section 28‑1601). Money in the Fund may be invested by the state investment officer under the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and Nebraska State Funds Investment Act. Victims may apply to the Attorney General for reimbursement; the Attorney General determines eligibility and may prorate payments if funds are insufficient. Funds remain in the Fund for at least 36 months or until application resolution, after which remaining monies are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under the Nebraska Constitution. Prosecuting authorities must specifically plead forfeiture in charging papers; district courts conduct pretrial and post‑conviction evidentiary forfeiture hearings and may allow intervention.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates the Financial Fraud Victims' Reimbursement Fund, to be administered by the Attorney General. The fund will consist of assets forfeited from financial transaction offenses (per §28-1601(8)(b)(ii)). The state investment officer shall invest available funds pursuant to the Nebraska Capital Expansion Act and the Nebraska State Funds Investment Act. Victims may apply to the Attorney General for reimbursement; the AG determines eligibility and amounts, and will distribute pro rata if claims exceed available funds. Funds tied to a specific offense remain in the fund until the later of 36 months after transfer or resolution of applications filed in that period; afterward remaining funds are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Neb. Const. art. VII, §5. No separate appropriations or other budget allocations are specified.
