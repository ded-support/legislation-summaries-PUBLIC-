Summary

LB400 amends the Nebraska Workers' Compensation Act (section 48-1,110) to create rebuttable presumptions that certain cancers in firefighters are compensable as arising out of employment. Key provisions:

- Presumes cancers diagnosed during employment are work-related.

- For retired firefighters: a rebuttable presumption applies if diagnosis occurs within a period (not to exceed 60 months) beginning at last work date and extending three months per full year of service (applies to professional retirees before Jan 1, 2026, and all volunteer retirees).

- For professional firefighters retiring on/after Jan 1, 2026: presumption lasts post-separation equal to years worked if under 20 years’ service; if 20+ years, presumed at any time in life.

- Purchased retirement service credit cannot be used to calculate years of service for these presumptions.

- Post-retirement claims under the new post-2026 rule are limited to medical benefits only.

The bill repeals the original section 48-1,110. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds specific carcinogen findings and workers'‑comp presumptions for firefighters. Benzene and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons are listed as known carcinogens linked to uterine cancer; certain hydrocarbons are linked to urinary tract and ureteral cancers. The bill allows case‑by‑case inclusion of other agents, including those classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) as Group 1 or 2A. It creates rebuttable presumptions that cancers diagnosed during employment (and in specified post‑retirement windows) arose out of firefighting service, with differing timeframes for retirees (rules vary for retirements before/after Jan 1, 2026 and for volunteers). Purchased service credit cannot be used to calculate service years. Claims under the post‑retirement presumption are limited under the Nebraska Workers' Compensation Act to medical benefits only. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
