Summary

LB366 creates a Legislative Economic Analysis Unit and a Chief Economist. Agencies proposing covered rules must publish notice, submit a report and any cost‑benefit analysis (including jobs added/lost, public vs. private), list related regulatory actions and aggregate economic effects, and electronically provide materials to the Legislature and Chief Economist before filing with the Secretary of State. The Unit must report on each major rule to the relevant legislative committee within 15 legislative days assessing procedural compliance and regulatory impact. Major rules cannot be published until ratified by law or 60 legislative days pass without a final vote; the Governor may permit earlier emergency effective dates only with a published justification. Emergency rules remain effective 90 days (renewable once); agencies must file them with the Secretary of State and publish online. The bill amends Administrative Procedure Act definitions and allows courts to review whether statutory requirements for a rule’s effect have been met. No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural requirements for agencies and legislative bodies. Key agencies/departments mentioned: the Legislature (must designate an email/electronic address for rule delivery), the Secretary of State (publication filing), the Chief Economist (receive rule submissions), the Legislative Economic Analysis Unit (must report on each major rule to the relevant legislative committee within 15 legislative days), affected state agencies (must publish rules in a newspaper, submit copies, a concise statement, major/nonmajor classification, related actions, proposed effective date, and a cost‑benefit analysis including jobs added/lost and public vs. private jobs), the Governor (may seek early effect with a public statement), and the courts (may review compliance and whether a rule is “major”). No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included; “federal funding” is only cited as a possible justification for expedited effect.
