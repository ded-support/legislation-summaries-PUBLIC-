Summary

LB366 creates a Legislative Economic Analysis Unit and a Chief Economist within the Legislature to review regulatory economic impacts. State agencies (boards, commissions, departments, etc.) must publish proposed rules, notify the Legislature and Chief Economist, and submit cost‑benefit analyses (including public vs. private jobs impacts) and related information before filing rules with the Secretary of State. Agencies must classify rules as major or nonmajor. The Unit must report on each major rule to the subject committee within 15 legislative days, assessing procedural compliance, private‑sector impacts, and the regulatory impact analysis. Major rules cannot be published by the Secretary of State until ratified by the Legislature or 60 legislative days pass; the Governor may approve limited emergency early effective dates with required findings. The bill amends Administrative Procedure Act definitions and preserves limited judicial review. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures requiring agencies to electronically submit proposed rules to the Legislature and the Chief Economist and to publish notice in a statewide newspaper before filing with the Secretary of State. Agencies must provide copies, concise statements, a major/nonmajor classification with justification, related regulatory actions and their economic effects, proposed effective dates, and cost‑benefit analyses (including jobs added/lost, public vs. private). The Legislative Economic Analysis Unit must report on each major rule to the relevant legislative committee within 15 legislative days, assessing procedural compliance, regulatory impact, and effects on private‑sector mandates; agencies must cooperate. Major rules generally cannot be sent to the Secretary of State or take effect until ratified by the Legislature or after 60 legislative days, unless the Governor seeks earlier effect and publishes a written emergency/federal‑funding justification. Courts may review whether statutory requirements were completed. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
