Summary

LB366 creates a Legislative Economic Analysis Unit and a Chief Economist and amends the Administrative Procedure Act. Agencies (boards, commissions, departments, etc.) must publish proposed rules in a statewide newspaper and electronically submit to the Legislature and the Chief Economist a report (rule text, concise statement, major/nonmajor classification, related actions, proposed effective date) and any cost‑benefit analysis including job impacts before filing with the Secretary of State. The Unit must report on each major rule to the committee of jurisdiction within 15 legislative days, assessing procedural compliance and regulatory impacts; agencies must cooperate. Major rules may not be published until ratified by the Legislature or after 60 legislative days; emergency major rules require Governor approval with a published justification and are effective up to 90 days (renewable once). Courts may review whether statutory requirements for a rule’s effect or major classification were met. No funding or appropriation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the Legislature (within one month) to provide an email/electronic address for delivery of rules and related information. Before any agency files a rule with the Secretary of State, the agency must publish it in a statewide newspaper and electronically submit to the Legislature and the Chief Economist: the rule, a concise statement, major/nonmajor classification and rationale, related regulatory actions with individual/aggregate economic effects, proposed effective date, and a complete cost‑benefit analysis (including jobs added/lost, public vs. private). The Legislative Economic Analysis Unit must report on each major rule to the subject committee within 15 legislative days, assessing procedural compliance, private‑sector impacts, and regulatory impact. Major rules cannot be published until ratified by the Legislature or 60 legislative days pass (with limited Governor exceptions requiring a published justification). Courts may review whether procedural requirements were met and whether a rule is a major rule. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
