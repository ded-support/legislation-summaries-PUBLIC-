Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) must establish a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in metropolitan-class cities, operating 24/7 and terminating five years after the act's operative date. DHHS will design the center designation process, require comprehensive self-assessments and data systems, and conduct annual program evaluations of youth/family outcomes and juvenile justice impacts. Funding: designated centers may receive up to $500,000 per center annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund; if those funds are unavailable, no General Funds may be used. Up to 10% of pilot program funds (maximum $50,000/year) may be used for administration. The Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund statute is amended to permit funding the pilot.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

DHHS must create a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in metropolitan-class cities, operating 24/7, developed with local grassroots organizations and advisors, and ending five years after the act's operative date. DHHS will set designation standards, require centers to complete self-assessments against national family‑strengthening benchmarks, implement outcome data systems, provide ongoing post‑service support, and run annual program evaluations. Funding: designated centers may receive up to $500,000 per center annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund only; if those funds are unavailable, General Funds cannot be used. Up to 10% of program appropriations, capped at $50,000 per year, may be used for administration.
