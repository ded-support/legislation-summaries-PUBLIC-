Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) must establish a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in metropolitan-class cities (24/7 operation; developed with local partners; terminates five years after the act's operative date). DHHS will (1) set the designation process and quality/data requirements for centers and (2) conduct an annual program evaluation.

Funding provisions: each designated center may receive up to $500,000 per year from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund; if those funds are unavailable, no General Funds may be used. Up to 10% of program funds (maximum $50,000/year) may be used for pilot administration. The bill amends section 68-996 to authorize the pilot as an allowable use of that fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

DHHS must create a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in metropolitan-class cities, operating 24/7 and developed with local grassroots groups and advisors; the pilot ends five years after the act’s operative date. DHHS will set a designation process requiring centers to complete self-assessments (e.g., national Standards of Quality), implement outcome-focused data systems, and provide ongoing post‑service supports (alumni mentoring, check‑ins). Funding prioritizes local partnerships and, subject to availability, designated centers may receive up to $500,000 per center annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund; General Funds are prohibited if those funds aren’t available. Administration of the pilot is capped at 10% of program funds (max $50,000/year). DHHS must conduct annual evaluations of program impact.
