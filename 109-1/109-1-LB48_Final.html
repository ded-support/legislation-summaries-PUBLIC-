Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) is directed to establish a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in cities of the metropolitan class. The pilot must operate 24/7, be developed with local grassroots organizations and impacted youth/family advisors, and terminate five years after the act's operative date. DHHS must create the designation process for centers, require comprehensive self-assessments and data systems tied to national standards, and conduct an annual evaluation of program impact on youth, families, community trust, and juvenile justice involvement.

Funding: designated centers may receive up to $500,000 annually per center from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund (no General Funds if those funds are unavailable). Up to 10% of program funds, capped at $50,000/year, may be used for administration. The Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund statute is amended to include this pilot program as an authorized use.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

DHHS must create a Family Resource and Juvenile Assessment Center Pilot Program in metropolitan cities (24/7 operation; terminates five years after the act’s operative date). DHHS will set a designation process with quality standards, require centers to complete self‑assessments (e.g., national Standards of Quality), implement outcome data systems, and provide ongoing post‑service supports (mentoring, check‑ins). Funding: designated centers may receive up to $500,000 per center annually from the Medicaid Managed Care Excess Profit Fund; if those funds are unavailable, General Funds cannot be used. Administrative spending for the pilot is capped at 10% of program funds, up to $50,000 per year. DHHS must perform annual program evaluations to inform future policy.
