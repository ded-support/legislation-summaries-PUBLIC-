Summary

LB704 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 43-512.12 and 43-512.13 governing Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) review of Title IV-D child support orders. Key agency-related changes:

- DHHS must initiate review within 15 business days after learning a noncustodial parent will be incarcerated >180 days, notify both parents and the incarceration facility administrator (per §47-1003), and require financial submissions within 30 days.

- DHHS must complete review and notify parties within 120 calendar days and refer orders to the county attorney or other authorized attorney for district-court modification when criteria are met.

- If DHHS finds a decrease in support unwarranted after an incarceration-initiated review, it must offer the incarcerated parent a conference (in-person or virtual) within 30 days and issue a final determination within 30 days after the conference.

- References include Supreme Court child support guidelines and the Medical Assistance Act. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural duties for "the department" to review a noncustodial parent's child‑support order when the parent is to be incarcerated. Key agency/actor points:

- The department must require parties and the facility administrator (per §47‑1003) to submit financial information within 30 days of notice and complete the review within 120 calendar days, notifying parties under §43‑512.13.

- If no decrease is warranted, the department must offer the incarcerated parent a conference (in‑person or virtual) within 30 days of review; notices to both parents by first‑class mail must include findings, format, date/time/location, evidence deadlines (10 business days), waiver option (5 business days), etc.

- Within 30 days after the conference the department makes a final determination and may refer the matter to the county attorney or authorized attorney; waiver triggers a direct final determination.

- Notices to incarcerated parents must be sent to the facility address and facility administrator. Final determinations are not treated as contested cases under the Administrative Procedure Act.

- No funding or appropriation changes included.
