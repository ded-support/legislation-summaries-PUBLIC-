Summary

LB510 amends state sales-tax and Good Life district statutes and assigns specific duties to the Department (Department of Revenue). Key points for agencies and fiscal effects:

- Department: retains authority to approve/adjust/terminate Good Life districts, evaluate eligibility, accept evidence of investment commitments from applicants, cities, property owners or others, and transmit application/maps to affected cities or villages (with confidentiality limits). It may approve district development/design standards after a hearing. Termination limited to failure-to-commit thresholds (10% in 3 years; 50% in 7; 75% in 10) and not before three years.

- Sales tax: reduced state rate (2.75%) on transactions in Good Life districts continues, but commencing July 1, 2025 it applies only after the city or village has imposed a local sales or occupation tax of at least 2.75%.

- Cities/villages: must obtain voter approval before appropriating local revenues (including local option sales/use taxes and occupation taxes) to a Good Life District economic development program; election procedures specified.

- Applicants must certify anticipated state sales-tax diversions will be offset or exceeded by sales taxes generated by development. The act is effective on enactment.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Changes the state sales tax effective July 1, 2025: a general rate of 5.5%, but transactions within a portion of a Good Life district located inside a city or village are taxed at 2.75% if that city or village imposes a local sales or occupation tax of at least 2.75% on those transactions.

- Clarifies that, on and after July 1, 2025, transactions in a Good Life district receive the reduced state rate referenced in statute.

- Refers repeatedly to "the department" (agency administering Good Life district matters): it must accept applicant evidence and treat the property owner(s) or the city/village as the Good Life district applicant for statutory purposes, and it may not terminate a Good Life district until at least three years after establishment.

- Prohibits a city or village from using eminent domain to acquire property in a Good Life district for transfer or sale to a private party.
