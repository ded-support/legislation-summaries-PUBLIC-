Summary

LB510 amends sales-tax and Good Life district administration. It assigns the Department (Revenue) authority to approve applications, adjust boundaries, accept evidence of investment commitments, transmit application/approval materials to affected cities/villages (subject to confidentiality), and terminate districts only if investment commitments miss 10%/50%/75% thresholds at years 3/7/10. The bill conditions the post‑July 1, 2025 reduced state sales-tax rate within Good Life districts to apply only on portions inside a city/village corporate limit and only after that city/village has imposed a local sales or occupation tax of at least 2.75%. Cities must obtain voter approval to appropriate local revenue (including local option sales/use and occupation taxes) to Good Life district programs and file election resolutions with election commissioners/county clerks per statutory deadlines. Nebraska State Fair Board and public ownership exceptions remain; the act takes effect immediately.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Commencing July 1, 2025 the state sales tax rate (section 77-2703) becomes 5.5%, but transactions inside the portion of a Good Life district located within a city or village are taxed at 2.75%—only if that city or village has imposed at least a 2.75% local sales or occupation tax on those transactions.

- On and after July 1, 2025 transactions in a Good Life district are subject to the reduced state rate referenced in subdivision (7) of section 77-2701.02.

- "The department" (the administering state department for the Good Life Acts) must accept evidence from property owners or cities/villages and may treat such owner(s) or city/village as the Good Life district applicant for all statutory purposes, and may not terminate a Good Life district until at least three years after establishment.

- Cities/villages may not use eminent domain to acquire district property for transfer or sale to private parties.

- No new appropriations or budget allocations are specified beyond the tax-rate changes.
