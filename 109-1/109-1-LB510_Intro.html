Summary

LB510 amends revenue statutes governing Good Life Transformational Projects and Good Life Districts. Key agency roles and changes:

- "The department" (statutory reviewing authority under these chapters) retains sole authority to approve, modify boundaries, transmit application materials to affected cities/villages, hold hearings, adopt commercially reasonable development/design standards, and terminate districts only under specific investment‑commitment thresholds and not before three years.

- Cities/villages may become applicants, must approve boundary removals/standards in certain cases, and must obtain voter approval before appropriating local revenue to a Good Life District program.

- Nebraska State Fair Board facilities are excepted from gaming exclusions.

- Fiscal changes: state sales tax reductions for transactions in Good Life Districts are limited to portions within municipal corporate limits and, beginning July 1, 2025, apply only after the city/village has imposed at least a 2.75% local sales or occupation tax. Applicants must certify that any diverted state sales tax will be offset or exceeded by new sales tax on development. Emergency clause makes law effective on passage.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Beginning July 1, 2025, state sales tax treatment for Good Life Districts changes: the state rate (per §77-2703) becomes 5.5% generally, but transactions within the portion of a Good Life District inside a city or village are taxed at 2.75% — only after that city/village imposes a local sales or occupation tax of at least 2.75%. Transactions in Good Life Districts will be subject to the reduced state rate referenced in §77-2701.02(7). The bill directs "the department" to accept evidence from property owners or cities and to deem such parties the official Good Life District applicant for all related statutes, and prohibits the department from terminating a Good Life District within three years of its establishment. Cities or villages are prohibited from using eminent domain to acquire district property for transfer or sale to private parties. No explicit appropriations or other budget line-item funding changes are specified.
