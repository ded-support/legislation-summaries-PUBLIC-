Summary

The bill explicitly grants the Department of Agriculture authority to investigate all credible complaints that food products are misbranded or falsely advertised as meat products, regardless of whether a political subdivision or another state agency serves as the regulatory authority for food-establishment inspections. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department of Agriculture are included in the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions for "agricultural food animal," "cultivated‑protein," "insect‑protein," "plant‑protein," "manufactured‑protein," "identifying meat term," and "qualifying term." Imposes labeling and advertising rules for manufactured‑protein products: any identifying meat term must be accompanied nearby by a conspicuous qualifying term; products sold at food establishments must be stored separately from animal‑derived meat and clearly distinguished (e.g., distinctive shelf tags). Sets criteria for false advertising based on absence of qualifying terms or inadequate separation/identification at retail. Grants the Department of Agriculture authority to investigate credible complaints of misbranding or false advertising of meat products regardless of whether another state or local agency is the food‑inspection authority. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
