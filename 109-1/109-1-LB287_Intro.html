Summary

LB287 amends the Nebraska Housing Agency Act (section 71-1572) to impose specific bed-bug duties on housing agencies for cities of the metropolitan class. Agencies must visually inspect units before renting, not lease units known or reasonably suspected to be infested, disclose adjacent-unit treatments, and respond to complaints with acknowledgement (within 5 days), inspection by or procurement of a licensed pest control professional (within 5 days), remediation if infestation is confirmed (within 14 days), adjacent-unit inspections, tenant notice before entry, notice of determinations (within 5 days), and two-year retention of complaint/control records. The bill defines pest control professional as a commercial applicator under the Pesticide Act and makes the housing agency financially responsible for investigation and remediation costs. No specific appropriations or new budget allocations are included. Original section repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for the "housing agency for a city of the metropolitan class." Key points:

- Defines "bed bug" and "pest control professional" (must be a commercial applicator licensed under the Pesticide Act).

- Imposes pre-rental duties: visual inspection for bed bugs, prohibition on showing/renting units known or reasonably suspected to be infested, and disclosure to prospective tenants if adjacent units are infested or being treated.

- Sets response timelines after a complaint: acknowledge within 5 days; inspect or hire a pest control professional within 5 days; inspect adjacent units within 5 days; provide remediation within 14 days after infestation is confirmed; notify affected tenants of findings within 5 days; give advance notice before entering units; keep written records (including chemicals used) for two years.

- Fiscal change: the housing agency is made responsible for all costs of investigating and remediating bed bug infestations.
