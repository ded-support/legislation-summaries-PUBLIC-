Summary

LB628 adopts the Recreational Trail Easement Property Tax Exemption Act and amends filing and exemption procedures in §§76-214 and 77-202. It requires the Tax Commissioner to prescribe recording statements and the Property Tax Administrator to provide processing instructions; registers of deeds must forward statements to county assessors, who in turn forward them to the Tax Commissioner. The bill excludes most easements from the recording-statement requirement except conservation, preservation, and easements used to qualify for the new recreational-trail exemption. Section 77-202 adds a property-tax exemption for a portion of property pursuant to the new Act. The State Records Administrator retains records-disposition oversight. Operative date: January 1, 2026. No appropriations or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds standards and procedures for qualifying recreational-trail easements: alignment with public access and conservation goals; procedures to certify an easement’s public benefit (including required connectivity to existing or planned trail systems); and methods to ensure ongoing compliance with the statute’s public-purpose requirements. Also cross‑references easements and portions of property that qualify for the property tax exemption under the Recreational Trail Easement Property Tax Exemption Act.
