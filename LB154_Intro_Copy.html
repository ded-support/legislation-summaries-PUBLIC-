Summary

This bill revises the Hearing Instrument Specialists Practice Act and related Audiology and Speech‑Language Pathology provisions. It makes the Department (the state licensing department) the agency that issues and renews hearing‑instrument specialist and temporary training licenses, receives annual filings from organizations that employ licensed specialists, and retains practitioner records for seven years. It clarifies that audiologists credentialed by the State Department of Education and licensed audiologists are exempt from separate hearing‑instrument licensure in certain circumstances. The Hearing Instrument Specialists Board (five professionals, one public member) is adjusted in composition and makes licensure recommendations to the Department, including examination and training standards. The Board must consult the Board of Medicine and Surgery when adopting cerumen‑removal course requirements. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions give the state "department" (the licensing/health department) and the relevant licensing "board" expanded administrative roles: organizations employing licensed hearing instrument specialists must annually file lists and compliance statements on department forms; the department, with the board's recommendation, will determine eligibility for examinations, may waive exam components, approve/accept foreign training, prescribe forms (including receipts) and require seven-year record retention. The board must consult the Board of Medicine and Surgery before adopting rules for initial cerumen‑removal courses. Private certifying bodies (International Hearing Society, National Board for Certification in Hearing Instrument Sciences) are referenced for credentialing. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
