Legislative Bill 319 delegates several responsibilities to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) under the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). The Department is tasked with maximizing federal funding to minimize the use of state General Funds for SNAP and is required to employ necessary personnel to explore available federal options for Nebraska.

Additional provisions include the development of a state outreach plan conforming to the Food and Nutrition Service's criteria, which is part of the United States Department of Agriculture. DHHS may seek gifts, grants, or donations to fund the outreach initiative, which must be submitted to the Food and Nutrition Service for approval. If approved, it may secure federal matching funds, aiming to supplement state funds for increased caseloads due to the outreach plan implementation.

The bill explicitly prohibits the use of General Funds by the Department of Labor for any action regarding a SNAP benefit partnership program, focused on encouraging higher employment among recipients. This program requires no state General Funds but may act with federal or other non-state funding. 

Lastly, the DHHS is mandated to provide annual reports detailing the efficiencies and costs of these implementations to the Health and Human Services Committee of the Legislature.

The legislative text modifies eligibility criteria for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) related to individuals convicted of felonies involving controlled substances. According to the new provisions under 21 U.S.C. 862, individuals convicted of such felonies will now be eligible for SNAP benefits only if they have either completed their sentence or are currently serving parole, probation, or post-release supervision. This change pertains to policies administered by the Department of Agriculture, which oversees SNAP. The amendment does not include any direct changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations within the specified departments.
