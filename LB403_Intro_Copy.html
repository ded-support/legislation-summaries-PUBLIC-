Summary

Creates an Office of Grants in the executive branch, led by a Director of Grants appointed by the Governor (serving at the Governor’s pleasure) after a nationwide search; the Director must have at least ten years’ experience in writing or managing federal grants. The office may hire additional staff “for which adequate funding is available.” Duties: identify national (including federal) grant programs for state agencies, political subdivisions, and in-state private organizations; assist those entities with identification, application, and compliance; and by July 1, 2026 (and annually thereafter) adopt and update a statewide plan to maximize grant funding. The office must submit an annual electronic report to the Legislature summarizing the plan, legislative impacts on grant eligibility, entities served, and resulting awards and dollar amounts. No specific appropriations or budget allocations are set.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates an Office of Grants in the executive branch. The Governor appoints a Director (serving at the Governor’s pleasure) who must have ≥10 years’ experience with federal grants and be chosen after a nationwide search; the Director may hire staff “for which adequate funding is available.” The office will identify national/federal grant programs for state agencies, political subdivisions, and in-state private organizations and assist those entities with identification, applications, and compliance. It must produce a statewide plan to maximize grant funding by July 1, 2026, update it annually, and submit an annual electronic report to the Legislature (due July 1) summarizing the plan, analyzing effects of recent state legislation on federal grant eligibility, listing entities served, and reporting awards obtained and total dollars. No specific appropriations or budget allocations are provided.
