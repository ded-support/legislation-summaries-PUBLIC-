Summary

LB302 eliminates observance of daylight saving time in Nebraska and amends related statutes. The change: Nebraska would be exempt from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a) (no clock advancement) but that exemption becomes operative January 1 following enactment of matching laws in Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming. Section 49-1302 (current DST dates) is nullified once the exemption is operative.

Agencies and administrative effects:

- Secretary of State/election administration: polling hours and ballot-receipt deadlines remain set by mountain (7 a.m.–7 p.m.) and central (8 a.m.–8 p.m.) time zones.

- Department heads/employing agencies: vacation/sick leave accounting deadlines set at 11:59 p.m. central time on Dec. 31; employing agencies must reasonably allow use of leave or pay forfeited leave.

- Governor and Executive Board of the Legislative Council retain discretion to extend annual leave in special cases.

- Director of Personnel must adopt rules to administer leave provisions.

- Public Employees Retirement Board: cash payments for denied vacation are treated as compensation for defined contribution or cash-balance plans but not for other defined-benefit plans.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added language sets Nebraska's standard time per the Uniform Time Act of 1966 for both central and mountain zones but specifies Nebraska will not observe daylight saving time and will be exempt from 15 U.S.C. §260a(a) (federal time-advancement provision). The change becomes effective January 1 after Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming each adopt similar laws. The section is void if subsection (2) of §49-1301 becomes operative. No state agencies or departments are newly created or referenced, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.
