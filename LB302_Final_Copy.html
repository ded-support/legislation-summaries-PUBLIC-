Summary

LB302 eliminates Nebraska’s observance of daylight saving time by exempting the state from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a), with the change becoming effective January 1 following identical action by Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming. The bill harmonizes time references across statutes: election polling hours and ballot-deadline language remain tied to mountain/central zones, and sick‑leave and vacation‑leave account balancing deadlines are fixed at 11:59 p.m. central time on December 31. Administrative and oversight provisions reference: the Director of Personnel (rulemaking for vacation leave), the Public Employees Retirement Board (treatment of cash payments as compensation only for defined contribution/cash‑balance plans), the Governor and the Executive Board of the Legislative Council (discretion to extend leave in special cases), and employing agencies (obligation to provide or cash out forfeited vacation under conditions). No appropriations, funding changes, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a provision that Nebraska’s official time follows the Uniform Time Act in both its central and mountain zones but that the state will not observe daylight saving time and is exempt from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a) (the federal provision advancing time). The subsection becomes effective January 1 after Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming each adopt laws to likewise not observe DST. It also includes a clause voiding the section if subsection (2) of §49‑1301 becomes operative. No state agencies or departments are named, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
