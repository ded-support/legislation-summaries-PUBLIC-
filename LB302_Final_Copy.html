Summary

LB302 eliminates observation of daylight saving time in Nebraska (exempting the state from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a)) contingent on Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming adopting similar laws; section making DST effective becomes null when that contingency is met.

Agency-specific provisions and impacts:

- Director of Personnel: authorized to adopt rules to administer vacation/sick leave provisions.

- Employing agencies: must provide opportunity to use accrued vacation; if a reasonable written request is denied and leave is forfeited, the agency must pay the employee the cash equivalent within 30 days.

- Public Employees Retirement Board: cash payments for forfeited vacation count as compensation for defined contribution or cash balance plans only.

- Governor (or Executive Board of the Legislative Council for legislative employees): may extend annual leave in special cases.

- Beneficiaries: paid unused vacation upon an employee’s death.

No appropriations or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions set Nebraska’s standard time under the Uniform Time Act of 1966 but exempt the state from observing daylight saving time and from 15 U.S.C. 260a(a) (the federal provision authorizing advancing clocks). The exemption becomes effective January 1 following the date when Iowa, Kansas, South Dakota, and Wyoming each enact similar laws. The provision also states it becomes null and void if subsection (2) of §49-1301 becomes operative. No executive agencies or departments are named, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
