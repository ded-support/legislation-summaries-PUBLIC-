Summary

The bill designates the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) as "the department" and creates the Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act. DHHS must adopt rules by July 1, 2027 to set core competencies, application/approval/renewal processes and fees, disciplinary procedures, provider approval standards, and Administrative Procedure Act hearing rights; endorsements will not constitute licensure or permit licensed-professional tasks. For initial health-professional licensure, §38-131 is clarified to reference the Division of Public Health within DHHS for fingerprinting/FBI checks; the Nebraska State Patrol will report results to DHHS. DHHS must seek federal funds to offset fingerprint/background-check costs for child care staff (costs otherwise fall to applicants unless DHHS funding becomes available), may adopt implementing rules with the State Patrol, maintain a licensing complaint-tracking system, and adopt rules to include volunteers in child care staff definitions. No specific state appropriations or budget allocations are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions involving the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS):

- Establishes the Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act. DHHS must adopt rules by July 1, 2027 to define core competencies, approve/renew/deny endorsed CHW training programs, set reasonable application/renewal fees, and provide APA hearing rights. DHHS will align competencies with public health, behavioral health, workforce development, and medical assistance program requirements; endorsed programs qualify CHWs for Medicaid and private-insurer reimbursement.

- DHHS jointly with the relevant board will approve nurse practitioner fluoroscopy education/training and may adopt rules for pharmacy delivery/automated pickup kiosk provisions.

- For child care screening, DHHS may authorize provisional employment after qualifying fingerprint checks (per federal rule) and must adopt rules to include volunteers in staff‑to‑child ratios; certain provisions terminate Dec. 31, 2028.

No explicit appropriations, budget allocations, or new funding amounts are included; only authority to set fees.
