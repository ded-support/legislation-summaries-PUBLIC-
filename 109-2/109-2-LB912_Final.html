Summary

The bill centers duties for the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). It creates the Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act, defines “department” as DHHS, and requires DHHS by July 1, 2027 to adopt rules establishing CHW core competencies, application/approval/renewal processes and fees, denial/suspension/revocation procedures, program/provider approval, and administrative hearing rights; endorsed training is intended to qualify CHW services for Medicaid and private-insurer reimbursement but does not constitute licensure under the Uniform Credentialing Act. It clarifies that, for FBI purposes under §38-131, “department” means DHHS’s Division of Public Health. DHHS must receive national criminal-history reports, provide qualifying background-check results to child-care providers and staffing entities, seek federal funds to cover fingerprint/FBI-check costs (and may subsidize costs if funds are available), adopt related rules, and maintain a licensing-violations complaint tracker. No specific appropriations or dollar amounts are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) is designated as the administering agency for the new Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act. DHHS may, by July 1, 2027, adopt rules to: define and periodically update core CHW competencies; establish application, approval, renewal, denial/suspension/revocation procedures; set reasonable application/renewal fees; approve training programs/providers; and provide APA hearing rights. The Act enables endorsement to support Medicaid and private insurer reimbursement but does not create licensure. Separately, DHHS (jointly with the relevant board) must approve nurse practitioner fluoroscopy education/training and is authorized to adopt rules on pharmacy delivery procedures and child-care background-check/volunteer staffing provisions under child-care licensing. No new funding, appropriations, or specific budget allocations are included in the added text.
