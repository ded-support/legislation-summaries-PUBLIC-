Summary

LB1227 amends state law governing local building, energy, and electrical codes.

- Department of Water, Energy, and Environment (DWE&E): counties, cities, or villages must notify DWE&E within 30 days if they delete specified portions of the 2018 IBC/IRC. Localities must use DWE&E energy impact studies to determine whether a proposed new local energy code is "cost efficient" (defined as implementation costs for new buildings being less than five‑year energy cost savings). A locality may not adopt a new local energy code it determines is not cost‑efficient. Local authorities that waive specific Nebraska Energy Code requirements must submit their analyses to DWE&E, which will review and respond.

- State Electrical Board: local electrical codes remain required to conform to the Board's standards; local inspection by certified electrical inspectors is authorized and inspector certificates must be provided to the Board.

- Funding/fees: localities may set fees sufficient to cover costs under the energy code provisions. No state appropriations are changed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds two limits on local energy/electrical rules. First, counties, cities, and villages may not adopt or enforce local electrical codes that differ from standards set by the State Electrical Board under the State Electrical Act, though they may require inspections by a certified electrical inspector. Second, before adopting any new local energy code a locality must determine it is “cost efficient” (implementation costs < five‑year energy cost savings), using expected costs based on energy‑impact studies published by the Department of Water, Energy, and Environment. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
