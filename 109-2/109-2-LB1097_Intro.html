Summary

LB1097 creates the State and Political Subdivisions Sexual Abuse Liability Act, declaring that state agencies and political subdivisions are liable like private parties for torts arising from child sexual abuse and sexual abuse of individuals with developmental disabilities. It waives sovereign immunity for those claims but provides that no writ of execution may issue against the state, a state agency, or a political subdivision. The bill excludes actions under this new Act from the State Tort Claims Act and the Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act — removing those acts' caps, notice, jury, and statute‑of‑limitations rules for these claims. It amends definitions in sections 13‑903 and 81‑8,210 (affecting political subdivisions, state agencies, state employees, the State Claims Board, and the Risk Manager) to exclude such actions from the existing “tort claim” definitions. Section 25‑228’s statute‑of‑limitations rules are revised for victims and third‑party/entity suits. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Establishes the "State and Political Subdivisions Sexual Abuse Liability Act," making the state, state agencies, and political subdivisions liable like private parties for tort claims arising from child sexual abuse and sexual abuse of persons with developmental disabilities.

- Defines "state agency" (ref. §81-8,210), "political subdivision" (ref. §13-903), and employee terms by cross-reference to those sections.

- Permits victims (children or individuals with developmental disabilities) to bring tort claims directly against state agencies and political subdivisions.

- Waives sovereign immunity and exempts these actions from the State Tort Claims Act and Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act (including caps, notice rules, jury limits, and statutes of limitations).

- Provides that no writ of execution may issue against the state, state agencies, or political subdivisions for claims under this Act.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
