Summary

LB996 restricts the use of mechanical restraints on juveniles and amends provisions governing juvenile transportation policies. Key agency impacts:

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): DHHS policies and procedures on transporting juveniles placed at youth rehabilitation and treatment centers must prohibit shackling as defined in the bill and ban the routine use of mechanical restraints during transport. Exceptions must be narrowly tailored, documented in writing, and subject to review.

- Office of Juvenile Services (OJS): DHHS transportation policies apply to any private contractor used by OJS to transport juveniles at youth rehabilitation and treatment centers.

- The bill adds a statutory definition of "shackling" and limits permissible use to situations with recent behavior showing substantial risk, no less-restrictive alternative, and only for the strictly necessary duration.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition on shackling juveniles except when an authorized person reasonably determines a juvenile poses a substantial, recent risk of physical harm, no less‑restrictive alternative exists, and shackling is limited to the strictly necessary duration. Defines “shackling” (handcuffs, leg irons, waist/belly chains, restraint chair, etc.) but allows brief, temporary handcuffs for immediate articulable risk. Excludes restraints governed by section 43-251.03. Requires policies to bar shackling that violates this section and to prohibit routine mechanical restraints during juvenile transportation; any exceptions must be narrowly limited, documented in writing, and subject to review. No specific government agencies or departments are named and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are added.
