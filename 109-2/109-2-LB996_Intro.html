Summary

LB996 amends the Nebraska Juvenile Code to restrict shackling of juveniles and to require Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) transportation policies to extend to private contractors used by the Office of Juvenile Services (OJS) for transporting youth placed at youth rehabilitation and treatment centers. Section 1 prohibits shackling except where (1) a juvenile poses a recent, substantial risk of physical harm, (2) no less‑restrictive alternative will prevent the harm, and (3) shackling is limited to the minimum duration necessary. Shackling is defined to include handcuffs, leg irons, waist/belly chains, restraint chairs, etc.; only brief, temporary handcuffing for immediate articulable risk is allowed. DHHS policies must also prohibit routine mechanical restraints during transport; any exceptions must be narrowly limited, documented, and subject to review. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition on shackling juveniles except where narrowly necessary: authorized persons may use mechanical restraints only if the juvenile poses a recent, substantial risk of physical harm, no less-restrictive alternative exists, and restraints are limited to the time strictly required. Defines “shackling” and permits only very brief handcuffing for immediate safety. Requires policies to bar shackling in violation of the new rule and to prohibit routine mechanical restraints during transportation; exceptions must be documented and reviewed. No specific government agencies or departments are named, and no funding or appropriation changes are included.
