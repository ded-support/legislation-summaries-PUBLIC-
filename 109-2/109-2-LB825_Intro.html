Summary

LB825 amends the Mental Health Practice Act to add domestic-abuse training requirements and modifies provisions for provisional mental health practitioner licenses. It requires: (1) biennial minimum of two hours of continuing education in domestic abuse counseling for all licensed mental health practitioners, with trainers who meet specified qualifications (have prior required training, at least three years' experience with victims or offenders, and an additional 40 hours of domestic-abuse training); and (2) a three-hour domestic-abuse training requirement for provisional license applicants covering screening, lethality indicators, documentation, referrals/advocacy, child impact, ethics/boundaries, and trauma-informed safety planning. The bill preserves the board’s rulemaking role and the department’s promulgation of rules, sets a Jan. 1, 2027 operative date, and contains no funding or appropriation provisions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds mandatory domestic-abuse training requirements for licensed mental health practitioners under the Mental Health Practice Act: (1) every two years a minimum of two hours of continuing education in domestic abuse counseling; trainers must have completed the training required by §38-2123, have at least three years' experience with victims or offenders, and an additional 40 hours of related training; (2) requires a three-hour domestic-abuse training covering screening tools, high-lethality indicators, documentation, referrals/advocacy/systems navigation, impact on children, ethics/boundaries, and trauma‑informed, victim‑centered safety planning. Domestic abuse is defined by §42-903. No agencies, appropriations, or funding changes are specified.
