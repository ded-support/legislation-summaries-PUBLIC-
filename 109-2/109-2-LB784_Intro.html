Summary

LB784 amends sheriff residency, certification, and continuing-education requirements and clarifies multi-county appointments. Key agencies and changes:

- Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice: must issue law enforcement certificates/diplomas to sheriff candidates/appointees and award completion certificates for the Sheriff's Certification Course.

- Nebraska Law Enforcement Training Center: conducts the Sheriff's Certification Course; its director must certify issuance of certificates for filing.

- Nebraska Police Standards Advisory Council: may waive certification or accept equivalent training.

- County boards: may appoint a qualified Nebraska law enforcement officer from any county to fill a sheriff vacancy and contract for compensation; such appointees may serve multiple counties under that appointment.

- Law enforcement agencies: annual continuing education requirements specified in amended 81-1414.07 (24 hours/year; specified topics; triennial requirements).

Penalties: fines equal to a sheriff’s monthly salary per month of training noncompliance. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. Sections 4 and 7 operative Jan 1, 2027.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds residency and training requirements for county law enforcement. Key points:

- Sheriff residency: In counties without a metropolitan/primary/first-class city, a candidate need not reside in the county when filing, but must reside in that county or an adjoining county while in office.

- Training: Law enforcement officers (except noncertified conditional officers) must complete 24 hours of continuing education annually. At least once every three years that training must include refresher courses on de‑escalation, mental‑health and substance‑abuse issues; a minimum of two hours of anti‑bias/implicit‑bias training; officer wellness training; and a review of vehicular pursuit policies. Those hours count toward other required training totals.

- No agencies/departments are newly created or renamed, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
