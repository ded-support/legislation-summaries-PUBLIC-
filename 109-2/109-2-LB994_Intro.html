Summary

LB994 assigns multiple specific duties to the Department of Correctional Services (the department):

- Recalculate parole eligibility dates for all committed offenders incarcerated on or after July 1, 2011 by April 15, 2027, in coordination with the Board of Parole using phased implementation; administrative delays may not alone deny parole eligibility; affected parolees discharged before the act may request reduced parole terms.

- Within 60 days of initial classification, develop department‑approved personalized program plans with offenders and provide required programming (education, treatment, work, etc.).

- Apply and administrate good‑time and sentence reductions (six months per year; three days/month for compliance), including rules for credit application, forfeiture, withholding, and restoration.

- Ensure release/reentry planning at 80% of sentence, provide community‑standard mental‑health care, evaluate mentally ill offenders pre‑release, create parolee case plans within 30 days of parole, and share incarceration records with probation for post‑release supervision planning.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Correctional Services must, by April 15, 2027, recalculate parole-eligibility dates for all committed offenders incarcerated on or after July 1, 2011, to reflect the bill’s changes to section 83-1,107. Recalculations are to be done in coordination with the Board of Parole, using a phased schedule to avoid administrative overload and protect public safety. No one may be denied parole eligibility solely because recalculation is delayed administratively. Individuals on parole who were discharged after July 1, 2011 and before the act’s effective date may ask the Board to reduce their parole term if affected. The bill clarifies that good-time reductions apply to tentative release and parole-eligibility dates (including non‑mandatory minimum terms and related maximums). No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes for the Department are included.
