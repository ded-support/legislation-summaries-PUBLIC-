Summary

- Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV): directs the DMV to design and issue Military Honor Plates, verify applicant eligibility by consulting the Department of Veterans' Affairs registry (section 80-414), provide application forms via county treasurers, and remit collected fees to the State Treasurer for credit as specified. DMV may charge postage/handling for mailed plates and receives credit to the Department of Motor Vehicles Cash Fund for certain fees (postage/handling, a $3 transfer fee, and 25% of the $40 personalized-plate fee).

- Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA): DMV eligibility verification is tied to the DVA registry; applicants may qualify by registering with the DVA pursuant to section 80-414.

- Military Department: creates the Military Department Aid Fund, administered by the Adjutant General, funded by gifts/grants/legislative transfers and monies credited under section 60-3,122.04 (including 75% of the $40 personalized-plate fee). The fund is investable by the state investment officer; interest and gains credit to the fund. The Adjutant General may award nonreimbursable grants to needy Military Department uniformed service members and must adopt qualifying rules by January 1, 2027. If plate manufacturing costs exceed statutory plate fees, the excess is credited first to the Highway Trust Fund before the designated recipients.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Motor Vehicles: directed to create license-plate designs honoring each armed force and persons who have served. Fees for specific plates are allocated to designated recipients.

- Department of Veterans' Affairs: license-plate fee revenue for certain plates is designated to the Nebraska Veteran Cemetery System Operation Fund.

- Military Department: creates the Military Department Aid Fund, administered by the Adjutant General. Fund sources include gifts, grants, bequests, public/private money, amounts credited under section 60-3,122.04, and legislative transfers. The state investment officer may invest available balances; interest and gains return to the fund. Uses are limited to this statute; the Adjutant General may award non‑repayable grants to Military Department members under rules to be adopted by Jan 1, 2027, which must limit eligibility to needy uniformed service members.
