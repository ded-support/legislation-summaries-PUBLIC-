Summary

This bill (LB958) places the Nebraska Prenatal Plus Program within the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), adds a minimum of two breastfeeding support sessions to eligible prenatal services, and requires DHHS to electronically report annually to the Legislature (by December 15 each year through 2034) on mothers served, services offered, and birth outcomes. The Legislature intends the state share of doula service reimbursement to come from the vital statistics subfund of the Health and Human Services Cash Fund. For the low-income home energy assistance program, DHHS must use 150% of federal poverty guidelines for eligibility and may make crisis assistance payments up to $800 per program year (with authority to exceed for extenuating circumstances).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions require the Department of Health and Human Services to: communicate waiver eligibility, tier, assessment results, and appeal rights clearly and promptly; base waiver services on individualized, person‑centered medical/functional assessments and ensure services prevent unnecessary institutionalization; conduct immediate supervisory review before implementing any reduction in tier, hours, or services; and submit reports to legislative oversight by Aug. 1, 2026 and Aug. 1, 2027. The department must provide the maximum retroactive Medicaid coverage allowed under section 71112 of Public Law 119‑21 (as of Jan 1, 2026). New, detailed reporting on community‑engagement requirements and related vendor contracts is required. A doula workgroup/definition is added, with legislative intent to fund the state share of doula reimbursements from the Vital Statistics subfund of the Health and Human Services Cash Fund. Crisis assistance payments are capped at $800 per program year, with exceptions for extenuating circumstances.
