Summary

LB1232 amends Nebraska’s Game Law to authorize limited mountain lion permits under the same framework used for other limited permits. The commission (the agency administering the Game Law) may issue limited mountain lion permits to qualifying landowners, leaseholders, or their immediate family, valid for hunting within the applicant’s mountain lion management unit. Eligibility mirrors elk rules: resident owners of ≥320 acres, resident lessees/combined ≥640 acres, and land within a designated mountain lion management zone. The commission may set documentation, application, and designation rules; may prioritize applicants who did not receive permits previously; and limits one qualifying person per property. Resident application fees for landowner mountain lion permits are capped at no more than one-half the regular permit fee. Issuance in any mountain lion management unit is capped at 75% of regular permits. No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds criteria and limits for resident landowner "limited" mountain lion permits. The bill gives "the commission" authority to designate mountain lion management zones and to adopt rules (including identifying qualifying landowners via partnerships/corporations/trusts) and to prefer applicants who did not receive a permit in prior years. It caps the resident landowner limited-permit application fee at no more than one-half the regular mountain lion permit application fee. It limits applications per farm/ranch based on acreage (acreage ÷ minimum acreage), bars more than one qualifier per described property, and caps limited permits in a management unit at 75% of the regular permits. No new agencies, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.
