Summary

LB1146 amends §79-209 to tighten school attendance reporting and intervention procedures. School districts must have a written attendance policy developed and reviewed annually in collaboration with the county attorney in the county of the district’s principal office. Superintendents, principals, teachers, or board members must report suspected violations to the school attendance officer, who must investigate within three days. The bill requires provision of services after twenty days of absence, outlines required collaborative meetings (involving attendance officers, school social workers or administrators, caregivers, and the student when appropriate), and allows referral to the county attorney only after documented interventions have failed and the student has accrued 20+ unexcused absences. Schools must notify families in writing before referral; failure to document required efforts is a defense to prosecution. Exceptions include illness, certain pregnancy/parenting policies, IEP/IFSP/504/McKinney-Vento cases. No funding or appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds exclusions from "unexcused" absences for: parent/guardian/educational-decisionmaker‑excused illness per Neb. §79‑530; pregnant/parenting students per policy under Neb. §79‑2,151; and students with an IFSP, an IEP under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (20 U.S.C. 1400 et seq.), Section 504 accommodations (29 U.S.C. 794), state-defined special education (Neb. §79‑1125), or McKinney‑Vento homelessness services (42 U.S.C. 1141 et seq.) when absences relate to those plans/services. Federal programs referenced are administered/overseen by the U.S. Department of Education (including the Office for Civil Rights for Section 504). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
