Summary

LB1146 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §79-209 (truancy reporting). Key provisions affecting government actors:

- School districts/school boards must adopt and annually review a written attendance policy developed in collaboration with the county attorney of the county where the district’s principal office is located.

- Superintendents, principals, teachers, school boards, and attendance officers retain reporting and investigatory duties; school social workers and administrators are specified participants in required intervention meetings.

- Services to address attendance barriers must be provided upon twenty days of absence and include written notice to families and collaborative meetings to develop an intervention plan (including possible educational evaluation and referrals to community agencies or restorative justice).

- A school may refer a child to the county attorney after documented intervention efforts and 20+ unexcused absences; specified exceptions (parent-excused illness, pregnancy/parenting policies, IFSP/IEP/Section 504/special education/McKinney-Vento-related absences) are excluded from “unexcused.”

- Schools must notify families in writing before referral; failure to document required efforts or illness that makes attendance impracticable is a defense to prosecution/adjudication. County attorneys may be involved at any stage.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds exceptions to "unexcused absences," excluding absences: excused by a parent/guardian or the "educational decisionmaker" (per state section 79-530) for illness; for pregnant/parenting students in line with a policy under state section 79-2,151; or when tied to federally or state‑provided special services — specifically IFSPs, IEPs under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (20 U.S.C. 1400 et seq.), accommodations under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (29 U.S.C. 794), state-defined special education (section 79-1125), or services under the McKinney‑Vento Education for Homeless Children and Youth program (42 U.S.C. 1141 et seq.). Implicated entities: state education law provisions and federal programs administered/overseen by the U.S. Department of Education. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included.
