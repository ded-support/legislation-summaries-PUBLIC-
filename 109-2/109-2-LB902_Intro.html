Summary

LB902 explicitly defines "Department" as the Department of Correctional Services and assigns multiple duties and reporting relationships involving the department. The medical director must develop standardized medical-care protocols to be used by the department for inmates with chronic or acute conditions. The department must promptly report deaths, serious injuries, and employee hospitalizations occurring in custody or while acting as department employees to the Inspector General, and must notify the Inspector General when related criminal investigations begin and end. The Inspector General must audit the department’s compliance with the Medical Standards for Incarcerated Individuals Act (first audit due by May 15, 2028, and annually thereafter) and deliver investigation reports to the Director of Correctional Services within three business days after legislative submission. The Director of Correctional Services has 30 business days to respond and must provide a corrective action plan. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) medical director to develop and implement standardized medical-care protocols for inmates’ chronic and acute conditions (ongoing monitoring; timely access to medications, diagnostics and specialists; documentation/tracking; routine screening). DCS will be subject to annual compliance audits of those protocols (due May 15, starting 2028) by the Inspector General/office; the IG must present audit reports to the Director of Legislative Oversight and to the Director of Correctional Services (within three business days). The DCS Director has 30 business days to accept/reject/request modifications and must submit a corrective action plan. Death investigations with suspected medical neglect require a public IG report within 15 days after specified steps. Annual public summary reports (redacted) are due Oct. 1 each year. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes are included.
