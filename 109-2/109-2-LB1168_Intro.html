Summary

LB1168 (2026) amends the Community Development Law to allow redevelopment authorities to issue conduit revenue bonds and to enter into taxpayer agreements that can (a) guarantee or secure bond payments (including shortfalls in pledged real‑estate tax revenues) and (b) create property‑tax‑like liens enforceable under section 77‑203. Authorities may pledge up to 100% of annual excess tax revenues from tax‑increment financing to pay such bonds. Conduit revenue bonds are carved out from the statute treating authority bonds as general obligations and tax‑exempt. The bill affirms obligees’ rights to enforce taxpayer agreements, mortgages, and pledges against authority property, rents, fees, grants, or revenue. Procedural changes affect city bodies, planning commissions, county assessors, county boards of equalization, county clerks/treasurers, the Property Tax Administrator (annual reports to the Legislature), and the Tax Equalization and Review Commission. Notices to county assessors must be filed by July 1 of the effective year.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates authority power to issue conduit revenue bonds for redevelopment and to pledge up to 100% of annual excess ad valorem (property) tax revenues into an authority special fund solely to pay bond principal, interest, and premiums; any unpledged taxes continue to flow to the respective public bodies. Allows an authority issuing such bonds to enter taxpayer agreements that (a) limit challenges to tax assessments and/or (b) guarantee or otherwise secure bond payments (including covering tax shortfalls) when the taxpayer's property is inside the redevelopment area and taxes are subject to division. Payments under such agreements that create a property tax lien are treated as property taxes under section 77-203. Those liens have parity with property tax liens, take priority over mortgages and other encumbrances, and may be enforced and collected as real property taxes. No specific state agency is named beyond “authority” and “public bodies.”
