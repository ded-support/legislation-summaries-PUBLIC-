Summary

LB1168 (Community Development Law) — key agency and fiscal provisions:

- Authorizes redevelopment authorities to issue conduit revenue bonds (secured by taxpayer agreements) and to pledge up to 100% of excess tax-increment revenues to pay those bonds.

- Permits authorities to enter taxpayer agreements with property owners that (a) limit assessment challenges or (b) guarantee/secure conduit bonds; such obligations may be treated as property taxes under section 77-203 if the agreement creates a property tax lien. Those liens take priority over mortgages and are enforceable like real property taxes.

- Modifies enforcement/exemption rules: obligees may foreclose or enforce taxpayer agreements, mortgages, pledges, or liens against authority property.

- Reporting and notice roles: cities must annually report redevelopment plans to the Property Tax Administrator (compiled and transmitted to the Clerk of the Legislature); authorities must notify county assessors (forms prescribed by the Property Tax Administrator) by July 1 of an effective tax-division date. County assessor/board of equalization, county clerk, county treasurer, and the Tax Equalization and Review Commission retain prescribed valuation and appeal processes.

- Metropolitan-class cities: tax-increment funds for qualifying housing must make best efforts to allocate ≥30% to single-family housing and use a qualified allocation plan prioritizing lowest-income occupants.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority for a redevelopment authority to issue conduit revenue bonds and pledge up to 100% of annual excess ad valorem tax revenues to a special authority fund to pay bond principal, interest, and premiums. Any unpledged ad valorem taxes revert to the funds of the respective public bodies. Permits the authority to enter taxpayer agreements that limit tax-assessment challenges or guarantee/enhance bonds (including covering shortfalls). Payments under such taxpayer agreements, if the agreement creates a property tax lien, are to be treated as property taxes for purposes of section 77-203. Liens from these agreements have priority over mortgages and other encumbrances, have parity with section 77-203 property tax liens, and may be enforced and collected as real property taxes.
