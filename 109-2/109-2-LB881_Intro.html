Summary

LB881 requires prior governmental approval before Nebraska law enforcement agencies, county or city jails, and the Nebraska State Patrol may enter immigration‑enforcement agreements.

- Local law enforcement and jails must notify the appropriate local governing body at least 30 days before an agreement, obtain approval after a public hearing (Open Meetings Act), and provide the Nebraska Commission on Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice copies of the agreement, the notice, and meeting minutes within 60 days. Pre‑July 1, 2026 agreements automatically terminate Dec. 1, 2026 unless notice is given by Oct. 15, 2026 and the local governing body approves.

- The Nebraska State Patrol must notify and obtain approval from the Commission (30‑day notice); preexisting Patrol agreements follow the same termination/notice/approval deadlines and the Patrol must file copies with the Commission within 60 days.

Agreements entered without required approval are void. Violations count as Open Meetings Act violations; residents may sue under section 84‑1414. No appropriations or funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements that law enforcement agencies and jails must notify and obtain approval from the appropriate local governing body before entering immigration‑enforcement agreements, with written notice at least 30 days before the agreement would take effect. Agreements entered before July 1, 2026, automatically terminate Dec 1, 2026 unless the agency notifies the local body by Oct 15, 2026 and the body approves. The local governing body must place the notice on its next regular public‑meeting agenda, hold a hearing under the Open Meetings Act, and may approve or disapprove. Within 60 days of entering an agreement, agencies/jails must file the agreement, the notice, and meeting minutes with the commission. The Nebraska State Patrol is subject to parallel notice/approval, termination, and a 60‑day filing requirement to the commission. Violations render agreements void and are Open Meetings Act violations; residents may sue in district court. No funding or appropriations changes.
