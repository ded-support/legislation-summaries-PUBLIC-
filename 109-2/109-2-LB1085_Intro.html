Summary

LB1085 amends statutes governing the Nebraska Liquor Control Commission (the commission) and local clerks/governing bodies regarding retail, bottle club, craft brewery, and microdistillery licenses. Key agency-focused changes:

- The commission may waive the 45‑day objection period and issue licenses (signed by chairperson, attested by executive director) and may deliver licenses electronically to city/village/county clerks or licensees contingent on local fee/tax confirmation.

- The commission’s license‑approval factors now explicitly include whether the applicant or affiliated entities are delinquent on federal, state, or local taxes (affiliated entities defined).

- The commission may overturn a local governing body’s denial of a retail application only if the applicant proves the denial was arbitrary and capricious and the proposed premises do not include an area with a craft brewery or microdistillery license.

- Hearing notice and protest procedures clarified; adequacy of law enforcement remains a consideration.

- Criminal penalties for unlicensed activity are restated: certain violations — first offense Class IV misdemeanor, second/subsequent Class II misdemeanor; unlicensed manufacture or retail sale — first offense Class I misdemeanor, second/subsequent Class IV felony.

No appropriations, funding, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds standards for retail liquor license review: the commission (under the Nebraska Liquor Control Act) may consider evidence that a city, village, or county acted arbitrarily and capriciously when recommending denial. After a hearing, the commission may approve a retail-license application despite a local recommendation of denial if the proposed premises do not include an area with a craft brewery or microdistillery license and the applicant proves the local entity acted arbitrarily and capriciously. The bill also requires inquiry into whether the applicant or any affiliated entities are delinquent on federal, state, or local taxes; “affiliated entities” is defined as other corporations, partnerships, or LLCs that hold a liquor license under the Nebraska Liquor Control Act in which the applicant has an ownership interest. No funding or appropriation changes are included.
