Summary

LB1235 amends liquor and medical cannabis regulation. Key agencies/departments and fiscal changes:

- Nebraska Liquor Control Commission (NLCC): processes entertainment-district and consumption-only license applications. A $25 consumption-only license fee is paid to the commission and remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the Nebraska Liquor Control Commission Rule and Regulation Cash Fund. Annual entertainment-district supplemental fee of $300 is payable to the local clerk. NLCC retains notification/issuance roles and licensing oversight.

- Local governing bodies and clerks: designate entertainment districts, approve consumption-only areas, collect the $300 fee, may impose occupation taxes within statutory limits, and may adopt ordinances or revoke designations.

- Nebraska Medical Cannabis Commission: created with 3–5 members (three NLCC members ex officio; up to two Governor-appointed, Legislature-confirmed). Members receive $12,500 annual salary. A Nebraska Medical Cannabis Commission Cash Fund is created (receipts from legislative transfers, fees, gifts, grants; excludes civil penalties) to fund administration/enforcement; funds may be invested by the state investment officer under existing acts. The commission may set fees (cap $50,000) and requires FBI criminal-history checks via the Nebraska State Patrol (fee payable to the patrol).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Nebraska Liquor Control Commission: adds a consumption-only class of entertainment-district license. Applicants submit a $25 fee to the commission, which remits it to the State Treasurer for credit to the Nebraska Liquor Control Commission Rule and Regulation Cash Fund. The commission issues licenses if the local governing body designated consumption-only areas and preapproved the applicant.

- Nebraska Medical Cannabis Commission: members receive $12,500 annual salary (paid in equal installments). Creates the Nebraska Medical Cannabis Commission Cash Fund, funded by legislative transfers and all fees, gifts, grants, and other money (excluding civil penalties). The fund finances administration and enforcement and may be invested by the state investment officer under existing state investment law. The commission may set fees up to $50,000. Applicants must submit two fingerprint sets to the FBI via the Nebraska State Patrol, with the criminal-history-check fee payable to the Patrol.
