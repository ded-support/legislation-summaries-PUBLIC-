Summary

LB831 establishes procedural and pleading requirements for civil actions alleging exposure to ethylene oxide. Key provisions require: an affidavit from a qualified expert detailing exposure, dose-response, and causation; a statement with the complaint specifying each element of claimed damages and the factual basis for calculations; and, for claims dependent on a particular state of mind, factual allegations creating a strong inference the defendant acted with that state of mind. Sections 1–5 create an exclusive cause of action and apply to cases pending on the effective date and to actions filed thereafter. The bill does not reference any state agencies or departments and contains no funding, appropriation, or budgetary provisions.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds pleading requirements for ethylene oxide exposure suits: complaints must itemize each element of damages and factual basis; claims requiring proof of a particular state of mind must plead facts creating a strong inference of that mental state. Declares sections 1–5 to be the exclusive cause of action and the sole means by which a plaintiff may prevail. Applies these rules to cases pending on the act’s effective date and to those filed afterwards. No government agencies, departments, or funding/appropriations changes are referenced.
