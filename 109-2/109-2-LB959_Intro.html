Summary

This bill requires the Department of Health and Human Services to establish two programs:

- A youth afterschool credential for 16–17 year olds seeking work in school-age or temporary nonresidential child care. Requirements: registry and background checks, submission of background documentation, and a minimum seven-hour approved new-staff orientation. Provisional employment while checks pend is allowed with restrictions (not left alone with children, excluded from staff-to-child ratios, limited program types). Credentials expire after one year and may be renewed via an expedited process. The department may set rules, fees, training approval, and privacy/data-sharing protections.

- An adult eligibility passport for persons 18+ to verify program-ready status and portable background-check compliance (including fingerprint-based criminal history). Passports are valid one year, renewable annually, auto-updated if new check data appears, and the department may charge fees up to actual administration cost and adopt implementing rules.

No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services must create (1) a youth afterschool credential for 16–17-year-olds seeking work in school‑age or specified temporary nonresidential child care, and (2) an adult eligibility passport for adults 18+. Requirements: registry and criminal checks, background documentation, and a minimum seven‑hour new‑staff orientation for youth applicants; fingerprinting for adults. Youth applicants may work provisionally (not alone with children, not counted in ratios, limited to specified programs) while checks pend. Credentials/passports are valid one year, renewable via expedited processes; the department will accept them as portable verification across licensed child‑care and approved youth programs. The department may set rules (application, renewals, fees, training approval, privacy). An adult passport fee may be charged but limited to administrative cost. No appropriations or budget allocations are specified.
