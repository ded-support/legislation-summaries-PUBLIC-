Summary

LB766 locates the Charitable Gaming Division within the Department of Revenue and requires annual electronic reports to the Governor, Legislature, Auditor, and Attorney General on tax revenue received, expenses, and related activities. It creates the Charitable Gaming Operations Fund: 40% of taxes collected under specified sections are made available to the Charitable Gaming Division for administration/enforcement and to the State Racing and Gaming Commission to provide administrative support to the Nebraska Commission on Problem Gambling; the remaining 60% is transferred to the General Fund. The bill directs quarterly transfers of $100,000 from the Charitable Gaming Operations Fund to the Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund through June 30, 2026, and permits remaining fund balances to be transferred to the General Fund and the Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund per legislative direction. It authorizes Tax Commissioner investigators for enforcement, requires authorized gaming operators to remit winnings setoff deductions to the Department of Revenue (per section 9-1306), and allocates certain taxes and fees to the Operations Fund for division use.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No provisions explicitly amend the Department of Revenue’s duties, budget, or appropriations.

Key additions affecting funding flows:

- Licensees must remit monies deducted (per §2-1207) to the official breed registrars for distribution; registrars must annually spend at least 80% of those deductions on purse supplements and breeder/stallion awards.

- Failure to register an eligible Nebraska-bred horse within 90 days triggers an administrative fee up to $1,000 payable to the applicable official registrar; fees are earmarked to cover the registrar’s administrative costs.

- Adds a requirement for the Racing and Gaming Commission to consult quarterhorse breeders before approving certain interstate simulcasts and to collaborate with the Nebraska Commission on Problem Gambling regarding annual gaming tax proceeds (citing §§9-1204, 9-1006), but it does not specify changes to Revenue Department allocations.
