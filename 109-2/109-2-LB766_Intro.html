Summary

LB766 assigns administration and enforcement of charitable gaming laws to a newly created Charitable Gaming Division within the Department of Revenue and requires the Department to annually report tax revenue, expenses, and related activities to the Governor, Legislature, Auditor, and Attorney General (Legislature report submitted electronically). It creates the Charitable Gaming Operations Fund. Taxes collected under sections 9-239, 9-344, 9-429, and 9-648 are split: 40% available to the Charitable Gaming Division for administration/enforcement and to the State Racing and Gaming Commission for administrative support of the Nebraska Commission on Problem Gambling; 60% transfers to the General Fund. Quarterly transfers ($100,000) from that fund to the Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund are specified through June 30, 2026. The Tax Commissioner (Department of Revenue) may employ investigators with limited law-enforcement authority. Authorized gaming operators/licensees must remit setoff deductions from winnings to the Department for credit against state debts. Taxes under section 77-3012 fund Mechanical Amusement Device enforcement and central server maintenance.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No direct changes to the Department of Revenue’s appropriations, budget, or explicit duties are added. The amendments:

- Allow the Racing & Gaming Commission to waive/modify racetrack requirements for events (e.g., disasters).

- Require breed registrars to spend ≥80% of certain deductions on purses/awards and permit a $1,000 administrative fee for late horse registration payable to the official registrar (not the Dept. of Revenue).

- Reference annual gaming tax proceeds (sections 9-1204, 9-1006) to be directed to the State Racing and Gaming Commission to collaborate with the Nebraska Commission on Problem Gambling. The bill does not alter the Department of Revenue’s collection or appropriation role explicitly.
