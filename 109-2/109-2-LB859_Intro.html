Summary

LB859 creates and integrates a new county conflict counsel role across Nebraska statutes. Counties may employ or contract for county conflict counsel and must notify presiding district, county, and juvenile court judges, child support referees, and the district court clerk/mental health board upon hiring. District, county, and juvenile courts, and mental health boards are directed to appoint county conflict counsel to represent indigent clients when the public defender is unavailable, except for conflicts or other good cause. In counties over 170,000, county conflict counsel must serve full time and may not privately practice. County conflict counsel must provide representation like the public defender and may only be paid county compensation. County boards are required to allow payment of fees and expenses as fixed by courts; no new statewide appropriations are specified. The Commission on Public Advocacy and Supreme Court rulemaking references are updated accordingly.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition and duties for “county conflict counsel” — attorneys employed by or under contract with a county to represent indigent clients when the public defender has a conflict or other good cause prevents representation. Counties with a public defender may hire one or more conflict counsel; when first hired the county board must notify: presiding judges of the county and district courts, presiding juvenile court judges, child support referees, and the district court clerk (for the local mental health board). Conflict counsel must provide the same indigent-defense services as the public defender, may not solicit private fees (only county compensation), and in counties over 170,000 must serve full time and not engage in private practice. No new appropriations or statewide funding changes are specified.
