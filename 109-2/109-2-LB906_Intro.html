Summary

LB906 amends the State Tort Claims Act to state that, in cases of adoption or placement, claims for misrepresentation or deceit by the Department of Health and Human Services are actionable where DHHS failed to warn, notify, or inform regarding a ward's mental and behavioral health history, educational history, and medical history, including any history as a victim or perpetrator of sexual abuse. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for DHHS are included in the bill.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added language sets definitions and restricts law-enforcement use of facial coverings, lists specific safety and operational exceptions (disease-prevention masks, protection from bodily fluids/toxins/smoke, combat/hostage/terror situations, clear face shields, SCBA for underwater work, motorcycle helmets, retinal-protection eyewear), makes violations a Class III misdemeanor (unless the officer’s agency has a compliant policy), requires each law enforcement agency to post a written facial-covering policy by Oct. 1, 2026, and renders evidence collected while an officer violated the rule inadmissible in most proceedings (with narrow due-process and self‑defense exceptions). There is no assignment of duties to the Department of Health and Human Services and no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for DHHS.
