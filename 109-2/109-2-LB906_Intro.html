Summary

LB906 amends the State Tort Claims Act (section 81-8,219) to create a specific exception for the Department of Health and Human Services: although claims for misrepresentation or deceit are generally excluded, the Act will apply to claims alleging that DHHS, in adoption or placement cases, failed to warn, notify, or inform about a ward's mental and behavioral health history, educational history, medical history, or any history as a victim or perpetrator of sexual abuse. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are addressed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions define “facial covering,” permit law enforcement officers to conceal identity only for covert operations, and list specific exceptions allowing facial coverings (including masks/respirators to prevent airborne disease, protection from bodily fluids, smoke/toxins, physical-harm situations, clear shields, SCBAs for underwater work, motorcycle helmets, and protective eyewear). Violations are a Class III misdemeanor unless the officer’s agency has an adopted policy. By Oct. 1, 2026, each agency must publicly post a facial-covering policy. Evidence gathered while an officer violated the rule is generally inadmissible, with limited due-process and self-defense exceptions.
