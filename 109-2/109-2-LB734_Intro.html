Summary

LB734 directs the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to maximize federal SNAP options and funding, minimize use of General Funds, staff necessary personnel, and submit an annual report to the Health and Human Services Committee. DHHS must develop and submit a SNAP outreach plan to the Food and Nutrition Service of the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), may accept gifts/grants, partner with counties/nonprofits, and may require private or federal funds to cover outreach implementation and caseload costs (and is exempt from implementation if such funds are insufficient). The bill references the Department of Labor’s SNAP Next Step Program for certain households (through 9/30/2023) and states the intent that the Department of Labor use no General Funds. DHHS must file annual USDA waiver applications (beginning 10/1/2026) to restore work‑requirement exemptions for homeless, veterans, and foster‑care individuals.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Agriculture (USDA): The bill requires the state department to apply to USDA (under section 17(b) of the Food and Nutrition Act) to waive SNAP work requirements for homeless individuals, veterans, and certain former foster youth, restoring exemptions that existed before Public Law 119‑21, sec.10102.

- Department of Health and Human Services (state "the department"): Must submit the waiver application by October 1, 2026 and annually thereafter; defines "foster care individual" as age ≤24 who was in foster care at 18.

- Department of Labor: No direct funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are specified. The change may reduce work‑requirement referrals/administrative obligations for these populations, but no explicit labor‑program funding adjustments are included.
