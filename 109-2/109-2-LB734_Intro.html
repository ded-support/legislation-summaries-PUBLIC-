Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): Must maximize use of federal SNAP options, seek federal funding and minimize use of General Funds, hire necessary staff, and submit an annual report to the Health and Human Services Committee. DHHS shall develop a state outreach plan, may accept gifts/grants/donations, submit the plan to the USDA Food and Nutrition Service for approval and request federal matching funds, and may be exempt from implementing the plan if private or federal funds are insufficient. DHHS is required to apply annually (no later than Oct. 1, 2026, and each Oct. 1 thereafter) to the U.S. Department of Agriculture to waive work requirements for homeless individuals, veterans, and certain foster care youth, restoring pre–Public Law 119-21 exemptions.

- Department of Labor: The SNAP Next Step Program (for 131–165% FPL households) is to be administered by the Department of Labor to the extent federal funds are available; the bill states the intent that no General Funds be used by the Department of Labor for those processes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires the state agency to annually (by Oct 1, beginning 2026) submit to USDA a Section 17(b) waiver restoring pre–Public Law 119-21 SNAP work‑requirement exemptions for homeless people, veterans, and “foster care individuals” (defined as age ≤24 who were in foster care when they turned 18).

Impacts by agency:

- Department of Agriculture (USDA): will receive and adjudicate the waiver; state seeks federal approval to suspend work requirements for these groups under SNAP.

- Department of Labor: potential downstream effect reducing mandatory work‑program referrals/enrollment for these populations, but no direct rulemaking or funding changes specified.

- Department of Health and Human Services (state-level role): likely responsible for preparing/submitting the application and verifying populations; may incur administrative workload.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
