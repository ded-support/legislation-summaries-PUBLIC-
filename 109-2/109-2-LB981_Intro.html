Summary

LB981 amends powers of cities of the metropolitan class and the Nebraska Housing Agency Act to regulate housing agencies and impose operational duties. Cities of the metropolitan class may require housing authorities to comply with city rental-inspection/registration ordinances, code enforcement, and residential rental inspections. The bill requires metropolitan-area housing agencies to implement a formal complaint process with timelines and tracking, mandates bed‑bug protocols (inspection by or via a pest control professional within 10 days, remediation within 21 days, disclosure before leasing, agency responsibility for remediation costs, two‑year recordkeeping), and to report semiannually to the city and the Legislature’s Urban Affairs Committee (pest control activity, evictions, complaints, vacancy rates, board updates). Pest control professionals must be licensed under the Pesticide Act. No new appropriations or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for housing authorities in a "city of the metropolitan class" to comply with local rental inspection/registration ordinances and code enforcement. Defines "bed bug" and "pest control professional" (licensed commercial applicator). Establishes procedural duties for the housing agency: regular complainant updates; advance tenant notice before unit entry; for bed‑bug complaints, inspection (including adjacent units) within 10 days and remediation secured within 21 days; maintain written complaint/remedy records for two years. Imposes pre‑rental visual inspections, prohibition on renting units known or suspected to be infested, mandatory disclosure of adjacent infestations, and assigns the agency responsibility for investigation and remediation costs. Requires semiannual reports to the city and the Legislature’s Urban Affairs Committee (pest activities, evictions, complaints/resolutions, vacancy rates, board updates). No appropriations or new funding lines specified.
