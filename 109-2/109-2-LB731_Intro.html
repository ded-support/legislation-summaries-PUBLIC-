Summary

Creates the Gender Transition Malpractice Accountability Act: establishes a civil cause of action for harms from "gender-altering" procedures (defined in the bill), allows suit within 12 years of a provider's recommendation/procedure, waives sovereign immunity for such claims, bars contractual waivers of provider liability, and permits recovery of actual damages and reasonable attorney fees. Requires providers to give written pre-procedure risk disclosures (and to parents/guardians if patient is under 19). Requires any health insurance policy that has ever covered a gender-altering procedure to cover all adverse consequences, necessary baseline and annual follow-up testing, and treatments to manage/reverse/reconstruct effects — including for persons not enrolled in the plan when the procedure occurred. Amends malpractice-timing statutes to exempt these claims. Operative January 1, 2027. No state agencies or departments are explicitly named and the bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds extensive definitions and rules for "gender-altering" procedures and surgery, plus exceptions (e.g., disorders of sex development, life‑threatening indications). Key government-related items:

- Cross-references statutory sections 79-3803 and 44-3,131.

- Waives sovereign immunity for claims arising from recommendation, approval, or provision of gender‑altering procedures, creating state exposure to suit.

- Establishes a 12‑year time limit to sue providers, prohibits contractual waivers of provider liability, and allows recovery of actual damages and attorney fees.

- Requires providers to give written risk disclosures to patients and parents/guardians if under 19.

- Effective January 1, 2027.
