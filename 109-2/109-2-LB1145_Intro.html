Summary

LB1145 amends Neb. §84-1411 (Open Meetings Act). It requires each public body to use a notice method designated by the body and recorded in its minutes and to transmit notice to members and the public. If a newspaper refuses publication, the public body must (i) post the notice on its website if available, (ii) request the newspaper post to a statewide repository if available, and (iii) post in a conspicuous public place, keeping written records. Agenda alteration cutoffs are specified (generally 24 hours; 48 hours for some off‑site municipal meetings).

The bill clarifies virtual‑meeting authority and procedures for numerous entities including state agencies/boards/committees and advisory committees, interlocal and joint public agencies, public power and irrigation districts, educational service units and their coordinating council, community college boards, the Nebraska Brand Committee, local public health departments, metropolitan utilities districts, regional transit authorities, natural resources districts, risk‑management pools, and similar bodies. It authorizes expanded virtual meeting rules during gubernatorial emergency declarations and repeals the original section. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds notice rules for "public bodies": each must designate a notice method and record, at least twice yearly in its minutes, the regular meeting schedule, location, and how it will provide reasonable advance publicized notice. It requires notice by publication in a legal newspaper of general circulation in the public body's jurisdiction. Exempted are governing bodies of cities of the second class, villages, their advisory committees, and governing bodies of rural or suburban fire protection districts. Noncompliance with the cited subdivision does not render actions (motions, resolutions, ordinances, etc.) void or voidable. The insertion includes cross‑reference/numbering language about newspaper publication options. No state agencies or departments beyond local governing bodies are named, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are added.
