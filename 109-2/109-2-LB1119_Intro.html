Summary

LB1119 amends the Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act. It revises definitions (including "actual knowledge," covered design features, covered minor/service, and dark patterns) and adds duties and prohibitions on covered online services: stricter default protections, limits on collection/retention/use of minors' personal data, prohibition on targeted advertising and dark patterns, notification and geolocation disclosure requirements, restricted notification hours during school time, limits on profiling, and mandated account deletion/unpublishing within 15 days. The bill references federal authorities: the Federal Trade Commission (dark patterns and COPPA rules) and the Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index for adjusting the revenue threshold. Original sections amended are repealed. No state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds definitions of disallowed or regulated design features for covered online services (e.g., infinite scroll, auto-play, visible engagement counts, gamification like streaks/badges, clustered/timed notifications, virtual currencies/purchases, image‑altering filters, repeated account-creation prompts, ephemerality, barriers to deleting accounts, and faux-human intimacy). Requires services not to offer a single setting that weakens all privacy defaults for minors or to prompt minors to reduce protections unless strictly necessary. Requires a prominent tool allowing a minor to unpublish/delete their account and mandates honoring such requests within 15 days. No government agencies or funding/appropriations are mentioned.
