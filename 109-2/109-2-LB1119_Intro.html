Summary

LB1119 amends Nebraska’s Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act. It cites federal agencies and statutes: the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — delegating determination of “dark patterns” to the FTC and referencing FTC rules implementing the federal Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) for the definition of “parent” — and the Federal Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) for periodic adjustment of the revenue threshold using the Consumer Price Index. The bill tightens obligations on covered online services (data minimization, retention limits, prohibitions on targeted ads to minors, notification/time restrictions, deletion/unpublishing within 15 days) and repeals prior sections. No changes to state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a list of specific design features (e.g., infinite scroll, autoplay, visible engagement counts, gamification like streaks/badges, clustered notifications, virtual currencies/digital purchases, image‑altering filters, repeated account‑creation prompts, ephemerality, barriers to deleting accounts, and features that mimic human intimacy) that can trigger coverage. Requires covered online services to prohibit a single setting that simultaneously makes all default privacy protections less protective for covered minors and to avoid prompting minors to weaken privacy unless strictly necessary to access a requested feature. Requires a prominent tool for covered minors to request account unpublishing/deletion and mandates honoring such requests within 15 days. No government agencies or departments are mentioned, and no funding or appropriation changes are included.
