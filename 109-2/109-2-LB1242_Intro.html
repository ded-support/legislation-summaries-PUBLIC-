Summary

LB1242 directs the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to establish and enforce child‑support obligations for biological fathers of unborn children upon a mother's request; obligations may begin as early as the month of conception (physician‑determined), may be retroactive, and paternity measures require the mother's consent and must not risk the unborn child. The bill permits applications to be filed with DHHS or offices it designates and authorizes DHHS‑employed authorized attorneys; DHHS may periodically review cases and refer them to county attorneys. Clerks must provide DHHS monthly statistics and enter agreements to receive reimbursement. DHHS may set an application fee (not to exceed Title IV‑D limits), establish cost‑recovery schedules, and adopt reimbursement rules that account for federal law, available federal funds, and any legislative appropriations; reimbursement funds are added to county officials’ budgets.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

DHHS must establish and enforce child‑support obligations for the biological father of an unborn child (and after birth) at the mother's request. DHHS may designate the county attorney, an authorized attorney, or another DHHS‑designated office to accept applications. Support can begin as early as the month of conception if a physician so determines and the mother requests it; payments may be retroactively collected, including when paternity is later established. Courts will set payment amounts based on the best interests of the mother and child. Paternity measures require the mother's consent and may not be undertaken if they risk harm to the unborn child. The bill defines “unborn child” as a Homo sapiens at any stage in utero. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes for DHHS are specified.
