Summary

LB977 revises Nebraska’s Rules of the Road and clarifies the roles of state and local authorities. Key agency-related points:

- The Department of Transportation (DOT) and local authorities retain authority to set or alter minimum speed limits on freeways and to regulate where electric personal assistive mobility devices (EPAMDs) may be operated.

- Peace officers, authorized emergency personnel, and road assistance personnel may direct motorists when approaching or passing vulnerable road users.

- The bill expands the definition of “vulnerable road user” to explicitly include persons leading, herding, or driving livestock and certain device operators, and requires motorists to yield and exercise due care when passing them.

- It prohibits individuals leading/herding/driving livestock from traveling on roadways with posted minimum speeds of 20 mph or more.

- EPAMD operators must yield to vulnerable road users; violations carry a $10 fine for a first offense and impoundment (up to 30 days) for subsequent offenses.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions concerning persons leading, herding, or driving livestock (defined as horse, mule, donkey, cattle, swine, sheep, or goat) on highways (see sec. 60-6,378.02). Motorists must use caution when approaching/passing, promptly stop if given a distress signal (raising a hand), and yield the right of way to livestock being led/herded/driven; the duties do not apply if moving the vehicle is necessary to avoid an accident. No government agencies or departments are mentioned, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
