Summary

LB977 revises Nebraska Rules of the Road and delegates/clarifies authorities primarily for the Department of Transportation and local authorities. Key agency-related changes:

- Expands the definition of “vulnerable road user” to include persons on shoulders/crosswalks, operators of bicycles, e-bikes, motorcycles (not autocycles), mopeds, similar devices, animal riders/drivers, implements of husbandry, wheelchairs, coasters, skate/board/sled users, and electric personal assistive mobility devices.

- Grants the Department of Transportation and local authorities authority to alter freeway minimum speeds, regulate where electric personal assistive mobility devices may operate, and require signage before minimum-speed rules take effect.

- Allows peace officers, authorized emergency personnel, and road assistance personnel to direct lane movements when passing vulnerable road users.

- Adds livestock-specific rules requiring motorists to use caution, stop for a distress signal, and yield to livestock.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included. Original sections referenced are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions create rules for motorists encountering people leading, herding, or driving livestock on highways: livestock is defined as horse, mule, donkey, cattle, swine, sheep, or goat; drivers must use caution when approaching or passing, stop if the person herding gives a distress signal (hand raised), and yield right of way to the livestock. The yield/stop requirement does not apply when moving the vehicle is necessary to avoid an accident. The text repeatedly references section 60-6,378.02 and the term “vulnerable road user.” No government agencies or departments are named, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
