Summary

LB728 requires proxy advisors to disclose when recommendations against company management are or are not based on a "written financial analysis" and to provide such analyses to clients and, for certain services, to the subject company's board upon request. Violations are designated deceptive trade practices under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act, making them subject to the Act's remedies and penalties. The Nebraska Attorney General may exercise investigative powers under that Act if a violation is suspected and must receive written notice within seven days of any private civil action, with a right to intervene. The bill creates a private cause of action for aggrieved recipients of proxy services and affected companies or shareholders. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Requires proxy advisors to disclose when recommendations against company management are or are not based on a "written financial analysis" (which must analyze short‑ and long‑term financial impacts, recommend the value‑maximizing vote, and describe methods and personnel). If not based on such analysis, advisors must notify shareholders, affected company boards (for certain advisors), and state this prominently on their websites. If based on an analysis, advisors must disclose that fact, make the analysis available to clients on request, and provide it to company boards (for certain advisors). Violations are treated as deceptive trade practices under the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act; the state Attorney General may investigate and use all UDTPA powers and may intervene in civil actions (notice to the AG required within seven days). No funding or appropriation changes.
