Summary

LB755 creates the Emergency Service Peer Support Act, authorizing emergency service agencies—defined to include law enforcement agencies, fire departments, emergency medical services, correctional facilities, hospitals, dispatch centers, rescue services, and similar emergency-response entities—to establish or approve peer support programs and designate trained peers (current, former, or approved individuals). Communications in peer support sessions are privileged, exempt from disclosure under Neb. Rev. Stat. §§84-712 to 84-712.09, and inadmissible in proceedings, with specified exceptions (threats of suicide/homicide/felonious assault, mandated reporting, admissions of criminal conduct, or refusal to protect others). Agencies must adopt policies, may discipline confidentiality breaches, may refer but not compel employee participation, and peers receive civil immunity for good-faith assistance. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates the "Emergency Service Peer Support Act," defining "emergency service agency" to include law enforcement agencies, fire departments, emergency medical services, correctional facilities, hospitals, dispatch centers, rescue services, and similar emergency-response entities. Establishes privileged, confidential status for peer support communications and program records (exempting them from disclosure under sections 84-712–84-712.09), with exceptions for threats of suicide/homicide/felonious assault, mandated reports (e.g., abuse), admissions of criminal conduct, or refusal to protect others. Agencies may establish/approve peer programs, refer employees, and must not mandate participation; they must adopt disciplinary rules for breaches. Peers receive civil-immunity for good-faith assistance. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
