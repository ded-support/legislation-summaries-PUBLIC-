Summary

LB816 creates a confidentiality privilege for peer support services provided to public safety personnel. The privilege covers communications during peer support meetings, communications among peer support team members (and their supervisors/clinical supervisors), and related records. Designated public safety personnel include law enforcement officers, correctional/jail/juvenile detention officers, firefighters, emergency care providers, law enforcement support staff, and ambulance service personnel. Privileged materials are not public records, are not subject to discovery, and are inadmissible in proceedings, except when: the recipient gives written consent; the recipient is deceased and the surviving spouse or personal representative consents in writing; or there are articulable facts creating a reasonable fear for safety. No specific state agencies or departments are named, and the bill contains no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a confidentiality privilege for peer support communications involving "public safety personnel" (defined to include law enforcement, correctional/jail/juvenile detention officers, firefighters, emergency care providers, law enforcement support staff, and ambulance personnel). Communications during peer support meetings, between peer support team members and supervisors/clinical supervisors, and records arising from those interactions are designated privileged: not public records, not subject to discovery, and inadmissible in proceedings. Disclosure is allowed only with written consent of the recipient (or the recipient’s surviving spouse/personal representative if deceased), or when articulable facts create a reasonable fear for the safety of the recipient, others, or society and the information otherwise would be discoverable or admissible. No specific government agencies or departments are named, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are affected.
