Summary

LB1118 amends Nebraska’s Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act to classify certain social‑media platform behaviors as deceptive trade practices. For platforms that accept payment for advertising, the bill requires advertiser identity verification; unlawful‑impersonation detection/mitigation; automated and manual fraud‑detection systems; measures to prevent repeat offending advertisers; a conspicuous user reporting tool for suspected fraud; and a process allowing law enforcement to report suspected fraudulent advertisements. Platforms must investigate reported ads within seven days and act if determined fraudulent (specific remedial steps truncated in the draft). The bill excludes telecommunications and broadband Internet access services per 47 U.S.C. §254 and cross‑references several state criminal and consumer statutes (e.g., sections 28‑807, 28‑830, 28‑1802, 28‑318, and the Nebraska Foreclosure Protection Act). No changes to appropriations or funding are included. The original section is repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for paid social‑media advertising platforms to implement advertiser identity verification; unlawful‑impersonation detection/mitigation; automated and manual fraud‑detection systems; measures to prevent repeat offending advertisers; a clear user fraud‑reporting tool; and a process enabling law enforcement to report suspected fraudulent ads. Platforms must investigate reported ads and, within seven days, determine whether an ad is fraudulent and take action if so. Only "law enforcement" is referenced; no specific government agencies or departments are named and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
