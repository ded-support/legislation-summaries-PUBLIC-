Summary

References to the Department of Administrative Services are limited to the State Building Administrator. Under amended section 72-804, the State Building Administrator of the Department of Administrative Services, in consultation with the Department of Water, Energy, and Environment, may (a) specify a more recent edition of the International Energy Conservation Code, (b) set additional energy efficiency or renewable energy requirements, and (c) grant waivers supported by life-cycle cost analyses; agencies receiving state funding must provide those analyses to the State Building Administrator. Under amended section 72-805, the Director of Water, Energy, and Environment may adopt rules in consultation with the State Building Administrator. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements limiting local code updates: counties, cities, and villages cannot adopt or enforce local building, electrical, or energy code updates unless independent third‑party evidence shows the change is necessary to protect the public from "eminently dangerous situations" or required by state/federal law. The third party must have no financial interest and possess expertise in public safety or municipal codes; "eminently dangerous" = imminent, unreasonable risk of death or severe injury. Until Jan. 1, 2031, local construction/building, electrical, and energy codes may not be more stringent or more up‑to‑date than the state building code, the National Electrical Code adopted by the State Electrical Board, or the Nebraska Energy Code. No changes to the Department of Administrative Services' duties, appropriations, or budgetary allocations are included.
