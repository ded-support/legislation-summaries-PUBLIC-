Summary

LB1134 assigns the State Building Administrator of the Department of Administrative Services explicit authority (amending section 72-804) to: (1) specify a more recent edition of the International Energy Conservation Code for state buildings, (2) impose additional energy-efficiency or renewable-energy building requirements, and (3) grant waivers based on life-cycle cost analyses. Agencies receiving state funding must provide that life-cycle cost analysis to the State Building Administrator. The bill also requires the Director of Water, Energy, and Environment to consult with the State Building Administrator when adopting rules under section 72-805. No direct changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions: counties, cities, and villages may not adopt local building, electrical, or energy code updates unless independent third‑party evidence (from a financially disinterested, public‑safety/code‑expert source) shows the change is necessary to address an imminently dangerous situation or is required by state/federal law. “Eminently dangerous” is defined as presenting imminent, unreasonable risk of death or severe injury. Until January 1, 2031, local jurisdictions may not enforce codes more stringent or more recently updated than the state building code, the adopted National Electrical Code, or the Nebraska Energy Code. No text references the Department of Administrative Services, nor does it include any funding, appropriation, or budgetary changes for that agency.
