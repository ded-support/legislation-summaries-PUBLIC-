Summary

LB1169 explicitly references both agencies:

- The bill amends definitions to include employees of the Department of Correctional Services as "public safety officers."

- It includes employees of the Department of Health and Human Services as "public safety officers" only when the person committing the offense is committed as a dangerous sex offender under the Sex Offender Commitment Act.

- Assault provisions are applied to incidents occurring in secure youth confinement facilities operated by the Department of Correctional Services when the offender is committed to such a facility.

- The habitual‑criminal statute references incarceration in a Department of Correctional Services adult correctional facility and adds the new assault offense (section 4) to offenses that can trigger enhanced mandatory minimums.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds Department of Correctional Services employees to the statute’s definition of "public safety officer" and includes Department of Health and Human Services employees as public safety officers only when the offender is committed as a dangerous sex offender under the Sex Offender Commitment Act. Clarifies/creates aggravated-assault offenses protecting public safety officers and health care professionals (on duty or performing official duties) — covering serious bodily injury, use of dangerous instruments, and assault by throwing bodily fluids. Expands the scope of "health care professional" protections to include employees of counties, secure facilities, and youth rehabilitation and treatment centers. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
