Summary

LB1059 creates a new criminal prohibition on knowingly installing a mobile tracking device on another's property, tracking a person or property without consent, or failing to remove a device after consent is revoked. District courts retain authority to issue warrants/orders authorizing installation and use under section 86-2,103; the bill also revises that definition of mobile tracking device to include software on electronic devices.

Exemptions cover compliance with existing tracking statutes, court-ordered monitoring (e.g., pretrial release, probation, parole, post‑release supervision), certain parental installations, caregiver installations for vulnerable or senior adults with physician certification, business or governmental entities acting in good faith (excluding private investigators), licensed private investigators acting with owner consent for specified investigative purposes, vehicle owners/lessees, and certain aircraft tracking.

Penalties: default Class I misdemeanor; elevated to Class IIIA felony for prior convictions, violations while subject to a protection order, or a history of violence. The bill amends stalking statutes to reference these offenses as predicate convictions. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions define terms and make installing or using a mobile tracking device without consent a crime, with specified exceptions. Government-related references/exemptions include: compliance with statutes in sections 86-271 to 86-2,115; use pursuant to a court order (including as a condition of pretrial release, probation, parole, or post‑release supervision); actions by a “governmental entity” for a legitimate government purpose; and adherence to federal aviation rules (14 C.F.R. parts 91 and 135) for aircraft. The text also cites licensing statutes (sections 71-3201 to 71-3213) and multiple state criminal and protection‑order statutes (e.g., Protection Orders Act; sections 28-311.02, 28-311.03, 28-366.01, 28-371). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
