Summary

LB826 amends homestead-exemption filing and late-application procedures and names the responsible agencies.

- County board: may extend annual application deadline to July 20 (not if extended the prior year).

- County assessor: receives applications and specified late filings (medical impairment, spouse death, or VA status certification received after June 30); late filings are limited to the current tax year and must be filed on or before June 30 of the year real estate taxes become delinquent. The assessor must approve or reject late filings within 30 days, use Tax Commissioner forms for notices, and applicants may appeal to the county board of equalization.

- Tax Commissioner: prescribes medical-certification and notice forms; medical certification may be from a physician, physician assistant, or advanced practice registered nurse.

- U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs: timing of VA status certification can justify a late filing.

- Veterans: certain veterans need file only every five years; a surviving spouse retains the five-year exemption remainder.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budgets. Emergency clause: effective on passage.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions tied to U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) certification: owners may file a late application under §77‑3514.01 or §77‑3512 if they provide documentation that the VA’s certification (per §77‑3506(2)) was received after June 30. Such late filings are limited to the current tax year and must be submitted to the county assessor on or before June 30 of the year the property taxes become delinquent. Applications under the relevant subsection must include the VA certification showing receipt after June 30. No state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
