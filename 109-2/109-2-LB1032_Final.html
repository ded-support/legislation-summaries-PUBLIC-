Summary

The bill assigns extensive duties to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). DHHS (or a licensed child‑placement agency) must conduct pre‑ or postplacement adoptive home studies, set or waive related fees, perform national criminal and central‑registry checks, and provide required rules and medical‑history forms. For children committed to DHHS, prospective adoptive parents may review the department case file and DHHS must file verification with the court. DHHS may recommend termination/referral for adoption (or tribal customary adoption for eligible Indian children), perform medical assessments of adoptive placements, and maintain current placement contact information. DHHS may make post‑adoption maintenance/medical payments (ending by age 20), pay specified treatment costs, and reimburse up to $2,000 for nonrecurring special‑needs adoption expenses. For Indian children, DHHS must notify parents/tribes within five days, provide remedial services until unsuccessful, submit specified court information when involved, notify tribes within 30 days if concurrent tribal jurisdiction is granted, and may adopt implementing rules. Courts may ask the DHHS secretary to pay attorney fees "out of funds which may be appropriated."

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The additions expand Department of Health and Human Services (the department) duties regarding tribal customary adoptions. Key changes: DHHS may recommend referral for tribal customary adoption (and, if it does, must not recommend termination of parental rights); within 30 days after a state court grants temporary concurrent jurisdiction to a tribe, DHHS must notify the tribe about required elements/documents for state acceptance, provide the tribe and proposed adoptive parents a written report on the child (medical, educational, developmental, tribal affiliation information where permitted), and provide copies of court filings. The department will consult on finalizing adoption assistance agreements and may adopt rules and regulations to implement these provisions. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
