Summary

The bill gives the Director of Public Health (Division of Public Health, Department of Health and Human Services) authority to: contract with licensees to develop/operate child-care pilot programs; grant licensure for pilot facilities that waive applicable child-care licensing rules and staffing requirements while imposing alternative conditions to protect child health, safety, and welfare; deny such licenses if a danger is found; and limit pilot licenses to a maximum of five years. If a pilot is successful, the director must provide statutory recommendations to the Legislature. Separately, licensed facilities may request case-by-case waivers from Child Care Licensing Act requirements in a director‑approved form. The bill contains no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions authorize the Director of Public Health (Division of Public Health, DHHS) to contract with licensees to run pilot programs aimed at increasing day‑care availability/capacity. The director may issue time‑limited (up to five years) licenses that waive existing day‑care/youth development licensure rules — including staffing standards — provided alternative conditions are imposed to protect children’s health, safety, and welfare. Licenses cannot be granted if they would endanger children. If a pilot is successful, the director must recommend statutory changes to the Legislature. Licensed operators may also seek case‑by‑case waivers from the Child Care Licensing Act via director‑approved forms listing rules to be waived. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
