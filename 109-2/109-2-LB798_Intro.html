Summary

LB798 amends municipal election and planning commission statutes.

Key government entities referenced:

- Mayors and city council members; chairperson and members of village boards of trustees — retain appointment/removal authority for planning commissions and alternates (appointments require council/board majority approval).

- Planning commissions (cities of first class, second class, villages) — may now be established with three, five, seven, or nine regular members (city council or village board sets size by ordinance); alternate member provisions remain.

- County clerks and election commissioners — responsible for petition verification and placing election questions on ballots.

- Filing officers and governing bodies — must accept candidate filings consistent with approved at-large/ward arrangements; districts must be drawn by Oct. 1 following approval or follow statutory fallback procedures.

- Counties, school districts, community redevelopment authorities, and citizen advisory review committees are referenced regarding eligibility and limits on holding simultaneous offices.

Fiscal impact: no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a provision allowing cities of the second class (except those with commissioner or city manager government) that elect council members by ward to adopt a mixed ward/at-large system by ordinance (majority vote) or voter petition at a general election. Requires at least two at-large members and no more than half the council to be at-large. Establishes staggered initial term lengths for at-large members based on how many are elected (two‑ or four‑year initial terms as specified); thereafter at-large terms are four years. Prohibits candidates from filing both as at-large and by ward. No state agencies, departments, or funding/appropriation changes are mentioned.
