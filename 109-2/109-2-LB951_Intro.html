Summary

LB951 requires Nebraska municipalities to incorporate their extraterritorial zoning jurisdiction (ETJ) into municipal governing-board districts and to treat ETJ residents as municipal electors. Key government actors and changes:

- Election commissioner: divides metro-class cities into seven council districts; census data (U.S. Dept. of Commerce, Bureau of the Census) governs population equality for districts.

- City councils: apply zoning, building, and subdivision rules in ETJ; responsible for redrawing council districts.

- City planning commission, city planning director, and public works and utilities department: approve subdivision plats and certify required improvements; register of deeds may not record plats without approval.

- County boards: receive 30-day notice of proposed ETJ zoning changes (exceptions for large counties or joint planning bodies) and may comment.

- Board of adjustment: may exempt qualifying farm buildings.

- Section 32-553: cities/villages electing by district must redraw districts to include ETJ by January 1, 2027; governing boards draw boundaries.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions requiring cities and villages to include their extraterritorial zoning jurisdiction (ETJ) within districting and voter rolls. Specifically:

- When a metropolitan-class city redraws council districts, its seven districts must include the ETJ.

- Electors living in the ETJ are deemed electors of the city/village for metropolitan, primary, first-class (in areas), second-class cities, and villages as specified.

- Any city or village that elects by district must redraw boundaries to include the ETJ by January 1, 2027, and must include the ETJ in future redistricting.

No state agencies or departments are newly created or mentioned; no funding or appropriations changes.
