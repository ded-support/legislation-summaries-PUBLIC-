Summary

LB1088 requires courts (district and county) to order surrender of firearms and ammunition when issuing domestic-abuse protection orders, sentencing misdemeanor domestic-violence convictions, or enforcing foreign domestic-abuse orders. Qualified third parties for storage include county sheriffs, police, Nebraska State Patrol, federally licensed firearm dealers (18 U.S.C. 923), or other court-named adults. Courts must transmit orders to the sheriff, local law enforcement, and the FBI’s NICS. County jails and prisons must notify respondents before release. Law enforcement agencies must securely store surrendered weapons, establish return procedures, and verify lawful possession before return. The State Court Administrator must develop and publish standardized affidavit and order forms (in consultation with advocacy groups) and distribute forms via clerks of the district court. County attorneys may file related petitions. Noncompliance is a Class I misdemeanor; certain protection-order violations carry escalated penalties. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures assigning responsibilities to multiple government entities but contains no funding changes. Key agency actions:

- Courts/district courts: must enter surrender orders at issuance of domestic abuse or related misdemeanor orders, transmit orders to the county sheriff, other local law enforcement where the respondent resides, and to the FBI’s NICS; clerks must file petitions and schedule evidentiary hearings within 14 days for foreign orders.

- Law enforcement (county sheriffs, police, Nebraska State Patrol): may serve as “qualified third parties,” receive and securely store seized firearms/ammunition, develop return procedures, and verify lawful possession before return.

- County jails/prisons: must provide written notice to inmates before release about surrender obligations.

- County attorneys, peace officers: may file petitions or receive affidavits.

- Judicial branch administrator: must develop and publish the affidavit/form in consultation with domestic-violence advocacy groups.

No appropriations or budget allocations are added.
