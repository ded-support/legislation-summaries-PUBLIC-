Summary

LB1088 assigns specific duties to courts, the State Court Administrator, county attorneys, law enforcement, sheriffs, the Nebraska State Patrol, clerks of district court, county jails, and prisons related to firearm surrender and recordkeeping in domestic-abuse cases.

Key agency actions:

- Courts (county/district) must order respondents to surrender firearms/ammunition when issuing domestic-abuse protection orders, sentencing misdemeanor domestic-violence convictions or certain protection-order violations, or adjudicating foreign domestic-abuse orders; courts must send orders to the county sheriff, local law enforcement, and the FBI’s NICS.

- Law enforcement agencies/sheriffs must take possession, securely store firearms/ammunition, create return procedures, and verify lawful possession before return.

- State Court Administrator must develop and publish standardized petition/affidavit and return forms on the judicial branch website (in consultation with advocacy groups); clerks must provide forms.

- County attorneys, peace officers, or protected individuals may seek surrender orders for foreign respondents.

- County jails/prisons must notify respondents of obligations prior to release.

Failure to comply is a Class I misdemeanor. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds duties for multiple government entities: courts must issue firearms-surrender orders in specified domestic-violence proceedings and transmit copies to the county sheriff, local law enforcement in the respondent’s county, and the FBI’s NICS. County sheriffs, police officers, and the Nebraska State Patrol are named as “qualified third parties” to receive surrendered weapons. County jails/prisons must give respondents written notice of surrender obligations before release. Law enforcement agencies taking possession must securely store firearms, adopt return procedures, and verify lawful possession before return. The judicial branch administrator must create/post required affidavit/forms. District court clerks receive petitions for foreign protection-order relief and courts must hold hearings within 14 days. No funding or appropriations are added.
