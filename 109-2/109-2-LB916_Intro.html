Summary

LB916 affects oversight of carbon dioxide (carbon oxide) pipelines and geologic storage. Key agencies and actions:

- Nebraska Oil and Gas Conservation Commission: may approve construction of liquid CO2 pipelines only if applicants demonstrate compelling need to reduce greenhouse gases, operational safety, no cost to Nebraska taxpayers, environmental protection, and economic benefit. The commission's permit criteria for geologic storage are expanded/clarified to require safety, groundwater protection, monitoring plans, EPA Underground Injection Control–compliant geologic/seismic study, definition of reservoir boundaries, protection of mineral interests, and that the operator obtain consent of all reservoir-estate owners. The commission must not issue a permit without that unanimous consent.

- Governor/Major Oil Pipeline Siting Act: Governor approval remains required for major oil pipelines; condemnation rights lapse if not acted on within two years.

- Eminent domain: the bill bars exercising eminent domain to acquire rights-of-way for pipelines whose preponderant purpose is transporting carbon oxide.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds authority for the Nebraska Oil and Gas Conservation Commission to approve pipelines transporting liquid carbon dioxide only if the applicant demonstrates: a compelling need to reduce greenhouse gas emissions in Nebraska; operational safety; no cost to Nebraska taxpayers; actual greenhouse‑gas reductions; no environmental harm; and an economic benefit to Nebraska and its citizens. Also prohibits use of eminent domain to acquire rights‑of‑way, construct, or operate a pipeline whose preponderant purpose is transporting carbon oxide. No other state agencies or departments are referenced, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
