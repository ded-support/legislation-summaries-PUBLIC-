Summary

- Department of Economic Development (DED)

- Will administer the Site and Building Development Fund and the ImagiNE Nebraska Cash Fund (fees for ImagiNE Nebraska Act credited to that fund).

- Intent to transfer $5 million from the General Fund to the Site and Building Development Fund for FY2026-27 earmarked for key‑employer retention capital improvement grants following specified changes of ownership.

- DED may finance loans, grants, subsidies and other assistance for site/building development and must allocate not less than 40% of annual available fund dollars to nonmetropolitan areas.

- New grant to key employers: $5 per square foot of relevant capital improvements, aggregate cap $5 million; such commitments reduce funds otherwise available for other Site and Building Development Fund activities.

- Department of Labor (DOL)

- Required to establish a key‑employer grant program to support workforce retention/attraction after specified ownership changes.

- Grants capped at $300,000 annually per key employer; application and prioritization rules set (including prioritizing employers with largest historical Nebraska employment).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Economic Development / Site & Building Development Fund: Adds a definition for a covered "change of ownership and control" (merger with an out‑of‑state/foreign entity) limited to deals from July 1, 2025–Dec 31, 2029 where the in‑state key employer had >3,000 full‑time employees for each of the prior 10 years and the acquirer had enterprise value > $25 billion. Legislature intends a one‑time $5.0 million General Fund transfer (FY2026‑27) to the Site & Building Development Fund to finance key‑employer retention capital improvement grants. Grants may be paid at $5 per square foot of improvements, capped at $5.0 million aggregate per award.

- Department of Labor: Must establish a key‑employer grant program for such ownership‑change events. Awards limited to $300,000 per key employer annually. Grants require an application with a retention/relocation plan; when funds are insufficient, priority goes to the employer with the largest Nebraska employee count in the prior ten years.
