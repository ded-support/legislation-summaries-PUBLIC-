Summary

The bill designates the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) as the agency responsible for implementing the Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act. DHHS shall determine core competencies, define “endorsed” training programs, and may adopt rules by December 31, 2026, to: identify and periodically update core competencies; set application, approval, and renewal processes; establish reasonable application and renewal fees; approve training providers; prescribe denial/suspension/revocation procedures; and provide administrative hearing rights. The bill grants DHHS rulemaking authority for program oversight. No specific appropriations or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added text creates the "Community Health Worker Training Endorsement Act" and assigns primary responsibility to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). Key DHHS duties and authorities:

- Define and maintain statewide core competencies for endorsed community health worker (CHW) training, with periodic updates reflecting best practices and needs.

- By Dec 31, 2026, adopt rules on application, approval, renewal, fees, denial/suspension/revocation procedures, program/provider approval, and contested-case hearings under the Administrative Procedure Act.

- Align core competencies with public health, behavioral health, workforce development, and medical assistance program (Medicaid) reimbursement eligibility so endorsed programs qualify for Medicaid and private insurer reimbursement.

- Clarifies DHHS will not create CHW licensure/credentialing or expand licensed practitioners’ scopes.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
