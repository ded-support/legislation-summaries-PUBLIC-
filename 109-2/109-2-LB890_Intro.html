Summary

This bill repeatedly references the Department of Revenue. Key provisions: the Department may require background checks, deny/suspend/revoke licenses, and receive setoff remittances of withheld winnings. License/fee changes and remittances to the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund: distributor biennial license fees of $200 per cash device (capped at $10,000) (effective Jan 1, 2025); manufacturer biennial license fee $10,000; cash-device application fees ($500 before 1/1/2027; $650 in 2027; thereafter adjusted annually for inflation) and annual decal fees ($250 before 1/1/2027; $350 in 2027; thereafter adjusted annually) — all remitted to the State Treasurer for credit to the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund. Occupation tax for non-cash devices rises to $70 in 2027 and is thereafter inflation-adjusted by a formula tied to the Producer Price Index published by the U.S. Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics; collections are credited 20% to the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund and 80% to the General Fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Revenue: required to collect the fees below and remit them to the State Treasurer for credit to the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund. Application fee: $500 before 1/1/2027; $650 for 2027; beginning 1/1/2028 and annually thereafter, indexed to the BLS Producer Price Index (all commodities) and rounded to the nearest $50. Annual decal fee for each cash device: $250 before 1/1/2027; $350 for 2027; indexed to the PPI from 2028 and rounded to the nearest $50. Occupation tax for non‑cash mechanical amusement devices: $35 before 1/1/2027; $70 for 2027; indexed to the PPI from 2028 and rounded to the nearest $5. Allocation: occupation taxes remitted are split 20% to the Department of Revenue Enforcement Fund and 80% to the General Fund.

- Department of Labor: mentioned only as the publisher (Bureau of Labor Statistics) of the PPI used for indexing; no funding or appropriation changes for the Department of Labor.
