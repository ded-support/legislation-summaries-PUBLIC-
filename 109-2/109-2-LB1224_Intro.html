Summary

This bill defines "department" as the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) and (a) treats DHHS employees as "persons" and DHHS clients as "protected individuals" for the sexual-abuse provisions, prohibiting convicted persons from monitoring or instructing at schools that elect exemption from accreditation; (b) requires DHHS to notify a student's current school and the Commissioner of Education to restrict transfers to exempt schools when the subject of a child‑abuse report is the student's parent/guardian/educational decisionmaker, to keep such notices confidential, and to establish procedures to implement that restriction; and (c) directs DHHS to adopt rules on response timelines, assessments, case transfers, Review, Evaluate, and Decide Team criteria, report categorization, and operation of the hotline, and authorizes rules to implement the transfer‑restriction section. No funding or appropriation changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary (DHHS focus):

- The Department of Health and Human Services must notify a student's current school and the Commissioner of Education that any election to transfer a student to an "exempt school" (one opting out of state accreditation/approval) is invalid while a child abuse/neglect report names the student's parent, guardian, or educational decisionmaker as the subject.

- DHHS must also notify the school and Commissioner when the restriction is lifted (alternative response assignment or formal determination).

- Such notices are confidential investigative records, limited to the student’s name and whether transfer is allowed.

- DHHS must establish procedures and may adopt rules/regulations to implement these requirements (section 13).

- The bill additionally bars persons convicted of listed sexual, domestic, or child-abuse offenses from monitoring or instructing at exempt schools.

- No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are mentioned.
