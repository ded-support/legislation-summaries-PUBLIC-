Summary

The bill revises the Community Improvement District Act, assigning specific duties to state and local officials and expanding fiscal and oversight powers. City/village councils and clerks must approve CID formation, plans and levies; county treasurers serve ex officio as CID treasurers, create CID funds/accounts, collect/disburse CID property taxes, special assessments and municipal sewer charges, and must segregate/manage special assessment funds per directions. County clerks, election commissioners and registers of deeds receive filings/notices; election commissioners administer trustee elections by mail. The Auditor of Public Accounts receives CID budgets/audits (filed annually), may waive or order audits, and may appoint an administrator for troubled CIDs who can assume board powers, negotiate/issue bonds or warrants and levy a separate tax. District courts review bonds/warrants, adjudicate appeals, may approve warrant-extension proceedings (including limited court-ordered levy increases) and enforce court settlements. The Secretary of State will design uniform recall petition forms. The act takes effect immediately.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions identify and assign roles to government entities and clarify fiscal processes for community improvement districts (CIDs). Key agencies and duties:

- City/village clerk and city council/village board: file and publish formation/amendment/annexation/dissolution/assessment notices, hold hearings, approve plans, ordinances, and levy limits.

- County treasurer: serves as ex officio treasurer for the CID, establishes funds/accounts, collects taxes and special assessments, deposits and disburses CID funds per trustees/administrator.

- Register of deeds, election commissioner, county clerk: receive filings, notices, and handle election administration (filing clerk defined as election commissioner or county clerk).

- Auditor of Public Accounts: receives annual budgets and audits, may waive audits, has access to records, and—if a CID is in default—may appoint an administrator.

- District court: oversees contests, approves debt compromises and certain tax-levy increases.

- Secretary of State: designs uniform recall petition papers.

Funding: CIDs may levy property taxes up to a stated maximum, create general/bond/other funds, issue bonds/warrants, and levy special assessments. Audits and bond premium costs are paid from CID funds; audit fees can become a lien if compelled by the Auditor.
