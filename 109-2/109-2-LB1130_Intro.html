Summary

Creates a Community Improvement District (CID) framework assigning clear duties to state and local officials. City and village councils and clerks process CID formation, plans, levies, and filings; CID clerks must file annexation/dissolution/merger/resolution documents with city/village clerks and the Secretary of State and notify county clerks, election commissioners, and registers of deeds. County clerks receive levies/filings; registers of deeds record boundaries. County treasurers serve ex officio as CID treasurers, establish CID funds/accounts, collect CID taxes and special assessments, segregate funds, and disburse per court‑approved compromise settlements. Election commissioners or county clerks administer trustee elections (sealed mail ballots) and act as recall filing clerks; the Secretary of State supplies uniform recall papers. CIDs must file annual budgets and audits with the Auditor of Public Accounts by Dec. 31; the Auditor may waive audits, appoint an administrator (who assumes board powers), or assess liens if noncompliant. District courts review bond/warrant compromises, hear appeals/special proceedings, may refer CIDs to the Auditor, and can order remedies including adjusted tax levies. No state appropriations; CIDs are authorized to levy property taxes (to approved maxima per $100 valuation), impose special assessments, and issue bonds or warrants to meet obligations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds administrative and fiscal rules for Community Improvement Districts (CIDs). Key agencies and duties:

- City/village councils/boards: approve CID formation, plans, levy caps, annexations, amendments, and accept improvements.

- County treasurer: serves ex officio as CID treasurer, establishes CID funds/accounts, collects/deposits taxes and special assessments, and disburses funds per trustees/administrator.

- County clerk, register of deeds, and election commissioner: receive filings/notice of CID actions, elections, annexations, dissolutions, mergers, and detachment.

- Auditor of Public Accounts: receives annual budgets and audits, may waive audits, access records, can be referred by district court to appoint an administrator for troubled CIDs.

- District court: reviews contest/compromise of warrants/bonds and may order levy adjustments; approves bond/warrant compromises.

- Secretary of State: designs uniform recall petition papers; filing clerk (election commissioner/county clerk) manages recall/election procedures.

Funding changes: CIDs may levy a property tax up to a stated per-$100 cap (placeholder) to fund general corporate purposes and debt service; audit and bond premium costs are paid from CID funds; Auditor can require/arrange audits with fees becoming liens.
