Summary

The bill amends definitions and procedures for the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) and the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). It defines "Department" as DCS and "inmate"/“felony defendant” by confinement in DCS facilities, expands sexual‑abuse offenses (first‑degree: Class IIA; second‑degree: Class IIIA) to cover DCS employees, contractors and delegated personnel, and adds Brady/Giglio coverage for correctional officers while listing DCS as a public‑safety agency with procedural/confidentiality protections. County attorneys must forward victims’ names/addresses to the Board of Parole, DCS, county corrections, DHHS, and Board of Pardons for inclusion in nonpublic offender files. DCS must notify victims of releases, furloughs, escapes, transfers, program placements, discharges, and release‑date calculations and forward victim information to DHHS for commitment proceedings; DHHS must notify victims of commitment‑related events, receives sexual‑assault reports for victims under 18, and may receive child‑abuse/neglect information. DCS/Parole oversee work‑camp placements and exclude certain sexual‑ and commitment‑offense offenders. No appropriations or budget changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions affecting the Department of Correctional Services (DOC) and Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS):

- DOC is explicitly defined as "Department" and correctional officers are included among "officers" and "public safety agencies" covered by new Brady‑Giglio procedures and protections. Officers employed by jails or DOC receive notice/hearing rights before prospective Brady‑Giglio disclosures, confidentiality of personal data, limits on discipline solely for being named, and a court review process for disputes.

- Sexual‑abuse offenses expanded to expressly cover abuse of inmates, parolees, probationers, detainees, conservators/guardians/guardians ad litem, and child welfare service providers; sexual abuse by child welfare service providers is added as a registrable offense (implicating DHHS‑regulated providers).

- Probation employees acting in good faith who administer opioid‑overdose reversal medication are granted civil and disciplinary immunity.

- No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are contained in the added text.
