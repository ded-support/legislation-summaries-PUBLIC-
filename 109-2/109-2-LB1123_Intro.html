Summary

LB1123 explicitly treats correctional officers employed by the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) as "officers" and lists the Department as a "public safety agency." The bill: (1) extends Brady‑Giglio procedural protections to such officers (including notice, reconsideration, and judicial review if placed on a Brady‑Giglio list); (2) prohibits discharge, discipline, certification suspension, or retaliation solely because a prosecuting agency placed an officer on a Brady‑Giglio list or for exercising the bill’s rights; and (3) requires public safety agencies (including DCS) to keep officers' photographs and personal information confidential and redacted before public release. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds correctional officers employed by the Department of Correctional Services to the bill’s definitions and treats the Department as a “public safety agency.” Establishes that placement on a Brady‑Giglio list by a prosecuting agency alone may not be the sole basis for discharge, discipline, or certificate action against an officer; provides a written-notice and a 10‑business‑day reconsideration window (or until Feb 1, 2028 if notice not given), and allows prosecuting agencies to remove names at any time. Creates district court judicial review with in‑camera, sealed proceedings and narrow standards for removal. Requires public safety agencies (including DOCS) to withhold officers’ photos and redact personal info from public records; bars retaliation for exercising these rights. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
