Summary

LB1123 explicitly includes the Department of Correctional Services in its definitions and coverage. Correctional officers employed by the Department are treated as "officers" and the Department is listed as a "public safety agency," making its employees subject to the bill's Brady‑Giglio procedures (notice, reconsideration, and judicial review), confidentiality protections (redaction of personal information and limits on public release of photographs), and anti‑retaliation provisions. The bill imposes duties on prosecuting and public safety agencies that therefore apply to the Department. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department of Correctional Services are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Key additions affecting the Department of Correctional Services (DCS):

- Defines "officer" to include correctional officers employed by DCS and names DCS as a "public safety agency."

- Bars discharge, discipline, certificate suspension/revocation solely because a prosecuting agency placed an officer on a Brady‑Giglio list.

- Requires notice and allows officers ten business days to request reconsideration (or until Feb 1, 2028 if no notice received). Prosecutors may later remove names; duty to disclose Brady‑Giglio material remains.

- Provides district court review (in camera, sealed) of denied reconsideration requests with limited standards for reversal.

- Restricts public release of officer photos and mandates redaction of personal information; permits release to counsel/representatives.

- Prohibits retaliation for exercising these rights.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for DCS are included.
