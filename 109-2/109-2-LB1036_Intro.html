Summary

LB1036 (2026) amends Nebraska Evid. R. §27-404 to permit admission of evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts when such evidence is "inextricably intertwined" with the charged offense or for other recognized non‑propensity purposes (motive, intent, identity, etc.). The bill defines "inextricably intertwined" by three criteria (part of the factual setting; so blended that proof of the charged offense requires proof of the other acts; or necessary to present a coherent picture). In criminal cases, the prosecution must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the accused committed the other act and, when relevant, that it is inextricably intertwined; proof must be shown outside the jury's presence, and the court shall give a limiting instruction on request. No state agencies or funding changes are referenced.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition and procedure for admitting evidence of other crimes/wrongs as "inextricably intertwined" with the charged offense: such evidence is admissible when it (1) forms part of the factual setting, (2) is so blended/connected that proving the charged crime requires proving the other acts, or (3) is necessary for a coherent prosecution narrative. If offered under the specified subdivision, the prosecution must show the intertwinement, and upon the defendant’s request the court must give a limiting jury instruction.
