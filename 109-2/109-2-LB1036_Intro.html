Summary

LB1036 amends Neb. Evid. Rule 27-404. It expressly permits admission of extrinsic evidence that is "inextricably intertwined" with the charged crime and clarifies when evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts may be used for non‑character purposes (motive, intent, identity, etc.). Procedural requirements: the prosecution must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the accused committed the other act and, when relying on the inextricably‑intertwined basis, that the evidence meets that standard; the proof must be made outside the jury's presence; and, upon request, the court must give a limiting jury instruction. A repeal of the original section is included. Agencies affected: Nebraska courts and prosecuting attorneys. No changes to funding or appropriations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition and procedural rules for "other crimes, wrongs, or acts" being "inextricably intertwined" with the charged offense: such evidence is inextricably intertwined if it (1) forms part of the factual setting, (2) is so blended/connected that proving the charged crime requires proving the other acts, or (3) is necessary to present a coherent picture of the charged crime. It also provides that, when offered under the specified subdivision, the court must (implicitly) address that intertwining and, upon the defendant’s request, must instruct the jury to consider the evidence only for the limited purpose for which it was admitted. No government agencies or departments are mentioned and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget changes.
