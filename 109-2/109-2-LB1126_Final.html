Summary

LB1126 creates an Infrastructure Development Investment Program administered by the Department of Transportation (DOT), establishing federal, state/local, and departmental administrative accounts, authorizing loans to political subdivisions, crediting debt‑service remittances to the program fund, and permitting DOT to request the Legislature redirect a borrower’s appropriation to cure defaults. Under a Transportation Innovation Act DOT may accept unsolicited proposals, charge up to $500 review fees, spend department appropriations to review/develop/implement proposals, and contract accordingly. DOT must collect/enter accident reports and may issue oversize/overweight permits with capped fees and require security. The Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) will suspend licenses on DOT-reported accidents, require notices/security deposits, and the Motor Vehicle Operator’s License Act adds antitrafficking training for commercial driver training. The Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) will contract/regulate Medicaid nonemergency transportation, set safety standards, require commission licensure (annual fees ≤ $250) for DHHS contractors effective 1/1/2027, and set reimbursement limits. The Department of Revenue must provide prepaid wireless surcharge data to the Public Service Commission on request.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Health and Human Services: New requirement that, beginning Jan 1, 2027, any provider contracting with DHHS, a DHHS-contracted Medicaid managed care org, or DHHS agents to provide intrastate Medicaid nonemergency medical transportation must hold a commission-issued license. The commission sets qualifications, may hold hearings, issues one‑year licenses (renewable), may charge fees up to $250, and may suspend/revoke noncompliant licenses.

- Department of Motor Vehicles: No substantive additions affecting the DMV were included in the underlined text.

- Department of Revenue: Obligations issued under the new Infrastructure Development Investment Program are exempt from state and local taxation and are designated lawful investments/security — affecting tax treatment and investment/admin responsibilities. The program may also seek legislative appropriation reductions from defaulting borrowers to repay program debt.

- Department of Transportation: Creates an Infrastructure Development Investment Program administered by the commission/department with federal, state/local, and administrative accounts; authorizes loans, limited special obligations (bonds up to 40 years), audits, debt‑coverage standards, and rulemaking (by July 1, 2027). Also establishes tiered superload permit fee caps ($250/$400/$800 by weight) plus recoverable direct costs, and expands PPP/unsolicited‑proposal procedures.
