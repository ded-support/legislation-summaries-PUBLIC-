Summary

The bill affects DOT, DMV, DHHS, and the Department of Revenue as follows.

- Department of Transportation (DOT): Creates an Infrastructure Development Investment Program administered by DOT via a commission, with federal, state/local, and administrative accounts; authorizes loans, other financial assistance, reserve/interim financing, and issuance of special limited obligations payable solely from program revenues (tax‑exempt). Debt‑service receipts go to the State Treasurer and are credited to the Program Fund. DOT may use monies appropriated to review/implement unsolicited proposals, charge up to $500 for proposal reviews, and, on borrower default, request the Legislature reduce a borrower’s appropriation and redirect it to the program. DOT also centralizes accident reporting, transmits Part II reports to DMV, and issues oversize/oversight permits with capped fees.

- Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV): Must suspend operator licenses after DOT accident reports for injury/death or ≥ $2,000 property damage unless financial responsibility is shown; security deposits minimum $2,000.

- Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS): May contract for Medicaid nonemergency medical transportation only with qualified carriers or, in limited cases, with individual drivers paid no more than the state employee reimbursement rate (section 81‑1176). DHHS must be consulted on carrier/driver standards; reimbursement to carriers is capped at the state employee rate. Beginning Jan 1, 2027, NEMT contractors must hold a commission license (one‑year term; renewal fee cap $250).

- Department of Revenue: Must provide prepaid wireless surcharge calculation/collection data to the Public Service Commission on request.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Health and Human Services: New requirement (effective Jan 1, 2027) that anyone contracting with DHHS, a Medicaid-managed care org, or its agents for intrastate Medicaid nonemergency medical transportation must hold a commission-issued license. Licenses are annual, fee up to $250, subject to hearings, suspension, or revocation.

Department of Transportation: Creates an Infrastructure Development Investment Program with federal, state/local, and administrative accounts; authorizes loans, pooled financing, reserve funds, and issuance of special limited obligations (up to 40-year maturities). Program must audit annually, maintain debt service coverage, set aside annual debt service, and may solicit/enter public‑private partnerships and evaluate unsolicited proposals (administrative review fee up to $500; may use appropriated funds). Superload single‑trip permit fees capped at $250/$400/$800 by weight, plus recoverable direct costs.

Department of Revenue: Obligations issued under the program are exempt from state and local taxation (affecting tax revenue). Legislature may be asked to redirect a borrower’s appropriation to repay program debts.

Department of Motor Vehicles: No explicit new duties or funding changes in the added text.
