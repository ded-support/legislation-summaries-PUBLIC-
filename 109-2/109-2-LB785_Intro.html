Summary

LB785 adds a new Class IV felony prohibiting obtaining, possessing, duplicating, transferring, or using a key or lock for any postal-service mail receptacle with intent to defraud or deprive another. "Postal service" is defined to include the United States Postal Service, its contractors, and commercial couriers. The bill incorporates the new offense into existing financial-transaction and covered-offense provisions, enabling district courts and prosecuting authorities to seek forfeiture. Forfeited money, securities, and negotiable instruments from financial transaction offenses are to be credited to the Financial Fraud Victims' Reimbursement Fund. Law enforcement/seizing agencies may retain, use, sell, or destroy forfeited property per statutory procedures.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a definition of “postal service” to include the United States Postal Service, USPS contractors, and any commercial courier that delivers mail. Creates a new criminal offense—unlawful conduct involving a mail receptacle key or lock—covering knowingly obtaining, possessing, duplicating, transferring, or using a key or lock adopted by a postal service for any mail box or authorized receptacle with intent to defraud or deprive another. Violation is classified as a Class IV felony. No government funding, appropriations, or budgetary changes are mentioned.
