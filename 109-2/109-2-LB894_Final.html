Summary

LB894 references the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) and related agencies as follows:

- Warehouses licensed under the federal United States Warehouse Act must provide the Public Service Commission copies of all regulatory filings submitted to or received from the United States Department of Agriculture. The commission may not take administrative action against such warehouses without the USDA’s written consent.

- The commission may share inspection results with the U.S. government or its agencies, including the Commodity Credit Corporation, for fees covering actual costs.

- Annual license fees for nonfederal warehouses (effective July 1, 2027) may not exceed the fee listed in the 2025/2026 “Rates Charged for AMS Services” notice published by the Agricultural Marketing Service of the United States Department of Agriculture.

- The bill creates the Grain Dealer and Grain Warehouse Cash Fund, directs fee revenue (beginning July 1, 2027) to that fund, and expresses legislative intent to appropriate fund monies to the commission for administration (license-fee revenue limited to 25% of commission implementation costs).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates a Grain Dealer and Grain Warehouse Cash Fund administered by the commission (within the Dept. of Agriculture context) to defray administration of the Grain Dealer and Grain Warehouse Acts. Fees and specified credits fund it; the Legislature intends to appropriate all fund money to the commission. Investment earnings stay in the fund; transfers out are prohibited. License fees: commission must set fees by Dec. 1, 2026 (effective July 1, 2027), capped at $1,500 per license and limited so fee revenue does not exceed 25% of commission implementation costs. Fees collected before July 1, 2027 are credited to the General Fund; fees on/after that date go to the new cash fund. Warehouses licensed under the federal U.S. Warehouse Act receive state licenses, submit USDA regulatory filings to the commission, and the commission generally may not act against them without USDA consent.
