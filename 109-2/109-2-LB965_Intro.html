Summary

The bill amends provisions affecting the Department of Correctional Services (DOC) and Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). It defines DOC terms and expands victim‑notification duties: county attorneys must provide victim names/addresses to DOC, and DOC must notify victims of furloughs, releases, escapes, discharges, and sentence calculations. DOC and DHHS must promulgate rules (with the Board of Parole and Board of Pardons) to implement victim‑notification provisions. Victim addresses/telephone numbers held by DOC and DHHS are exempt from public disclosure. The Board of Parole may recommend, and the DOC director may assign, eligible felony inmates to a short-term (up to 180-day) incarceration work camp before parole; persons convicted of certain sexual or capital offenses are ineligible. DHHS employees count as protected victims for pregnancy-related sentence enhancements; DHHS also manages alternative‑response child protection functions, report sharing, and must receive forwarded petitions and victim lists in commitment proceedings. No appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Defines “Department” to mean the Department of Correctional Services.

- Adds new criminal offenses: sexual abuse by a conservator/guardian/guardian ad litem and sexual abuse by a child welfare service provider. Sexual penetration by a child welfare service provider is a Class IIA felony; sexual contact is a Class IIIA felony. “Minor” is defined as under 19. Convictions for these offenses are made subject to the Sex Offender Registration Act. These changes affect DHHS‑overseen child welfare providers.

- Provides immunity for probation employees (as defined in section 29‑2246 and under the Office of Probation Administration) who, in good faith and per office policy, obtain and administer opioid‑overdose reversal medication: no administrative, criminal, or civil liability absent willful, wanton, or gross negligence.

- No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included in the added text.
