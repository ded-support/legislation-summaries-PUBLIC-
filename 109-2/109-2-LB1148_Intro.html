Summary

LB1148 establishes statutory parentage rules for children conceived by assisted reproduction. Key provisions: donors are not parents; an individual who consents to assisted reproduction with intent to be a parent is a parent (consent generally must be in a signed record); exceptions allow courts to find consent based on clear-and-convincing evidence or two years of cohabitation and holding out. It sets posthumous parentage conditions (written consent or clear evidence plus embryo/ birth time limits). Parentage disputes are adjudicated in court using best-interest and equitable factors; a child may not have more than two parents. The act excludes births by sexual intercourse, surrogacy agreements, and preserves Nebraska Indian Child Welfare Act rules. No state agencies, appropriations, or funding changes are mentioned.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds detailed definitions and parentage rules for children conceived by assisted reproduction: who is a parent when an individual consents, required signed consent (with clear-and-convincing-evidence exceptions), posthumous reproduction conditions (consent/evidence plus embryo in utero within 36 months or birth within 45 months), limits to two parents, and factors courts must use to adjudicate competing claims (best interest criteria and considerations for genetic-test challenges). It restricts a spouse’s ability to challenge parentage with time and evidentiary limits. It also states these sections do not apply to children conceived by sexual intercourse, do not recognize surrogacy agreements, and do not supersede the Nebraska Indian Child Welfare Act. Government bodies mentioned: courts (judicial branch) and implicit tribal jurisdiction under the Nebraska Indian Child Welfare Act. No funding, appropriations, or budget changes are included.
