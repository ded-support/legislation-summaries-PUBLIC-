Summary

LB816 references the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) by requiring that persons taken into emergency protective custody who are convicted sex offenders be admitted to a jail or Department of Correctional Services facility (unless a medical/psychiatric emergency requires temporary medical treatment), and by defining “administrator” to include the administrator of a Department of Correctional Services facility. The bill directs law enforcement to complete a written certificate “prescribed and provided by the Department of Health and Human Services” upon admission and forward it to the county attorney. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions define “law enforcement agency/officer,” “law enforcement support personnel,” “peer support services,” “peer support team,” and “peer support team member” (requires ≥24 hours training and official agency designation). Peer support communications and records are made confidential and privileged: not public records, not subject to discovery, and inadmissible in proceedings. Permitted disclosures: written consent of the recipient (or surviving spouse/personal representative if deceased); where there is a reasonable fear for safety (limited disclosure to victims, family, law enforcement, appropriate authorities); or when a team member is a defendant/respondent and disclosure is necessary for that proceeding. No references to the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services and no funding, appropriation, or budget changes are included.
