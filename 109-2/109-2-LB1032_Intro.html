Summary

The bill assigns extensive child‑welfare duties to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). DHHS (or licensed agencies) will conduct pre‑ and postplacement adoptive home studies, set and may waive related fees, and require prospective adoptive parents of DHHS‑committed children to review and sign verification of the department case file, which DHHS must file with the court. DHHS may make post‑adoption maintenance and medical payments (ending by the child’s 20th birthday), pay treatment costs within three years for undisclosed preexisting conditions, and reimburse up to $2,000 for nonrecurring adoption expenses. DHHS must perform medical assessments (tribes may do so for eligible Indian children and share results), recommend permanency actions, adopt guardianship‑assistance rules, maintain timely placement contact information for courts, and promulgate rules to implement related sections. When tribal concurrent jurisdiction applies, DHHS must notify tribes of procedural rights, provide a written child report, allow tribal participation in voluntary foster/in‑home cases, and submit specified case information to courts. No direct appropriations to DHHS are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds Tribal Customary Adoption provisions that assign several new duties to the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). DHHS must:

- Receive tribal medical assessments before entry of a tribal customary adoption order.

- Be provided a written child report (medical, educational, developmental, tribal affiliations, diagnostic and psychological records) when temporary concurrent jurisdiction is granted.

- Provide notice to tribes describing required order elements, filing requirements, and tribes' rights to conduct/designate adoptive home studies and medical assessments.

- Work with tribes and proposed adoptive parents to finalize adoption assistance agreements to ensure access to state, federal, and tribal resources (e.g., Indian Health Service).

- Not recommend termination of parental rights for an Indian child eligible for tribal customary adoption until court finds such adoption inappropriate; may recommend referral.

- Adopt rules to implement these sections.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
