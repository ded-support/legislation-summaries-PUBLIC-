Summary

The bill charges the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) to establish the Youth Reentry and Transitional Support Program in coordination with the Office of Juvenile Services, the Office of Probation Administration, and the Division of Rehabilitation Services. DHHS must collaborate on individualized transition plans, assign aftercare coordinators, ensure continuity of behavioral health and Medicaid services, enroll eligible youth in rehabilitation services, and coordinate with family resource centers. DHHS (with the State Department of Education) must electronically submit annual reports to legislative committees detailing youth served, outcomes, and any federal or private funding leveraged. The Department of Labor is named as a co‑regulator: DHHS, Juvenile Services, Probation, DRS, and the Department of Labor must adopt rules on caseloads, training, allowable expenditures, interagency protocols, and outcome tracking. The bill contains no explicit appropriations or funding allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The Department of Health and Human Services must create a Youth Reentry and Transitional Support Program (with OJS, Probation, and the Division of Rehabilitation Services). DHHS duties: start transition/employment planning within 30 days, assign aftercare coordinators, provide up to 12 months of aftercare, ensure continuity of behavioral health and Medicaid, enroll youth in rehabilitation services, develop individualized employment plans, use credible-messenger mentoring, coordinate family supports, and file annual legislative reports (numbers served, education/housing/employment outcomes, estimated savings, and any federal/private funds leveraged).

The Department of Labor joins DHHS and other agencies to adopt rules (e.g., 1:15 coordinator caseload, training standards, allowable housing/employment expenditures, interagency protocols) and to track employment/education outcomes at 3-, 6-, 9-, and 12-month intervals and assess workforce/system impacts. No explicit appropriations or new funding allocations are included.
