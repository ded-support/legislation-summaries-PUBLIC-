Summary

LB765 amends school bond voting and joint-entity bond procedures. For Nebraska school districts (section 10-702) it requires that bond questions be submitted to district electors, receive votes from at least 50.1% of qualified electors, and obtain a majority of votes cast; retains filing deadlines with county clerks or election commissioners (March 1 for primary, September 1 for general), special‑election procedures, 20‑day publication notice, canvass/certification responsibilities, and a six‑month resubmission bar after defeat. For joint entities including a Nebraska school district or educational service unit (section 13-809) bonds require similar per-district approval, a 50‑day election notice, and Election Act conduct; Public Facilities Construction and Finance Act voting rules remain. The bill reiterates prohibitions on using public funds or paid employee time to advocate for bond campaigns and bans campaign materials on school/ESU property. No appropriations or funding changes are made.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds prohibitions applying to school districts, educational service units (ESUs), and joint entities that include them: public funds — explicitly including building funds, cash funds, and discretionary funds — may not be used to establish, conduct, underwrite, promote, subsidize, or otherwise support or advocate for campaigns in school district or joint-entity bond elections. Privately raised funds (e.g., concessions, booster clubs, PTOs) are exempt. No district or ESU employee may use paid work time to advocate for such bond campaigns, though employees may provide neutral factual information (subject, amount, election date). Campaign materials advocating for a bond may not be posted or distributed on any facility, grounds, or vehicles associated with a district or ESU; generic informational materials are allowed. No new appropriations or budget allocations are created—these are restrictions on use of existing public funds.
