Summary

LB1259 (Grid Modernization Act) centers authority in "the board" (state review/approval body) and establishes regulatory duties and timelines. Key agency actions: public power utilities and large-scale generation providers must obtain board approval for large-load contracts; providers must register with the board, demonstrate technical/financial qualifications, post security, and file permits and air‑quality state implementation plan impacts. The board must approve/deny contracts within 60 days, may suspend/revoke provider registrations, adopt rules, and expedite reviews. Funding provisions: the board may charge semi‑annual, proportionally allocated fees to each large-load customer submitting requests to cover retention of independent consultants and other board proceeding costs; fees are restricted to those uses. Transmission cost allocation remains subject to applicable federal law and FERC approval.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures and approvals centered on the state "board" under the Grid Modernization Act. Key agency roles and requirements:

- The board: must approve large-load and private-generation contracts (60‑day review), may adopt rules, retain independent consultants, and suspend/revoke registrations. It reviews contract compliance and may set cost‑allocation methodologies.

- Public power utilities: must obtain board approval before construction or serving large-load customers, submit contracts to the board within 15 business days, and may elect to buy up to 10% of a provider's output (subject to board-approved agreements).

- Large-scale generation providers: must register with the board, meet technical/financial qualifications, submit permits and operational histories, and post security.

- Transmission service providers and FERC: transmission requests go to the transmission service provider; cost allocation and payment obligations for interconnection, upgrades, tariffs, and ancillary services are required to the extent allowed by federal law and FERC.

Funding/fees:

- The board may charge semi‑annual, proportionally allocated fees to large-load customers to cover consultant and evaluation costs; fees must be used exclusively for those purposes.
