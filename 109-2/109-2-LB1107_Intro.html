Summary

LB1107 revises the Rural Road Improvement District Act and primarily affects county governments and their officers. Key agencies/actors: county boards and county clerks (petition, notice, hearing, levy, bond issuance), county road departments (maintenance), county advisory committees (resident members), the engineer in charge and special counsel, and state/federal governments where contract assistance is used.

Funding and budget provisions:

- Counties may create per‑district or a single county rural road improvement fund.

- Funds may include transfers from the county general fund, special assessments, special levies, and bond sale proceeds.

- Counties may issue general‑obligation bonds (≤20‑year maturity) and progress warrants; bonds are backstopped by special assessments and an annual county tax for any deficiency and are exempt from county bond limits.

- Boards set interest on assessments (capped by statute); contractor partial payments carry 8% interest after 45 days.

- Advisory committee expense reimbursements are chargeable to project costs; leftover funds on dissolution revert to the county general fund.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedural and financing rules for county road improvement districts. Key government actors: the county board (must wait to adopt a §39‑1639 resolution until after the meeting it is proposed), the county clerk (receives written objections and petitions), and county officials administering a rural road improvement fund. Property owners may file written objections before hearings; owners exceeding 50% of district area can stop a hearing. The board may obtain, accept, contract for, and purchase road improvements; pay costs by issuing bonds under §39‑1648 or other obligations and by levying special assessments or taxes. Counties may create either a fund per district or a single county rural road improvement fund to receive money, special assessments/levies, bond proceeds, and other credits. Delinquent assessments accrue interest; bonds mature in 20 years and are payable from district levies and sinking‑fund balances. Procedures for boundary changes, withdrawals, consolidation, and dissolution require petitions to the county clerk.
