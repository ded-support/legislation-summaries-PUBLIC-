Summary

LB1107 amends the Rural Road Improvement District Act, authorizing county-level procedures and finance mechanisms. Key actors: county boards, county clerks, county governments, county engineers, special counsel, and local advisory committees; counties may also contract with state or federal agencies. Financial changes: counties may use progress warrants and issue general-obligation bonds (≤20-year maturity) to fund improvements; bond repayment relies on a sinking fund of special assessments and contributions and, if needed, an annual countywide tax to cover deficiencies. Counties may create per-district or a single county rural road improvement fund; advisory-committee expenses are reimbursable and charged to project cost. Special assessments become liens, may be paid in up to ten annual installments with interest (rate capped by section 45-104.01 or as set by the board prior to due date), and delinquent installments bear statutory interest. Completion transfers roads to county maintenance; districts may impose a special maintenance levy up to $0.035 per $100. The bill repeals several existing sections.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds procedures for forming rural road improvement districts, emphasizing county-level actors: the county board (which cannot adopt a resolution in the same meeting it is proposed) and the county clerk (who receives written owner objections and petitions). Property owners owning >50% of area can block a hearing by filing objections. The board may create either a separate fund per district or a single county "rural road improvement fund." That fund is credited with money, special assessments, bond proceeds, special levies and related receipts. Counties may accept grants, contract for and purchase improvements, and issue bonds under §39‑1648 to pay costs (bonds mature 20 years). Unpaid special assessments accrue interest after the due date. Petitions for boundary changes, withdrawals, consolidations, or dissolution are filed with the county clerk.
