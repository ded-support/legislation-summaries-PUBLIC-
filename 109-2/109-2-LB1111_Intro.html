Summary

LB1111 amends utilities law to require the state-designated representative organization to file an annual data center load report, in addition to existing long-range and load/capability reports, with the Nebraska Power Review Board. It defines "data center," "large data center" (≥20 MW), and "public power supplier" (public power districts, municipal electric utilities, or other government electric providers). The bill grants public power suppliers express powers and duties to: ensure large data centers pay full costs for new service and infrastructure upgrades; impose terms (including decommissioning plans, bonds, community benefit agreements, and interconnection rules); conduct load studies before imposing requirements; post detailed data-center project information on supplier websites; and regulate cryptocurrency mining operations similarly. Local governments must be provided required permits; suppliers may interrupt service per established policies. No funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included. Original sections 70-1025 and 70-1506 are repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for "large data centers" (≥20 MW) and assigns duties to: the unspecified "board" (annual data-center load report filed by a representative organization), public power suppliers, and local governmental entities (county/municipal zoning/permit authorities). Key provisions require public power suppliers to ensure data centers pay full costs of new electric service and infrastructure upgrades, not shift costs or stranded-asset risk to other retail customers, and bear all decommissioning costs. Suppliers must impose terms requiring infrastructure cost payment, decommissioning plans and a security bond within one year of commercial operation, proof of permits, community benefit agreements, and to limit service where private suppliers or on-site generation suffice (with interconnection). Suppliers must report detailed data for each proposed/existing center (owners, size, location, demand, suppliers, contracts, T&D upgrades, efficiency and renewable commitments, service life). No changes to state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
