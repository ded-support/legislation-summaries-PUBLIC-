Summary

LB1111 amends electric utility law to add reporting and regulatory duties centered on the Nebraska Power Review Board and public power suppliers. The bill requires the representative organization to file an annual data center load report with the Nebraska Power Review Board. It defines "data center," "large data center" (peak demand ≥20 MW), and "public power supplier" (public power districts, municipal electric utilities, or other government electric providers). Public power suppliers must: ensure large data centers pay full costs for new service and infrastructure upgrades, avoid cost-shifting to other retail customers, require decommissioning plans and security, conduct load studies before imposing requirements, and publish specified project details on their websites. Public power suppliers may impose cost-recovery measures (e.g., letters of credit) on cryptocurrency mining operations and may interrupt service under established policies. No appropriations or budgetary changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds an annual data‑center load report requirement: a representative organization must file detailed data (name, owners, size, address, electricity demand and suppliers, contracts, T&D upgrades, efficiency/renewables commitments, service life, etc.) with the board.

Assigns duties and rate‑setting constraints to public power suppliers: ensure large data centers (≥20 MW peak) pay full costs for new electric service and infrastructure upgrades, cannot shift costs or stranded‑asset risk to other retail customers, and must bear decommissioning costs. Public suppliers must require centers to post a decommissioning bond within one year of commercial operation, submit decommissioning plans, provide county/municipal permits, enter community benefit agreements, and propose private/self‑generation options. Public suppliers must limit service if private/self‑supply is available and interconnect such sources.

No explicit changes to agency funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
