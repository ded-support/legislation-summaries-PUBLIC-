Summary

LB1163 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §13-3206 (Property Assessed Clean Energy Act) to revise PACE lien procedures and priorities. Municipalities must record a PACE lien in the county register of deeds within 14 days after an annual assessment becomes delinquent (or within 14 days of notice from a third-party lender), certify the delinquency to the county treasurer, and the lien is treated as a special assessment collected under Chapter 77 (sections 77-1801 to 77-1863, including 77-1858). For single-family residential property the initial assessment creates a lien recorded upon imposition; residential PACE liens are subordinate to prior-recorded liens and to a first mortgage recorded after the PACE notice and have priority over other subsequently recorded liens. Nonresidential PACE liens require a recorded consent/subordination per §13-3205 to attain parity with tax liens. Foreclosure proceeds follow these priorities; delinquent assessments may be satisfied from the loss reserve fund (§13-3208) or extinguished if proceeds are insufficient. Loan servicers may raise escrow payments to cover PACE assessments. The bill repeals the original section and takes effect immediately (emergency).

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

If a third‑party lender notifies a municipality that an annual assessment for an energy project is delinquent, the municipality must, within 14 days, certify the delinquent amount and lien to the county treasurer. The lien will be treated as a special assessment under section 77-1858 and collected using the same county treasurer procedures and real estate tax/special assessment processes set out in Chapter 77 (sections 77-1801 to 77-1863). No new agencies are created and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
