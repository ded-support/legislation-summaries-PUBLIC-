Summary

LB1001 gives the Department of Revenue (DOR) expanded regulatory, enforcement, and funding roles for gaming. It creates a Charitable Gaming Division within DOR, requires annual electronic tax/expense reports, and establishes a Charitable Gaming Operations Fund. Taxes under specified sections (9-239, 9-344, 9-429, 9-648) are reallocated: 40% to charitable gaming administration/enforcement and problem‑gambling support (through the Operations Fund and the State Racing and Gaming Commission) and 60% to the General Fund. The bill continues $100,000 quarterly transfers from the Operations Fund to the Compulsive Gamblers Assistance Fund through June 30, 2026, allows DOR to collect fees (credited to the Operations Fund), and directs taxes under §77‑3012 to enforcement and a central server. The Tax Commissioner may employ investigators with limited law‑enforcement authority. DOR gains rulemaking and suit/enjoin authority over gift enterprises; operators must remit withheld winnings to DOR for setoff against state liabilities.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added provisions require organizations (including qualifying nonprofits) that run progressive‑jackpot lotteries/raffles (e.g., Queen of Hearts style) to register each game with the Department of Revenue, post game rules where conducted/tickets sold, and permit ticket sales via vending/dispensing devices. The Department is expressly authorized to adopt rules and regulations governing such games. For lotteries authorized by section 23, only 50% of gross proceeds must be used for prizes. The text references annual gaming tax distributions and the Nebraska Commission on Problem Gambling, but contains no explicit changes to appropriations, funding allocations, or budgetary appropriations for the Department of Revenue.
