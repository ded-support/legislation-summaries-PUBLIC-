Summary

LB883 defines "Department" as the Department of Economic Development (DED) and assigns DED responsibility for the Civic and Community Center Financing Fund. DED shall use the fund to provide grants for new or renovated civic and recreation centers, preservation/rehabilitation of historic buildings/districts, and construction/upgrades of public spaces, and shall evaluate applications per section 13-2707. DED may conditionally approve grants subject to statutory limits (minimum $15,000; varying maximums by municipal class and tribal governments, with higher caps when fund balances meet thresholds), assistance capped at 50% of project cost, one grant per applicant per two-year period, and a five-year sale restriction on assisted facilities. DED must give priority to cities of the second class and villages and preference to new applicants. The bill expresses intent to reappropriate unobligated grant appropriations to the next fiscal year.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

The added language gives the State Historic Preservation Officer (SHPO) — typically within the Department of Economic Development — authority to approve demolition or preservation conditions for locally designated or National Register-eligible properties, requiring either SHPO-concurred certification that a commercial property is substandard and abandoned (by a licensed engineer/architect or chief code official) or agreed preservation-based mitigation with the locality. It defines “certified building professional” and “substandard and abandoned commercial property.” It also specifies that the referenced fund may be used to pay reasonable and necessary costs incurred by the SHPO under §19-1202. No specific appropriation amounts or new budget allocations are included.
