Summary

LB753 amends the Protection Orders Act (sections 26-101, 26-102, 26-112) and adds a duty for peace officers. Key agency-related points:

- Defines "law enforcement agency" to include municipal police departments, town marshals, county sheriffs, and the Nebraska State Patrol.

- Adopts the federal definition of "military protective order" (32 C.F.R. 635.19).

- Permits admission of military protective orders as evidence of past conduct and need for protection orders.

- Requires a peace officer who arrests a U.S. armed forces member to check the FBI's NCIC for registered military protective orders and, if there is probable cause of a violation, notify the authority that issued the order.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Defines "military protective order" by reference to 32 C.F.R. 635.19 (as of Jan 1, 2026) and allows such orders to be admitted as evidence of a respondent's past conduct and need for protection. It requires peace officers who arrest a member of the U.S. armed forces to check the Federal Bureau of Investigation's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) for a registered military protective order and, if there is probable cause of a violation, to notify the authority that issued the order. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are changed.
