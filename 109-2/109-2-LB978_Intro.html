Summary

This bill creates a new statutory civil cause of action targeting persons who create, distribute, or otherwise enable access to defined "prohibited content" (obscene materials, child sexual abuse materials, child sexual exploitation devices/images). Enforcement and remedies: the Attorney General and county attorneys may seek injunctions, equitable relief, and civil penalties (up to $10,000 per violation). Sovereign immunity is not an affirmative defense. Collected civil penalties are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution in accordance with Article VII, §5 of the Nebraska Constitution. The bill grants specified immunities: Internet utilities (for passive provision of access), courts, attorneys, and their agents/employees, and limited protections for judges viewing/possessing prohibited content in good-faith official contexts. The statute supplements—rather than replaces—other remedies and applies regardless of publisher status or criminal conviction. No explicit appropriations or new budget allocations are provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds enforcement and penalty provisions: the Nebraska Attorney General and county attorneys may bring civil actions to restrain or enjoin violations and recover civil penalties (up to $10,000 per violation). Sovereign immunity is not an affirmative defense, and remedies may be awarded regardless of a criminal conviction. Internet utilities are shielded from liability for merely providing access, transmission, storage, or connection to content they did not help create or develop. Judges acting in good faith in the course of official duties are exempt from liability for viewing/possession of prohibited content. Collected civil penalties must be remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Neb. Const. Art. VII, §5. No separate appropriations or new agency budget allocations are specified.
