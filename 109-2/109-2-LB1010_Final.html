Summary

The bill directs the Department of Revenue to collect and enforce the nameplate-capacity tax for renewable generation and newly included energy storage resources (≥100 kW). Owners must file an annual nameplate-capacity report by March 1; taxes are due April 1 (with provisions for quarterly payments and penalties for late or underpayment). The Department may adopt implementing rules. The Department must separately identify tax proceeds and remit them to the county treasurer of the county where the facility/resource is located within 30 days of receipt. The bill expressly prohibits the Department from retaining, diverting, or allocating any revenues collected under sections 77-6201 to 77-6204 to its holdings or the General Fund; revenues must be distributed as statutorily provided.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Revenue implications: The bill creates a new annual nameplate capacity tax for commissioned energy storage resources of 100 kW or greater — calculated as total nameplate MW × $3,518 per MW — shifting taxable treatment away from ordinary property tax. Depreciable tangible personal property used in electricity storage and subject to the nameplate capacity tax is expressly exempted from the regular property tax (ref. section 77-6203). Data-center provisions require reporting of sales/use tax exemptions and incentive payments, which may affect tax oversight but do not allocate funds to the Department. No direct appropriations, funding increases, or budget allocations to the Department of Revenue are included in the added text.
