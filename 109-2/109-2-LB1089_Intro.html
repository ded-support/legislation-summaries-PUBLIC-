Summary

LB1089 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §48‑3808, directing enforcement of the Nebraska Healthy Families and Workplaces Act to the Commissioner of Labor (Nebraska Department of Labor). The commissioner must issue citations when violations are found, notify employers (certified mail/other verified delivery), and propose administrative penalties (max $500 for a first violation; $5,000 for subsequent violations). Employers have 15 working days to contest citations; contested citations are excluded from public disclosure, from contracting bars, and from evidentiary admission until resolution. Employers with unpaid (uncontested or resolved) citations are barred from contracting with the state or political subdivisions. Employees may sue for legal and equitable relief, recover judgments, costs and attorney fees; citations related to the dispute are admissible unless contested; suit must be filed within one year. The original section is repealed.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds private enforcement provisions: an employee may sue in the proper court for violations of the Nebraska Healthy Families and Workplaces Act; courts have jurisdiction to grant legal and equitable relief and may award the full judgment plus costs and reasonable attorney’s fees to a prevailing employee. Citations issued against an employer under subsection (1) that directly relate to the dispute are admissible as evidence unless excluded by the court; contested citations may not be admitted until the contest is resolved. Establishes a one‑year statute of limitations. No specific state agencies or departments are named, and there are no funding, appropriation, or budget allocation changes.
