Summary

LB1114 assigns several duties to state agencies:

- Department of Economic Development (DED): must receive and evaluate inland port authority proposals, certify compliance with statutory/prioritization criteria and statewide caps, and annually update workforce housing construction-cost thresholds using the Producer Price Index (U.S. Dept. of Labor, BLS). DED also administers the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Act and Cash Fund (funded by legislative transfers, gifts, grants, bequests), awards grants up to $250,000/year (20% local match required), and disburses payments 50/50 at award and upon meeting halfway goals. DED may adopt rules; the state investment officer may invest unused fund balances.

- Department of Revenue (DOR): must provide a standard certification form for county assessors to certify tax-division increases for expedited redevelopment projects; no appropriations provided.

- Department of Transportation (DOT): CID proposals that affect state highways or state-owned property must coordinate with DOT, and public airport payments to retire interest-free Division of Aeronautics loans are excluded from specified property tax levy limits.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Economic Development (DED): Establishes the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Act. DED defined as administering the program, prescribing applications, approving grants (first-come basis), enforcing a $250,000-per-applicant annual cap, requiring a 20% local match, and disbursing 50% at award and 50% upon meeting half the household goal. DED must adopt rules and collect semiannual outcome reports from grantees and households.

- Funding/appropriations: Creates the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Cash Fund, administered by DED, to be financed by legislative transfers and gifts/grants/bequests; fund monies may be invested. Grants are subject to available funding; unused second-half disbursements may be reawarded.

- Department of Transportation (DOT): Community improvement districts must coordinate proposed changes to state highways or state-owned property with DOT; the state is not responsible for paying for such changes and DOT concerns must be addressed by the district/developer.

- Department of Labor & Department of Revenue: No substantive changes, roles, or funding actions referenced.
