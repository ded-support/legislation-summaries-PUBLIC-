Summary

The bill assigns these agency duties and limited fiscal changes:

- Department of Economic Development (DED): will receive and evaluate inland port authority creation applications (Director certifies cap compliance; authorities are deemed created if criteria and cap permit); annually update workforce-housing unit cost limits using the BLS Producer Price Index; and administer the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Act and its New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Cash Fund (funded by legislative transfers and gifts/grants/bequests, investable). Grants to cities/tribes/nonprofits require a 20% local match, are capped at $250,000 per applicant per fiscal year, and pay 50% at award/50% on reaching half the household goal.

- Department of Transportation (DOT): CIDs must coordinate proposed revisions affecting state highways with DOT, with the district/developer/city bearing costs and resolving DOT concerns; Division of Aeronautics—airport payments to retire interest‑free DOT loans are excluded from statutory property tax levy/allocation limits.

- Department of Revenue (DOR): must provide a standard certification form for county assessors to certify completion and valuation increases so redevelopment authorities may begin using tax‑increment funds. No direct appropriations to these agencies are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Economic Development (DED)

- Creates the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Act (Sections 109–116). DED administers grants to cities, tribes, or qualifying nonprofit economic/community development organizations to incentivize households to relocate to Nebraska.

- Establishes the New Taxpayer Recruitment Grant Cash Fund (funded by legislative transfers, gifts/grants/bequests — including federal funds). Funds may be invested by the state investment officer.

- Grant rules: applicants must provide ≥20% match (cash or in-kind); awards limited to $250,000 per applicant per fiscal year; DED pays 50% at award and 50% after meeting half the household goal; unused remainder is reawarded. DED reviews applications in order received, subject to fund availability, and requires semiannual reporting from recipients.

Department of Transportation

- Community improvement districts must coordinate proposed changes affecting state highways or state-owned property with the Department of Transportation; costs for such changes are not borne by the state (developer or city/village pays).

Department of Labor and Department of Revenue

- No substantive changes, funding, or new duties for these agencies appear in the added text.
