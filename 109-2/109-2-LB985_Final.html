Summary

LB985 amends the Nebraska Probate Code to modify court procedures and oversight in guardianship and conservatorship matters. Key governmental entities affected:

- Nebraska Supreme Court: authorized to promulgate rules governing standby guardian qualifications, training, and compliance.

- County courts (and courts generally): required to set hearings, may appoint attorneys and guardian ad litem (per sections 30-4201–30-4210), may send court-appointed visitors or court officers/employees to investigate, must receive notices and inventories by mail, and retain authority to limit powers, remove or accept resignations, and require court permission for out‑of‑state relocations.

- Public Guardian: explicitly barred from appointment as a standby guardian.

- Court-appointed officials: physicians, guardians ad litem, and court staff involvement formalized; bonding companies receive certain notices.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds standards for guardianship oversight: guardians must make quarterly personal contacts with wards (in person or virtually if adequate), keep written visit records, and document attempts to contact the ward to the court if contact fails; the court may modify or waive these rules. Guardians must try to enable wards to attend guardianship hearings in person or virtually. A person under protection (including minors) has the right to attend hearings in person or virtually. It also caps permanent guardianship/conservatorship appointments: a person may not accept a permanent appointment if they already serve as permanent guardian or conservator for 20 or more individuals (temporary roles excluded). Agency mention: courts (judiciary). No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.
