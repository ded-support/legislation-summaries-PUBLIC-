Summary

LB1041 requires cities (including villages) and counties to allow at least one accessory dwelling unit (ADU) on the same lot as a single‑family residence, subject to building codes and size limits (≤1,000 sq ft or ≤50% of the main residence). Cities/counties may only limit ADUs for historic‑building rules, deed/HOA/Condominium Act restrictions, or similar. Municipalities/counties may not impose more restrictive placement, appearance, parking, occupancy, utility, impact‑fee, or street/sidewalk requirements than those that apply to the single‑family residence; ADU permits must be approved administratively on the same timeline as single‑family permits, and denials must be written with remedies. Manufactured/mobile homes used as ADUs must be placed on permanent foundations and assessed as real property. No appropriation or budget/funding changes are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds parallel statewide requirements for cities (including villages) and counties: each must allow at least one accessory dwelling unit (ADU) on a lot with a single‑family residence (size and building‑code limits apply; manufactured/mobile homes used as ADUs must be placed on permanent foundations and assessed as real property). Cities/counties are prohibited from imposing requirements on ADUs that are more restrictive than those for the primary single‑family residence (placement, appearance, parking, utility connections, impact fees, rental regulation beyond the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, occupancy limits, etc.). Permit approvals must be ministerial and follow the same timeline as single‑family permits; denials must be written with remedies. Local ordinances/resolutions conflicting with this section are void; more permissive local rules remain allowed. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are added.
