Summary

LB993 creates a state cause of action for deprivation of federal or Nebraska constitutional rights by law enforcement officers and removes qualified immunity as a defense. It requires public employers — defined to include the State of Nebraska, state agencies, and political subdivisions — to indemnify officers for judgments under the new cause of action unless the officer acted in bad faith or with malicious intent, with notice procedures if indemnification is withheld. Sovereign immunity is waived for these claims and such claims are exempted from the State Tort Claims Act and the Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act (amendments to sections 13-903 and 81-8,210). Any punitive damages awarded are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Article VII, §5. Operative date: January 1, 2027.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a state civil cause of action for deprivation of constitutional rights by law‑enforcement officers and defines covered actors and employers. Key government entities and fiscal effects:

- Definitions: “public employer” means the State of Nebraska, any state agency, or any political subdivision (but not contractors). “Law enforcement officer” includes officers defined in section 81‑1401, peace officers under section 49‑801, correctional officers, and probation officers; “state agency” and “political subdivision” reference existing statutory definitions.

- Fiscal/legal duties: public employers must indemnify officers for judgments under the new cause of action unless the officer acted in bad faith or maliciously; if indemnification is denied, the employer must notify officer and plaintiff within 30 days.

- Punitive damages are remitted to the State Treasurer for distribution under Article VII, §5 of the Nebraska Constitution.

- Sovereign immunity is waived for these claims, which are excluded from the Political Subdivisions Tort Claims Act and the State Tort Claims Act. No specific appropriation amounts are provided.
