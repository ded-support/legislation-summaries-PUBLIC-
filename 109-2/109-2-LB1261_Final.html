Summary

LB1261 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §70-670 to restrict eminent domain use by consumer-owned electric suppliers. Key agency references and provisions:

- Attorney General: may represent districts in condemnation proceedings at district request; districts must reimburse the state for all AG costs and expenses incurred.

- Nebraska Power Review Board: must approve any privately owned generation co-located with an industrial customer (>1,000 MW new load) to qualify for the eminent-domain prohibition.

- Regional transmission organization, applicable transmission owner, and consumer-owned wholesale power supplier are included among parties required to execute long-term commercial agreements that preserve local service rights and include a contractual waiver of eminent-domain authority.

- Consumer-owned suppliers are also barred from condemning privately developed renewable facilities that meet §70-1014.02. No new appropriations are specified; only reimbursement to the state for AG expenses.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition on Nebraska consumer-owned utilities using eminent domain to acquire privately owned generation built to serve a single industrial customer with new load >1,000 MW, conditional on: Nebraska Power Review Board approval; co-location/equivalent interconnection; executed long-term commercial agreements (preserving the utility’s exclusive retail service and, where applicable, the wholesale supplier’s rights, forbidding resale, including a contractual waiver of eminent domain) approved by the consumer-owned utility’s governing body; the industrial customer reimbursing all costs, fees, congestion charges, and system upgrade costs incurred by affected consumer-owned utilities; and the facility serving only that industrial customer unless waived. No changes to appropriations or budget allocations are included.
