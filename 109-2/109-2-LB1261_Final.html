Summary

LB1261 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §70‑670 to restrict the use of eminent domain by consumer‑owned electric utilities. It bars such utilities from condemning privately owned generation facilities built to serve a single‑site industrial customer with new load >1,000 MW if: the facility is co‑located/adjacent with an equivalent grid interconnection and approved by the Nebraska Power Review Board; the private supplier, local consumer‑owned retail utility (and, where applicable, its consumer‑owned wholesale supplier and relevant transmission owner) execute long‑term contracts that preserve the utility's exclusive retail service rights, include a waiver of eminent domain, and are approved by the consumer‑owned utilities' governing bodies; and the industrial customer pays all resulting costs, fees, congestion charges, and system upgrade costs. The bill retains the Attorney General's role representing districts in condemnation proceedings and the district's obligation to reimburse the state for those costs. No new appropriations are established.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds a prohibition on consumer‑owned utilities in Nebraska using eminent domain to acquire a privately owned electric generation facility built to serve a single industrial customer with new load >1,000 MW, provided conditions are met. Key government/sector entities: the Nebraska Power Review Board must approve the facility; relevant consumer‑owned wholesale power supplier (where applicable), the regional transmission organization (RTO) responsible for resource adequacy, and the applicable transmission owner participate in required contracting; and the governing body of the consumer‑owned utility must approve any contract. Financial terms: the industrial customer must pay all costs, fees, congestion charges, and electric‑system upgrade costs incurred by any consumer‑owned utility due to the project. No state funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are created or modified.
