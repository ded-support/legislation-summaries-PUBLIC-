Summary

LB1126 assigns significant roles and funding authority to the Department of Transportation (DOT) and updates Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) duties:

- Infrastructure Development Investment Program: a commission-administered fund (with DOT administrative accounts) may receive appropriations, federal/state/local money, bond proceeds, etc.; intent that funds be appropriated each fiscal year for program purposes. The investment program makes loans via the department; repayments, earnings, and debt‑service remittances are credited to the fund. If a borrower defaults, the program may seek legislative re‑appropriation from the borrower's appropriations.

- Transportation Innovation Act/unsolicited proposals: DOT may charge/retain up to $500 for evaluations, spend money appropriated to it to review/implement unsolicited proposals, enter contracts, and must report P3 activity annually to legislative committees.

- Accident reporting/licensing/security: peace officers must report accidents to DOT; DOT classifies reports and electronically forwards Part II to DMV. DMV may suspend operator licenses after DOT receipt; minimum security under the Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act set at $2,000.

- Permits: DOT permit fees adjusted—general permit cap $50; continuous permits tiered ($50/90‑day; $100/180‑day; $200/year); certain 30‑day permits cap $50 (renewable); superload single‑trip fees set ($250/$400/$800) plus direct cost recovery; building‑move permit cap $50.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Department of Transportation (DOT)

- Creates the Infrastructure Development Investment Program Fund, administered by a commission. Fund sources: legislative transfers, federal/state/local grants, donations, repayments, bond proceeds, etc. Repayments and investment earnings are credited to the fund; debt service remitted to the State Treasurer is credited to the fund. Investment and accounting must follow state law, GAAP, annual independent audit. Legislature intends the fund be appropriated each fiscal year for program purposes.

- Fund may be used for loans/financial assistance to eligible entities, reserves/credit enhancements, administrative costs (including DOT portfolio monitoring/collection), refinancing, and pooled financings. Account structure includes federal, state/local, and administrative accounts; administrative accounts are for DOT program administration.

- Political subdivisions may apply to DOT for loans; on default DOT can pursue remedies, draw on reserves/credit facilities, or request the Legislature reduce a borrower’s appropriation and transfer funds to repay debt.

- DOT may charge and retain up to $500 for initial evaluation/review of unsolicited project proposals, spend appropriated funds to review/develop/implement such proposals, and enter contracts for proposals.

Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV)

- No substantive changes or funding/appropriation provisions affecting the DMV in the added text.

Fees

- Caps single-trip superload permit fees at $250 (≤300,000 lbs), $400 (>300,000–≤500,000 lbs), and $800 (>500,000 lbs); DOT may also recover direct permit-issuance costs.
