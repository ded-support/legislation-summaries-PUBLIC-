Summary

LB1181 creates and clarifies multiple victim-notification and record-sharing duties for the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) and the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS):

- County attorneys must forward convicted victims' names/addresses to the Board of Parole, DCS, county corrections agencies, DHHS, and Board of Pardons; agencies must include victim names in offender files (nonpublic) and accept victim requests for ongoing notification.

- DCS must furnish criminal records to probation officers without cost, receive presentence reports for incarcerated offenders and provide copies to the Board of Parole and Board of Pardons, and notify victims of furloughs, releases, escapes, discharges, release-date calculations, and sentence reductions.

- For mental-health/sex-offender commitment petitions, county attorneys notify DCS, which forwards victim contact information to DHHS; DHHS must notify victims of commitment proceedings, escapes, discharges, furloughs, and community releases and may release portions of patient records to comply with notification.

- DCS, DHHS, Board of Parole, and Board of Pardons must adopt implementing rules; victim contact information is exempt from public disclosure.

- No funding, appropriation, or budgetary changes are specified.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added text expands definitions and victims' rights. It defines "intimate partner" and "plea agreement," and broadens "victim" to include listed violent‑crime victims (including homicide, various degrees of assault, sexual assaults and child sexual‑abuse victims), surviving relatives/Immediate family of homicide victims, persons seriously injured in motor‑vehicle cases, certain theft victims when intimate partners, and sexual‑assault victims under existing law. Victim protections: access to public criminal‑justice records; consultation before plea agreements; ability to submit written statements for bond‑modification proceedings in abuse cases; to read (or have read) victim impact statements at sentencing (with reasonable time limits but not to foreclose oral statements); and limited court exclusion if necessary to protect the defendant's fair‑trial rights. No references to the Department of Correctional Services or the Department of Health and Human Services, and no funding, appropriation, or budget allocations are included.
