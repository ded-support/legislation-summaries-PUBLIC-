Summary

Key provisions referencing the Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV):

- The Director of Motor Vehicles must approve ignition interlock device types, review applicants’ driving records, and issue or revoke ignition interlock permits; the DMV also adopts rules for device approval, installation, and program administration.

- Applicants must submit documentation to the director and surrender licenses to the department; the DMV verifies installation before permit issuance.

- Ignition interlock service providers must notify the department if a device installed under a DMV order is disabled, bypassed, or altered.

- The Department of Motor Vehicles Ignition Interlock Fund is created, administered by the DMV, may receive fees under section 60-4,115, may pay indigent defendants’ device costs if funds are available, and may be transferred to the General Fund per legislative direction; funds are investable by the state investment officer.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds provisions creating the Department of Motor Vehicles Ignition Interlock Fund, administered by the DMV. The fund may receive fees under section 60-4,115, may be invested by the state investment officer under applicable state investment laws, and its monies may be transferred to the General Fund at the Legislature's direction and as specified in section 60-6,211.05. The bill requires ignition interlock service facilities to notify the DMV when a device installed under a DMV order is disabled, bypassed, altered, or an attempted tamper is detected (failure to notify is a Class V misdemeanor). It clarifies that persons ordered by the DMV must not operate vehicles without a functioning interlock or without a permit; violations carry misdemeanor/felony penalties. Applicants must submit documentation to the director and pay applicable fees. No specific appropriation amounts are included.
