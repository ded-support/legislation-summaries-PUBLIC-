Summary

Creates the "Nebraska Protection of Seniors from Insurance Exploitation Act." Key agency roles:

- Director of Insurance: defined as "Director"; must be notified by an insurer within seven business days when a disbursement is delayed due to suspected exploitation, receive status updates of the insurer's internal review, and may petition a court to terminate, extend, or modify a delay and order protective relief.

- References the Insurance Fraud Act and law enforcement as possible participants in reviews/investigations.

- Courts (a court of competent jurisdiction) may adjudicate delays and provide relief.

Operational rules affecting agencies:

- Insurers may initiate internal reviews and delay disbursements when exploitation is suspected, with specified maximum delay timelines (15, 25, or 55 business days depending on review status) unless earlier resolved or ordered by court.

- Insurers and trained individuals receive broad civil, administrative, and criminal immunity for actions taken in good faith under the act.

No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Creates the "Nebraska Protection of Seniors from Insurance Exploitation Act" and defines "Director" as the Director of Insurance. Key agency roles and actions:

- Insurers or trained individuals may notify permissible third parties and may initiate internal reviews and temporarily delay disbursements or transactions suspected to facilitate financial exploitation.

- Insurers must notify the Director of Insurance within seven business days of a delay, provide reasons and review status, and supply updates on request.

- Delay periods automatically expire (generally 15, 25, or 55 business days depending on continued concern); the Director may petition a court, and courts may modify delays or order protective relief.

- References to law enforcement and the Insurance Fraud Act appear.

- Strong liability immunities are provided for insurers, trained individuals, and good‑faith permissible third parties.

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
