Summary

The bill requires the Department of Revenue to establish, by rule and regulation, a mechanism for withholding an "income tax equivalent" from compensation paid to permitholders who lack Social Security numbers. The department will determine the income tax equivalent amount and, to the extent feasible, coordinate with federal agencies to align withholding with federal work-authorization verification programs. Confidential application information may be disclosed to the Department of Revenue solely for implementing this withholding provision (section 11 and section 13). No explicit appropriations, budget allocations, or other funding changes for the Department of Revenue are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

- Department of Labor (the "department"): will administer Guest Worker and Immediate Family permits — set eligibility, application content, fingerprint/criminal checks, issue/renew within 30 days, design two‑year permit cards, revoke for serious offenses or loss of eligibility, and enforce violations (civil penalties up to $750; Class II misdemeanor for falsification). Fees: application fee up to $50; initial issuance fee $1,000 (entered legally but out of status) or $2,500 (entered illegally); immediate‑family fees mirror guest worker fees; department may require a $10,000 bond or foreign‑country guarantee. Installment payments allowed without interest; permits revoked for default. Department must keep applicant data confidential (with limited disclosures) and remit collected fees to the State Treasurer for credit to the General Fund.

- Department of Revenue: must provide a withholding mechanism for permitholders lacking Social Security numbers, set an "income tax equivalent" by rule to approximate state income tax, and coordinate with federal work‑authorization verification programs. Dept. of Revenue will receive confidential permit data to implement withholding. No separate appropriations or new budget allocations are specified beyond fee remittances.
