Summary

LB1080 amends civil-procedure and related statutes to authorize unsworn declarations (signed under penalty of perjury) for most uses in Nebraska civil courts. Relevant government entities and offices referenced or affected:

- Nebraska courts/the judiciary: broadened authority to accept unsworn declarations in civil proceedings, with limited exceptions; Supreme Court rules may still prohibit use in specific instances.

- Nebraska Supreme Court: retains rulemaking authority to prohibit unsworn declarations where specified.

- Notary public/officials who administer oaths: affidavit requirements clarified; notaries remain relevant where statutes still require sworn affidavits or specific oaths.

- Nebraska Political Accountability and Disclosure Act filings: expressly excluded from these unsworn-declaration provisions.

Criminal statutes (perjury and related offenses) are amended to treat false unsworn declarations meeting statutory requirements as subject to perjury/falsification penalties. No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added text clarifies affidavits and authorizes unsworn declarations in Nebraska civil proceedings. Key points: affidavits must be sworn before an authorized officer (including attorneys), include the officer’s signature, notary seal, title, and commission expiration, and may be signed physically or online and inside or outside the state. An unsworn declaration executed within U.S. boundaries (including Puerto Rico, USVI and other territories) must use specified penalty-of-perjury language, include date and county/state, and may substitute for an affidavit except for ex parte motions/orders without notice, depositions, oaths of office, or oaths required before officials other than notaries. False unsworn declarations meeting sections 25‑1241 or 49‑1808 are a Class II misdemeanor. No state agencies, departments, funding, or appropriations are mentioned.
