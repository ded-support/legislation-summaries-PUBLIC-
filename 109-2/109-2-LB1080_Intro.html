Summary

LB1080 amends Nebraska civil procedure and related criminal statutes to authorize unsworn declarations as substitutes for affidavits in state courts, subject to form requirements (signed under penalty of perjury, date, county/state of signing, executed within U.S. boundaries) and exceptions (ex parte motions and where statute or Supreme Court rule prohibits). It authorizes attorneys' unsworn written statements to have the same effect as sworn attorney statements when signed in a specified form, except not for depositions, oaths of office, or oaths required before specified officials other than a notary public. The bill amends perjury and false-statement statutes to cover materially false unsworn declarations meeting the Uniform Unsworn Foreign Declarations Act or the new statutory requirements. A number of prior statutory sections are repealed. No funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions clarify affidavit and unsworn-declaration use in civil proceedings and reference the following government entities/rules:

- Courts of this state (civil proceedings) and Nebraska Supreme Court rules (may prohibit use of unsworn declarations in specific instances).

- Notary public requirements (officer signature, seal, title, commission expiration; physical or authorized online presence).

- Attorneys may serve as officers before whom affidavits are signed.

- Statutory cross‑references to sections 25‑1241 and 49‑1808 (false unsworn declarations meeting those statutes are Class II misdemeanors).

No changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
