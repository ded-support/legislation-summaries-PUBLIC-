Summary

LB995 amends juvenile justice statutes to strengthen appointment and waiver rules for counsel. It requires juvenile courts to appoint counsel when petitions allege jurisdiction under specified subsections of §43-247 and to continue that right at subsequent hearings. Juvenile courts must inform parties of rights and provide age-appropriate written materials explaining the right to counsel. Waivers must be made in open court, recorded, and signed; be affirmative, intelligent, voluntary, and informed (with courts considering age, intelligence, education, emotional stability, and case complexity); and follow the written-materials requirement. The court must ensure consultation with counsel before any waiver. Waiver is prohibited for juveniles under 14, detention hearings, dispositional hearings seeking out-of-home placement, and transfer-to-adult-court motions; parents may not waive counsel. Appointed counsel is paid by the county for indigent juveniles, and courts may later order parental reimbursement and enforce collection. The Supreme Court, juvenile courts, county governments, and county attorneys are implicated.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds requirements for juvenile courts to ensure juveniles (and their parents/guardians) are informed of the right to counsel and, when applicable, appoint counsel at subsequent juvenile‑court hearings. If counsel is not appointed, the court must advise parties of the right to retain counsel, ask whether they wish to retain counsel, and provide written materials explaining the juvenile’s right to counsel. Materials must be written at a level understandable to the juvenile and developed using best‑practice methods to ensure comprehension. The bill also ties recordkeeping/waiver elements to having informed parties and having provided the written materials (see subdivision (1)(b) of §43‑279). No executive agencies or departments beyond the juvenile court are named, and no funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are included.
