Summary

LB995 amends Neb. Rev. Stat. §§43-272, 43-279, and 43-3102 to strengthen appointment-of-counsel and waiver protections in juvenile court. Juvenile courts must appoint counsel when petitions allege jurisdiction under specified subdivisions and must provide parties written, youth‑friendly materials explaining the right to counsel. The Nebraska Supreme Court is directed to adopt standards/guidelines and a rule‑based process to ensure juveniles can consult with counsel before waiving counsel. Waivers must be made in open court, recorded, signed, affirmative, and evaluated for age, intelligence, emotional stability, and case complexity; juveniles must receive the written materials. Parents/others may not waive the juvenile’s right; waiver is categorically barred in certain proceedings (e.g., under‑14 juveniles, detention hearings, dispositional hearings seeking out‑of‑home placement, transfer motions). Counties bear appointed counsel costs when families cannot afford counsel; courts may later order parental reimbursement under existing collection procedures. No new appropriations are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Added provisions require juvenile courts to advise juveniles and their parent/guardian of the right to counsel at subsequent hearings and to inquire whether they wish to retain counsel. Courts must provide each juvenile and parent/guardian with written materials explaining the juvenile’s right to counsel; the materials must be written at a level understandable to the juvenile and developed using best practices to ensure comprehension. The bill also adds documentary/waiver conditions (including that the juvenile has been provided the required written materials). No executive agencies or departments are named, and there are no changes to funding, appropriations, or budget allocations in the added text.
