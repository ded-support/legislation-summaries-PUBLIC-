Summary

LB966 (Hunger‑Free Schools Act) references the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) in defining "federal reimbursement rate" (Sec. 3(3)) and in tying qualified‑school participation to USDA child nutrition programs (Sec. 3(4)). State reimbursements to schools (Sec. 5) are calculated using USDA‑published federal reimbursement rates for free vs. reduced‑price meals. The bill contains no provisions altering funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department of Agriculture, nor does it appropriate funds to USDA. It does state legislative intent to appropriate General Fund money to the State Department of Education to implement the Act.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Summary focused on the Department of Agriculture:

The added text creates the "Hunger-Free Schools Program" administered by the State Department of Education. Qualified schools must serve and report eligible reduced-price students' meals. The Department of Education will annually reimburse each qualified school an amount equal to the difference between the federal free- and reduced-price meal reimbursement rates (using USDA rates as of Jan 1, 2026), based on meals served in the second preceding school fiscal year. The State Board of Education may adopt rules, and the Legislature intends to appropriate General Fund money to the State Department of Education to implement the Act. No new duties, funding, appropriations, or budget allocations are directed to the State or federal Department of Agriculture; USDA is referenced only as the source of federal reimbursement rates.
