Summary

LB975 (Private Attorney Retention Sunshine Act) imposes new transparency requirements on the Nebraska Attorney General when retaining private counsel. Key agency actions:

- Attorney General: must use an open, competitive bidding process (per §73-807) for legal-service contracts expected to exceed $10,000 (fees plus expenses). Proposed contracts must be filed with the Legislature’s Appropriations Committee (if in session) or the Executive Board of the Legislative Council (if not).

- Appropriations Committee / Executive Board: must hold a public hearing within 10 days and issue a report; the AG must consider the report, file a proposed final contract, and, if omitting recommended changes, provide an explanation; the AG may execute the contract no sooner than 30 days after filing the final version.

- Private attorneys: on contingent-fee matters must disclose hours, expenses, aggregate fee, and an hourly-rate breakdown. The state’s payable rate is capped at $1,000/hour.

Applies to AG contracts executed after the act’s effective date; does not alter the AG’s substantive contracting authority. No appropriations or budget allocations are created, aside from the $1,000/hour fee cap.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds the "Private Attorney Retention Sunshine Act" imposing oversight on Attorney General (AG) use of private counsel. Key agency steps: the AG must use an open, competitive bid process (per §73‑807) and file any legal‑services contract over $10,000 with the Legislature’s Appropriations Committee (if in session) or the Executive Board of the Legislative Council (if not). That body must hold a public hearing within 10 days, issue a report with recommended changes, and the AG must file a proposed final contract (or explain omissions); execution may occur no sooner than 30 days after submission. For contingent‑fee matters, retained attorneys must disclose hours, expenses, aggregate fee, and an hourly‑rate breakdown to the AG. The state fee is capped at $1,000 per hour. The Act does not expand the AG’s contracting authority.
