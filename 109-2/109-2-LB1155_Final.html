Summary

LB1155 revises Department of Revenue confidentiality, access, and audit rules and specifies which state agencies may receive tax or related information. Auditor of Public Accounts and Legislative Audit Office must notify the Tax Commissioner 30 days before audits, provide plans and lists of returns, and comply with the Department’s employee-level confidentiality, secure on‑site or remote inspection, restricted access, secure storage of workpapers, standardized records, and return-of-records requirements. The Tax Commissioner may permit limited inspections by other tax officials and must compile school‑district data (including total AGI) while protecting returns. Municipal sales/use tax access is expanded with certified representatives and secure transmission. Department of Labor may receive tax data for Employment Security, Contractor Registration, and Employee Classification administration; DMV may receive limited return data for IFTA enforcement; Department of Insurance may receive Revenue data for certain tax‑credit/job‑creation programs; Department of Administrative Services is added to misdemeanor disclosure prohibitions. DHHS: probation missing‑child notices may not state DHHS custody status; DHHS may receive absent‑parent information for child‑support statutes. Inspector General staffing is limited to amounts appropriated through the Division of Legislative Oversight; otherwise the bill does not change general appropriations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary
