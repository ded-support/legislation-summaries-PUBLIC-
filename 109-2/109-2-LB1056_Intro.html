Summary

LB1056 references the United States Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics as the source for Consumer Price Index data used in benefit adjustments. The bill ties the annual cost‑of‑living adjustment (COLA) for weekly workers’ compensation income benefits (beginning 2027) to the 12‑month percent change in the CPI for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI‑W) as prepared by the U.S. Department of Labor, BLS (with a 1% floor). It also ties the annual burial expense cap adjustment (beginning 2023) to the 12‑month percent change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI‑U) as prepared by the U.S. Department of Labor, BLS (with a 1% floor and 2.75% annual cap). The bill contains no changes to Department of Labor funding, appropriations, or budget allocations.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

No new funding, appropriations, or budget allocations for the Department of Labor are included.

Substantive additions that may affect Dept. of Labor operations:

- Employers must pay reasonable costs for interpreter services (medical, surgical, hospital, employer- or examiner‑required exams, and independent medical exams); interpreters chosen from the Nebraska Supreme Court register (Rule 6‑702). Employers must provide notices in a language the employee understands.

- Beginning Jan 1, 2027, weekly workers’ compensation benefits lasting >12 months receive annual COLA adjustments: COLA = greater of 1% or the 12‑month CPI‑W change (BLS). The Nebraska Workers’ Compensation Court sets the annual COLA; adjustments are computed to the next whole dollar and capped by statutory maximums.

- Maximum weekly benefit increases to 200% of the state average weekly wage (computed per section 48‑121.02) starting Jan 1, 2027, subject to possible annual suspension by the Governor after a public hearing.

Potential Dept. of Labor impacts: annual provision of the state average weekly wage figure and coordination on wage/CPI data used to compute caps and COLAs.
