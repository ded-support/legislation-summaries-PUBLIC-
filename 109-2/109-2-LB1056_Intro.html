Summary

The bill cites the United States Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics as the source for consumer price index data used in two provisions:

- Cost-of-living adjustments (COLA): Beginning Jan. 1, 2027, annual COLAs for weekly workers’ compensation benefits lasting longer than 12 months are set as the greater of 1% or the 12‑month percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI‑W) as prepared by the U.S. Department of Labor, BLS; the Workers’ Compensation Court determines the annual COLA each Jan. 1.

- Burial expense cap: The annual adjustment to the $11,000 burial expense limit uses the percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI‑U) as prepared by the U.S. Department of Labor, BLS (effective July 1), subject to a 2.75% annual cap.

No appropriations, funding increases, or budget allocations to the Department of Labor are included.

Underlined (Added) Text Summary

Adds employer obligations and benefit adjustments to Nebraska workers’ compensation law. Key points affecting the Department of Labor:

- Employers must pay reasonable interpreter costs for medical/surgical/hospital exams and independent medical examinations when an employee cannot readily understand English; interpreters are chosen from the Rule 6‑702 register. Employers must also give required notices in a language the employee understands.

- Beginning Jan 1, 2027, weekly benefits lasting >12 months receive an automatic annual COLA (greater of 1% or annual CPI‑W change); the Nebraska Workers’ Compensation Court sets the COLA each Jan 1 and applies annual adjustments, capped by statutory maximums.

- Commencing Jan 1, 2027, the maximum weekly benefit is set at 200% of the state average weekly wage (per section 48‑121.02). The Governor may suspend that change after a public hearing.

No direct appropriations or funding changes for the Department of Labor are included, though the department’s published state average weekly wage determines the new cap.
